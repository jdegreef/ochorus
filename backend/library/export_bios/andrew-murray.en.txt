Andrew Murray was born on 9 May 1828 in Graaff-Reinet, in the Cape Colony, the son of a Scottish minister who pleaded week after week that God would pour out His Spirit upon South Africa. At ten he was sent to school in Aberdeen, and he later read theology at Utrecht, where his faith came to a settled assurance. “I have cast myself on Christ,” he wrote home. He was ordained on his twentieth birthday.

His first charge, Bloemfontein, covered some hundred thousand square miles, and he rode for weeks at a stretch to reach lonely farms. In 1856 he married Emma Rutherford, and in 1860 he went to Worcester, where revival broke out — the answer to his father’s prayers. When he tried to restore order, a farmer told him no man could dam what God was doing. In 1871 he settled at Wellington for thirty-five years.

Six times moderator of his church’s Synod, he founded the Huguenot Seminary for women in 1874 and helped form the South African General Mission in 1889. He wrote more than 240 books, testimonies and sermons, above all on prayer, among them Abide in Christ, Humility and The Mystery of the True Vine. In 1879 he lost his voice for two silent years, and a carriage accident in 1893 left him with a bent frame.

He died at Wellington on 18 January 1917, in his eighty-ninth year. His books still teach believers to abide and to pray: “Though in its beginnings prayer is so simple that the feeble child can pray, it is at the same time the highest and holiest work to which man can rise.”
