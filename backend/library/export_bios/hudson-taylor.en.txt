James Hudson Taylor was born on 21 May 1832 in Barnsley, Yorkshire, the son of a Methodist chemist who, with his wife, had prayed over their unborn child, “Grant that he may work for Thee in China.” In June 1849, aged seventeen, he picked up a gospel tract in his father’s library, was struck by the finished work of Christ, and knelt to give himself to the Saviour — on the very afternoon his mother, miles away, prayed for him until she knew the work was done.

Training in medicine at Hull, he set out to learn “to move man, through God, by prayer alone.” In 1853 he sailed for Shanghai, adopted Chinese dress, and in 1858 married Maria Dyer. Back in England, burdened for China’s unreached interior, he surrendered the whole weight to God on the sands at Brighton on 25 June 1865, praying for twenty-four labourers. So the China Inland Mission was born — never to go into debt, never to appeal for money.

The mission spread across province after province, but the cost was heavy: several of the Taylors’ children were buried in China, and Maria died in 1870. Taylor found rest in abiding in Christ, and in 1881 asked God for seventy new missionaries in three years; seventy-three sailed. In 1900 the Boxer Uprising killed fifty-eight of the mission’s missionaries and twenty-one of their children, yet Taylor refused every penny of compensation.

After his second wife, Jennie, died in 1904, he made one last journey to China, and died quietly at Changsha on 3 June 1905. His mission continues today as OMF International. His own words sum up his life: “God’s work done in God’s way will never lack God’s supply.”
