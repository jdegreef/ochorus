Augustine of Hippo — Aurelius Augustinus — was born on 13 November 354 in Thagaste, in Roman North Africa, to a pagan father, Patricius, and a Christian mother, Monica, whose prayers shaped his story. Brilliant and ambitious, he studied at Carthage, lived for years with a woman who bore him a son, Adeodatus, and spent almost nine years among the Manichees, while his mother wept and prayed.

In 384 he won the chair of rhetoric in Milan, where the preaching of Bishop Ambrose began to work on him, though his will would not move. In the late summer of 386, weeping under a fig tree in a garden, he heard a child chanting, “Take up and read.” He opened Paul’s letters at Romans 13, and all the darkness of doubt vanished away. At Easter 387 Ambrose baptised him with his son. Monica died soon after, at Ostia.

In 391 the people of Hippo pressed him into ordination, and he served as their bishop for some thirty-five years — preaching, settling disputes and writing letters across the empire. He wrote the Confessions and The City of God, and contended for grace against the Donatists and Pelagians. Near the end he published his Retractations, correcting himself in public.

In 430 the Vandals laid siege to Hippo. He died on 28 August 430, with the enemy still at the gates, leaving no will but his library to his church. His words still speak for every restless heart: “Thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless, until it repose in Thee.”
