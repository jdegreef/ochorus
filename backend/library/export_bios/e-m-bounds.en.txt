Edward McKendree Bounds was born on 15 August 1835 in Shelby County, Missouri, and named for the frontier bishop of American Methodism. His father died when he was fourteen. After going west in the gold-rush years, he came home, read law, and was admitted to the bar while still a teenager. Converted under the preaching of a Methodist revival, he gave up the practice and was ordained in 1859.

When war came he refused, as a matter of conscience, to swear the oath demanded of him, and was imprisoned for roughly a year and a half. He then served as a chaplain to Confederate troops and was wounded at Franklin, Tennessee. Yet after the war he returned to Franklin as its Methodist pastor, and for years gathered half a dozen men every Tuesday night to kneel and pray for revival.

He later edited the St Louis Christian Advocate. His first wife, Emmie, died in 1886; he married her cousin Harriet, and his home was full of children. Around 1894 he moved to Washington, Georgia, held no prominent pulpit again, and made prayer his principal work — rising at four in the morning and praying until seven, and writing eight manuscripts on prayer.

Only one, Power Through Prayer, appeared in his lifetime. He died at home on 24 August 1913, and friends published the rest; they have since been read in dozens of languages. Shortly before the end he wrote: “When He is ready, I am ready; I long to taste the joys of the heavenlies.”
