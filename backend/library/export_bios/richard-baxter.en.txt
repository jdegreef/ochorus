Richard Baxter was born on 12 November 1615 at Rowton in Shropshire. He grew up with poor religious teaching and was awakened not by a preacher but by books — among them Richard Sibbes’s The Bruised Reed, bought from a pedlar at the door. He never went to university and felt the lack for sixty years, but taught himself ferociously and was ordained in 1638.

In 1641 he came to Kidderminster, a weaving town known for drink and ignorance. He preached, and with his assistant sat down with fourteen families every week, house by house. The congregation outgrew the church and five galleries were added; on Sundays a hundred families could be heard singing psalms in their homes. Out of this came The Reformed Pastor.

Plagued for some fifty years by illnesses, he expected death constantly. Convalescing with no book but his Bible, he wrote The Saints’ Everlasting Rest, one of the most loved devotional books in English. At the Restoration he refused the bishopric of Hereford, and in 1662 he was ejected with nearly two thousand ministers. That year he married Margaret Charlton, and the marriage was exceptionally happy. In 1685 he was tried before Sir George Jeffreys and imprisoned for about eighteen months.

He wrote something in the region of a hundred and sixty books. Near the end, asked how he did, he answered, “Almost well.” He died on 8 December 1691, aged seventy-six, and was buried beside Margaret. His own lines remain his portrait: “I Preach’d, as never sure to Preach again, And as a dying man to dying men!”
