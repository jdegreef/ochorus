Gareth Evans was born in 1938 in Merthyr Tydfil, among the coal valleys of South Wales. His mother died when he was thirteen, and at sixteen, through a Friday-night youth meeting and a young preacher’s direct question — “What will you do with the Lord Jesus Christ?” — he gave his life to Christ. A word from an older believer became his lifelong motto: “I will walk with Jesus, not ahead of Him nor lag behind.”

He read physics at the University of Swansea, where he met Anne; they married in 1961. For two decades his pulpit was a classroom — in Wales, in army schools in Germany and Hong Kong, where he worked alongside Jackie Pullinger in the walled city of Kowloon, and later in Toronto — and in every school he taught he also started a Christian fellowship. The precision of the laboratory still gives his Bible teaching its clarity.

In the early 1980s he left teaching to become a pastor with the Christian and Missionary Alliance, first in Kitchener, Ontario, and then at Victoria Alliance Church on Vancouver Island, where he and Anne still make their home. From 1991 to 1994 they served as chaplains aboard the Mercy Ship Anastasis in West Africa, and invitations followed to teach pastors and students in Australia, Brazil, Mexico, India, Nepal, Nigeria, Haiti and beyond.

Out of that long walk came his books — unhurried, plain and pastoral, written for the hurting church and for believers who need to be reminded that God holds their tomorrows. Gareth and Anne have three daughters, eight grandchildren and fourteen great-grandchildren. Today he ministers largely through his website, still carrying a burden to disciple the young, one stepping stone at a time.
