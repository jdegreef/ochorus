Hannah Whitall Smith was born Hannah Tatum Whitall on 7 February 1832 in Philadelphia, into a prominent and devout Quaker family that opposed slavery and threw itself into the reform causes of the day. Her childhood, she said, was “one fairy scene of sunshine and of flowers,” yet at about sixteen she began a long, inward search, and her early diary “is one long record of wrestlings and agonies.” In 1851, at nineteen, she married Robert Pearsall Smith.

The decisive turn came in 1865. A Christian for nine years, yet “continually disappointed in myself,” she discovered that deliverance from sin’s power was God’s work in those who surrender to Him — “Not I, but Christ.” That became the heart of The Christian’s Secret of a Happy Life (1875), which sold more than two million copies in her lifetime and has never gone out of print.

In 1873 and 1874 the Smiths crossed to England, where her Bible readings made her the “angel of the churches” and their meetings helped give rise to the Keswick Convention. Then came sorrow: Robert’s ministry ended abruptly in 1875 and he drifted into agnosticism; four of her seven children died in youth; and the three who lived grew away from her faith. She helped found the Woman’s Christian Temperance Union and wrote The God of All Comfort (1906).

Arthritis confined her to a wheelchair in her last years, yet her outlook only brightened. She died in England on 1 May 1911, aged seventy-nine, having written: “Secure in the knowledge that God is, and that He is enough, I find old age delightful in the present, and death a delicious prospect for the future.”
