Reuben Archer Torrey was born on 28 January 1856 into a wealthy Hoboken, New Jersey family, the son of a mother of deep and persistent prayer. At Yale he gave himself to the theatre, the racetrack, cards and drink. On a spring night in 1875, resolved to end his life, he found himself praying instead, remembering his mother’s prayers, and promised God he would do whatever He asked — “I’ll even preach!”

Ordained in 1878, he studied at Leipzig and Erlangen and came home a lifelong defender of the Scriptures. In Minneapolis in 1888 he gave up his salary to work among the poor, trusting God for every meal his family ate. In 1889 D. L. Moody called him to Chicago, where he led the school that became the Moody Bible Institute until 1908 and pastored the Chicago Avenue Church.

From 1898 as many as three hundred people met with him each Saturday night to pray for worldwide revival. From 1902 to 1905, with Charles M. Alexander, he preached in Australia, New Zealand, China, Japan, India and the British Isles, with around one hundred and two thousand conversions usually reported. He wrote more than forty books, chiefly on prayer and the Holy Spirit.

In 1912 he became first dean of the Bible Institute of Los Angeles. He died at Asheville, North Carolina, on 26 October 1928, remembered as a man of regular and uninterrupted prayer. His own words in How to Pray remain his counsel: “Prayer is God’s appointed way for obtaining things, and the great secret of all lack in our experience, in our life and in our work is neglect of prayer.”
