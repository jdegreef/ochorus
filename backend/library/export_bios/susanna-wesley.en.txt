Susanna Wesley was born Susanna Annesley in London on 20 January 1669, the youngest of some twenty-five children of Dr. Samuel Annesley, a leading Dissenting minister. With the run of her father’s books she taught herself theology, philosophy and above all Scripture, and by thirteen had chosen the Church of England for herself. In 1688 she married Samuel Wesley, a difficult clergyman, and settled at the rectory of Epworth.

It was not an easy marriage: money was always short, and Samuel was imprisoned for debt. Of nineteen children she bore, nine died in infancy; ten lived to adulthood. She taught each of them herself — school began on a fifth birthday — and set aside an evening every week to talk with each child alone about their soul. When she could not leave the room to pray, she drew her apron over her head, and the children knew not to disturb her.

In February 1709 the rectory burned, and five-year-old John was pulled from an upper window moments before the roof fell. She resolved to be “more particularly careful of the soul of this child.” Her Sunday evening kitchen devotions grew to some two hundred people, and her letters followed her sons, John and Charles, whose ministry kindled the evangelical revival and founded Methodism.

Widowed in 1735, she spent her last years in London and died on 23 July 1742, aged seventy-three, with her children around her. She left them a last charge: “Children, as soon as I am released, sing a psalm of praise to God.”
