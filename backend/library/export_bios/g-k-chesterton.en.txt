Gilbert Keith Chesterton was born on 29 May 1874 in Kensington, London. While training as an illustrator at the Slade School of Art he fell into a near despair, and held on, he said, by a thin thread of thanks — the sense that it was good to exist at all. In 1901 he married Frances Blogg, whose quiet, practised faith drew him into the Church of England.

He became a journalist, and kept a weekly column in the Illustrated London News for the rest of his life. He loved an argument and chose the biggest opponents he could find: Blatchford, Wells, and Shaw, who remained his friend through thirty years of debate. When critics of Heretics (1905) complained that he never stated his own philosophy, he answered with Orthodoxy (1908): “I am the man who with the utmost daring discovered what had been discovered before.”

A friendship with Father John O’Connor gave the world Father Brown. In 1909 he and Frances moved to Beaconsfield. He nearly died in the winter of 1914–15, and both of them spoke of his recovery as a miracle. In 1922 he was received into the Roman Catholic Church. St Francis of Assisi followed in 1923 and The Everlasting Man in 1925 — a book C. S. Lewis counted among those that brought him to faith.

He died at Beaconsfield on 14 June 1936, with Frances beside him. Christians of every tradition still read him for a joyful, fighting faith that saw the world as a gift calling for thanks. “Joy, which was the small publicity of the pagan, is the gigantic secret of the Christian.”
