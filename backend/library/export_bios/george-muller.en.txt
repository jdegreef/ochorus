George Müller was born on 27 September 1805 at Kroppenstedt, in the Kingdom of Prussia, the son of a tax collector. By his own account he was a liar and a thief as a boy, and at sixteen he spent several weeks in prison for fraud. Then, in November 1825, a divinity student at Halle, he went with a friend to a small meeting in a private house and saw a man kneel to pray. “I had never seen any one on his knees,” he wrote. He went home a changed man.

In 1829 he sailed for England. At Teignmouth he gave up his fixed salary, resolving never to ask anyone for money but to tell his needs only to God, and in 1830 he married Mary Groves. In 1832 they moved to Bristol, where he preached at Bethesda Chapel for the rest of his life.

There he set out to prove that God still hears prayer. From a rented house on Wilson Street in 1836, the orphan work grew to five great houses on Ashley Down; he cared for 10,024 orphans without once issuing an appeal, recording every answer in his journals. Once the children sat before empty plates while he gave thanks — and the baker knocked with bread. From 1875 to 1892 he travelled some two hundred thousand miles, preaching in forty-two countries.

He died in his room at Ashley Down on 10 March 1898, in his ninety-third year, and tens of thousands lined Bristol’s streets. He left behind, as he had wished, a record of a God who hears — “that it may be seen that God is faithful still, and hears prayer still.”
