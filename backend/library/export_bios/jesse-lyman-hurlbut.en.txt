Jesse Lyman Hurlbut was born in New York City in 1843. Little is recorded of his early years, and no account of how his faith began survives. He graduated from Wesleyan University in 1864, taught languages at Pennington Seminary, and entered the Newark Conference of the Methodist Episcopal Church. He married Mary Matilda Chase and served pastorates across northern New Jersey. In the Sunday school, the least glamorous corner of church life, he found his work.

In 1875 he came to the Sunday School Assembly at Chautauqua Lake, and never really left. As one of John Heyl Vincent’s closest helpers he ran the teacher-training Normal Courses, taught an adult Bible class for decades, and helped direct the Chautauqua Literary and Scientific Circle. From 1888 he edited the Methodist Sunday-school literature, and from 1889 to 1892 he led the new Epworth League as its general secretary.

At sixty-one he published Hurlbut’s Story of the Bible (1904), retelling Scripture from Genesis to Revelation in 168 stories, kept close to its own words so as to lead young readers to the Bible itself; more than four million copies have been counted. Hurlbut’s Life of Christ (1915) followed, with no invented scenes or talk.

He was superintendent of the Newark District from 1909 until he retired in 1914, and in 1921 wrote The Story of Chautauqua. He died in New Jersey in 1930, aged eighty-seven, and the next year a memorial church in his name opened by the lake where he had taught for more than fifty summers. As he wrote: “Those who in childhood learn the Story of the Bible are fortunate, for they will never forget it.”
