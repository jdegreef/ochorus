Fred Francis Bosworth was born on 17 January 1877 on a farm near Utica, Nebraska, and raised in a Methodist home. He left home at sixteen, and around that time gave his life to Christ at a revival meeting in Omaha. Later, given up to die of tuberculosis, he went to Fitzgerald, Georgia, to say good-bye to his family; there a woman evangelist prayed for him, and “from that self-same hour Fred began to mend.”

At Zion City, Illinois, in 1906, he received the baptism in the Holy Spirit and a call to preach. About 1909 he planted a church in Dallas, where the family put their heads into an empty bread tin and said “Glory.” In 1911, after preaching to Black and white crowds at Hearne, Texas, he was beaten by a mob, and walked nine miles through the night to Calvert, interceding for the men who had beaten him.

An early leader in the Assemblies of God, he resigned in 1918 rather than teach that all must speak in tongues, and soon buried his son Vernon and his wife Estella. From 1920 in Lima, Ohio, he preached that it was “God’s will to heal as well as to save,” and his campaigns filled halls across the United States and Canada. His Christ the Healer (1924) has never gone out of print, though its teaching is seriously contested.

In 1944 he publicly apologised for a doctrinal error, and in 1948 joined William Branham’s team, preaching to tens of thousands in South Africa past seventy-four. He died in Miami on 23 January 1958. The heart of his message he had put in one line: “Faith begins where the will of God is known.”
