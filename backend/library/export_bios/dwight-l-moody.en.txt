Dwight Lyman Moody was born on 5 February 1837 on a small farm at Northfield, Massachusetts, the sixth of nine children. His father died when he was four, and he had perhaps five years of schooling. At seventeen he went to work in his uncle’s shoe store in Boston, and there, on 21 April 1855, his Sunday school teacher Edward Kimball found him wrapping shoes and told him of the love of Christ. Among the shelves, Moody gave his heart to the Saviour.

In Chicago he built a Sunday school in a slum called “Little Hell” that drew fifteen hundred children, then gave up a flourishing business to give God all his time. In 1871, after the Great Chicago Fire and the prayers of two women, God’s presence broke upon him so overwhelmingly that his ministry was transformed. In 1873 he sailed for Britain with the singer Ira Sankey and came home the best-known evangelist alive.

Never ordained, he preached, by sober estimate, to as many as a hundred million people. The man with five years of schooling founded the Northfield Seminary, Mount Hermon School, the summer conferences that kindled the Student Volunteer Movement, the Moody Bible Institute in Chicago, and a colportage work printing Christian books cheap enough for a farmhand’s wages.

He died at Northfield on 22 December 1899, in the room where he had been born. Months before, he had said: “Some day you will read in the papers that D. L. Moody, of East Northfield, is dead. Don’t you believe a word of it! At that moment I shall be more alive than I am now.”
