Smith Wigglesworth was born in June 1859 at Menston, near Bradford, into a family so poor that some days there was no food in the house. He went to work in the fields at six and into a woollen mill at seven, and was robbed of an education. Yet he could never recollect a time when he did not long for God, and at eight, at a revival in his grandmother’s Wesleyan chapel, he looked to Christ and knew he was born again.

At sixteen he joined the Salvation Army, and learned plumbing from a godly Brethren steam-fitter. On 4 December 1882 he married Mary Jane Featherstone — Polly — a gifted Salvation Army preacher, who taught him to read. Together they ran the Bowland Street Mission in Bradford for some thirty years, and after he was instantly freed of pain while being prayed for, his ministry of divine healing grew.

In October 1907, at All Saints’, Monkwearmouth, in Sunderland, he received the baptism in the Holy Spirit. In 1913 Polly died at the door of the mission; “She is Mine; her work is done,” he heard God say. From 1914 he preached across America, Europe, Australia and New Zealand, India, Ceylon and South Africa, often weeping through his addresses. His sermons were gathered in Ever Increasing Faith and Faith That Prevails.

He went on preaching into his late eighties and died at Wakefield on 12 March 1947, aged eighty-seven, at a friend’s funeral. He always gave God the credit, for, as he said, God “has made the condition as simple as He possibly could — ‘Only believe.’”
