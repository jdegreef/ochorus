Julia A. J. Foote was born on May 21, 1823, in Schenectady, New York, to parents who had known slavery. Sent out at ten to work as a servant, she learned to read so that she could read the Testament herself. At fifteen she was converted at a quarterly meeting, and soon, after an old saint opened the Scriptures to her, she entered what she called “the work of full salvation” — sanctification as a distinct gift of the Holy Spirit.

She married George Foote, a sailor, and moved to Boston, where her testimony divided her class-meeting. The call to preach came against her will — “No, Lord, not me” — until she could refuse no longer. Her AME Zion minister had her tried and cut off, and the conference gave her no redress. She kept preaching as an independent evangelist, trusting God alone.

For decades she travelled through New England, New York, Ohio, the Midwest and Canada, often poor and ill, preaching wherever a door opened; in 1878 some five thousand heard her at Lodi, Ohio. Her defense of women’s preaching was simple: whatever the Spirit gives, he gives to women and men alike. In 1879 she published her life story, A Brand Plucked from the Fire.

In 1894 the AME Zion Church, which had once cast her out, made her its first woman deacon, and in 1900 an elder, only the second woman in that office. She died that same year, in her late seventies. Of her trials she wrote: “I had constant access to God, and a clear consciousness that he heard me.”
