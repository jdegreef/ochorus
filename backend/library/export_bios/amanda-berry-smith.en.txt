Amanda Berry Smith was born into slavery on 23 January 1837 in Long Green, Baltimore County, Maryland, one of thirteen children. Her father worked deep into the nights making brooms and mats until he had bought his family’s liberty. She had only a few months of school, went into service at thirteen, and long earned her bread as a washerwoman. In 1854 she married; about two years later, in a grave illness, she was converted.

Grief stayed long: by thirty-two she had buried two husbands and four of her five children. In September 1868, at Green Street Methodist Episcopal Church in New York, she received what holiness people called “a clean heart.” The only Black woman there, she overcame her fear, rose and shouted “Glory to Jesus!” The next year she felt called to preach, and with no ordination or salary she sang and spoke at camp meetings across the land.

In 1878 she sailed for England, beginning twelve years abroad: Britain, some eighteen months in India, and eight years in West Africa as an independent missionary, trusting God for every fare, which often came just as it was needed. Her Autobiography appeared in 1893, and its proceeds went toward the dream of her old age: in 1899 she opened an orphanage for destitute Black children in Harvey, Illinois.

In 1912 she retired to Sebring, Florida, where she died on 24 February 1915. She liked to say she had been set free twice: “I have been bought twice, and set free twice, and so I feel I have a good right to shout.”
