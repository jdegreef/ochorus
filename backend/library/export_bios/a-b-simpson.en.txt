Albert Benjamin Simpson was born on 15 December 1843 at Bayview, Prince Edward Island, Canada, into a devout Scottish Presbyterian home, and grew up on a farm in western Ontario. As a teenager, worn down by study and unsure he knew the God he meant to serve, he found light in Walter Marshall’s Gospel Mystery of Sanctification, and came to Christ at about fifteen.

After Knox College, Toronto, he was called in 1865 to Knox Church, Hamilton, and in 1873 to Louisville, Kentucky. In 1879 he took a New York pulpit, but was burdened for the immigrants and the poor outside its doors. In the summer of 1881, sickly for years, he took Christ as his healer, and that November he resigned, with no salary and six children to feed, to found a Gospel Tabernacle where every seat was free.

In 1883 he opened the first Bible college in North America, later settled at Nyack. Two societies he formed in 1887 united in 1897 as the Christian and Missionary Alliance, which became a missionary force on every continent. He wrote hymns, edited a magazine and wrote more than a hundred books, among them Days of Heaven Upon Earth and The Fourfold Gospel — Christ our Saviour, Sanctifier, Healer, and Coming King.

He worked at full stretch into his seventies, and died at Nyack on 29 October 1919. His message never changed: not the blessing, but Christ Himself. As he sang in his hymn, “Once it was the blessing, now it is the Lord.”
