Aiden Wilson Tozer was born on 21 April 1897 near La Jose, in the farm country of western Pennsylvania. He never sat in a high school classroom or a seminary; he taught himself from libraries and second-hand bookshops. At about seventeen, walking home in Akron, Ohio, he heard a street preacher urge his listeners to call on God for mercy, went up to the attic, and gave his life to Christ.

In 1918 he married Ada Cecelia Pfautz, and in 1920 he was ordained in the Christian and Missionary Alliance without a single formal credential. In 1928 he came to Southside Alliance Church in Chicago, where he served for thirty-one years, and from 1950 he edited the Alliance’s magazine. He preached against a shallow, comfortable faith and called believers back to God Himself. “What comes into our minds when we think about God is the most important thing about us.”

He often prayed lying face-down on his study floor, for hours, gazing on God and worshipping. Out of that hunger came The Pursuit of God (1948): “O God, I have tasted Thy goodness, and it has both satisfied me and made me thirsty for more.” His biographers do not hide that the same absorption made him distant from his wife and seven children.

In 1959 he moved to Avenue Road Church in Toronto, where he died on 12 May 1963. He was buried at last in Akron, Ohio, under a stone that reads simply: “A Man of God.”
