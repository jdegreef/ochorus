— 2 Peter 3:17–18

Second Peter ends where it began. Its first chapter called its readers to grow, adding goodness, knowledge and love to their faith; its last sentence calls them to keep growing in grace and in knowing Christ. Between the two stand the false teachers and the scoffers. The letter’s answer to both is not simply to argue with them, but to grow so deep in Christ that their words lose their pull.

The question the letter leaves

The question 2 Peter leaves is what to do with the waiting. Its readers had been told the Lord was coming, and he had not come, and some were laughing at them. Peter does not set a date. He tells them that God’s patience is itself a kind of salvation, giving time for people to turn back, and that the right response to the waiting is a holy and godly life. “Regard our Lord’s patience as salvation.”

Where to go from here

A few places in this library to read further:

  • On growing in grace (3:18). J. C. Ryle’s chapter Growth, in Holiness; Charles G. Finney’s Growth in Grace, the last of his Lectures on Revivals of Religion; and two sermons on the verse, A. W. Tozer’s How to Grow in Grace and A. B. Simpson’s Spiritual Growth.
  • On God’s call and choice (1:10). Ryle’s Election, in Old Paths.
  • On the prophetic word (1:19–21). Simpson’s The Holy Spirit in the Epistles of Peter, in The Holy Spirit, or Power from on High, Part II; and R. A. Torrey’s chapter on inspiration in The Fundamental Doctrines of the Christian Faith.
  • On holy living as we wait (3:11). Andrew Murray’s Holiness and Heaven, the last reading in Holy in Christ.
  • On sharing the divine nature (1:4). Athanasius of Alexandria’s On the Incarnation, the classic account of the Word made flesh to restore human beings to God’s life.
  • On how the letter was received. Eusebius of Caesarea’s The Church History, which records which books the early churches accepted and which they still weighed.

Every reference to 2 Peter in this library can be tapped to read the verse, and the Scripture pages gather what the great writers of the church have said about each passage.

But the best next step may be the one Peter asks for: to remember. He wrote so that his readers could recall these things whenever they needed them. Read the letter again when the waiting feels long, and hold on to its promise:
