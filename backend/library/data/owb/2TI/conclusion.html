— 2 Timothy 4:8

Paul’s last look is forward. Behind him are the race, the fight and the faith he kept; around him are chains, cold and empty seats where his friends should be. But ahead of him is a crown, given by a righteous judge, on “that day”. And he will not wear it alone. It is for “all who have longed for his appearing”, which means it is for Timothy too, and for every reader who loves the Lord’s coming.

The question the letter leaves

Who will carry it on? That is the anxious question under the whole letter. Paul is about to die. The churches of Asia have turned away from him. False teachers are spreading. Demas has gone. Everything seems to depend on one young, gifted, perhaps fearful man. So Paul tells him to stir up the gift, to guard the treasure, to pass it on to trustworthy people, to continue in the Scriptures and to preach the word.

But the letter is just as clear that it does not finally depend on Timothy, or on Paul. “God’s word is not chained.” “God’s solid foundation stands firm.” “He remains faithful.” “The Lord stood by me and gave me strength.” The one Paul has believed is able to guard what Paul has entrusted to him. The gospel has outlived Paul’s chains, and Nero’s empire, and every generation since, because the Lord who stood by Paul has stood by his people still.

Every reader is in Timothy’s place: given something precious by those who went before, and asked to hand it on. For most of us that will not mean a prison cell. It may mean a grandmother’s faith passed to a grandchild, a Bible class faithfully taught, a friend visited when others stay away, as Onesiphorus searched Rome until he found Paul.

Where to go from here

This library holds many who heard Paul’s charge as addressed to them:

  • On the gift and the fire (1:6–7). Gareth Evans’s sermon Guard the Fire, preached on Pentecost Sunday.
  • On the Scriptures (3:14–17). John Calvin’s sermon All Scripture Is Given by Inspiration, on 3:16–17.
  • On preaching the word (4:1–5). Charles H. Spurgeon’s Lectures to My Students and An All-Round Ministry, his addresses to ministers; and John Chrysostom’s On the Priesthood.
  • On Paul’s last imprisonment and death. Eusebius’s The Church History, whose second book quotes this letter in telling of Paul’s first defense and his martyrdom in Rome; and The First Epistle of Clement to the Corinthians, written from Rome a generation later, which remembers Paul as “a striking example of patience”.
  • On keeping the faith to the end (4:7). The Epistle and Martyrdom of Polycarp, the account of an old bishop who would not deny his King; and Fox’s Book of Martyrs, which begins with the apostles, Paul among them, and tells of William Tyndale’s imprisonment and death at Vilvoorde.

And read 1 Timothy and Titus, in this same edition, to see the same apostle earlier, setting churches in order.

Every reference to 2 Timothy in this library can be tapped to read the verse, and the Scripture pages gather what the great writers of the church have said about each passage.
