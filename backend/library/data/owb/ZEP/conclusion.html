— Zephaniah 3:20

Zephaniah ends with a homecoming. The book that opened with the LORD sweeping away everything on the face of the earth closes with him gathering the scattered, rescuing the lame, and turning disgrace into honour “before your very eyes”. The last words belong to God: “says the LORD”.

The question the book leaves with you

Zephaniah’s first readers lived in a prosperous city that had grown used to God. They had not stopped believing in him; they had stopped expecting him to do anything. The book’s question is whether we have done the same. Do we live as if the LORD “will do nothing, neither good nor bad”?

Its answer is that he will do both. He will come in judgement, and no wealth will shelter anyone from it. And he will come to save, and his joy over his people will be greater than theirs over him. The way between the two is the narrow one the prophet pointed to in 2:3: seek the LORD, seek righteousness, seek humility. The people who are left at the end are not the strong or the clever, but “a people humble and lowly”.

Where it points

The day of the LORD did come on Jerusalem, a generation later, when Babylon burned the city in 586 BC. But Zephaniah’s day is larger than any one disaster, and the New Testament still looks ahead to it. And the promise that God would purify the speech of the peoples, “so that all of them may call on the name of the LORD”, began to be kept at Pentecost, when people from every nation heard the apostles speak in their own languages, and Peter declared that “whoever calls on the name of the Lord will be saved”. The God who sings over his people is the Father of the parable, who runs to meet his returning son and fills the house with music and dancing.

Where to go from here

A few places in this library to take Zephaniah further:

  • On the God who sings (3:17). Daughters of the King – 30 Days with God for Girls – Book 1, an Ochorus Original, whose “Day 6 — He Sings over You” is on Zephaniah 3:17; and Alexander Maclaren’s sermon The Prodigal and His Father (Luke 15:11–24).
  • On seeking humility (2:3; 3:12). Andrew Murray’s Humility, and Dwight L. Moody’s sermon Humility (Matthew 11:29).
  • On divided loyalty (1:5). Charles H. Spurgeon’s Morning by Morning, the reading for November 14, on Zephaniah 1:5.
  • On turning before the day comes (2:1–3). R. A. Torrey’s sermon The Day of Golden Opportunity (Hebrews 3:7).
  • On Zephaniah and Christ. Augustine’s The City of God, Book XVIII, chapter 33, on what Jeremiah and Zephaniah foretold of Christ and the calling of the nations.

And read Acts 2 in this same edition, where the speech of the peoples is made new.
