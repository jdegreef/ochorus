Joel ends where it began, with God and his people and the land. The book opened on a temple with no offerings and a people in sackcloth. It closes with a spring flowing from the house of the LORD, and with the plainest promise a prophet can make: God is here, and he is staying.

The question the book leaves with you

Joel never explains why the locusts came. He does not list the people’s sins, as Amos and Hosea do. What he asks is what they will do now, in the ruins, and he answers for them: turn back to the LORD with all your heart. His “Yet even now” is the hinge of the book. However late it is, however bare the land, it is not too late to return to a God who is “gracious and full of tender compassion”.

And the book asks whom we expect God to pour out his Spirit on. Joel’s answer, everyone, sons and daughters, old and young, servants as well, is the answer of Pentecost. The church began on the day Peter read Joel aloud and said that it was happening.

Where to go from here

  • On the Spirit poured out (Joel 2:28–29). A. B. Simpson’s The Holy Spirit, or Power from on High, with its chapter “The Holy Spirit in the Book of Joel”; A. W. Tozer’s sermon All with One Accord (Acts 2:1), on the praying church to which Joel’s promise came; and Robert Murray M’Cheyne’s The Cry for Revival (Psalm 85:6), which pleads that promise for his own congregation.
  • On calling on the name of the LORD (Joel 2:32). Charles H. Spurgeon’s A Simple Sermon for Seeking Souls, on Paul’s use of Joel’s words in Romans 10:13; and his reading for January 16 in The Cheque Book of the Bank of Faith.
  • On returning with the heart (Joel 2:12–13). Spurgeon’s reading for December 18 in Morning by Morning, and the chapter on repentance in R. A. Torrey’s What the Bible Teaches.
  • On the years the locust has eaten (Joel 2:25). Spurgeon’s reading for May 18 in The Cheque Book of the Bank of Faith.
  • On telling the next generation (Joel 1:3). Spurgeon’s reading for July 11 in Evening by Evening.
  • On Joel and the gospel. Augustine’s The City of God, book XVIII, chapter 30, where he sets Joel beside Micah and Jonah.
