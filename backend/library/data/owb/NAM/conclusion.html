— Nahum 3:18–19

Nahum ends at a graveside. The king of Assyria’s shepherds are asleep, his people are scattered across the mountains, and the nations he ground down stand around clapping their hands. The great city that seemed as if it would last for ever is gone, and two centuries later a Greek army marching past its mounds did not even know its name.

The question the book leaves with you

Nahum’s last question is put to the king of Assyria: who has not felt your cruelty? But its first question is put to everyone: “Who can stand before his indignation?” The book does not let us stand on Judah’s side of the walls and enjoy the spectacle. It asks where we take refuge. Nineveh trusted its rivers, its walls, its merchants “until they outnumber the stars of the sky”, and they scattered like locusts when the sun came up. The LORD, Nahum says, “is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble”.

The book also leaves a quieter question for anyone who has suffered at the hands of the powerful: will God do anything? Nahum’s answer is yes. He is slow to grow angry, and he waits, as he waited for Nineveh in Jonah’s day. But he sees, and he will not acquit the guilty for ever.

Where it points

The good news on the mountains in 1:15 did not stop with Assyria. Isaiah heard it again for the exiles in Babylon, and Paul heard it in the sending of the apostles: “How lovely are the feet of those who bring the good news of good things!” (Romans 10:15). The New Testament announces a greater freedom than Judah’s, from a greater tyranny than Nineveh’s, and the end of every city built with blood. In Revelation, the last Babylon falls, and the merchants who grew rich from her weep.

Where to go from here

A few places in this library to take Nahum further:

  • On the jealousy and patience of God (1:2–3). Charles H. Spurgeon’s Morning by Morning, the reading for September 12, on “God is jealous” (Nahum 1:2), and his Evening by Evening, the reading for February 22, on “The Lord is slow to anger, and great in power” (Nahum 1:3).
  • On Nahum and Christ. Augustine’s The City of God, Book XVIII, chapter 31, on what Obadiah, Nahum and Habakkuk foretold of the salvation of the world in Christ.
  • On the wrath of God (1:6). Jonathan Edwards’s sermon Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God (Deuteronomy 32:35), and Martyn Lloyd-Jones’s The Wrath of God (Romans 1:18).
  • On God as a stronghold (1:7). A. W. Tozer’s God Is Our Refuge and Strength (Psalm 46:1), and Alexander Maclaren’s The City and River of God (Psalm 46:4–7).
  • On the feet of those who bring good news (1:15). A. B. Simpson’s sermon The Logic of Missions (Romans 10:14–15).

And read Revelation in this same edition, especially chapters 17 and 18, where the fall of Babylon the great is told in words that echo Nahum’s.
