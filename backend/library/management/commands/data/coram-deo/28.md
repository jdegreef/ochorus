## Conclusion: Moving Forward with God

> "Of this I am convinced: the One who started a good work in you will keep working at it until it is finished on the day of Christ Jesus."
> — Philippians 1:6 (OWB)

When a traveller reaches the top of a long hill, she often stops and turns round. Below her lies the whole road: the river she crossed at dawn, the market town where she rested, the steep places where she nearly turned back. On the road it was one step after another. From the hilltop it looks like a journey.

We have come to that kind of place. This book began with a face. In the Introduction we heard David's prayer: "My heart says to You what You have said: 'Seek My face.' So I will seek Your face, LORD" (Psalm 27:8). We saw that every one of us lives before someone's face, and that the Reformers named a better way: coram Deo, living before the face of God. Since then we have walked a long road together.

So let us stop on the hilltop, look back, and then look ahead. This is not the end of anything. As the Introduction said, this is not a book to finish, but a life to begin.

### Looking back on the journey

Part One, Foundations, asked the first question of all: whose face are we seeking? We met Almighty God, our Father, holy and sovereign and yet near enough to be called Abba (Chapter 1); Jesus Christ, our Redeemer, who died and rose so that the hiding that began in Eden could end (Chapter 2); and the Holy Spirit, our Helper, who gives new birth, lives within us and empowers us (Chapter 3).

Part Two, Walking with God, showed how we meet this God day by day. Love stands at the heart of it all (Chapter 4). We hear Him in His Word (Chapter 5), answer Him in prayer (Chapter 6), give Him the first moments of the day in the morning watch (Chapter 7), bear fruit by abiding in the vine (Chapter 8), and keep near to Him through the spiritual disciplines (Chapter 9).

Part Three, Becoming Like Christ, explored how God changes us. We learned what it means to be a disciple (Chapter 10) and how to live the upside-down life of the Kingdom from the Sermon on the Mount (Chapter 11). We learned to trust God (Chapter 12), to walk in holiness and victory over sin (Chapter 13), and to let Him heal and grow the soul (Chapter 14). We faced the reality of spiritual warfare and Christ's victory (Chapter 15), and we stood with believers in their suffering under the sovereign hand of God (Chapter 16).

Part Four, Living as God's People, carried all of this into marriage and family, work, the Church, its ministries, spiritual gifts and servant ministry (Chapters 17 to 23). Part Five, Living for God's Purposes, lifted our eyes to mission, stewardship, finishing well and the new heavens and new earth (Chapters 24 to 27).

That is a lot of road. If you have not yet put half of it into practice, you are in good company. The point of looking back is not to grade yourself. It is to see that every part of the road leads to the same place: the face of God.

### The thread that runs through it all

If you pulled one thread through every chapter of this book, it would be this: we were created for relationship with God, restored to it through Christ, and empowered for it by the Spirit.

We were created for it. God did not make us because He needed servants. He made us out of the overflow of His love, to know Him and enjoy Him. Jesus said, "And eternal life is this: that they know You, the only true God, and the one You sent, Jesus Christ" (John 17:3). Not knowing about Him, but knowing Him.

We were restored to it through Christ. Sin sent us into hiding, but God came looking for us, and in the fullness of time He came in person. Paul says that God "is the one who shone in our hearts, to bring the light of the knowledge of God's glory, the glory seen in the face of Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6). In Christ we can stand before God's face without shame.

We are empowered for it by the Spirit. We could never keep this relationship alive by our own strength. "Since our life comes from the Spirit, let us also follow the Spirit's lead in each step we take" (Galatians 5:25). The Spirit cries "Abba, Father" in our hearts (Romans 8:15), opens the Word to us and slowly changes us. "But all of us, our faces uncovered, look upon the Lord's glory as in a mirror, and we are being changed into the same image we see, from glory to ever greater glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

Created, restored, empowered. Everything else in this book, from the morning watch to the way we manage money, hangs on that thread.

### Building a rule of life

How do you carry all of this forward without being crushed by it? Christians down the centuries have found help in what they called a rule of life. Rule here does not mean a list of laws. Think of a trellis, the frame a farmer builds so that a vine can climb towards the sun. The trellis does not make the vine grow. Only life does that. But it gives the life a shape.

A rule of life is a written pattern of the few practices that keep you near to God, in a rhythm you can actually live. A rule that works only on your best week is a wish.

From the chapters of this book, five strands belong in almost every rule of life:

1. **Scripture.** A daily portion of God's Word, read slowly and with a listening heart (Chapter 5).
2. **Prayer.** Talking with God and listening to Him, through the day as well as at set times (Chapter 6).
3. **The morning watch.** A first meeting with God before the day's demands rush in (Chapter 7). Jesus Himself did this: "Long before daylight, He got up and went out. He went off to a lonely place and spent time there in prayer" (Mark 1:35).
4. **Community.** Regular worship and fellowship with a local church, and at least one or two friends who know you well (Chapter 20). The first believers "gave themselves fully to the apostles' teaching and to the shared life, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers" (Acts 2:42).
5. **Service.** Some regular way of using your gifts to serve others, in the church and beyond it (Chapters 22 and 23).

Andrew Murray, the South African pastor who guided us in Chapter 8, warned that reading good books about Christ is not enough. We need time with Him:

> "No, it needs day by day time with Jesus and with God. We all know the need of time for our meals each day, — every workman claims his hour for dinner; the hurried eating of so much food is not enough."
> Andrew Murray, *Abide in Christ*

A farmer in western Kenya may meet God before he walks to his fields, and pray as he weeds. A nurse in Manila on night shifts may keep her morning watch in the afternoon, when her day begins. A mother of small children may have ten quiet minutes before the house wakes, and a verse on a card above the cooking fire. The shape changes. The strands stay the same.

Write yours on one page. Micah gives a good test for any rule: does it help you live out what God asks? "And what does the LORD ask of you except to do justice, to love loyalty, and to walk humbly with your God?" (Micah 6:8).

### Next steps for new believers

Perhaps you came to faith recently. If so, welcome. You do not need to do everything in these pages at once. Here are four first steps.

**Be baptized.** Baptism is the first step of obedience Jesus gave His followers, a public sign that you belong to Him. On the day of Pentecost Peter told the crowd, "Repent, and every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, so that your sins may be forgiven; and you will receive the Holy Spirit as a gift" (Acts 2:38). Christians practise baptism in different ways (see the Appendix), but all agree that Jesus commands it. Speak to a pastor about it.

**Join a local church.** A coal taken out of the fire soon grows cold. Find a church where the Bible is taught and love is real, and do not only attend; belong.

**Find a mentor.** Ask a mature believer whose life you respect to read the Bible and pray with you regularly. Most will be glad to be asked.

**Begin a daily time with God.** Start small: one chapter of a Gospel each morning, a prayer about what you read, thanks for one thing. Ten faithful minutes a day will do more than two hours once a month.

Behind all four is a simple decision, the one Joshua set before Israel: "Whom will you serve? ... My household and I will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15). Make that choice today, and make it again tomorrow.

### Next steps for mature believers

Perhaps you have walked with God for many years. What does moving forward look like for you?

**Go deeper, not just wider.** We can know more about God while praying less. Return to first love (Chapter 4).

**Let God keep healing your soul.** Old wounds and fears do not always disappear with age, and God heals and grows the soul through the whole of life (Chapter 14).

**Disciple others.** Paul wrote to Timothy, "And what you heard from me before many witnesses, pass on to trustworthy people who will be able to teach others as well" (2 Timothy 2:2). Count the generations: Paul, Timothy, trustworthy people, others. Who is your Timothy?

**Leave a legacy.** Legacy is not mainly about buildings or reputation, but about people who know God because you walked with them (Chapter 26).

And keep running. Even the apostle Paul wrote: "I do not mean that I already have all this, or that I have already been made perfect. But I keep pursuing it ... I put out of mind what is behind me and stretch out for what is in front of me, and I run straight for the goal to win the prize promised by God's upward call in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 3:12-14).

Corrie ten Boom is a picture of this. She was in her fifties when she came out of Ravensbrück concentration camp at the end of 1944, having lost her father and her sister Betsie because the family had hidden Jewish people in their home in Haarlem. Instead of retiring, she spent more than thirty years travelling the world, telling of God's love and of the forgiveness He gives us power to offer. Even when they are old, the psalmist says, the righteous "will still bear fruit; they will stay rich with sap and green" (Psalm 92:14).

### Living on mission

Whether you are new or mature, every follower of Jesus is sent: "But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power, and you will bear witness to Me in Jerusalem, through all Judea and Samaria, and out to the farthest end of the earth" (Acts 1:8). Jesus said to His disciples, "You did not choose Me; I chose you. I appointed you for this: to go and bear fruit, and for your fruit to remain" (John 15:16).

Think of it as four circles moving outwards: your home, where people see your faith at breakfast; your work, whether office, shop, field or ward; your church, where your gifts build up the body; and the nations, which you can pray for, give to and, if God sends you, go to (Chapters 17 to 24).

The East African Revival, which began in the 1930s, shows how this spreads. Ordinary men and women who had met Jesus walked in the light with one another, confessed their sins openly, and told their neighbours what Christ had done. It crossed tribe and church boundaries. Festo Kivengere, the Ugandan evangelist and bishop, was one of its fruits, and carried its simple message of Jesus and His cross from Kigezi to many nations. John wrote, "But if we walk in the light, just as He is in the light, we share fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus, His Son, goes on cleansing us from every sin" (1 John 1:7).

And we do not go alone. The Great Commission ends with a promise: "And remember this: I Myself am with you every day, until the close of the age" (Matthew 28:20). Every day. That is coram Deo carried into the world.

### Grace for the journey: Hudson Taylor

Perhaps you look ahead with fear, because you have started well before and faltered. Then Hudson Taylor's story is for you.

James Hudson Taylor was born in 1832 in Barnsley, Yorkshire, the son of a chemist who was also a Methodist lay preacher. At seventeen, reading a gospel tract in his father's library, he was struck by the words "The finished work of Christ." If the whole work was finished, what was left for him to do but kneel and accept the Saviour? He reached Shanghai in 1854, in the middle of a civil war, and in 1865, on the beach at Brighton, he gave himself to God for inland China, and the China Inland Mission was born.

Outwardly a hero of faith, by the late 1860s he was inwardly close to despair. He had buried a daughter, seen his workers attacked by a mob, and been criticised in Parliament. Worse, he felt his own heart was failing:

> "I was painfully conscious that I was not living all that I was trying to teach the Chinese. Struggling for victory, too often I found myself defeated, until I asked myself whether I ought not to cease to preach, and to retire from missionary work."
> Hudson Taylor, *Unfailing Springs*

Then, in about 1869, the change came. His biographers connect it with a letter from a younger colleague, John McCarthy, about resting on the Faithful One instead of straining after faith. In Taylor's own telling, it came one afternoon in his ordinary Bible reading, when Jesus' words in John 4 became a present message to his soul: "But no one who drinks the water I will give will ever be thirsty again" (John 4:14). He took Jesus at His word. He received the living water, he wrote, "believing from His own Word that my thirsty days were all passed, not from any present feeling, but because of His promise."

That was the secret of the rest of his life. He stopped trying to hold on to Christ by effort and rested in the fact that Christ was holding him. The years that followed brought his deepest sorrows: in 1870 two of their young sons died, and then his wife Maria. Yet he went on. The mission grew far beyond anything he had asked, and when he died at Changsha in 1905, hundreds of missionaries were serving across China.

Looking back in *A Retrospect* on times his own trust had been weak, Taylor wrote: "He is wholly true whether we trust or not." That is the grace that carries us forward. Paul says the same: "If we are unfaithful, He remains faithful, for He cannot be false to Himself" (2 Timothy 2:13).

This is why the opening verse of this chapter matters. The One who started a good work in you will keep working at it until it is finished. Your growth does not finally rest on your discipline or your rule of life, good as those are. It rests on Him. Andrew Murray wrote that believers who grieve over their ups and downs need one thing above all:

> "What they need is the simple faith that the stablishing in Christ, day by day, is God's work, — a work that He delights to do, in spite of all our weakness and unfaithfulness, if we will but trust Him for it."
> Andrew Murray, *Abide in Christ*

"Let us run the race laid out ahead of us, and run it with endurance. Let us keep our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer who leads the way in faith and brings it to perfection" (Hebrews 12:1-2). And when you fall, remember who is keeping you: "Now to the One who is able to guard you from stumbling, and to bring you into the presence of His glory without blemish and rejoicing greatly, to God alone, our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, might, and authority, before time began, now, and for all the ages to come. Amen" (Jude 24-25).

### A prayer of commitment

Sixteen centuries ago in North Africa, Augustine spent years searching for happiness in pleasure, success and clever ideas, and found none of them enough. When at last he came home to God, he began his *Confessions* with words that sum up this whole book:

> "Thou madest us for Thyself, and our heart is restless, until it repose in Thee."
> Augustine, *Confessions*

If you want to answer God's call to seek His face, you may pray in your own words, or use this prayer:

*Father, You made me for Yourself, and my heart is restless until it rests in You. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for Your cross and Your empty tomb, for taking away my sin and bringing me near. Holy Spirit, fill me afresh today. I give You my mornings and my evenings, my home and my work, my money and my time, my gifts and my weaknesses. Teach me to walk before Your face every day. Make me like Jesus. Use me to bring others to You, near and far. When I stumble, lift me up; when I wander, call me back. You began this good work in me; I trust You to finish it. My household and I will serve the LORD. In the name of Jesus, Amen.*

### The face that shines

The Introduction ended with a blessing: may the LORD make His face shine toward you. So this book ends there too, with the words God gave the priests of Israel to speak over His people. They are words to receive, not to earn. Read them slowly, as spoken to you:

> "May the LORD bless you and watch over you. May the LORD make His face shine toward you and show you favor. May the LORD lift up His face to look on you and grant you peace." (Numbers 6:24-26)

The face that Adam and Eve hid from now turns towards you in favour. The face that Moses longed to see has shone on us in Jesus Christ. And one day, the Bible promises, the hiding and the longing will both be over: "They will look upon His face, and His name will be written on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4).

Until that day, seek His face. Live before Him. Walk with Him. And may the LORD make His face shine toward you.

### Questions for reflection

- Looking back over the five parts of this book, which chapter has meant the most to you, and why? Which one do you most need to return to?
- How would you explain, in your own words, the thread that runs through it all: created for relationship with God, restored through Christ, empowered by the Spirit?
- What would the five strands of a rule of life (Scripture, prayer, the morning watch, community and service) look like in your present season of life?
- Are you at the beginning of your walk with God, or further along? Which of the next steps in this chapter is God asking you to take now?
- Hudson Taylor found rest when he stopped straining and trusted Christ to hold him. Where are you still trying to live the Christian life in your own strength?

### A step for this week

This week, write your own rule of life on a single sheet of paper. Under five headings (Scripture, prayer, the morning watch, community and service) write one simple, realistic practice for each, the kind you could keep even on a hard week. Share it with one Christian friend. Then, each morning this week, before you begin your day, read Numbers 6:24-26 aloud over yourself and your household, and receive it as God's word to you: that He sees you, that His face shines toward you, and that you live this day before Him.
