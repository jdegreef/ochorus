## The Holy Spirit, Our Helper

> "I will ask the Father, and in answer He will give you another Advocate, who will be with you forever."
> — John 14:16 (OWB)

Chapter 3 ended on a mountain in Galilee. The risen Jesus gave His followers an enormous task: to make disciples of all the nations. Think of who they were. Fishermen, a former tax collector, a handful of ordinary men and women who had run away on the night of His arrest. How could people like that carry the gospel to the ends of the earth?

Jesus did not expect them to do it alone. Before He ascended, "He charged them not to go away from Jerusalem but to wait there for what the Father had promised" (Acts 1:4). Then He told them what that promise was: "But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power, and you will bear witness to Me in Jerusalem, through all Judea and Samaria, and out to the farthest end of the earth" (Acts 1:8).

This chapter is about that promise. We have met God the Father, who made us for Himself, and God the Son, who came to bring us home. Now we meet God the Holy Spirit, who lives within us and makes the presence of God a daily reality. If coram Deo means living before the face of God, the Holy Spirit is the One who brings that face near.

### Who the Holy Spirit is

Many Christians are less sure about the Holy Spirit than about the Father and the Son. Some think of Him as a power, like electricity, or a feeling that comes in a lively church service. But the Bible shows that the Holy Spirit is a person, and that He is fully God.

He is a person. Jesus never called the Spirit "it". He said, "the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father is going to send in My name, is the one who will teach you everything and remind you of all I have said to you" (John 14:26). Only a person can teach and remind. The Spirit also speaks, guides, prays for us and chooses how to give His gifts. He can even be hurt: "do not grieve God's Holy Spirit" (Ephesians 4:30). You cannot grieve a force. You can only grieve someone who loves you.

The word Jesus used, translated "Advocate" in our Bible and "Helper" or "Comforter" in others, describes someone called alongside to help: a friend who stands beside you, speaks up for you and strengthens you. Jesus said the Spirit would be "another" Advocate. In other words, the Spirit would be to the disciples what Jesus Himself had been: a constant companion and guide.

He is fully God. When Ananias lied about the money from a field he had sold, Peter asked him, "why did Satan fill your heart, to make you lie to the Holy Spirit" (Acts 5:3). A moment later he said, "It is not human beings you have lied to, but God" (Acts 5:4). Lying to the Spirit is lying to God. The Spirit shares the work of God: He was present at creation, He gives life, He knows the deep things of God. That is why Jesus commanded us to baptise "into the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). One name, three persons. This is the mystery of the Trinity, and the Holy Spirit is fully part of it.

The American evangelist D. L. Moody, whom we will meet properly later in this chapter, pressed this point on his hearers in his little book *Secret Power*:

> "I want to call attention to this fact that whenever Christ spoke of the Holy Ghost He spoke of Him as a person, not a mere influence; and if we want to honor the Holy Ghost, let us bear in mind that He is one of the Trinity, a personality of the Godhead."
> D. L. Moody, *Secret Power*

### What the Spirit does

The Bible describes many works of the Spirit. Four are especially important at the start of the Christian life.

**He convicts.** Jesus said that when the Spirit came, "He will show the world to be in the wrong about sin, about righteousness and about judgment" (John 16:8). No preacher can make a person see their need of Christ. Only the Spirit can open blind eyes. When a hard-hearted neighbour suddenly begins to ask about God, or a sermon you have heard many times pierces your heart, that is the Spirit at work.

**He gives new birth.** Jesus told Nicodemus, "unless someone is born of water and the Spirit, they cannot enter the kingdom of God" (John 3:5). We saw in Chapter 3 that when we believe, God gives us new life. The Spirit is the One who does it. Paul says God "saved us through the washing of new birth and renewal by the Holy Spirit" (Titus 3:5).

**He dwells within.** The Spirit does not visit believers now and then. He makes His home in them. "If anyone lacks the Spirit of Christ, that person is not His" (Romans 8:9). Every true Christian, however young in the faith or weak they feel, has the Holy Spirit living within.

**He seals.** Paul writes, "you believed; and in Him you were sealed with the Holy Spirit God had promised. The Spirit is the down payment on our inheritance" (Ephesians 1:13-14). In the ancient world a seal on a letter or a sack of grain showed who owned it and kept it safe. A down payment, like a deposit on a piece of land, is a guarantee that the full payment will follow. The Spirit within you is God's mark of ownership and His promise that He will bring you all the way home.

### Going deeper: spirit, soul and body

*This section is for going further. If you are new to the faith, you can skip to the next section and come back to it later.*

How does the Spirit of God relate to us on the inside? Here Christians have found different ways of describing the same truth.

Watchman Nee, the Chinese church leader whose story is told in Chapter 14, taught that a human being has three parts. The body relates to the physical world. The soul, which includes the mind, emotions and will, relates to ourselves and others. The spirit is the deepest part of us, the part made to relate to God. He drew this from verses like Paul's prayer: "may your spirit, soul and body be kept whole and blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Thessalonians 5:23). When we are born again, Nee said, the Holy Spirit comes to dwell in our human spirit and join with it. Paul writes, "anyone who is joined to the Lord is one spirit with Him" (1 Corinthians 6:17), and "The Spirit Himself joins our own spirit in testifying that we are God's children" (Romans 8:16). From that inner place, the Spirit then works outward, renewing our soul and finally our body.

Many theologians see it differently. They believe the Bible describes two parts rather than three: a body and an inner self, which Scripture sometimes calls the soul and sometimes the spirit, using the words almost interchangeably. Faithful Christians hold both views. This book offers Nee's three-part picture as a helpful lens rather than a test of faith, and we will return to it in Chapter 15 when we think about the healing of the soul. What both views agree on is the main thing: God's Spirit lives in the deepest part of every believer.

Nee loved to remind Christians how great a treasure they carry:

> "Has it really come home to you that wherever you go, you carry with you the Holy Spirit of God? You do not just carry your Bible with you, or even much good teaching about God, but God Himself."
> Watchman Nee, *The Normal Christian Life*

### Going deeper: baptism in the Spirit, three views

*This section is for going further. If you are new to the faith, you can skip to the next section and come back to it later.*

Few subjects have caused more discussion among Christians than the baptism in the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised it: "John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit before many days have passed" (Acts 1:5). But what is it, and when does it happen? Here are the three main views, presented as fairly as we can.

**At conversion.** Many evangelical Christians (those who stress personal faith in Christ and the authority of the Bible), including many Reformed churches (those in the tradition of John Calvin) and Baptists, believe that baptism in the Spirit happens once, when a person first believes. They point to Paul: "all of us were baptized in one Spirit into one body" (1 Corinthians 12:13). Paul says "all of us", not only some. On this view every Christian has already been baptised in the Spirit, and later experiences of power are better described as being filled again.

**As a later experience.** Pentecostal Christians (a worldwide movement that began in the early 1900s and stresses the power and gifts of the Spirit), and many in the older Holiness tradition (a movement of the 1800s, mostly among Methodists, that stressed a holy life), believe the baptism in the Spirit is a distinct experience, usually after conversion, which gives power for witness and service. They point to the disciples, who already believed in Jesus before Pentecost, and to the believers in Samaria and Ephesus, who received the Spirit in a fresh way after they had come to faith (Acts 8 and Acts 19). Many Pentecostals also teach that speaking in tongues usually accompanies this experience.

**Both.** Many others, including many charismatic Christians (believers in many older churches who have welcomed the gifts of the Spirit since the 1960s), hold a middle view. They believe the Spirit is fully given at conversion, but that believers may also have later, powerful experiences of the Spirit coming upon them, sometimes called a release or overflow of the Spirit already within. Moody himself spoke this way. In *Secret Power* he wrote, "The Holy Spirit dwelling in us, is one thing; I think this is clearly brought out in Scripture; and the Holy Spirit upon us for service, is another thing."

These are real differences, and they matter to those who hold them. They are explained further in the Appendix. But it is important to notice how much all three views share.

### The common ground: keep on being filled

Every tradition agrees on this: every believer needs to be continually filled with the Spirit and to rely on Him for everything. Paul writes, "Do not get drunk on wine, for that is reckless, wasteful living. Instead, let the Spirit fill you" (Ephesians 5:18). The form of the command in Paul's language suggests something ongoing: keep on being filled. It is not a single event to look back on but a daily dependence.

We see this in the book of Acts. The disciples were filled with the Spirit at Pentecost. A few weeks later, after praying together under threat of persecution, "every one of them was filled with the Holy Spirit, and they went on proclaiming God's word fearlessly" (Acts 4:31). The same people, filled again.

Moody put it in a homely picture:

> "The fact is, we are leaky vessels, and we have to keep right under the fountain all the time to keep full of Christ, and so have a fresh supply."
> D. L. Moody, *Secret Power*

Think of a clay water pot with a small crack. It may be full in the morning, but by evening it is half empty. The answer is not to throw it away, but to keep returning it to the well. So whatever view you hold on the baptism in the Spirit, the daily need is the same: come back to the fountain, ask to be filled, and depend on Him.

### D. L. Moody: a man filled for service

Dwight Lyman Moody was born in 1837 in Northfield, Massachusetts. His father died when he was a small child, and the family was poor. He left school early, and as a young man he worked in his uncle's shoe shop in Boston. There, in 1855, his Sunday school teacher, Edward Kimball, came into the shop and spoke to him about Christ, and Moody gave his life to the Lord.

Moody moved to Chicago, where he sold shoes successfully but soon poured his energy into reaching poor children. His Sunday school grew to many hundreds. He became known as a tireless worker, and later as a popular preacher. Yet by 1871 he knew something was missing. Two women in his meetings told him that they were praying for him to receive the power of the Spirit. At first he did not see why he needed it. Before long he became hungry. He began to pray with them, crying out to God for power.

In October 1871 the Great Chicago Fire destroyed much of the city, including Moody's church and his home. Soon afterwards, while he was in New York raising money to rebuild, the answer came. Walking along a street in the city, Moody was overwhelmed by a sense of God's presence and love, so strong that he went to a friend's house to be alone with God. He spoke of it only rarely afterwards, but he said that from then on, though he preached the same sermons, far more people came to Christ.

In 1873 Moody sailed for Britain with the singer Ira Sankey, and for two years they held meetings that drew enormous crowds in Scotland, Ireland and England. Back in America he preached to many thousands more, and he founded schools in Northfield and a Bible institute in Chicago to train ordinary men and women for ministry. He died in 1899.

Moody never became a scholar, and he never claimed to be anything special. That is exactly the point. The Spirit took an uneducated shoe salesman and made him a witness to two continents. What He did for Moody, in his own way and measure, He longs to do for every believer who will depend on Him.

### Empowered to live a holy life

The Spirit's power is not only for preaching to crowds. It is first of all for living a holy life in an ordinary home, workplace and village.

Every Christian knows the struggle Paul describes: "For the flesh sets its desires against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh" (Galatians 5:17). The "flesh" here does not mean our physical bodies. It means our old, self-centred nature, which pulls us towards anger, lust, jealousy and pride. We cannot defeat it by willpower alone. But Paul gives a promise: "walk by the Spirit, and you will never carry out what the flesh desires" (Galatians 5:16). And again: "if by the Spirit you keep putting the body's practices to death, you will live" (Romans 8:13).

As the Spirit works in us, He produces fruit: "the fruit the Spirit produces is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control" (Galatians 5:22-23). Notice that it is fruit, not a list of achievements. A mango tree does not strain to produce mangoes. It stays rooted, receives rain and sunshine, and the fruit grows in season. In the same way, as we stay close to God, the Spirit grows Christlike character in us. We will see more of this in Chapter 9 and Chapter 14.

### Empowered for the Great Commission

The Spirit also empowers us to witness. On the day of Pentecost the believers were gathered in one place when "a sound came out of heaven, like a mighty wind sweeping through" (Acts 2:2). Then "Every one of them was filled with the Holy Spirit, and they began to speak in other languages as the Spirit gave them the words to say" (Acts 2:4). That same day Peter, who had denied Jesus only weeks before, preached boldly, and about three thousand people believed.

This fulfilled the promise of the prophet Joel, that God would "pour out My Spirit upon all people" (Joel 2:28): sons and daughters, young and old, servants and masters. The Spirit is not reserved for pastors and missionaries. He is given to the market trader, the nurse, the student and the grandmother alike.

The Spirit also gives gifts to every believer for the good of the Church and its mission. "Gifts are given out in different ways, but it is the same Spirit who gives them" (1 Corinthians 12:4). Some receive wisdom, some faith, some gifts of healing, some prophecy, some tongues and their interpretation. "Each person is given a way in which the Spirit is openly at work, and it is given for the good of all" (1 Corinthians 12:7). We will look at the gifts more fully in Chapter 23. For now, it is enough to know that you have been equipped. The Spirit who sent the apostles out to the ends of the earth also sends you across the street, into your office and into your family with good news.

### Walking in step with the Spirit

How, then, do we live with the Spirit day by day? Paul gives a beautiful picture: "Since our life comes from the Spirit, let us also follow the Spirit's lead in each step we take" (Galatians 5:25). Think of walking beside a friend on a long road. You match your pace to theirs. You listen when they speak. You turn when they turn.

Walking with the Spirit is like that. It means beginning the day by asking Him to fill you. It means listening for His quiet prompting: to apologise, to give, to speak to someone, or to keep silent. It means confessing sin quickly when you grieve Him, rather than letting distance grow. It means depending on Him in small things, like a hard conversation or a difficult task at work, and not only in great crises. This is what it means to live coram Deo in practice: the Spirit within us makes God's face near in every hour.

And the first fruit the Spirit grows in us is love. Paul names it at the head of the list, and Jesus called love the mark by which the world would recognise His disciples. That is where Part Two of this book begins, in Chapter 5.

### Questions for reflection

- Before reading this chapter, did you think of the Holy Spirit more as a person or as a power? What difference does it make that He is a person who can be grieved?
- Which of the Spirit's works (convicting, giving new birth, dwelling within, sealing) are you most thankful for today?
- How do you find Watchman Nee's picture of spirit, soul and body helpful? Where do you see the Spirit renewing your mind, emotions or will?
- What view of the baptism in the Spirit have you been taught? How does the common ground, being continually filled, apply to you whatever your view?
- Moody said we are "leaky vessels". What drains your spiritual strength, and how can you return to the fountain more often?
- Which part of the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23) do you most need Him to grow in you right now?

### A step for this week

Each morning this week, before you leave your home or start your work, pray a simple prayer: "Holy Spirit, fill me today. Help me to walk in step with You." Then choose one part of the fruit of the Spirit to focus on for that day (love on the first day, joy on the second, and so on through the list). In the evening, take five minutes to look back. Where did you sense the Spirit's prompting? Where did you resist Him? Thank Him for His help, confess where you fell short, and ask to be filled again tomorrow.
