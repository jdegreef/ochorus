## Glossary: Words Used in This Book

Christians use many words that are not part of everyday speech. This glossary explains the ones you will meet in this book, in plain language. Where a chapter teaches the subject more fully, its number is given in brackets.

### A to C

- **Abba.** A warm word for "Father" in Aramaic, the language Jesus spoke. Jesus used it in prayer, and the Spirit teaches us to use it too (Chapter 2).
- **Anglican.** The family of churches that grew from the Church of England, found today in many countries.
- **Apostle.** "One who is sent." The twelve men Jesus chose, and Paul, who saw the risen Lord and laid the foundation of the Church. In a wider sense, someone sent to start the Church where Christ is not yet known (Chapter 22).
- **Atonement.** Making us "at one" with God again. Jesus did this by dying for our sins on the cross (Chapter 3).
- **Baptism.** Washing with water in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. It is the sign Jesus gave that a person belongs to Him (Chapter 21).
- **Baptism in the Spirit.** Jesus' promise to fill His people with the Holy Spirit. Christians differ on when it happens (Chapter 4).
- **Baptist.** A family of Protestant churches that baptise those who can confess their own faith, rather than infants.
- **Beatitudes.** The eight sayings that begin Jesus' Sermon on the Mount, each starting "Blessed are…" (Chapter 12).
- **Bishop.** A senior church leader who oversees other pastors and churches. In the New Testament the word ("overseer") is also used for the elders of a local church.
- **Born again.** The new life the Holy Spirit gives when we trust in Christ. Also called regeneration, or being "born from above" (Chapters 3 and 4).
- **Catechism.** A short book of questions and answers used to teach the Christian faith.
- **Catholic.** With a capital C, the Roman Catholic Church, led by the Pope. With a small c, it means "universal": the whole Church in every place.
- **Cessationist.** A Christian who believes that certain miraculous gifts, such as tongues and prophecy, ended with the apostles (Chapter 23).
- **Charismatic.** A Christian, often in an older church, who has welcomed the gifts of the Spirit, such as tongues, prophecy and healing. From the Greek word for "gift".
- **Christ.** The Greek form of "Messiah": the Anointed One, the King God promised to send. It is a title, not a surname.
- **Church.** All who belong to Jesus, everywhere and in every age. Also a local group of believers who meet together (Chapter 21).
- **Complementarian.** A Christian who believes men and women are equal in worth but have different roles in marriage and in church leadership. One who believes those roles are shared equally is called an **egalitarian** (Chapters 18 and 22).
- **Confession.** Telling God, and sometimes another believer, the truth about our sins. The word can also mean declaring what we believe.
- **Continuationist.** A Christian who believes all the gifts of the Spirit continue today (Chapter 23).
- **Conversion.** Turning from sin to trust in Christ: the start of the Christian life.
- **Covenant.** A binding promise that creates a relationship, such as the covenant God made with Israel, the new covenant made through Jesus' blood, or the covenant of marriage.
- **Creed.** A short statement of what Christians believe, such as the Apostles' Creed or the Nicene Creed.

### D to L

- **Deacon.** "Servant." Someone given a practical ministry of care in the local church (Chapter 22).
- **Deity.** Being God. "The deity of Christ" means that Jesus is truly God.
- **Denomination.** A family of churches that share a history, beliefs and way of organising themselves, such as Baptist, Methodist or Anglican.
- **Disciple.** A learner and follower of Jesus, an apprentice who learns to live as He lived (Chapter 11).
- **Elder.** A mature believer given the task of leading, teaching and caring for a local church. Also called a shepherd or overseer (Chapter 22).
- **Evangelical.** Christians, in many denominations, who stress personal faith in Christ, the authority of the Bible and the need to share the gospel.
- **Evangelism.** Sharing the good news of Jesus with others. An evangelist is someone especially gifted for this (Chapter 25).
- **Faith.** Trusting God and His promises, putting your whole weight on Him (Chapter 13).
- **The fall.** The moment when the first man and woman disobeyed God, and sin and death entered the world (Chapter 3).
- **Fasting.** Going without food for a time in order to seek God in prayer (Chapter 10).
- **Firstfruits.** The first part of a harvest, the promise that the rest is coming. Jesus' resurrection is the firstfruits of ours (Chapter 3).
- **Gentile.** Anyone who is not Jewish.
- **Glory.** The shining greatness and beauty of God.
- **Gospel.** "Good news": the message that God saves sinners through the life, death and resurrection of Jesus Christ (Chapter 25). The **Gospels** are the four books, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, that tell the story of Jesus.
- **Grace.** God's kindness and favour that we do not deserve and cannot earn.
- **Great Commission.** Jesus' command to His followers to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:18–20; Chapter 25).
- **Holiness tradition.** A movement of the 1800s, mostly among Methodists, that stressed a holy life lived in the power of the Spirit.
- **Holy.** Set apart for God, pure and good. God is holy, and He calls His people to be holy (Chapter 14).
- **Holy Spirit.** The third person of the Trinity, who lives in every believer (Chapter 4).
- **Incarnation.** "Coming in the flesh": God the Son becoming a real human being in Jesus (Chapter 3).
- **Justification.** God declaring us righteous, not because we are good, but because Christ's righteousness is counted as ours (Chapter 3).
- **Kingdom of God.** God's rule as King, which came near in Jesus and will one day fill the earth (Chapter 12).
- **Lament.** An honest prayer of grief or complaint, poured out to God in trust (Chapter 17).
- **The Law.** The commands God gave Israel through Moses. The phrase can also mean the first five books of the Bible.
- **LORD.** When printed in capital letters in the Bible, this stands for Yahweh, God's personal name (Chapter 2).
- **Lord's Supper.** The meal of bread and wine (or juice) that Jesus gave His followers to remember His death. Also called Communion or the Eucharist (Chapter 21).

### M to R

- **Martyr.** "Witness." A believer who is killed for their faith in Christ.
- **Messiah.** The Hebrew form of "Christ": the Anointed King God promised to send.
- **Methodist.** A family of Protestant churches that grew from the revival led by John and Charles Wesley in the 1700s.
- **Missionary.** Someone sent to make Christ known, often to another people or country.
- **Monk.** A man who has left ordinary life to live a life of prayer, often with others in a community called a monastery.
- **Morning watch.** A time with God at the start of each day, in His Word and in prayer (Chapter 8).
- **Nicene Creed.** A statement of faith first agreed at the Council of Nicaea in 325, confessing that Jesus is truly God.
- **Omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent.** All-powerful, all-knowing and present everywhere (Chapter 2).
- **Orthodox.** With a capital O, the Eastern Orthodox churches, such as the Greek and Russian churches. With a small o, it means holding to the true, historic faith.
- **Parable.** A short story Jesus told to teach a truth about God and His kingdom.
- **Pastor.** "Shepherd." A leader who cares for and teaches a church (Chapter 22).
- **Pentecost.** The Jewish harvest feast, fifty days after Passover, when the Holy Spirit was poured out on the first disciples (Acts 2).
- **Pentecostal.** A worldwide movement that began in the early 1900s and stresses baptism in the Spirit and the gifts of the Spirit.
- **Presbyterian.** A family of Protestant churches in the Reformed tradition, led by groups of elders.
- **Priesthood of all believers.** The truth that every Christian can come to God directly through Christ, and is called to serve Him (Chapter 22).
- **Prophet.** Someone who speaks a message from God. The Old Testament prophets, such as Isaiah and Jeremiah, wrote books of the Bible.
- **Protestant.** The churches that came from the Reformation of the 1500s, such as Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist and Pentecostal churches.
- **Puritans.** English Protestants of the 1500s and 1600s who wanted the church and the whole of life to be shaped by the Bible.
- **Redeemer.** One who pays the price to set someone free. Jesus is our Redeemer (Chapter 3).
- **Reformation.** The movement of the 1500s, led by the **Reformers** Martin Luther, John Calvin and others, that called the Church back to the Bible and to salvation by grace through faith.
- **Reformed.** Protestant churches in the tradition of John Calvin, such as Presbyterian churches.
- **Repentance.** Turning around: turning away from sin and towards God (Chapter 3).
- **Resurrection.** Rising from the dead to a new, lasting life. Jesus rose on the third day, and all who belong to Him will rise when He returns (Chapters 3 and 28).
- **Revival.** A time when God's Spirit moves powerfully, and many people turn to Christ or return to Him.

### S to Y

- **Sabbath.** The day of rest God gave Israel, the seventh day of the week. Christians understand how it applies today in different ways (Chapter 12 and the Appendix).
- **Sacrament.** An outward sign of God's grace that Jesus gave His Church. Most Protestants count two: baptism and the Lord's Supper (Chapter 21).
- **Salvation.** God rescuing us from sin and death and bringing us into His family.
- **Sanctification.** The lifelong work of the Holy Spirit in making us holy, like Jesus (Chapters 14 and 15).
- **Satan.** "The accuser." The devil, the chief of the fallen angels, who opposes God and His people but is already defeated by Christ (Chapter 16).
- **Sermon on the Mount.** Jesus' best-known teaching, in Matthew 5 to 7 (Chapter 12).
- **Sin.** Turning away from God to go our own way, in what we think, say and do (Chapter 3).
- **Sovereignty.** God's rule over all things. Nothing happens outside His knowledge or beyond His control (Chapters 2 and 17).
- **Spiritual disciplines.** Practices such as prayer, fasting, solitude and worship, through which we make room for God to change us (Chapter 10).
- **Spiritual gifts.** Abilities the Holy Spirit gives each believer to serve others and build up the Church (Chapter 23).
- **Steward.** A manager who looks after what belongs to someone else. We are stewards of all God has given us (Chapter 26).
- **Testament.** One of the two main parts of the Bible. The Old Testament was written before Jesus came, and the New Testament tells of Him and the first Christians.
- **Tithe.** "A tenth": giving a tenth of your income to God (Chapter 26).
- **Tongues.** Speaking, by the Holy Spirit, in a language the speaker has not learned (Chapter 23).
- **Trinity.** The one God who exists eternally as three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit (Chapters 2 to 4).
- **Yahweh.** God's personal name, which He revealed to Moses. It is linked to the words "I AM" (Chapter 2).
