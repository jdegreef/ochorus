## The Gifts of the Spirit and Calling

> "Each of you has received a gift; use it to serve one another, as good managers of the richly varied grace of God."
> — 1 Peter 4:10 (OWB)

Imagine a family gathering at a grandmother's home. Each grandchild arrives with something: one brings bread, another brings a goat, a third brings music, a fourth simply comes ready to wash the pots. No one brings everything, and no one comes with nothing. The meal is rich because everyone shares what they have been given.

That is a picture of the Church. In Chapter 22 we saw that every believer is a priest and a minister. Now we ask how God equips us for that ministry. Peter's answer is simple: "Each of you has received a gift" (1 Peter 4:10). Not some of you. Each of you.

### Everything is a gift

Before we speak of spiritual gifts, we need to see that everything we have is a gift. God "gives life and breath and all things to everyone" (Acts 17:25). Your next breath, your family, your abilities, your salvation: all of it came from His hand. James writes, "Every good act of giving and every perfect gift is from above. It comes down from the Father of the lights in the sky" (James 1:17).

This guards us against two errors. The first is pride. Paul asked the proud Corinthians, "What do you have that was not given to you? And if it was given to you, why do you take pride in it as though it were not a gift?" (1 Corinthians 4:7). The gifted singer, the able preacher and the successful farmer have nothing to boast of. They have only something to thank God for.

The second error is false humility, the feeling that "I have nothing to offer". That is not true either. If you belong to Christ, the Spirit lives in you, and He does not live in anyone without giving them something to share. We receive our gifts with gratitude and humility, and we use them before the face of the Giver.

### What the gifts are for

Spiritual gifts are not trophies or badges of rank. Paul says, "Each person is given a way in which the Spirit is openly at work, and it is given for the good of all" (1 Corinthians 12:7). Gifts exist to build up the Church, to glorify Christ and to serve others in love. Peter adds that whoever speaks or serves should do it "in the strength God supplies. Then in everything God will be glorified through Jesus Christ" (1 Peter 4:11).

That is why Paul places his great chapter on love right in the middle of his teaching on gifts. "Suppose I speak in the tongues of human beings, and of angels too. If I do not have love, I am no more than booming bronze or a clashing cymbal. I may be able to prophesy, and know every mystery and everything there is to know; I may have all the faith there is, enough to move mountains; but if I do not have love, I am nothing" (1 Corinthians 13:1–2). A gift used without love is noise. A small gift used with love builds the house of God. As Chapter 5 taught, love is the mark of a Christian.

### The gifts of the Spirit in 1 Corinthians 12

In 1 Corinthians 12:8–10, Paul lists nine ways the Spirit works. Many teachers group them in three sets of three.

**Revelation gifts** help believers know what God is showing.

- A *message of wisdom* is Spirit-given insight into how to act in a situation, applying God's truth to a hard problem.
- A *message of knowledge* is understanding given by the Spirit, whether into the truths of Scripture or into a situation that the speaker could not otherwise know.
- *The ability to tell spirits apart*, often called discernment, is the gift of recognising whether something comes from God, from the human heart or from an evil spirit (compare 1 John 4:1).

**Power gifts** show God's strength at work.

- *Faith* here is not saving faith, which every believer has, but a special confidence that God will act in a particular situation.
- *Gifts of healing* are God's healing of the sick through a believer's prayer. Paul says "gifts", plural, perhaps because healings come in many kinds.
- *The power to work miracles* is God's intervention beyond nature, for His glory and the good of His people.

**Speaking gifts** bring God's word to people, or people's praise to God.

- *Prophecy* is speaking a word from God whose words "build them up, encourage them and comfort them" (1 Corinthians 14:3), always tested by Scripture.
- *Different kinds of tongues* is speaking by the Spirit in a language the speaker has not learned.
- *The interpretation of tongues* makes the meaning of a tongue known, so that the church is built up.

And over all of these Paul writes: "One and the same Spirit is at work in all of these, giving to each person separately as He chooses" (1 Corinthians 12:11). The Spirit decides. We do not choose our gifts like goods in a market.

### Romans 12 and Ephesians 4

Paul gives other lists too, which shows that no single list is complete. In Romans 12 he describes gifts that shape how a person naturally serves, which some call motivational gifts: "We have gifts that differ, according to the grace given to us" (Romans 12:6). He names prophesying, serving, teaching and encouraging, then adds: "The one who shares should share generously; the one who leads, with eagerness; the one who shows mercy, with cheerfulness" (Romans 12:8).

You may recognise yourself here. Some people notice a broken chair and fix it before anyone asks: that is serving. Some cannot rest until a hard passage is explained clearly: that is teaching. Some always find the discouraged person at the back of the room. Some give quietly and gladly. Some organise. Some weep with the grieving. Each is a gift of grace.

Then there are the ministry gifts of Ephesians 4:11, the apostles, prophets, evangelists and pastor-teachers we met in Chapter 22. These are people given to the Church to equip all the others.

### Praying in tongues and the gift of tongues

Few subjects have caused more debate than speaking in tongues. In Acts, believers spoke in tongues when the Spirit came on them at Pentecost (Acts 2:4), in the house of Cornelius (Acts 10:44–46) and at Ephesus (Acts 19:6). In 1 Corinthians 14, Paul gives careful teaching about tongues in the life of the church.

Many continuationists, those who believe all the gifts continue today, draw a distinction between two uses of tongues. That distinction is the view this book teaches, and the Appendix sets out other readings fairly.

**Praying in tongues** is a personal prayer language for building oneself up and praying in the Spirit. Paul writes, "Someone who speaks in a tongue is speaking not to people but to God" (1 Corinthians 14:2), and "Those who speak in a tongue build up themselves" (1 Corinthians 14:4). He describes his own practice: "When I pray in a tongue, it is my spirit that prays, while my mind bears no fruit. What follows, then? I will pray in the spirit, and I will pray with my mind as well" (1 Corinthians 14:14–15). He even says, "I am thankful to God that, more than every one of you, I speak in tongues" (1 Corinthians 14:18). Jude urges believers to keep "praying in the Holy Spirit" (Jude 20), and Paul tells us that when we do not know how to pray, "the Spirit Himself pleads on our behalf, with groans that no words carry" (Romans 8:26). On this view, praying in tongues is available to many believers as a gift for private prayer.

**The gift of tongues** is a public utterance in the gathered church, and it must be interpreted so that all are built up. Paul asks, "Does everyone speak in tongues? Does everyone interpret?" (1 Corinthians 12:30), expecting the answer no. In public worship, "anyone who speaks in a tongue should ask God for the power to interpret it as well" (1 Corinthians 14:13). Paul would be "glad for all of you to speak in tongues, but gladder still for you to prophesy" (1 Corinthians 14:5), because prophecy in a known language builds up the church.

Paul's guidance for public worship is about order, love and edification. "Whenever you gather, each of you has something: a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, an interpretation. Everything should be done to build up the church" (1 Corinthians 14:26). Tongues should be spoken by two or three at most, in turn, with interpretation. "If there is no one to interpret, the speaker should keep silent in the church, speaking only to themselves and to God" (1 Corinthians 14:28). Prophecies are to be weighed. And the chapter ends with a balance every church needs: "do not stop anyone from speaking in tongues. But let everything be done properly and in good order" (1 Corinthians 14:39–40).

### Where Christians differ

Sincere, Bible-believing Christians hold a wide range of views on these gifts. It is a secondary issue, and it should not divide us.

- **Continuationists**, including Pentecostals and charismatics, believe all the gifts continue today. Many distinguish praying in tongues from the public gift, as above. Some Pentecostals also teach that tongues is the first evidence of baptism in the Spirit.
- **The open but cautious** believe the gifts may continue today, but should be tested carefully and practised in order and love. They do not hold that tongues is required as evidence of the Spirit.
- **Cessationists** believe the miraculous sign gifts, such as tongues, prophecy and healing, served to confirm the apostles' message and ceased with the apostles. They gladly affirm that God still heals and works wonders in answer to prayer.

Some also read 1 Corinthians 14 as describing private prayer in tongues, while others believe it refers only to the public gift.

Here is the common ground. The Spirit gives gifts to every believer for the good of the body. Love is greater than any gift. And everything must be done to build up the Church. Whatever your tradition, seek the Spirit eagerly, test everything by Scripture, honour believers who see it differently, and walk in love.

### Catherine Booth: a timid woman who became a fool for Christ

Catherine Booth was born in England in 1829. She was a serious, sickly girl who read the whole Bible through several times while still a girl. In 1855 she married William Booth, a young Methodist preacher, and together they would later found The Salvation Army.

Catherine had deep convictions, and in 1859 she wrote a pamphlet defending the right of women to preach. Yet for years she did not speak in public herself. She was shy, and the thought of standing before a crowd filled her with dread. During an illness she promised the Lord that if He would stir her again, she would obey Him.

In 1860, at the chapel in Gateshead where William was minister, she sat in the minister's pew with her four-year-old son while about a thousand people listened to testimonies. She later told what happened in her address *Witnessing for Christ*. She felt the Spirit come upon her and urge her to speak, and she said in her heart that she could not do it. Then a thought came, which she recognised as the devil's voice, telling her she would look like a fool and have nothing to say. That word settled it. She wrote:

> "I have never yet been willing to be a fool for Christ, now I will be one."
> Catherine Booth, *Witnessing for Christ*

She rose, walked up the chapel and spoke. The congregation, who knew her "timid, bashful nature", were astonished. From that day she became one of the most powerful preachers of her generation, speaking to crowds of rich and poor while raising eight children. She was clear about where the power came from: "It was not I that did this, but the Holy Ghost, the Holy Spirit of God" (*Witnessing for Christ*).

Christians hold different views on women preaching, as Chapter 22 and the Appendix explain. But every reader can learn from her story how gifts are discovered. Catherine's gift lay hidden for years under fear. It came to life not through a test or a course, but through obedience in a single moment. She stepped out, and God met her. Many gifts are found that way: in the doing.

### Discovering and growing in your gifts

How do you find your gifts? There is no magic formula, but Scripture and experience point to four companions.

1. **Scripture.** Read the lists in Romans 12, 1 Corinthians 12, Ephesians 4 and 1 Peter 4. Let God's Word stir your imagination about what He might do through you.
2. **Prayer.** Ask the Giver. Paul says, "set your hearts on spiritual gifts" (1 Corinthians 14:1). It is right to desire gifts that build up others.
3. **Community.** Other believers often see your gifts before you do. Ask mature Christians, "Where have you seen God use me?" Paul reminded Timothy "to stir up into flame the gift God gave you when I laid my hands on you" (2 Timothy 1:6). Gifts are recognised and encouraged in the body.
4. **Serving.** Most of all, start serving. Visit the sick, teach the children, pray for the troubled, welcome the stranger. You will discover what brings fruit and joy, and what others confirm.

Do not wait until you are sure of your gift before you serve. Serve, and your gift will become clear.

Gifts also grow. A young man who stammers through his first Bible reading in a home group may, ten years later, be teaching with clarity and warmth. A woman who first prays aloud in a trembling voice may become the intercessor her whole church leans on. Like a seed in good soil, a gift grows when it is watered with prayer, exercised in love and corrected by wise friends. And like a hoe left in the rain, a gift that is never used grows rusty. Paul's word to Timothy was to stir the gift up, not to wait for it to stir itself.

### Calling in everyday work

The word "calling" is often used only for pastors and missionaries. But Scripture gives every believer a calling. "We are God's own work. In Christ Jesus He created us for good works, which God made ready in advance so that we would live our lives doing them" (Ephesians 2:10).

Martin Luther helped recover this truth in the sixteenth century. In his day many believed that monks and priests had a holy calling, while farmers, shoemakers and mothers had merely worldly work. Luther taught that God calls people to every honest kind of work, and that a servant sweeping a floor or a mother caring for her child can please God as truly as a priest at the altar. In our own time, Os Guinness has written about this in *The Call*, reminding us that we are called first to Someone, Christ, and only then to something.

Paul says, "Whatever you are doing, put your whole self into the work, as for the Lord and not for people" (Colossians 3:23). As Chapter 20 showed, the teacher, the nurse, the driver and the shopkeeper all work coram Deo. Think of a bus driver in Nairobi who prays for his passengers as they climb aboard, or an accountant in Manila who refuses to falsify the figures when her manager asks. Think of a mother in a mountain village in Peru who teaches her children to read using the Gospel of Mark. None of them stands in a pulpit, yet each is answering God's call. God may call some to leave their nets and serve full-time in the Church. He calls all of us to serve Him wherever we are.

### Faithfulness in small things

Brother Lawrence spent many years as cook in a Paris monastery, a job he naturally disliked. He learned to do it for the love of God. Those who knew him recorded his counsel:

> "We ought not to be weary of doing little things for the love of God, who regards not the greatness of the work, but the love with which it is performed."
> Brother Lawrence, *The Practice of the Presence of God*

Jesus taught the same: "Someone who is faithful in the smallest matter is faithful in large matters too" (Luke 16:10). In His parable, the master says, "Well done! You are a good and faithful servant. You proved faithful in small matters; I will put you in charge of great ones" (Matthew 25:21).

Many people want a large gift for a large platform. God looks for faithful people with whatever He has given. Setting out chairs, praying for one neighbour, teaching five children: these are not small before His face. This leads into Chapter 24, where we see that the greatest gift is to serve as Jesus served.

### Questions for reflection

- Read 1 Corinthians 4:7. Which of your abilities or possessions do you most easily treat as your own rather than as a gift?
- Looking at Romans 12:6–8, which of these gifts do you see in yourself? Which have others noticed in you?
- How does 1 Corinthians 13:1–3 change the way you think about spiritual gifts?
- What is your church's view on the gifts and tongues? How can you honour believers who see it differently?
- Catherine Booth's gift came to life through one act of obedience. Is there a step God has been urging you to take that fear has held back?
- How does it change your daily work to see it as a calling from God?

### A step for this week

Ask two mature Christians who know you well this simple question: "Where have you seen God use me to help others?" Write down their answers. Then choose one small, practical way to serve in your church or neighbourhood this week that fits what they said, and do it as before the face of God. Before you begin, pray, "Lord, everything I have is Your gift. Use it for the good of others."
