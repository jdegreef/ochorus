## Prayer: Talking and Listening to God

> "But when you pray, go into an inner room, shut the door, and pray to your Father who is there in secret. Your Father sees what is hidden, and He will repay you."
> — Matthew 6:6 (OWB)

A small child does not need lessons before speaking to her father. She runs to him with a scraped knee, a drawing, a question, a complaint. She does not choose her words carefully. She simply talks, because he is her father and she knows he is listening.

In Chapter 6 we learned that God speaks to us through His Word. Prayer is our answer. He speaks first; we respond. George Müller found that when he began the day by meditating on Scripture, his reading turned almost by itself into confession, thanksgiving and requests. That is the natural shape of prayer: a conversation in which God has the first word and we reply as His children.

To live coram Deo is to live before a face that is turned towards us. Prayer is how we speak to that face, and how we learn to hear it speak back.

### What prayer is

Prayer is not a ritual to perform or a spell to get results. It is not a speech for others to admire. Jesus warned against praying "at the street corners where others can see them" (Matthew 6:5). He also warned against piling up empty words, as if God could be worn down by length: "do not keep piling up empty words as the pagans do. They think they will be heard because they say so much" (Matthew 6:7). Then He added something wonderful: "Your Father already knows what you need before you ask Him" (Matthew 6:8).

If God already knows, why pray at all? Because prayer is not mainly about giving God information. It is about relationship. A husband may know his wife is tired, but he still wants her to tell him about her day. Prayer is the way we share our life with God and receive His life in return.

So prayer can be very simple and very honest. "People, rely on Him in every season; empty your hearts out before Him. God is our shelter" (Psalm 62:8). Empty your heart. Tell Him your fear about the harvest, your anger at your employer, your worry about your son, your joy at a birth. Nothing is too small and nothing is too large.

Paul gives the same invitation, with a promise attached: "Do not worry about anything. Instead, in everything, bring your requests to God through prayer and petition, with thanksgiving. And God's peace, which is beyond all our understanding, will stand guard over your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 4:6–7). The picture is of a soldier on guard at the gate. When we hand our worries to God, His peace stands at the door of our hearts.

### The Lord's Prayer as a model

The disciples watched Jesus pray, and it made them want to pray too. "Lord, teach us how to pray," one of them asked (Luke 11:1). Jesus answered with the prayer we call the Lord's Prayer. It is good to pray these words as they stand, as Christians all over the world have done for two thousand years. It is also a pattern, a frame on which to hang our own prayers.

"Our Father in heaven, may Your name be kept holy. May Your kingdom come. May Your will be done on earth, as it is done in heaven. Give us today the bread we need. Forgive us what we owe You, as we too have forgiven those who owe us. And do not lead us into testing, but rescue us from the evil one" (Matthew 6:9–13).

Notice its shape.

1. **"Our Father in heaven."** Prayer begins with relationship. He is our Father, near and loving, and He is in heaven, great and powerful.
2. **"May Your name be kept holy. May Your kingdom come. May Your will be done."** Before we ask for anything for ourselves, we put God first: His honour, His rule, His purposes.
3. **"Give us today the bread we need."** Then we bring our daily needs, simply, one day at a time.
4. **"Forgive us ... as we too have forgiven."** We confess our sins and let go of others' sins against us.
5. **"Rescue us from the evil one."** We ask for protection in the spiritual battle.

You can pray through the Lord's Prayer slowly, line by line, filling in each part with your own words. When you reach "Give us today the bread we need," name the needs of your household. When you reach "as we too have forgiven," name the person you find hard to forgive.

### Kinds of prayer

The Bible shows many kinds of prayer. Five are especially helpful to remember.

- **Adoration** is praising God for who He is. "Bless the LORD, my soul; let all that is deep within me bless His holy name" (Psalm 103:1). Adoration lifts our eyes from our problems to our God.
- **Confession** is agreeing with God about our sin and receiving His forgiveness. David prayed, "God, create a pure heart for me, and make my spirit new and steady inside me" (Psalm 51:10).
- **Thanksgiving** is thanking God for what He has done. "Whatever happens, give thanks; this is what God wants for you in Christ Jesus" (1 Thessalonians 5:18).
- **Supplication** is asking God for our own needs, as Jesus invites us to do.
- **Intercession** is praying for others. "... pray for each other, so that you may be healed. When a righteous person prays, that prayer is at work, and it can do a great deal" (James 5:16).

A healthy prayer life has room for all five. If we only ask, our prayers become a shopping list. If we only praise, we forget that God wants to meet our needs. Many believers find it helpful to move through these kinds of prayer in turn, especially in a longer time of prayer.

### Praying Scripture

One of the best teachers of prayer is the Bible itself. The Psalms were Israel's prayer book, and they have been the Church's ever since. They give us words for every season: joy and grief, fear and trust, anger and repentance. When you do not know how to pray, open the Psalms and pray them as your own.

You can do the same with other passages. Paul's prayers for the churches are rich models (Ephesians 1 and 3, Philippians 1, Colossians 1). Turn his words into prayers for your family and church: "Lord, let my children's love overflow more and more." The promises of Scripture can also be turned into prayer. When you read, "The sheep that belong to Me listen to My voice; I know them, and they follow Me" (John 10:27), you might pray, "Lord, You know me. Help me to hear Your voice today and follow You."

Praying Scripture keeps our prayers in line with God's will, stretches them beyond our own small concerns, and gives words to the heart when it has none.

### Praying Hyde

Few people have shown the power of persevering prayer more clearly than John Hyde, the American missionary whom many in India came to call "Praying Hyde."

Hyde was born in 1865 in Illinois, the son of a Presbyterian minister. He sailed for India in 1892 to serve in the Punjab, in the north-west of the country. His start was not impressive. He was hard of hearing, and he found learning the local languages slow work. He was a quiet man, not a natural public speaker.

But Hyde learned to pray. He came to believe that the missionary task was first of all a spiritual battle, to be fought on his knees before it was fought in the villages. He gave himself to prayer with a seriousness that astonished those around him, often praying late into the night and sometimes through whole nights. He would pray for people by name, for hours, carrying them before God until he had peace.

In 1904 Hyde and others formed the Punjab Prayer Union, a fellowship of believers who committed themselves to pray for revival in India. That same year a convention for Christian workers began at Sialkot. While the meetings went on, Hyde and a few companions gave themselves to prayer in a room set apart for it, sometimes for days at a time. Many testified that God moved powerfully in those gatherings, with deep repentance among believers and many coming to faith in Christ. The Sialkot conventions continued for years, and Hyde's prayer was woven through them.

Those who knew him say that he prayed specifically and persistently for people to come to Christ, asking God for definite numbers of souls and holding on until he saw them. His praying cost him. His health broke down, and he left India in 1911. He died in America the following year, aged only forty-six.

What can an ordinary believer learn from such a man? Most of us will not pray through the night. But Hyde shows us that prayer is real work, perhaps the most important work, and that God answers the persistent prayers of ordinary people. He was not gifted in the ways the world admires. He simply took God at His word, and went to Him again and again. "When a righteous person prays, that prayer is at work, and it can do a great deal" (James 5:16).

### Listening prayer

Prayer is not only talking. It is also listening. The boy Samuel, sleeping near the ark of God, heard his name called in the night. When he finally understood that it was the Lord, he answered, "Speak, for Your servant is listening" (1 Samuel 3:10). That is a good prayer for any of us.

Elijah, worn out and afraid, met God on the mountain. The Lord was not in the mighty wind, nor in the earthquake, nor in the fire, but in "a faint sound, thin as silence" (1 Kings 19:12). God often speaks quietly. If our prayer is all speaking and no stillness, we may miss Him.

God speaks above all through Scripture, and that is where we should expect to hear Him first. But Christians also testify that the Spirit brings a verse to mind, lays a person on their heart, gives a sense of peace or unease about a decision, or prompts them to act. How should we treat such impressions?

With openness and with care. Paul writes, "Do not smother the Spirit; do not look down on prophecy. Test everything; keep hold of what is good" (1 Thessalonians 5:19–21). John adds, "do not believe just any spirit; test the spirits to find out whether they are from God" (1 John 4:1). We should neither dismiss every inner prompting nor accept every one as God's voice. Our own desires and fears can easily sound like God. Here are four simple tests.

1. **Scripture.** God never contradicts His written Word. An impression that leads away from what the Bible teaches is not from Him.
2. **Conscience.** Does it lead towards holiness, love and humility, or towards pride, fear or sin?
3. **Wise counsel.** What do mature believers who know you and know the Bible say? God's guidance is usually confirmed in the community of His people.
4. **Fruit.** Over time, does it bear good fruit: love, peace, faithfulness and the building up of others?

Listening prayer is not a special gift for a few. It is part of the relationship every child of God may enjoy. Jesus said His sheep listen to His voice (John 10:27). We learn that voice the way a child learns a parent's voice: by spending time together, and above all by knowing what He has already said.

### Persevering prayer and unanswered prayer

Jesus told His disciples "a parable about their need to pray at all times and not lose heart" (Luke 18:1). A poor widow kept coming to an unjust judge, demanding justice, until he gave in just to be rid of her. If even a bad judge yields to persistence, Jesus said, "surely God will give justice to the people He has chosen, when they call out to Him day and night" (Luke 18:7). In Luke 11 He told of a man who knocks on his friend's door at midnight and keeps on knocking until he gets the bread he needs. Then He said, "ask, and God will give to you; search, and you will find; knock, and God will open the door for you" (Luke 11:9).

But what about prayers that seem to go unanswered? Every honest Christian knows them. A sick child who did not recover. A marriage that broke. A job that never came. Here we must be gentle and truthful.

Sometimes God says "wait." Andrew Murray, the South African pastor, wrote about this in *With Christ in the School of Prayer*:

> "Let us learn to give God time. God needs time with us."
> Andrew Murray, *With Christ in the School of Prayer*

Sometimes God says "no," because He has something better. Paul begged the Lord three times to remove his "thorn in the flesh." The answer was not the removal he asked for, but a deeper gift: "My grace is enough for you, for power comes to its fullness in weakness" (2 Corinthians 12:9). Even Jesus, in Gethsemane, prayed, "Father, if You are willing, remove this cup from Me. Yet let Your will be done, not Mine" (Luke 22:42). The cup was not removed, and through that unanswered request the world was saved.

And sometimes we simply do not know why. In those times we hold on to what we do know: that God is our Father, that He is good, and that He hears. "Here is the boldness we have in coming to Him: whenever we ask for something in line with His will, He listens to us" (1 John 5:14). Unanswered prayer is not unheard prayer. Keep praying. Keep trusting. Tell Him honestly how you feel, as the psalmists did. He can bear your questions.

### Praying in the Spirit

We do not pray alone. "The Spirit also lends a hand in our weakness. What we should pray for, and how, we do not know. But the Spirit Himself pleads on our behalf, with groans that no words carry" (Romans 8:26). When we are too tired, too confused or too grieved to find words, the Holy Spirit prays within us and for us.

Paul tells us to "pray in the Spirit at every opportunity, with every kind of prayer and request" (Ephesians 6:18), and Jude urges believers to keep "praying in the Holy Spirit" (Jude 1:20). Christians understand this phrase in somewhat different ways. Many Pentecostal and charismatic believers include in it praying in tongues, a gift discussed further in Chapter 23. Others understand it more generally. But all agree on the heart of it: true prayer is not our own effort to reach God. It is led, prompted and empowered by the Spirit who lives in us. So whatever our tradition, we can begin every prayer by asking, "Holy Spirit, teach me to pray."

### Practical patterns of prayer

How can we build a life of prayer? Here are some simple patterns that believers across the centuries have found helpful.

**Short prayers through the day.** When the Persian king asked Nehemiah what he wanted, "I prayed to the God of heaven, and I said to the king" (Nehemiah 2:4–5). There was no time for a long prayer, only a quick lifting of the heart. You can do the same: a word of thanks when the bus arrives, a cry for help before a hard conversation, a blessing over a sleeping child. "Never stop praying" (1 Thessalonians 5:17) does not mean being on our knees all day. It means a heart that keeps turning towards God.

**Set times of prayer.** Daniel "got down on his knees and prayed, giving thanks to his God, just as he had always done" (Daniel 6:10), three times a day, even when it put his life in danger. Regular times give our prayer a backbone. Chapter 8 will look closely at one of the most important: the morning.

**Longer seasons of prayer.** Before choosing His twelve apostles, Jesus "stayed up all night in prayer to God" (Luke 6:12). From time to time, before big decisions or in times of need, set aside a longer time: half a day, an evening, a night with others.

**Praying through a list.** Intercession is easier when we are organised. Keep a list of people and needs. You might pray for your family every day, and give each day of the week a different focus: church leaders on Monday, neighbours and colleagues on Tuesday, missionaries on Wednesday, the nation on Thursday, and so on.

**A prayer journal.** Write down what you ask and the date. Later, write down how God answered. George Müller kept careful records of his requests and the answers he received, and they became a great encouragement to his faith. A journal helps us to remember, to give thanks, and to see God's faithfulness over the years.

The most important pattern is simply to begin. A short prayer, prayed honestly today, is worth more than a long prayer you plan to pray someday.

### Questions for reflection

- How does it change your prayer to think of it as answering God, who has already spoken in His Word?
- Which part of the Lord's Prayer do you find easiest to pray, and which hardest? Why?
- Of adoration, confession, thanksgiving, supplication and intercession, which do you most often leave out?
- What strikes you most about John Hyde's life? What might persistence in prayer look like for you?
- Have you had an inner impression you believed was from God? How might the four tests have helped you?
- Is there an unanswered prayer that has discouraged you? What would it mean to keep bringing it honestly to God?

### A step for this week

Each day this week, pray slowly through the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13), pausing after each line to add your own words: praise for who God is, the needs of your household, the sins you need to confess, the people you need to forgive. Then start a simple prayer list in a notebook with five people you will pray for each day, writing the date beside each request. At the end of the week, read over your list and thank God for anything you have seen Him do.
