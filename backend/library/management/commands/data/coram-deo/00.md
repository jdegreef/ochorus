## Introduction: Before the Face of God

> "My heart says to You what You have said: 'Seek My face.' So I will seek Your face, LORD."
> — Psalm 27:8 (OWB)

The hall in the German city of Worms was packed and hot. It was April 1521. At one end sat the most powerful man in Europe, the young Emperor Charles V, surrounded by princes, bishops and the envoys of the Pope. Torches were lit as the evening came on. On a table lay a pile of books. In front of the table stood a monk in a plain black habit, Martin Luther, a teacher of the Bible from the small town of Wittenberg.

The question put to him was simple. Were these books his? And would he take back what he had written in them?

Every face in that room was turned towards him. The emperor could have him outlawed. The Church could hand him over to be burned, as it had burned others before him. Many in the crowd wanted him to fail, and some wanted him dead. Luther had asked for a night to think. He had spent it in prayer. Now he gave his answer: unless he was shown from Scripture and plain reason that he was wrong, he could not take back what he had written. His conscience, he said, was held captive by the Word of God. To go against conscience was neither right nor safe. Then he asked God to help him.

What gave a frightened monk the courage to stand there? Luther had learned to ask one question above every other. Not, "What will these faces think of me?" but, "How do I stand before the face of God?"

### Living before faces

You will probably never stand before an emperor. But you know what it is to live before faces.

Every one of us lives before someone's face. We dress, speak and work with an eye on how others see us: our parents, our employers, our neighbours, our church, the crowd. A student lies awake before results day, imagining her father's face. A young man takes a loan he cannot afford so that his wedding will look as fine as his cousin's. A pastor measures his worth by the size of last Sunday's crowd. A mother hides her tears from the women at the well, because a believer is supposed to be strong. Much of our worry comes from this. We long to be approved, and we fear being exposed.

Today the faces never go home. They follow us on the phone in our pocket, counting who has seen us, who has liked us, who has passed us by. We can spend a whole life performing for an audience that is never satisfied.

The Reformers, the church leaders of the 1500s who called the Church in Europe back to the Bible, had a phrase for a different way to live: *coram Deo*, "before the face of God." Luther set it against living *coram hominibus*, "before people." The question that matters most is not what others think of us, but how we stand before the One who sees everything, knows everything, and loves us still.

That is what this book is about. It is not mainly about religion, rules or even good habits. It is about a face, the face of God, and what it means to live every day in His presence.

### The story of God's face

Read the Bible from beginning to end, and you will find that it tells the story of God's face.

In the beginning, the man and the woman walked with God in the garden. Nothing stood between them. Then sin entered, and the first thing they did was hide.

> "Then they heard the sound of the LORD God walking in the garden at the breezy time of the day, and the man and his wife hid from the LORD God among the trees of the garden." (Genesis 3:8)

In Hebrew, they hid "from the face of the LORD God." We have been hiding ever since: behind busyness, behind achievement, behind religion, behind shame. Yet notice who moves first. It is not the man who goes looking for God. It is God who comes looking for the man: "But the LORD God called to the man, 'Where are you?'" (Genesis 3:9). God has been asking that question ever since.

God never stopped seeking. Out of all the nations He drew near to one people, and to one man among them. "The LORD would speak with Moses face to face, the way a person talks with a friend" (Exodus 33:11). Moses wanted more. "Please, let me see Your glory" (Exodus 33:18), he prayed, and God showed him as much as a sinful man could bear.

God also gave His priests a blessing to speak over His people every day. It is a blessing about His face:

> "May the LORD bless you and watch over you. May the LORD make His face shine toward you and show you favor. May the LORD lift up His face to look on you and grant you peace." (Numbers 6:24-26)

Think about what that means. When a father's face lights up as his child walks into the room, the child knows he is welcome. When a face turns away, he knows he is not. God wanted His people to know, morning by morning, that His face was turned toward them in favour.

David knew it. When enemies surrounded him, he did not first ask God for an army. He answered God's invitation: "You have said, 'Seek My face.' So I will seek Your face, LORD" (Psalm 27:8).

Then, in the fullness of time, the face of God appeared among us. A baby was born in Bethlehem, and the eternal Son of God had a human face that could be seen and touched. "No one has ever seen God. The one and only Son, who is Himself God and is close beside the Father, has made Him known" (John 1:18). When Philip asked Jesus to show them the Father, Jesus answered, "To have seen Me is to have seen the Father" (John 14:9). Paul puts it this way:

> "For the God who said, 'Out of darkness light will shine,' is the one who shone in our hearts, to bring the light of the knowledge of God's glory, the glory seen in the face of Christ." (2 Corinthians 4:6)

On the cross that face was struck, spat upon and crowned with thorns. Jesus took our hiding and our shame so that the hiding could end. Now, because of His blood, we are invited to come near: "So let us approach with hearts that are true, fully sure in our faith" (Hebrews 10:22). Through the Spirit, we are changed as we look at Him: "But all of us, our faces uncovered, look upon the Lord's glory as in a mirror, and we are being changed into the same image we see, from glory to ever greater glory" (2 Corinthians 3:18).

And the story ends where it began, only better. In the last chapter of the Bible, God's servants are in the city of God, and "they will look upon His face, and His name will be written on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4). The garden was lost by hiding from His face. The city is entered by seeing it.

### Created to know Him

You were made for this. Jesus said, "And eternal life is this: that they know You, the only true God, and the one You sent, Jesus Christ" (John 17:3). Not to know about Him. To know Him.

That difference matters. You can know a great deal about the president of your country: where he was born, what he believes, what he did last week. That does not mean he knows you, or would let you into his house. Many people know a great deal about God. They can recite the creed and argue about doctrine. But eternal life is not a set of facts in the head. It is a relationship with a Person.

God Himself says that this is the one thing worth boasting about:

> "The wise must not boast about their wisdom, the strong must not boast about their strength, and the rich must not boast about their wealth. Instead, whoever boasts should boast of this: that they understand and know Me, that I am the LORD, who acts with loyal love, justice and righteousness on the earth." (Jeremiah 9:23-24)

We only see ourselves rightly in the light of God's face, and we only rest when we have found Him. John Calvin, a French pastor who led the church in Geneva, began his great teaching book, the *Institutes of the Christian Religion*, by saying that true wisdom comes down to two things, the knowledge of God and the knowledge of ourselves. More than a thousand years earlier, Augustine of North Africa looked back on his restless youth and confessed that God had made us for Himself, and that our hearts are restless until they rest in Him. You will hear both men in their own words in Chapter 2.

### Why this book

Many Christians believe the right things but feel far from God. Some came to faith with great joy and have slowly grown dry. Others love God sincerely but have never been shown how to walk with Him day by day, or how faith reaches into marriage, work, money, suffering and church life. They have a Sunday faith and a Monday life, and the two hardly meet.

And many new believers in the fast-growing churches of Africa, Asia and Latin America have been given a Bible but few tools to help them grow. Some have been taught a gospel of quick blessing that leaves them confused when trouble comes. Others have been given long lists of rules, but never told that God wants them, not only their obedience.

This book is for all of them. It is for the new believer taking first steps, and for the one who has walked with God for fifty years and wants to finish well. It is written to be simple enough for the first and deep enough for the second.

### The journey ahead

The book moves in five parts.

**Part One, Foundations**, asks who God is: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Everything depends on knowing whose face we seek. We will see how we are brought into relationship with Him through the cross of Christ and the gift of the Spirit.

**Part Two, Walking with God**, begins with love, the heart of everything, and then shows how we meet God daily: in His Word, in prayer, in the morning watch, in abiding in Christ and in the spiritual disciplines.

**Part Three, Becoming Like Christ**, explores how God makes us like His Son: discipleship, the life of the Kingdom in the Sermon on the Mount, faith, holiness, the healing of the soul, spiritual warfare and suffering.

**Part Four, Living as God's People**, turns to our life together: marriage and singleness, raising children, work, the Church and its ministries, spiritual gifts and serving one another.

**Part Five, Living for God's Purposes**, looks outward and forward: the Great Commission, stewardship, finishing well, and the hope of judgment, paradise and the new heavens and new earth. A final chapter gathers it all together.

Along the way you will meet men and women from every century and continent who learned to live before the face of God. You will meet Augustine and Athanasius of North Africa, and Polycarp, who would not deny his King. You will meet Luther and Calvin, Brother Lawrence in his kitchen and George Müller with his orphans. You will meet Hudson Taylor in China and Amy Carmichael in India, Andrew Murray in South Africa, and Festo Kivengere of Uganda, who learned to forgive the men who killed his friends. Each of them discovered that the presence of God is not a distant ideal but a daily reality. Many of their own books are in the Ochorus library, and you can read them in full.

### How to read this book

Each chapter follows the same pattern. It opens with a passage of Scripture. It teaches from the Bible, quoting it often, so that you hear God's voice and not only ours. It tells in some depth the story of one man or woman who lived what the chapter teaches. And it ends with questions for reflection and one practical step to take in the week ahead.

Read it with your Bible open. Look up the verses. Underline what speaks to you. Read it alone if you must, but better still, read it with others: with your husband or wife, with a friend, with a small group or a class of new believers. A chapter a week will carry a group through the book in about seven months.

Where faithful Christians disagree, this book says so honestly. On questions such as baptism in the Spirit, the gifts of the Spirit or the Sabbath, you will find the main views set out fairly, and then the common ground that all share. Where the book takes a view of its own, it says so plainly. These discussions sit in sections whose titles begin "Going deeper". If you are new to the faith, you can pass over them on a first reading and come back later; nothing you need to begin depends on them. The Appendix explains how to tell the beliefs that define the Christian faith from those where believers may differ, so that we can hold firm on the first and be gracious on the rest.

If you meet a word you do not know, look for it in the Glossary at the very end of the book.

Do not rush. This is not a book to finish, but a life to begin.

### New to faith? Start here

If you have only just come to faith in Jesus, welcome. You do not need to read thirty chapters before you can begin to walk with God. Here is a shorter path to start with:

1. This Introduction.
2. Chapter 2, Almighty God, Our Father: who God is.
3. Chapter 3, Jesus Christ, Our Redeemer: what Jesus has done for you, and how to receive Him.
4. Chapter 4, The Holy Spirit, Our Helper: God living within you.
5. Chapter 5, Love: The Mark of a Christian.
6. Chapter 6, The Word of God: how to read the Bible.
7. Chapter 7, Prayer: how to talk with God.
8. Chapter 21, The Church: why you need God's family, and what it does together.

After that, go back to Chapter 8 and read on in order. The rest can wait until you are ready.

Alongside your reading, take these four first steps. They are explained more fully in the Conclusion.

- **Be baptized.** Baptism with water is the first step of obedience Jesus gave His followers, a public sign that you belong to Him (Matthew 28:19; Acts 2:38). Ask a pastor about it. Chapter 21 explains baptism and the Lord's Supper, the meal Jesus gave His followers to remember Him.
- **Join a local church.** Find a church where the Bible is taught and love is real, and do not only attend; belong.
- **Find a mentor.** Ask a mature believer whose life you respect to read the Bible and pray with you regularly.
- **Begin a daily time with God.** Start small: one chapter of a Gospel each morning, a prayer about what you read, and thanks for one thing.

### Finding your way around the Bible

The Bible is not one book but a library of sixty-six books, written over many centuries and gathered into one volume. It has two main parts. The **Old Testament** (thirty-nine books, from Genesis to Malachi) tells the story of God's dealings with the world and with the people of Israel before Jesus came. The **New Testament** (twenty-seven books, from Matthew to Revelation) tells of Jesus and of the first Christians. Its first four books, Matthew, Mark, Luke and John, are called the **Gospels**. "Gospel" means "good news": the news that God saves us through Jesus Christ. The Gospels are the best place to begin reading.

This book quotes the Bible often, and each quotation is followed by a reference such as "Genesis 3:8". The name is the book of the Bible, the first number is the chapter, and the number after the colon is the verse. So Genesis 3:8 means the book of Genesis, chapter 3, verse 8. "Psalm 91:1–4" means verses 1 to 4. A number in front of a name, as in "1 Corinthians" or "2 Timothy", marks the first or second letter with that name. Every Bible has a contents page that tells you where each book begins. When you read this book on Ochorus, you can also tap any reference to read the verse.

### A short map of church history

You will meet men and women from many centuries in these pages. This short map will help you place them.

- **About AD 30: Jesus and the apostles.** Jesus dies, rises and returns to the Father. On the day of Pentecost, fifty days after His resurrection, He pours out the Holy Spirit. The apostles, the messengers Jesus chose and sent out, carry the good news across the Roman world.
- **AD 100 to 400: the early Church.** Christians are often persecuted, and many die for their faith, such as Polycarp (about 155) and Cyprian of Carthage (258). Antony of Egypt goes into the desert to pray. Church leaders meet at the Councils of Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381) and agree on the **Nicene Creed**, a short statement of what Christians believe about God. Athanasius of Alexandria and Augustine of North Africa (354–430) are the great teachers of this age.
- **1054: East and West divide.** The churches of the Greek-speaking East (today's Orthodox churches) and the Latin-speaking West (the Roman Catholic Church) split apart.
- **The 1500s: the Reformation.** Martin Luther in Germany, John Calvin in Geneva and others, known as the Reformers, call the Church back to the Bible and to salvation by grace through faith. The churches that come from their work are called **Protestant**.
- **The 1600s and 1700s: revival.** The Puritans in England seek a church and a life shaped wholly by the Bible. Brother Lawrence prays in a monastery kitchen in Paris. Susanna Wesley raises her children in England, and her sons John and Charles lead the great revival that gave birth to the Methodist churches.
- **The 1800s: the great age of missions.** Christians carry the gospel to every continent: Hudson Taylor to China, Alexander Mackay to Uganda, Amy Carmichael to India. George Müller cares for orphans in Bristol, Andrew Murray pastors in South Africa, and Pandita Ramabai serves the women of India.
- **The 1900s to today: a worldwide Church.** The Pentecostal movement spreads around the world. Believers such as Sundar Singh and Bakht Singh in India, Watchman Nee and Wang Mingdao in China, Corrie ten Boom in the Netherlands and Festo Kivengere in Uganda stay faithful through war and persecution. Today the Church is growing fastest in Africa, Asia and Latin America.

Over the centuries Christians have formed many families of churches, often called **denominations** or **traditions**: Catholic and Orthodox; Lutheran, Reformed and Presbyterian, and Anglican (the Church of England and its sister churches); and Baptist, Methodist and Pentecostal, among others. They differ on some questions but share the central truths of the faith. This book draws on witnesses from many of them. The Glossary explains these names, and the Appendix explains which beliefs all Christians share and which ones they differ on.

### Grace first

One more thing must be said before we begin, because everything else rests on it.

None of this is about earning God's favour. You do not walk with God so that He will accept you; you walk with Him because, in Christ, He already has. "So now that we have been declared righteous by faith, we are at peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ" (Romans 5:1).

That is why living before God's face is not a burden but a release. The faces of the crowd are never satisfied. They praise us one day and forget us the next. But the face that matters most has already turned toward us in Christ, and it shines with welcome. Luther could stand before the emperor because he had first stood before God, a sinner forgiven and accepted. When you know how you stand with God, you are free from the tyranny of every other face.

The practices in these pages, reading the Word, praying, keeping the morning watch, fasting, serving, giving, are not ladders to climb into His presence. They are doors He has already opened, and He is waiting on the other side.

So come. Seek His face. And may the LORD make His face shine toward you.

### Questions for reflection

- Whose faces do you live before most? Whose approval do you find yourself seeking, and whose disapproval do you fear?
- Read Genesis 3:8-9. In what ways do people hide from God today? In what ways have you hidden from Him?
- What is the difference between knowing about God and knowing Him (John 17:3)? Which describes you better right now?
- Which part of this book's journey do you most need, and why?
- Why does it matter that we walk with God because we are already accepted, and not in order to be accepted?

### A step for this week

Each morning this week, before you look at your phone or speak to anyone, read Psalm 27:8 and pray it back to God in your own words: "Lord, You have said, 'Seek My face.' Today I will seek Your face." Then, once during the day, when you notice yourself worrying about what someone thinks of you, stop and remind yourself quietly, "I am living before the face of God, and in Christ His face is turned toward me."
