## Spiritual Warfare: Angels, Demons and Victory in Christ

> "Finally, be made strong in the Lord and in His mighty strength. Clothe yourselves in God's complete armor, so that you can hold your ground against the devil's cunning plans. Our fight is not with flesh and blood."
> — Ephesians 6:10–12 (OWB)

In Chapter 15 we saw God healing and renewing the soul. But every believer soon discovers that this growth does not happen in peaceful surroundings. There is opposition. The Christian life is a walk with God, and it is also a battle.

Some Christians hardly think about this at all. Others see a demon behind every flat tyre and headache. The Bible avoids both mistakes. It tells us plainly that we have a real enemy, and it tells us even more plainly that Jesus Christ has defeated him. This chapter is about living coram Deo, before the face of God, in a world where the battle is real but the outcome is already settled.

### The reality of the unseen realm

When the king of Aram sent an army by night to capture the prophet Elisha, Elisha's servant woke to find the city surrounded by horses and chariots. He panicked. Elisha did not. "'Do not be afraid,' Elisha answered. 'There are more with us than there are with them.'" Then he prayed for his servant's eyes to be opened, "and he saw that the hill was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha" (2 Kings 6:16–17).

The servant's eyes did not create the army of God. They only showed him what was already there. That is how the Bible speaks of the spiritual world. It is as real as the physical world, even when we cannot see it. In many parts of Africa, Asia and Latin America, believers need no persuading of this; they have grown up surrounded by talk of spirits, curses and powers. In much of the West, people have been taught to laugh at such things. The Bible corrects both. The unseen world is real, but it is not ruled by chance or by the spirits. It is ruled by God.

### Angels: servants of God and of His people

Angels are created beings. They are not God, they are not to be worshipped, and they are not the spirits of people who have died. They are spirits made by God to serve Him. The book of Revelation pictures them around God's throne, a number "beyond counting: ten thousands of ten thousands, and thousands of thousands" (Revelation 5:11), worshipping the Lamb.

Angels also serve God's people. "Are not all the angels spirits in holy service, sent out to serve for the sake of those who are going to inherit salvation?" (Hebrews 1:14). In Scripture they bring messages, as Gabriel did to Mary. They protect, as when an angel shut the mouths of the lions for Daniel. They carry out God's judgments and His rescues. "For He will give His angels orders about you, to protect you wherever you go" (Psalm 91:11).

We do not pray to angels or seek experiences of them; our eyes are on the Lord who sends them. But it is a comfort to know that, when we walk with God, we are never as alone as we feel.

### Satan and demons: their origin, limits and tactics

The Bible does not tell us everything about where evil spirits came from, but it tells us enough. Satan and his demons are fallen angels. Jude speaks of "the angels who did not stay in the place of authority that was theirs, but abandoned their own dwelling" (Jude 6). Revelation describes "the great dragon, cast out: the serpent of old, the one called the devil and Satan, deceiver of the whole world" (Revelation 12:9). Jesus said, "I watched Satan fall from heaven like a flash of lightning" (Luke 10:18). Many Christians have also seen Satan's pride and fall reflected in the prophecies against the kings of Babylon and Tyre (Isaiah 14; Ezekiel 28).

Satan is not God's equal opposite. He is a creature, and his power has limits. In the book of Job he cannot touch Job without God's permission, and he must stop where God tells him to stop. He is not everywhere at once, he does not know everything, and he cannot snatch a believer out of the Father's hand.

Scripture shows three main tactics he uses.

**Deception.** Jesus called him "a liar and the father of lies" (John 8:44). His first words in the Bible twisted God's word: "Did God really tell you not to eat from any tree in the garden?" (Genesis 3:1). He still works by lies: about God ("He does not really love you"), about ourselves ("You are worthless") and about sin ("It will make you happy").

**Accusation.** Revelation calls him "the one who accused our brothers and sisters, accusing them day and night before our God" (Revelation 12:10). He reminds us of our failures and tells us God could never accept us. Often his accusations contain some truth, which is what makes them so painful.

**Temptation.** In the wilderness he tempted Jesus to meet His own needs His own way, to test God, and to gain the world by bowing down to him (Matthew 4:1–11). He tempts us the same way: to grab what God has not given, to doubt God's care, and to take short cuts around the cross.

### Principalities and powers

Paul writes, "Our fight is not with flesh and blood. It is with the rulers, with the authorities, with the world rulers of this darkness, with the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places" (Ephesians 6:12). Behind the visible evil of the world, behind cruel systems, wars, oppression and corruption, there are spiritual powers at work.

The book of Daniel gives a rare glimpse of this. Daniel had prayed and fasted for three weeks. Then an angel came to him and said, "From the first day that you set your mind to gain understanding and humbled yourself before your God, your words were heard, and it is in answer to them that I have come. But the prince of the kingdom of Persia stood in my way for twenty-one days, until Michael, one of the chief princes, came to my aid" (Daniel 10:12–13). Daniel's prayer was heard at once, but there was a battle in the unseen world before the answer arrived.

This teaches us two things. First, our real enemy is never the neighbour, the politician or the relative who opposes us. People can be used by evil, but they are not the enemy; they are people for whom Christ died. Second, prayer matters far more than we think. When we pray, we take part in a battle we cannot see.

### Christ's victory and our authority in His name

The heart of this chapter is not the devil's power but Christ's victory. "This is why the Son of God was revealed: to undo the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8). At the cross, what looked like Satan's greatest triumph became his defeat. "He disarmed the rulers and authorities and exposed them to public disgrace, leading them as captives in His victory procession by the cross" (Colossians 2:15). By His death Jesus stripped of power "the one who holds the power of death, the devil" (Hebrews 2:14).

The picture is of a Roman general leading defeated kings in chains through the streets. The enemy still fights, but the war has been decided.

Believers share in that victory. When Jesus sent out seventy-two disciples, they came back amazed: "Lord, when we use Your name, even the demons obey us!" Jesus answered, "I have given you authority to trample snakes and scorpions underfoot, and authority over all the enemy's power" (Luke 10:17–19). Then He added a warning: "Do not be glad because the spirits obey you. Be glad instead that your names stand written in heaven" (Luke 10:20). Our authority is real, but our joy is in our relationship with God, not in our power.

"As for you, dear children, you belong to God, and you have already won the victory over them, because the One in you is greater than the one in the world" (1 John 4:4).

### Martin Luther: a mighty fortress

Martin Luther, the German Reformer of the sixteenth century, took the devil seriously, perhaps more seriously than most Christians today. He had known terrible inner struggles as a young monk, seasons of fear, doubt and despair that he called his *Anfechtungen*, his attacks or assaults. After he stood before the emperor at Worms in 1521 and refused to take back his teaching, friends hid him for his safety in the Wartburg castle. There, lonely and often unwell, he felt these assaults sharply, and there he also translated the New Testament into German so that ordinary people could read it.

Luther learned to fight the devil not with his own strength but with the Word and with Christ. In his *Table Talk*, now in the Ochorus library, he described how the enemy attacked him with accusation, asking whether God could really be gracious to such a sinner. Luther knew it was useless to answer by pointing to his own love for God, because the devil would simply reply that he had not loved God with all his heart, and his conscience would agree. Instead, he said:

> "...at such a time I must arm myself and encounter him with this text, namely: 'That Jesus Christ died for me, and through him I have a gracious God and Father; Christ hath made an atonement for me.'"
> Martin Luther, *Table Talk*

That is exactly how we answer accusation. Not "I am good enough", but "Christ died for me."

Luther also faced deception. He tells of a time when, praying, he saw what seemed to be a shining vision of Christ with His wounds. He was tempted to treat it as a special revelation. But he recalled that Christ comes to us in His Word, in a humbler form, and he spoke to the vision: "Avoid, thou confounded devil; I know no other Christ than he who was crucified, and who in his Word is pictured unto me." The vision vanished. The Word of God was his test for every experience.

Luther's best-known hymn, "A Mighty Fortress Is Our God", is built on Psalm 46: "God is a shelter and a strength for us, a help always close at hand in times of distress" (Psalm 46:1). It admits that the world is full of devils and that the ancient foe is strong, but it insists that one champion, Christ Himself, fights for us and will win the field. For Luther, spiritual warfare was never mainly about the devil. It was about trusting Christ.

### The armour of God

Paul gives us a picture of a Roman soldier, and invites us to put on God's own armour (Ephesians 6:13–18). Notice that it is God's armour. We do not make it; we put it on.

- **The belt of truth.** "Tie truth around your waist as a belt" (Ephesians 6:14). A soldier's belt held everything else in place. Truth, God's truth believed and honesty lived out, holds the Christian life together. Lies, even small ones, open the door to the father of lies.
- **The breastplate of righteousness.** This guards the heart. It is first the righteousness of Christ given to us, which answers every accusation, and then a life of integrity that gives the enemy no foothold.
- **Shoes of the gospel of peace.** "Put on the readiness that the gospel of peace gives" (Ephesians 6:15). A soldier needs firm footing. The gospel gives us peace with God and makes us ready to carry that peace to others.
- **The shield of faith.** "With it you will be able to put out all the burning arrows of the evil one" (Ephesians 6:16). Fear, doubt, lust and despair fly at us like burning arrows. Faith takes God at His word and puts them out.
- **The helmet of salvation.** This guards the mind. Knowing that we are saved, kept and destined for glory protects us from the thoughts that would pull us down.
- **The sword of the Spirit.** "The sword that the Spirit gives, which is the word of God" (Ephesians 6:17). This is the one weapon for attack as well as defence.

Then Paul adds prayer: "Pray in the Spirit at every opportunity, with every kind of prayer and request" (Ephesians 6:18). The English Puritan William Gurnall wrote a huge book on these verses, *The Christian in Complete Armour*, published in the 1650s and 1660s. His great point was that a soldier must wear the whole armour, not just the pieces he likes, and must keep it on, because the enemy does not announce his attacks.

In *The Pilgrim's Progress*, John Bunyan shows his pilgrim, Christian, meeting the monster Apollyon in the Valley of Humiliation. Christian wonders whether to run, but he remembers something about his armour:

> "He considered again, that he had no armor for his back, and therefore thought that to turn the back to him might give him greater advantage with ease to pierce him with his darts; therefore he resolved to venture and stand his ground."
> John Bunyan, *The Pilgrim's Progress*

God's armour is made for facing the enemy, not for running.

### How to fight our enemy

How, then, do we fight in daily life? Scripture gives us several weapons.

**Truth and the Word.** When Jesus was tempted, He answered every time with Scripture: "No one lives on bread alone, but on every word that comes from the mouth of God" (Matthew 4:4), and finally, "Get away, Satan! ... You must worship the Lord your God and serve no one but Him" (Matthew 4:10). If the Son of God used the written Word, so must we. Know specific verses for your specific battles.

**Taking thoughts captive.** Much of the battle happens in the mind. "We capture every thought and lead it away to obey Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5). When a lie comes, do not entertain it. Name it, and answer it with truth.

**Prayer and fasting.** Jesus said of one stubborn case, "This kind cannot be made to come out by any means except prayer" (Mark 9:29). Daniel prayed and fasted. Fasting (see Chapter 10) is a way of saying to God that we need Him more than food.

**Praise.** Worship lifts our eyes from the enemy to God. "Out of the mouths of children and babies ... You have built a stronghold against Your foes" (Psalm 8:2). Paul and Silas sang in prison at midnight. Praise is a declaration of whose side we are on.

**The name of Jesus.** God has given Jesus "the name that is above every name, so that every knee will bend before the name of Jesus" (Philippians 2:9–10). We pray and command in His name, not as a magic word, but as servants acting on His authority.

**The blood of the Lamb and the word of our testimony.** Of the believers who overcame the accuser, Revelation says: "They have overcome him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony; they did not hold their lives dear, even in the face of death" (Revelation 12:11). The blood of Jesus answers every accusation. Our testimony, speaking aloud what Christ has done for us, declares His victory.

**Resisting and standing firm.** "So place yourselves under God. Stand against the devil, and he will run from you" (James 4:7). The order matters: first we submit to God, then we resist the devil. Peter adds, "The devil, your opponent, roams about like a lion that roars, hunting for someone to swallow up. Stand up to him and hold firm in your faith" (1 Peter 5:8–9). Four times in Ephesians 6 Paul tells us to stand. Often victory simply means not giving way.

### Hedges of protection

In the book of Job, Satan complains to God: "Have You not put a protective hedge around him and his household and everything he owns on every side?" (Job 1:10). God protects His people, and it is right to ask Him to.

Many believers pray daily for God's protection over themselves, their children, their homes and their work, often using Psalm 91: "Whoever makes a home under the cover of the Most High will spend the night in the shade of the Almighty" (Psalm 91:1). "The angel of the LORD pitches camp all around the people who fear Him, and sets them free" (Psalm 34:7). Luther's *Small Catechism*, a short book of questions and answers he wrote to teach the faith, includes an evening prayer that believers have prayed for centuries:

> "I put myself in your care, body and soul and all that I have. Let Your holy angels be with me, so that the evil enemy will not gain power over me."
> Martin Luther, *The Small Catechism*

Praying for protection is not a guarantee that we will never suffer; Job himself suffered greatly, and Chapter 17 will look at that. But nothing reaches us except what God allows, and He sets the limits.

### Deliverance and freedom

The Gospels show Jesus setting people free from evil spirits again and again. A man who had been possessed by a legion of demons was found "sitting there with clothes on and in his right mind" (Mark 5:15). The apostles did the same in Jesus' name (Acts 16:18).

All Christians agree on the most important thing: Jesus came "to announce freedom to the captives" (Luke 4:18), and He still sets captives free. Wise practice everywhere includes leading people to Christ and to repentance, renouncing any involvement with witchcraft, charms or occult powers, forgiving others, filling the life with the Word and prayer, and caring for the person in a loving church afterwards. Freedom is not only about driving something out. It is about bringing Christ in.

### Going deeper: deliverance ministry today

*This section is for going further. If you are new to the faith, you can skip to the next section and come back to it later.*

Christians hold different views about deliverance ministry, the practice of praying for people to be set free from evil spirits. Some churches practise it often and openly. Others believe it is needed only rarely and should be done quietly by mature leaders. Many also discuss whether a true believer, in whom the Holy Spirit lives, can be under the control of a demon, or only troubled and oppressed by one from outside. Sincere believers answer these questions differently. But all agree that Jesus sets captives free, and that the wise practice above belongs in every church.

### Staying balanced

C. S. Lewis, the British writer, wrote *The Screwtape Letters* in 1942, a clever book of imaginary letters from a senior devil to his young nephew, teaching him how to lead a man away from God. In its preface Lewis warned of two opposite errors about devils, both equally pleasing to them: to disbelieve in their existence, or to take an excessive and unhealthy interest in them.

Both errors are common. Some believers are spiritually asleep, unaware of the enemy's schemes. "We know well enough what he has in mind" (2 Corinthians 2:11), Paul wrote; many today do not. Others are so fascinated with the devil that they talk more about him than about Jesus, blame every problem on demons, and live in fear.

The cure for both is the same: keep your eyes on Jesus. "Let us keep our eyes on Jesus, the pioneer who leads the way in faith and brings it to perfection" (Hebrews 12:2). We do not walk through life staring at the enemy. We walk before the face of God, under the shelter of the Almighty, in the victory of the Lamb. And from that place, standing firm, we are not afraid.

### Questions for reflection

- Are you more likely to ignore the spiritual battle or to be fearful and fascinated by it? Which correction from this chapter do you most need?
- Which of the enemy's tactics, deception, accusation or temptation, do you face most often? How will you answer it?
- Luther answered accusation with "Christ died for me." Which truths from Scripture can you use when you feel condemned?
- Which piece of the armour of God do you most need to put on this week, and why?
- How does knowing that Christ has already disarmed the powers change the way you pray?
- Who in your family or community could you pray a hedge of protection around this week?

### A step for this week

Each morning this week, read Ephesians 6:10–18 aloud and "put on" each piece of the armour in prayer, naming it and thanking God for it. Then choose one verse to be your sword for the week, a promise that answers the lie or temptation you face most often, write it on a card, and carry it with you. Whenever the lie or temptation comes, speak the verse aloud, as Jesus did in the wilderness. Each evening, pray for God's protection over your household, using Psalm 91 or Luther's evening prayer.
