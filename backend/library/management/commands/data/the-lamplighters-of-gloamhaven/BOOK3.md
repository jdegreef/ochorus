# THE GLOWSTONE BARGAIN — Book 3 bible & beat sheet
Book 3 of *The Lamplighters of Gloamhaven*. 15 chapters, ~45,000–49,000 words. Ages 9–12.

**Read `OUTLINE.md` first** — the series canon (voice & style §1, world §2, the lamp's rules §3, cast §4, lore §6). Then `BOOK2.md` (Book 2's cast and things). Everything there still holds. Then read Book 2's last chapter (`the-black-stair.md`, "## The Lights of Vaelport") so you know exactly where everyone stands and how they talk now.

Theme (never quoted): 2 Corinthians 11:14 — the dark disguised as light; Luke 22 — Peter's denial and Judas's bargain; Matthew 4 — temptations that offer good things the wrong way. Core truths: **counterfeit light shines but does not warm**; **good things offered on the wrong terms are still a trap**; **the lamp belongs to no one — it is given** ("It was never mine. Give it."); **love lays down its life**. This is the darkest book of the six — the series' Good Friday — but it must never be hopeless: the reader should close it grieving and wanting Book 4, not despairing.

## 1. Where Book 2 left them
- Morning. Wren (12; the lamp hidden inside her coat on Ash's order; the red ribbon at her left wrist, which goes bleached WHITE in Vaelport's light and red again by the lamp; the Delvers' **singing-stone** in her pocket — "it hears the cold", hums when Hollow-cold is near, and has been oddly silent near Vaelport), Tobin (13; spectacles; his mother's compass, which points north-and-a-bit-east to the Bright Mountain and has *never once* pointed at Vaelport; **his father's letter to the Queen** in his coat pocket — Edric promised the lamp for "a throne over all the Rings", using Tobin's own loving letter as a map), **Mira** (about 12; healed by the Ferryman — she now SEES, and still echo-clicks; colours are brand new to her; she asks Wren the name of every colour; she finds bright light "loud"), **Old Ash** (very lame: his leg was greyed by a Hollow in Book 2 and restored, but it is weak; he walks slowly on **two poles**; he recognised the Ferryman and smiled and said nothing) and **Cinder** (her tail-spark went small and dim when she looked at Vaelport) stand at the open crystal gates of Vaelport.
- They have learned the sea, and the colour "sea-green". Dunstan, the freed courier, rode off to warn the Ring-towns about the Queen's letters.
- Wren now knows what a real gift feels like — and Vaelport's "free light" does not feel like one.
- Tobin is raw: his father has *chosen* the Queen's side. He secretly hopes Vaelport's light could make his father not need a throne.

## 2. New cast (FIXED names)
**Oskar Venn** — the merchant-prince of Vaelport, Master of the Glowstone Company. Fifty-ish, handsome, silver-white hair, beautifully dressed in white and pale grey, rings on every finger set with tiny glowstones. Charming, generous, warm-*voiced* — and he truly believes he is saving the world: "No more Rings. No more oil. No more Guilds charging the poor for a candle. Light for everyone, for ever, free." He is deceived, not cartoonish. He calls the children "my dears" and Ash "old friend". He never raises his voice. His one tell: his hands are always cold, and he never sits near a fire. Lives in the **Glass Palace**.
**Pim** (about 11) — sharp, small, quick leader of the harbour street-children; wears a man's coat with the sleeves rolled up eight times; has a glowstone of her own on a string (everyone in Vaelport has one). Talks fast, bargains hard, trusts no nob — Wren in a mirror. But she never laughs, and she says "the young ones have gone quiet" without understanding what it means. When Wren gives her a little of the lamp's warmth (Ch 3) she laughs — and is frightened of it. She is hollowed in Ch 9 (her glowstone finishes its work); restored by the lamp's light in Ch 14–15.
**The Pellows** — **Mr Amos Pellow**, a mender of fishing-nets, big and slow and gentle, and **Mrs Rose Pellow**, small, round, quick, kind, who bakes. They lost a daughter years ago and their house is too quiet. They are **genuinely good** and genuinely want Wren; they do not know Venn arranged the meeting. Their cottage on Lantern Steps has a blue door (in the lamp's light) and a real fire, which Mrs Pellow keeps lit out of old habit though "nobody needs a fire in Vaelport". The one condition (Venn's, passed on innocently): stay in Vaelport — "you can't go wandering off to mountains, love, not if you're ours."
**Captain Varrick** — reappears from Ch 7 standing half in shadow behind Venn. Smooth, polite, "voice like oiled iron", burn scar across his right palm which he rubs. He is cruel in Book 3: he lies to Tobin with a true thing. Still unnamed (never invent his real name). In Ch 12 Ash's appeal makes him hesitate — one long heartbeat — before he chooses wrongly (seed for Book 5).
**Lord Edric Harrowgate** — never appears. Varrick's proof (Ch 8): Edric's signet ring, and a page in Edric's own hand, begging, written from a cell. TRUE: the Queen has imprisoned him (he failed to deliver the lamp). Varrick's promise that Edric will be freed is the LIE.
**The Beggar** — Ch 15 only. A ragged man on the docks, hood up, a scar across his hand; knows Tobin's name and what he did; says only "Go to the Hill." (The Lampwright, unrecognised. Never say so.) A fisherman and a small child each say the same three words to Mira and to Wren.
**Hollowed miners** — grey, silent men and women working endlessly in the mines below the city.
**The Morvaine voice** — the Queen, as in Book 1: never seen, only a voice "like warm milk going cold", sweet and reasonable. She speaks once to each child in Ch 14 ("It's over now. You did your best. Lie down. It is easier in the grey.").

## 3. Vaelport & the glowstones
- **Vaelport**: a great harbour city on the south-west coast, climbing the hills in tiers of white towers, domes and walls, all lit day and night by **glowstones** — on every lamp-post, mast, doorway and windowsill. There is no Ring, no Lamp Guild, no oil, and (it seems) no Hollows. It is never dark, not even at midnight; people sleep with cloths over their eyes. Streets paved with glittering white stone. Busy, prosperous, full of refugees from failing Ring-towns. And everyone is a little **tired**: polite, quiet, pale, unhurried; nobody sings in the streets, children don't play chase, nobody laughs out loud. Under that light **every colour is bleached to white** (Wren's ribbon goes white; the lamp, inside her coat or out, brings colour back within its small circle).
- **Glowstones**: smooth white crystals from fingernail-size to the size of a man, giving a cold, steady, shadowless white light that never flickers, never leans, never warms, never goes out, needs no fuel. **The truth (discovered Ch 6)**: they are carved from the Queen's own darkness, pressed and hardened into crystal in the Great Forge under the city — each one holds a sleeping cold, *a Hollow folded small*. They do not attack. They drain whoever lives by them **slowly and kindly**, the way a Hollow does but over years: first laughter goes, then dreams, then names; at last the person goes quite grey — "goes quiet", Vaelport calls it, and thinks it is just old age or tiredness. That is why there are "no Hollows" in Vaelport: the whole city is being hollowed gently. **Vaelport is a Hollow city that happens to be brightly lit.**
- Glowstones and the lamp: side by side, the glowstone makes the lamp look small and dim and yellow ("like a candle at noon"). But the lamp's flame makes a glowstone go dull and grey in its circle, and a glowstone cracks if the lamp is held right up to it. The singing-stone stays silent by a glowstone (the cold is locked in) — but hums when the cold is let LOOSE (the mines, the forge, a stone cracking, Pim's hollowing, the end of Ch 12).
- **The pendants**: Venn's welcome gifts — a small glowstone on a fine white chain, worn at the throat. Ash quietly refuses his. Wren, Tobin and Mira accept (it would be rude not to; and they are beautiful). Each one numbs its wearer: Wren's makes her feel "a little emptier, the way you feel when you've forgotten something and can't think what" and makes the lamp feel heavy. Mira's makes the crowd's cheering feel like the only warm thing. Tobin's makes it easier not to feel anything about his father. They take them off in Ch 9 (Mira, Wren) and Ch 10–11 (Tobin — he tears it off after the betrayal).
- **The Glass Palace**: Venn's palace on the highest tier — crystal walls, mirrors, galleries, a banqueting hall where the ceiling is one vast glowstone. Light everywhere, warmth nowhere; your breath shows indoors. No shadows at all (which is creepy — note that the children cast none).
- **The harbour & docks**: fishing boats, nets, gulls, tar, the smell of salt and fish; the street-children's den in an upturned boat under the fish-market; low tide mud-flats. The real colour in the book lives here, in rare moments.
- **The lighthouse**: on a rock at the end of the long harbour wall; its great light is the largest glowstone in Vaelport; Varrick meets Tobin at the top (Ch 8) and takes the lamp there (Ch 10).
- **The glowstone mines & the Great Forge**: under the city, entered from the Company's works by the harbour. Tunnels lined with raw glowstone so bright that eyes water and go blind — everything white, no shadows, no depth. Grey hollowed miners work silently. Mira, who lived all her life in the dark, shuts her eyes and leads by echo-clicks (Ch 11): "I don't need it to be dark. I only need to not look." At the heart: **the Great Forge** — a vast round chamber where the Queen's darkness pours up through a black shaft like cold smoke and is pressed into crystal under huge pale hammers. A forge-door of iron and glass.
- **The Pellows' cottage**: Lantern Steps, a crooked stair-street near the harbour; small, warm, a real fire, bread, a cat (grey in the white light; ginger by the lamp), a little bedroom that was their daughter's, with a patchwork quilt.

## 4. The lamp in Book 3 (OUTLINE §3 rules hold — keep them exact)
- It is Wren's because it was given to her; it comes back to her only while she wants it.
- **Ch 5**: Wren, torn by the Pellows' offer, puts it away in a drawer in the Pellows' cottage — she doesn't throw it away or reject it, she just *puts it away* (it stays faintly warm in the drawer; she stops looking at it).
- **Ch 10**: Tobin takes it from the drawer to trade it for his father. Trading it is SELLING it: **it burns his hands** (Book 1 rule: it burns the hand of anyone who tries to buy or sell it). He wraps it in his coat and carries it anyway, the burns stinging — that's how badly he wants his father back. In Varrick's hands (gloved; grey gauntlet) it goes **dark and cold** — not out, just shut, a dull brass thing. Varrick laughs: Edric will not be freed.
- It does not "come back" to Wren on its own in Ch 10–11 because she had put it away and stopped wanting it; in Ch 9 she *does* want it again, takes it out — and it's gone. The children must go and get it (Ch 11).
- **Ch 12**: In the Forge, Ash reaches the lamp. He does not grab it to keep. He picks it up to *give it back to Wren* — and so it **blazes** in his hands (the same rule as Tobin carrying it back in Book 1 Ch 12). He hands it to Wren: **"It was never mine. It's never been anyone's. Give it."** Those are his last words.
- **Ch 13–14**: After Ash's death the lamp barely glows — a small, low, steady flame, like a coal under ash. It does not go out. (Grief, not rejection.) When Mira and the children hold it out to the recovering people in Ch 14, it grows a little.
- Glowstones near the lamp: see §3.

## 5. Old Ash's death (the founder has approved this — handle it with great care)
- It is the emotional centre of the series. Write it the way a wise children's book writes a death: **clear that it happened, but not gruesome, and not lingered on.** No blood, no broken body, nothing described of the death itself. The door, the darkness, the falling tunnel, and silence.
- Beat: the mine is collapsing as the Queen's darkness breaks loose from the cracked forge. Ash, lame, cannot run. He sets his back against the forge-door to hold the dark in so the children can get out. He hands Wren the lamp — "It was never mine. It's never been anyone's. Give it." — and says to Cinder "Go with them, girl. Go on." Then, to the children, the old gruff kindness: "Off you go. Mind the step." (Or similar — one small, very Ash, ordinary line.) Wren screams his name. The tunnel falls between them. Dust. Silence. Cinder howls, once.
- **Seeds of hope (light touch, NEVER explained)**: Cinder's tail-spark — Cinder was Ash's — dims almost to nothing but **does not go out**. Wren notices this in Ch 13 and doesn't understand why it matters. In Ch 15 it is very slightly brighter. (Book 4 will reveal Ash is "not lost but waiting".) Also: Ash's dreadful tea, his "Hm", his poles — let the children remember small funny things about him in Ch 13–14; grief in children is often like that.
- Nobody in the book says "he's in a better place" or anything religious. Wren simply cannot believe it. Mira cries properly. Tobin can't cry at all.

## 6. Lampwright appearances (light touch, no naming)
- Only one: **Ch 15**, the ragged beggar on the docks (hood, kind tired eyes, a scar across the back of his hand) who sits beside Tobin, knows his name and what he did, and says only "Go to the Hill." And the same three words come to Mira (from an old fisherman mending nets) and to Wren (from a very small child who then runs off). The reader should suspect; no character says so. Each child, alone, turns inland toward **Cinder Hill** (in Book 1's lore, where the Lampwright was snuffed out). The book ends there.
- Optional, very faint: in Ch 12, as the tunnel falls, Wren is sure for a second that Ash was not alone behind the door — "as if someone had come to stand beside him." Only if it can be done in one sentence, without explanation.

## 7. Voices (Book 3)
- **Wren**: still quick, cheeky, Soot Lane; but she now knows what real gifts feel like and trusts that instinct. In Ch 5 she is torn harder than ever — let us feel how much she wants a mum and a door of her own. In Ch 13 she says unforgivable things to Tobin ("You sold him. You sold Ash for a *letter*." / "I wish you'd stayed in your Hall.") — sharp, cruel, true-feeling, but no profanity ("rot", "blast" only).
- **Tobin**: precise, polite, "Actually—", "Technically speaking", pushes his spectacles up. In Vaelport he is dazzled by the engineering, then (Ch 6) the first to grasp the horror ("It's a Hollow. The whole city. It's a Hollow that's been switched on."). His fall must be *understandable*: love for his father, guilt, the pendant numbing him, Varrick's proof being true. He tells no one. After the theft he is broken — a Peter, not a Judas: he weeps bitterly (Ch 10's end), and in Ch 13 doesn't defend himself against Wren.
- **Mira**: precise, sensory, mischievous, delighted by every colour; formal Delver phrasing ("Your voice is bright today"). In Vaelport the light is "loud"; she is dazzled, then flattered by being "the Girl Who Sees". She is the FIRST to repent (Ch 9): she sees the tired children in the front row of her audience and steps off the stage. She apologises to Wren simply and fully. Her darkness-sense saves them in the mines (Ch 11).
- **Old Ash**: slow, dry, "child", "lad", "Hm." In Vaelport he is the only one never fooled; he refuses the pendant with perfect courtesy. In the market (Ch 7) he speaks plainly and is laughed at. In Ch 12 he calls Varrick "lad" and begs him to remember the oath-flame and the boy he was; he is tender, not angry.
- **Cinder**: as ever; sneezes sparks at glowstones; steals a biscuit from Venn's banquet; frees Ash from his cell in Ch 11 (she brings him the key, or chews the rope of the key-hook, or squeezes through the bars with the jailer's key-ring — choose something foxy and a little funny); in Ch 13–14 runs between the three children refusing to let them stay apart.
- **Venn**: warm, fluent, reasonable, never threatening; he believes his own speeches. **Pim**: fast, flat, tough, unsmiling. **The Pellows**: plain, kind, homely ("love", "pet", "there now").

## 8. Chapter beat sheet (~3,000–3,300 words each)

### 1. The City That Never Dims
- Through the crystal gates into Vaelport: white streets, glowstones everywhere, no shadows, no night, crowds of refugees. Nobody asks for a toll — "the light is free to all who come in".
- Mira is overwhelmed with wonder (and the "loudness"); she asks the colour of everything and Wren keeps having to say "white". Tobin is awed by the engineering (no flame-pipes, no oil, the glowstones just *shine*). Wren notices the people look tired and grey under the brightness; nobody laughs; children walk, they don't run.
- They find lodging at a harbour inn (the Salt Lantern) whose landlady is kind but strangely flat. At night it does not get dark; they can't sleep.
- Wren takes the lamp out in their room, under a blanket: beside the glowstone at the window it looks small and yellow and dim, "like a candle at noon". She is ashamed of it for a moment — then sees her ribbon go red under the blanket, and colour come back to Mira's face. Ash watches, and says "Hm."
- Hook: a white carriage stops outside in the morning. A footman with an invitation: "Master Oskar Venn has heard that travellers have come from the north with an old lamp. He would be honoured if you would dine with him."

### 2. Oskar Venn's Welcome
- The Glass Palace: crystal, mirrors, galleries; no shadows; your breath shows indoors. Venn is charming, generous, genuinely kind-seeming; he knows about the Rings failing and speaks movingly of the poor paying for oil and Flame tokens (he has heard of Emberwick's Guild — and of Prowle).
- At the banquet (vast glowstone ceiling, white food that tastes of very little; Cinder steals a biscuit and sneezes sparks at the ceiling), Venn speaks of a world that will never again need any Flame: "No more lamps to be kept. No more lamplighters to grow old." He looks kindly at Ash's lamp-pole.
- He gives each traveller a glowstone pendant. Ash refuses with perfect courtesy ("I'm an old man, sir. I've a lamp of my own, and I'm used to it."). The children accept.
- Venn asks, lightly, to see the famous lamp. Wren, on instinct, says it's back at the inn. (It's inside her coat.) Venn smiles.
- That night Wren notices her pendant is cold against her throat, and that wearing it she feels "a little emptier". She takes the lamp out — and for the first time it feels heavy.

### 3. The Children Who Don't Laugh
- Wren slips off to the harbour (where a sweep feels at home) and meets the street-children under the fish-market, led by Pim, who tries to pick her pocket and is outmatched. Grudging respect. Wren the old Soot Lane hand.
- She realises none of them laughs, plays, or dreams; the littlest ones sit and stare at their glowstones. Pim: "Everyone goes quiet in the end. Grown-ups say it's growing up."
- Under the upturned boat, in secret, Wren lets a little of the lamp's flame out for them — just a glow in her cupped hands. Warmth; colour (Pim's coat is *green*!); a small child giggles; Pim laughs out loud — then is frightened by her own laughter and shoves Wren out: "Put it away! What *is* that? Don't you bring that here again."
- Walking back, Wren holds her pendant up beside the lamp: in the lamp's circle the glowstone goes grey and dull, and inside it, for a heartbeat, she sees something move — like smoke folded small. The singing-stone is silent. Wren begins to suspect what glowstones really are. She tells Ash. Ash says, "Keep your eyes open, child. And keep that lamp close." She doesn't tell the others — yet.

### 4. The Girl Who Sees
- Venn hears of Mira's healing (from the inn; Mira has been telling everyone). He invites her to stand on the great stage of the Crystal Theatre as "the Girl Who Sees — the miracle of Vaelport's light".
- Crowds cheer; gifts pour in (white dresses, sweets, a glowstone circlet). Mira, who all her life was "the blind girl" and a burden, is called *special* for the first time. She loves it.
- On the second night, with Venn's hand on her shoulder, she starts to say it was Vaelport's light that healed her — not the Ferryman. "It might have been. How do *you* know it wasn't?"
- Wren is hurt and angry: "You were in the boat. I was there. He put his thumbs on your eyes." Mira gets defensive: "You just don't like anyone else having anything." A real rift. Tobin tries to explain both sides and makes it worse. Ash says nothing, but looks old.
- Hook: Mira moves into the Palace's guest wing "for the performances".

### 5. A House for Wren
- Venn "happens" to introduce Wren to the Pellows (Mrs Pellow saw her at the market and asked about the little sweep with the ribbon). They invite her to tea. The cottage on Lantern Steps: a real fire, fresh bread, a cat, a little room with a patchwork quilt that was their daughter's.
- They are truly, wholly kind. Wren, who has never been anyone's, is offered a home, a family, a mum. Write this with real longing — let the reader want it for her.
- Mrs Pellow passes on the one condition innocently: "Mr Venn says the adoption papers can be drawn up this week, pet. Only you'd have to stop here, in Vaelport. No more wandering off to mountains. Not if you're ours."
- Wren is torn harder than she has ever been. She doesn't say yes or no. She stays the night; and before bed she takes the lamp out of her coat and puts it in the drawer of the little bedside table, and shuts the drawer. It glows faintly through the crack. She turns over so she can't see it.

### 6. The Mines Beneath the Harbour  (Tobin POV)
- Tobin (curious about how glowstones are made — "Things don't just *shine*. Something has to be paying for it.") and Ash, slow on his two poles, with Cinder, slip into the Glowstone Company's works by the harbour at shift-change and down into the mines.
- Blinding white tunnels; Tobin wraps his spectacles in a scrap of smoked glass from the works. Grey hollowed miners work silently and don't look up. They find the black shaft where cold dark pours up like smoke and is pressed into crystal under great pale hammers — the Great Forge (seen from a gallery; they don't go in).
- Ash knows the cold at once: "That's her. That's the Queen's own dark." Tobin works it out aloud: each glowstone is a Hollow folded small; it drains its owner gently, over years. "It's a Hollow. The whole city. It's a Hollow that's been *switched on*." He rips off his pendant — then, slowly, puts it back on, because he can't stop thinking about what Venn could do for his father (plant it; don't explain).
- They escape, barely, as an overseer spots them.

### 7. Ash's Warning
- Ash will not keep quiet. He climbs onto the edge of the fountain in the great market square and speaks to the crowd, plainly: "This light shines, but it does not warm. Hold your stone up to a candle, and look inside it." Some do and see nothing (they won't look); the crowd laughs at the lame old madman with the poles. A boy throws a fish-head.
- Venn arrives, smooth and smiling and sorrowful: the poor old fellow is confused. He has Ash arrested for "disturbing the peace". Ash goes quietly, with dignity; he says to Wren, low, "Keep it close, child. Whatever happens."
- Behind Venn, half in shadow at the edge of the square, stands **Captain Varrick**, rubbing his palm. Wren sees him. The truth lands: Venn is the Queen's tool (and doesn't know it); the glowstones are her plan to hollow the world *kindly* — not by war but by comfort.
- Wren runs to the Pellows' house — the lamp is still in the drawer. She looks at it, and doesn't take it out. (Show her not choosing.)

### 8. The Offer  (Tobin POV)
- A note in Tobin's coat pocket, in a hand he doesn't know: *The lighthouse. Midnight. Come alone, if you want to see your father again.*
- At the top of the lighthouse, under the great glowstone, Varrick is courteous. He shows Tobin proof that Lord Edric is now the Queen's prisoner: his father's signet ring, and a page in his father's own handwriting, shaky, written from a cell — "Tobin, if you can hear of this, I am sorry. I was wrong." It is TRUE. Varrick's offer: bring the lamp here, and your father goes free. "She keeps her bargains, boy. It's the only thing she does keep."
- Tobin argues with himself all night on the harbour wall — it feels like love, duty and justice at once; he can't think straight (the pendant at his throat). He thinks of Ash in the cell; of Wren's lamp shut in a drawer she doesn't open any more; of his father's hand shaking.
- He tells no one.

### 9. What Each One Wanted
- The three temptations come to a head on the same evening.
- **Mira**, on stage at the Crystal Theatre, sees the front row: tired children with their stones, and Pim among them, gone quite grey — staring, not knowing her own name. Mira stops mid-speech, takes off her circlet and pendant, says to the whole theatre "It wasn't your light. It was a man in a boat, and he didn't ask for anything," and walks off the stage. She finds Wren and apologises simply and fully. Wren, astonished, forgives her at once.
- **Wren**, at the Pellows' kitchen table, pen in hand over the adoption papers (Mrs Pellow crying happily at the stove) — when Mira brings Pim to the door, hollowed and grey. Wren sees what the city does. She takes off her pendant. She goes to the drawer to fetch the lamp, and *wants it again* — and the drawer is empty. (Tobin had visited earlier that day, "to see how you're getting on".) She tells the Pellows, through tears, that she can't stay; they are hurt and kind and don't understand.
- She runs to find Tobin. He isn't at the inn. His bed hasn't been slept in. The compass is lying on his pillow.
- End: the singing-stone in Wren's pocket begins, very softly, to hum.

### 10. The Night of the Theft  (Tobin POV, beginning earlier that day)
- Go back a few hours: Tobin visits the Pellows' cottage, waits till Wren is out, takes the lamp from the drawer. It **burns his hands** — he is trading it — and he wraps it in his coat and carries it anyway, along the harbour wall to the lighthouse, crying with the pain and telling himself it's for his father. He leaves his mother's compass on his pillow because he can't bear it watching him (it would point the other way).
- At the top of the lighthouse he gives the lamp to Varrick. In Varrick's gauntlet it goes dark and cold — a dull brass thing. Varrick laughs — not loudly. "Freed? Your father? Boy, your father bargained with her and lost. People who bargain with her always lose. You should know that; you just did." Lord Edric will not be freed.
- Tobin realises he's been tricked and has betrayed his friends — and Ash, in a cell — for nothing. He tries to grab the lamp back and is thrown aside against the rail.
- Varrick carries the lamp down into the Glowstone mines, to the Great Forge, "to be pressed into the Queen's greatest stone. A glowstone made from the last true Flame. Think how it will shine."
- Tobin, alone on the lighthouse floor with burned hands, tears off the pendant and throws it into the sea, and weeps bitterly (Peter's weeping — this is the moment).

### 11. Into the Glowstone Mines
- In the town gaol, **Cinder** frees Ash (something foxy and a little funny with the jailer's keys). Ash finds Wren and Mira at the harbour. Tobin is nowhere. Ash guesses at once what has happened, and his face changes — not anger: grief.
- Ash goes down into the mines alone, slow on his poles, telling the girls to stay. They follow anyway, guided by the singing-stone, which hums louder the nearer the cold.
- The mines are full of hollowed miners and cold, beautiful, blinding light. Wren can't see — everything is white, no edges, no depth, she walks into walls. **Mira** shuts her eyes and leads by echo-clicks: "I don't need it to be dark, Wren. I only need to not look." She leads Wren by the hand through tunnels where the glowstones would have blinded them (Mira's gift from the Deeps saves them; she is the guide again).
- They come out on the gallery above the Great Forge. Below: the black shaft pouring cold, the pale hammers, Varrick setting the dark lamp on the great anvil, and Ash, alone, lame, hobbling out across the forge floor towards him.

### 12. Old Ash's Last Lamp
- Ash confronts Varrick, his former student. He calls him "lad". He begs him — tenderly, not angrily — to remember the oath-flame, the scar on his palm, the boy he was "on the wall at Emberwick, the night you lit your first Ring-lamp and laughed out loud". Varrick hesitates — a long heartbeat; his hand goes to his palm — then chooses wrongly and attacks.
- In the struggle Ash reaches the anvil and takes up the lamp — not to keep, but to give it back to Wren — and it **blazes** in his hands. The pale hammers stop. The black shaft shrieks. Glowstones all over the forge crack; the Queen's darkness breaks loose; the mine begins to collapse. Varrick flees into the dark (alive).
- Ash, who cannot run, gets the children to the forge-door. He hands Wren the lamp: **"It was never mine. It's never been anyone's. Give it."** "Go with them, girl," to Cinder. And one small ordinary Ash line (e.g. "Off you go. Mind the step."). He sets his back to the forge-door to hold the dark in.
- Wren screams his name. The tunnel falls between them. Dust. Silence. Cinder howls once. (See §5 — clear, not gruesome, not lingered on.)

### 13. Broken
- Wren, Mira and Cinder struggle up out of the collapsing mines (Mira leading again, eyes shut). Above, all over Vaelport, the glowstones crack and go dark at once. For the first time in a hundred years it is night in Vaelport: real dark, starless, cold. The city panics: people screaming, running, lighting nothing because nobody has a candle any more.
- On the harbour wall in the dark they find Tobin, with burned hands, waiting. Wren is shattered by grief, then furious. She says unforgivable things ("You sold him. You sold Ash for a *letter*. I wish you'd never come out of your Hall. I wish it had been you."). He doesn't defend himself; he just says, "I know." Mira tries to stand between them and can't.
- The lamp in Wren's hand barely glows — a small low flame like a coal under ash. Cinder's spark is almost out — but not out.
- The company breaks apart: Wren walks off into the dark alone. Tobin stays on the wall. Mira, not knowing whom to follow, goes to help a crying child.

### 14. The Docks at Low Tide
- Each child is alone, in the first grey days of a Vaelport with no false light (the pale cursed sun rises as usual; at night it's dark; people huddle round fires they've forgotten how to make).
- **Wren** sits in the empty upturned boat, holding the lamp, barely glowing, wondering whether light was ever real, or whether Ash was just a mad old man with dreadful tea. She remembers small funny things about him and cries for the first time.
- **Mira** helps the frightened people of Vaelport; she teaches them to find their way in the dark (as the Delvers do) and to light fires; she finds the tired children — Pim among them — beginning, slowly, without the glowstones, to recover; a little girl laughs. (Mrs Pellow helps her; give the Pellows a kind ending.)
- **Tobin** sits on the docks, sure he is beyond forgiveness, wishing he'd never left Emberwick; he doesn't eat; he looks at his burned hands.
- **The Queen's voice** whispers to each of them separately, sweet and reasonable: the story is over; you did your best; lie down; it is easier in the grey.
- **Cinder** runs between the three of them, day and night, nosing them, bringing Wren's bread to Tobin and Tobin's scarf to Wren, refusing to let them stay apart.

### 15. Go to the Hill
- Dawn, low tide, mud-flats, gulls. A ragged beggar sits down beside Tobin on the dock, hood up. He knows Tobin's name, and what he did. He doesn't accuse him. He says only, "Go to the Hill." Then he walks into the crowd and is gone. (A scar across the back of his hand; Tobin half-notices.)
- Separately, the same morning, Mira hears the same three words from an old fisherman mending nets, and Wren hears them from a very small child who then runs off giggling.
- Each of them, alone, not knowing about the others, turns inland — east, toward the hills and Cinder Hill (Tobin's compass, which he finds Wren left on the dock for him, swings and points there — not to the Mountain, to the Hill — and he follows it).
- Cinder sits on the harbour wall looking after all three roads; then she trots after them; her tail-spark, the reader may notice, is a little brighter. Close on a quiet, aching, hopeful last line that makes a child want Book 4.
