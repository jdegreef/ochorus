# THE HILL OF CINDERS — Book 4 bible & beat sheet
Book 4 of *The Lamplighters of Gloamhaven*. 15 chapters, ~45,000–50,000 words. Ages 9–12.

**Read `OUTLINE.md` first** (series canon: voice §1, world §2, the lamp's rules §3, cast §4, the lore of the Lampwright §6 — and read Book 1's chapter "## The Tale of the Lampwright" in `fire-against-the-dark.md`, where Ash tells it with shadow-puppets and a tea-cosy: Book 4 must honour every word of that telling). Then `BOOK2.md` and `BOOK3.md`. Then read Book 3's last two chapters (`the-glowstone-bargain.md`, "## The Docks at Low Tide" and "## Go to the Hill") so you know exactly where everyone stands.

Theme (never quoted, with ONE approved exception below): Isaiah 53 (he carried our grief; by his wounds we are healed); John 20–21 (the empty tomb; breakfast on the shore; "Do you love me?"). Core truths: **the one who was snuffed out is alive**; **he chose to carry the curse himself**; **love lays down its life — Tobin's offer echoes his Master's**; **forgiveness received becomes forgiveness given**; **restoration, not just pardon: the failed one is given his work back**. This is the turning of the whole series: it begins in the deepest grey and ends with the sun "a little warmer". It must earn its joy.

## 1. Where Book 3 left them
- Dawn after the fall of Vaelport's false light. Old Ash is dead (Ch 12 of Book 3: he held the forge-door; the tunnel fell; no body was found; his last words "It was never mine. It's never been anyone's. Give it."). His two poles were left at the forge-door under the rock.
- Each child heard "Go to the Hill" (Tobin from a ragged hooded beggar with a scar across the back of his hand, who knew his name; Mira from an old fisherman whose voice reminded her of mist on a river; Wren from a muddy child of about three who giggled and ran). Each, alone, not knowing about the others, walks east into the hills.
- **Wren**: cropped black hair (Mrs Pellow trimmed it); red ribbon at her LEFT wrist; Tobin's long knitted scarf (smells of ink and machine-oil) round her neck though she hasn't forgiven him; the lamp inside her coat, burning small and low "like a coal under ash" since the mine — but at the end of Book 3 it began to LEAN east. She said unforgivable things to Tobin ("You sold him. You sold Ash for a letter… I wish it had been you.").
- **Tobin**: no coat (left on the lighthouse floor); burned palms healed into a round lamp-base-shaped scar on each; one spectacle lens cracked across; he twice denied knowing Ash and traded the lamp to Varrick for his father's freedom — and was tricked. He has his mother's brass compass again (Wren laid it beside him while he slept); it swung from the Mountain to due east. He believes he is beyond forgiveness.
- **Mira**: about 12; healed of blindness by the Ferryman in Book 2 — she sees, and still echo-clicks; beaded braids; carries a currant loaf from Mrs Pellow; she was the first to repent in Vaelport and is at peace, but grieving Ash; she helped the recovering people of Vaelport. Pim stayed with the Pellows.
- **Cinder**: Ash's ember-fox; her tail-spark nearly went out when Ash died — it never did; it was a little brighter at the end of Book 3. She follows all three roads.
- **The singing-stone** (hums when Hollow-cold is loose) — Wren still has it.
- **Varrick** fled alive from the Forge; at Ash's appeal he hesitated one long heartbeat. Still unnamed. Lord Edric is the Queen's prisoner.
- Wren's lullaby: she knows "Morning comes, morning comes … and the dark shall not keep us" — no more.

## 2. New cast (FIXED names)
**The Heskeths** — a poor farm family on the cursed Scarecrow Farms: **Will Hesketh** (lean, silent, kind), **Mag Hesketh** (sharp-tongued, generous), and children; the youngest, **Posy** (about 5), is sick with **grey-fever** (a wasting, greying sickness of the cursed lands). They take Wren in for a night with almost nothing to share. Wren gives Posy the lamp's light; Posy is healed, and the lamp brightens for the first time since Ash died (Ch 2).
**Sister Hale** — leader of **the Remnant**: old, small, brown and wrinkled as a walnut, blind in one eye (a milky eye; the other bright and black), walks with a crutch, laughs a great deal, full of joy; sings in a cracked strong voice; calls everyone "duck". She has kept the old Lampwright stories and songs alive in secret all her life. Becomes a mentor in Ash's absence (never a replacement — she knew of him: "Old Ash of Emberwick? The one who wouldn't charge? Oh, duck."). Wounded (not killed) in Ch 10.
**The Remnant** — a hidden fellowship of perhaps a few hundred scattered across the land: farmers, a cobbler, a washerwoman, a retired Guild clerk, children; they meet in barns, cellars and old roofless chapels, sing quietly, and guide refugees; the Guild calls them cranks and harries them. They know the **Lamplighters' Psalm** (Book 2) — and more verses of it — and old songs that tell the same tale as the Delvers' Story-Wall (the Warm Man), which stuns Mira.
**Morvaine, the Umbral Queen** — appears IN PERSON for the first time (Ch 11): very tall, beautiful, silver-grey from head to foot — hair, skin, gown — like a statue of frost given life; gentle, sorrowful, reasonable voice ("like warm milk going cold"); never shouts; her eyes are the only wholly dark thing about her. She never lies outright when a half-truth will do. Her weapon is the offer of what you love most at the cost of the light.
**Captain Varrick** — leads the trap (Ch 10). At the Lampwright's coming (Ch 13) he stands longest, staring — at the Lampwright's scarred hands, then at his own scarred palm — then drops his sword and runs. Still unnamed (Book 5 reveals him as Aren).
**The Lampwright** — appears IN PERSON from Ch 13. See §5.
**Ash-crows** — grey crows that spy for the Queen; they follow Wren from the Burned Country.

## 3. Places & things
- **The Scarecrow Farms**: cursed farmland east of the coast hills; crops dead or sour and stunted; in the fields stand **"scarecrows"** that are hollowed farmers, grey and still, who stood in the fields one evening and never came in (frightening, sad, never gory).
- **The Remnant's hiding places**: a hay-barn with a hidden loft; a cellar under a mill; a roofless old chapel on a hill where swallows nest (call it "the old meeting-house" — never "church").
- **The Burned Country**: plains of ancient ash where nothing grows, soft grey dust underfoot, warm in places. **Memorial stones** — old Lamplighter waystones, worn smooth — mark the Lampwright's last road; each has a small carved flame, some chipped away by the Guild. Varrick's Hollow Knights patrol.
- **Cinder Hill**: black, bare, round, rising out of the Burned Country; the ground is warm underfoot ("They say the ground there is still warm" — Book 1). At the top: **the Lampwright's Workshop**, a long low ruined stone building: cold forges, tools hung neatly on the walls as if waiting for a hand, a workbench with a half-finished lamp on it, and racks of **a hundred unfinished lamps** (brass bodies, no chimneys, no flame). At the back, **an iron door** with no handle, set with a round brass lock-plate engraved with a compass rose — a hollow exactly the size and shape of Tobin's mother's compass.
- **The Chronicle**: a great book bound in worn leather behind the iron door. The first part is written in a clear, strong, unhurried hand (the Lampwright's own); the last part in another hand, small and cramped and shaking at first, then steadier — **the hand of one of the friends who ran, and came back** (unnamed). It tells: the Making of the world and the golden sun, and the Maker's joy in it ("the Maker" — say it rarely, plainly, never explain); Ardric's bargain and why no lamp, crown, treaty or Guild can break the curse (the fire must be given back, and the curse must be carried by someone who owes it nothing); the Lampwright's walking; and **the last night on the Hill** (Ch 9). Its last written line: **"On the third morning, the fire woke."** Then blank pages. (Seed: blank pages = a story still being written; nobody says so.)
- **Ash's pole**: one of Ash's two long brass-hooked lamplighter's poles, which were buried under the rock at the forge-door in Vaelport — Tobin finds it **lying across the road** in the Greywaste-edge, dry and whole, as if someone had set it there for him (Ch 4). Never explained. He walks with it from then on. (Correct earlier plans that call it a "staff": it is one of Ash's POLES.)
- **The compass**: Tobin's mother's brass compass, "Home is where the light is" inside the lid; Wren stole it in Book 1 and gave it back; Ash borrowed it overnight at the end of Book 1 and returned it "tuned" ("A compass ought to know the way home"); in Book 3 Wren carried it and laid it beside Tobin. It opens the iron door (Ch 8). The children realise: Ash *made it a key* that night — he knew they'd come here. (Ash's care reaching past his death.)
- **The dark mirror** (Ch 11): an oval of black glass in the Queen's grey tent, which shows what you most love.
- **The Queen's camp**: grey tents in the ash below the Hill; Hollow Knights; braziers burning with no heat; ash-crows on the tent-poles.

## 4. The lamp in Book 4 (OUTLINE §3 rules hold — keep them exact)
- Since Ash's death it burns low and steady (grief, not rejection). It **leans** toward where it wants to go (east, to the Hill).
- **Ch 2**: given to Posy, it brightens for the first time — "Give it," Wren remembers.
- It burns brighter each time it is given; it is never used as a weapon.
- **Ch 10**: Wren is captured; the Hollow Knights cannot touch the lamp (it burns them) — Varrick, with his grey gauntlet, takes Wren and lets her keep it: the Queen wants Wren to put it out HERSELF, with her own breath. (A lamp given can only be put out by being given up.)
- **Ch 11**: the dark mirror; Wren holds the lamp to her lips and breathes in — and the flame bows toward her breath, and does not go out, because she has not yet breathed out. Leave her there (cliffhanger).
- **Ch 12**: Tobin's coming breaks the moment; Wren does not blow it out.
- **Ch 13**: at the Lampwright's coming, the lamp blazes as it never has; it leans toward him like a flower to the sun; colour floods back over the whole hilltop.
- **Ch 15**: The Lampwright does NOT take the lamp back. It is still Wren's, given. (In Book 5 the Flame will rest on each of them.)

## 5. The Lampwright in person (the heart of the book — handle with great restraint)
- He has been glimpsed in every book: the old man with the bread basket (Book 1, Crookback Market: "No charge. It's given."), the voice in the chimney ("It was given, child. Not lost."), the warm hand on Wren's shoulder at the Beacon; the Warm Man of the Delvers' Story-Wall; the Ferryman who healed Mira (Book 2); the beggar who said "Go to the Hill" (Book 3). In Ch 13–15 the children **recognise** him as all of these — let each child realise it separately and quietly (Wren: the bread-man's eyes; Mira: the Ferryman's voice, and the scar; Tobin: the beggar's hand).
- How he looks: an ordinary man, not tall or splendid; travel-stained; brown, weathered, laugh-lines; plain clothes; nothing glows about him except that colour comes back wherever he walks, and the ground warms. **Scars on both hands** — old, deep, round, like burns (the reader may notice they are like Tobin's palm-scars, made bigger; let Tobin notice it in Ch 14, when the Lampwright takes Tobin's scarred hands in his own).
- How he speaks: little. Plainly. Warmly; with humour (he teases Cinder; he burns the first piece of toast on purpose to make Wren laugh, or not on purpose — he laughs either way). He asks more than he tells. He never lectures, never explains the allegory, never says "I am…". He calls Wren "Wren", Mira "Mira", Tobin "Tobin" — by name, as no one has for a long time.
- What he does not do: no fireworks, no speeches, no smiting. In Ch 13 he simply walks up the Hill at dawn, carrying nothing; the Hollow Knights fall back from him as shadows from a lamp; the Queen's mirror cracks across of its own accord; the Queen looks at him once — and flees screaming into the dark (the only time she ever raises her voice). He kneels beside Tobin, who is nearly grey, and puts his scarred hand on Tobin's head, and Tobin's colour comes back.
- **The ONE approved Scripture echo**: Ch 14, by the breakfast fire, he asks Tobin three times, **"Tobin, do you love me?"** (exactly those words each time), and three times gives him a task after Tobin's answer (e.g. "Then keep my lamps." / "Then look after the small ones." / "Then follow me." — keep them short; the third answer from Tobin is the hurt one: "You know everything. You know I do."). Three questions for Tobin's three failures (twice denying Ash; once trading the lamp). Nobody comments on it.
- "Old Ash isn't lost. He's waiting." — say exactly this to Wren (Ch 14). When Wren asks about her parents: "Trust me with them." Nothing more.
- Wren's lullaby: in her dream in the Workshop (Ch 6), the man at the forge whose face she can't see is **humming her mother's tune, "Morning comes"** — further than she knows it, and she wakes before she can catch the rest. In Ch 14 or 15 he hums it once again, very softly, while he makes the toast; she hears; he does not finish it; she does not ask. (The song will be finished in Book 6. Do NOT write any new words of it.)

## 6. Voices (Book 4)
- **Wren**: Soot Lane; sharp, grieving, angry; she talks to the lamp as if it were Ash ("You'd have said 'Hm', wouldn't you."); she refuses to look at or speak to Tobin in Ch 7–9 — but she is still wearing his scarf and won't take it off. In Ch 12 when Tobin is being drained she screams at him to stop being an idiot, and realises she is crying for him. In Ch 14 she asks HIS forgiveness, which costs her everything; they make up properly.
- **Tobin**: starts broken — few words; no "Actually"; he can't make himself explain anything any more. As he heals, the precision comes back. At the Chronicle (Ch 9) he must stop reading at "betrayed by the friend he loved best". His offer in Ch 12: walks into the Queen's camp unarmed, shaking, holding Ash's pole: "Take me. I'm the traitor. She's done nothing." Choosing love over fear at last.
- **Mira**: precise, sensory, mischievous, deep. Finds in the Remnant's songs the Delvers' Warm Man and weeps for joy. The peacemaker between Wren and Tobin (fails in Ch 7, keeps trying). Escapes with the Chronicle (Ch 10) because Wren insists.
- **Sister Hale**: joyful, earthy, "duck", cracked singing voice; "It isn't where he died, duck. It's where the morning began." Fearless of the Guild. Hurt in Ch 10 (an arrow/knight's blow to the shoulder — survives).
- **Cinder**: keeps Tobin alive in the waste (Ch 4: curls on his chest and won't let him sleep); sits exactly between Wren and Tobin all night (Ch 7); is **the first to run to the Lampwright** in Ch 13, as if she has always known him — her spark blazes up like a torch. (Seed: Cinder's bond with him.)
- **The Queen**: soft, kind, terribly reasonable. To Wren: "Only breathe out, little sweep. That is all. You have carried it so far, and it has cost you so much. Let me give you something, for once, that costs you nothing."
- **The Lampwright**: see §5.

## 7. Chapter beat sheet (~3,000–3,400 words each)

### 1. Three Roads Inland
- Open on the three roads (each child alone; alternate briefly between them). Wren, barely glowing lamp inside her coat, walks east and talks to it as if it were Old Ash. Mira falls in with a ragged band of refugees from the broken glowstone city heading inland, carrying her loaf and helping the slow ones. Tobin walks with no coat, no food and no plan, sure he deserves whatever happens to him, the compass shut in his fist (he won't look at it).
- Cinder runs between the three roads, refusing to choose; each child sees her once and thinks she has come for them alone.
- End: the lamp in Wren's coat leans east, harder.

### 2. The Scarecrow Farms  (Wren)
- Cursed farmland; dead crops; in the fields, the grey still "scarecrows" that are hollowed farmers. Wren hurries past them at dusk.
- The Heskeths take her in for a night despite having almost nothing (thin soup; Mag's sharp tongue; Will's silence). Their youngest, Posy, is sick with grey-fever: going grey and quiet, like the hollowed but slower.
- In the night Wren takes out the lamp and gives its light to Posy — truly gives, asking nothing (she nearly doesn't; she is so tired of giving). Posy's colour comes back; she sits up and asks for bread. The lamp brightens — the first time since Ash died. Wren remembers: "Give it."
- In the morning, before she leaves, she lights the Heskeths' cold hearth from the lamp; the nearest "scarecrow" in the field has turned its head toward the house.

### 3. The Remnant  (Mira)
- Mira's refugee band is secretly guided at night by people with shuttered lanterns: the Remnant. They hide the band in a hay-barn loft; at midnight, under the hay, people sing very quietly.
- Sister Hale, old and half-blind and full of joy. The Remnant's songs and stories: the Lampwright walking the cities, lighting lamps for nothing; and one song about a Warm Man who touched the eyes of the people under the earth — Mira's own Story-Wall. Mira weeps for joy: the Delvers' story and theirs are the same story.
- They sing the Lamplighters' Psalm, with a verse Mira has never heard (invent ONE new short verse, in the same style; don't change the Book 2 words).
- Sister Hale on Cinder Hill: "It isn't where he died, duck. It's where the morning began." Guild hunters raid; the band scatters into the dark; Sister Hale takes Mira east with her.

### 4. Tobin's Long Walk  (Tobin)
- Tobin wanders the Greywaste's edge, starving. Hollows drift past him and ignore him: there is no hope left in him to drain (a frightening, quiet moment).
- The Queen's voice every night, kind and cruel: "You are just like your father." "They'll never forgive you. Why should they?"
- He finds one of **Ash's poles** lying across the road, dry and whole, brass hook gleaming — it was under the rock in Vaelport. He cannot explain it. He sits down beside it and cries.
- He nearly lies down in the grey grass to give up. Cinder finds him, curls up on his chest, and will not let him sleep — she sneezes sparks in his face, nips his ear, sits on him — until he gets up. He walks on, leaning on Ash's pole.

### 5. The Burned Country  (Wren)
- All three roads converge on the Burned Country: plains of ancient grey ash, warm in places; nothing grows. Old memorial stones with carved flames (some chipped by the Guild) mark the Lampwright's last road.
- Ash-crows; Varrick's Hollow Knights patrol the approaches: the Queen expects someone to come.
- Wren slips past the patrols alone, using everything she learned as a thief and a sweep (soot on her face; moving with the wind; the lamp hidden). A tense, clever, satisfying sequence.
- From a ridge at dusk she sees Cinder Hill: black, bare, round, topped with a ruined stone workshop. The lamp, inside her coat, is warm against her ribs as it has not been for weeks.

### 6. The Workshop on the Hill  (Wren)
- Wren reaches the workshop first. Cold forges; tools hung neatly as if waiting for a hand; a half-finished lamp on the bench; racks of a hundred unfinished lamps. It is quiet in a way that is not empty.
- An iron door at the back with no handle and a round brass lock-plate engraved with a compass rose; it will not open for her, by force (she hurts her shoulder) or by trick (she picks at it with a nail for an hour).
- She falls asleep in the workshop, curled by the cold forge, holding the lamp.
- She dreams of a man at the forge, humming as he works — humming her mother's tune, "Morning comes", and further than she knows it — whose face she can't quite see in the glow. She wakes before she can catch the rest. The forge is cold. But it is warmer in the room than it was.

### 7. Together, Almost
- Mira arrives with Sister Hale and a handful of the Remnant; the two girls reunite with tears and laughter (Mira: "Your voice is so much smaller than it was. What have you been doing to it?").
- Tobin arrives last, alone and filthy, leaning on Ash's pole. Wren sees the pole and goes white. She refuses to speak to him or even look at him — but she does not take off his scarf.
- Mira tries to make peace and fails. Sister Hale watches and says nothing (yet).
- Cinder sits exactly between Wren and Tobin all night, by the Remnant's small fire.

### 8. The Chronicle  (Tobin)
- Tobin, who cannot sleep, examines the iron door — and finds a round hollow in the lock-plate exactly the shape of his mother's compass. He fits the compass in; it clicks; the needle spins and points straight up; the door opens.
- Realisation (Tobin works it out, then Wren, unwillingly): Ash "tuned" the compass that night at the end of Book 1. He made it a key. He knew they would come here. Ash's care reaching past his death — Wren has to go outside.
- Inside, the Chronicle: the first part in a clear strong hand; it begins with the Making of the world, the golden sun, the Maker's joy. Then Ardric's bargain, told plainly, and why no lamp, crown, treaty or Guild can break the curse.
- Sister Hale, holding the book, weeps and laughs: "We only ever had the songs."

### 9. The Night He Chose
- The children read the Chronicle aloud by lamplight, in turns, with the Remnant listening: the Lampwright's last night on the Hill, in the second, smaller, shaking hand of one of the friends who ran.
- He knew the Queen was coming, and could have left. He was betrayed by the friend he loved best, for a promise of power. **Tobin has to stop reading.** Mira takes the book from him gently and goes on.
- He chose to carry the whole curse into himself — to let the darkness take him, "so that the fire could go through the dark and out the other side" — and the friends ran. (Honour Book 1's telling: the darkness came up like a tide; it was dark the first day, and the second.)
- The last written line: "On the third morning, the fire woke." Then the pages are blank. Silence round the fire. Wren looks at Tobin for the first time since Vaelport — and looks away. Ash-crows on the workshop roof.

### 10. The Trap
- Before dawn: Varrick's Hollow Knights surround the Hill. The Queen let the children gather here on purpose.
- The Remnant scatters down the far slope. Sister Hale is wounded (shoulder; she survives — "Go on, duck. I've had worse from a goose.").
- Wren insists that Mira escape down the hill with the Chronicle ("It's the only one. Go!"). Mira goes, crying.
- Wren is captured with the lamp. The Knights cannot touch it — it burns them; Varrick in his grey gauntlet takes her by the collar and lets her keep it: "Her Majesty wants you to put it out yourself."
- Tobin hides in the ash under the workshop wall and watches them drag her away. He does nothing. He hates himself. Cinder lies down beside him.

### 11. The Dark Mirror  (Wren)
- The Queen's camp in the ash below the Hill: grey tents, braziers with no heat, ash-crows. For the first time the Umbral Queen, Morvaine, appears in person: tall, beautiful, silver-grey, gentle-voiced, sorrowful, kind.
- She shows Wren a dark mirror: Wren's mother and father, alive, smiling, calling her name — her mother singing "Morning comes, morning comes…" (and stopping exactly where Wren's own memory stops — "and the dark shall not keep us" — and going back to the beginning; plant it, don't explain it).
- The offer: blow out the lamp with your own breath, and you will be with them for ever. "Let me give you something, for once, that costs you nothing."
- Wren, worn out with grief and guilt, holds the lamp to her lips and breathes in. She can no longer tell whether the mirror is a lie. End the chapter there.

### 12. The Coward's Offer  (Tobin, then Wren)
- Tobin walks into the Queen's camp, unarmed, shaking, holding Ash's pole, Cinder at his heel. The Knights let him through; the Queen is delighted.
- He offers himself in Wren's place: "Take me. I'm the traitor. She's done nothing." "One Harrowgate after another," says the Queen, charmed, and accepts.
- The Hollow Knights close round Tobin and begin to drain him; his colour fades, his hair, his hands, his voice; he does not run.
- Wren, released, the lamp still burning in her hands, screams at him to stop being an idiot, to run, to come back — and realises she is crying for him. She tries to reach him with the lamp and the Knights hold her off. Night falls. Tobin is nearly grey.

### 13. Scarred Hands
- Dawn. A stranger walks up Cinder Hill out of the Burned Country, carrying nothing, the pale sun behind him. Colour comes back to the ash where he walks.
- Cinder sees him first — and runs to him across the whole camp, her spark blazing like a torch, as if she has always known him.
- He is not a beggar, or a ferryman, or a voice in a chimney. It is the Lampwright himself, with scars on his hands. (Restraint: describe him by what happens round him, and by his eyes and hands.)
- The Hollow Knights fall back from him like shadows from a lamp. The Queen's mirror cracks across of itself. The Queen looks at him once, and flees into the dark screaming — the only time she has ever raised her voice.
- Varrick stands his ground longest, staring at the scarred hands, then at his own palm. Then he drops his sword and runs.
- The Lampwright kneels beside Tobin, lying grey in the ash, and puts his hand on his head; Tobin's colour comes back, slowly, from the hair down. Wren's lamp blazes as it never has.

### 14. Breakfast by the Fire
- The Lampwright builds a campfire on the Hill and makes breakfast: toasted bread and roasted apples (he burns the first piece of toast and laughs). Mira and the Remnant come back up the Hill (Mira with the Chronicle; Sister Hale with her arm in a sling, laughing and crying at once).
- Each child recognises him, quietly, separately: Wren the bread-man's eyes at Crookback Market ("No charge. It's given."); Mira the Ferryman's voice; Tobin the beggar's hand.
- He asks Tobin three times, "Tobin, do you love me?" — and three times gives him a task. Tobin is restored, forgiven, and given his place back. The Lampwright takes Tobin's scarred palms in his own scarred hands; Tobin sees the scars are the same shape, only bigger.
- Wren asks Tobin's forgiveness for what she said in Vaelport. It costs her everything. They make up properly (she gives him back his scarf; he wraps it round her neck again).
- The Lampwright tells Wren: "Old Ash isn't lost. He's waiting." When she asks about her parents: "Trust me with them." While he turns the apples, he hums her mother's tune, very softly, and does not finish it.

### 15. Carry My Flame
- The Lampwright commissions Wren, Tobin and Mira (and the Remnant): "Carry my Flame to every Ring, and past them."
- He promises a new gift at Midsummer: "You won't carry it alone." (Seed for Book 5.)
- He looks at the hundred unfinished lamps in the workshop and smiles, and says nothing (seed: people will become lamps).
- He walks down the far side of the Hill. When they run after him to the brow, he's gone. Cinder sits and looks after him, her spark bright and steady.
- They know now that he is alive, and that he always was. For the first time in three hundred years, the sun over Cinder Hill looks a little warmer. Close on a quiet, glad, forward-looking line.

## 8. Rules for this book
- All OUTLINE §1 voice rules hold. Still: never name God, Jesus, the Bible, church, heaven, sin or salvation; never explain the allegory; "the Maker" may appear (in the Chronicle and Sister Hale's mouth) rarely and plainly.
- The Lampwright is never called anything but "the Lampwright", "he", or (by the Remnant) "the Master" — sparingly.
- Peril: real but not gory. The Queen in person is frightening by being lovely and kind. Tobin's draining (Ch 12) is grey, cold and quiet, never painful in a graphic way. Sister Hale's wound is mentioned, not shown.
- Varrick stays unnamed. Don't resolve Lord Edric. Don't finish the lullaby. Don't reveal Wren's parents' fate.
