# THE MIDSUMMER FLAME — Book 5 bible & beat sheet
Book 5 of *The Lamplighters of Gloamhaven*. 15 chapters, ~45,000–48,000 words. Ages 9–12.

**Read `OUTLINE.md` first** (series canon: voice §1, world §2, the lamp's rules §3, cast §4, lore §6), then `BOOK2.md`, `BOOK3.md`, `BOOK4.md`. Then read Book 4's last chapter (`the-hill-of-cinders.md`, "## Carry My Flame") so you know exactly where everyone stands. Books 5 and 6 were planned together: read `BOOK6.md` too before writing a word, because Book 5 must set up its ending.

Theme (never quoted): Acts 1–2 (wait for the gift; wind and fire resting on each of them); Acts 8 (Simon tries to buy the gift); Acts 9 (Saul on the road; Ananias sent to the man who cannot see; Barnabas vouches for him); Acts 12 and 16 (doors opened in the night; the jailer asks what he must do). Core truths: **no one carries the Flame alone**; **the gift is for anyone — the least likely most of all**; **it cannot be bought**; **the enemy can be made a brother**; **forgiveness for the thing you thought could never be forgiven**. This is the book of the Flame going out into the world: it should feel wider, brighter and faster than Book 4, full of small joys, with one hard, true knot at its heart (Wren and Aren).

## 1. Where Book 4 left them
- Spring dawn on Cinder Hill. The Lampwright is alive; he walked away east and was not there. He left a commission — "Carry my Flame to every Ring. And past them." — and a promise: "At Midsummer there will be a gift. For all of you. Wait for it. You won't carry it alone."
- **Wren** (12): the lamp is still hers, given; it leans a little toward him now, always. Red ribbon at her LEFT wrist; Tobin's striped knitted scarf round her neck (they are friends again). She said "thank you" to him — not a Soot Lane word. She still hates waiting, and is still a little proud of being the one with the lamp (a fault this book quietly mends).
- **Tobin** (13): forgiven and given his work back ("Do you love me?" three times). Leans on **Ash's pole**. Round lamp-base scars on both palms. One spectacle lens cracked. No longer afraid of the dark — it is "only the night". His question about arithmetic (how can a thousand lamps be lit from one flame and the flame be none the less?) is "nearly ripe".
- **Mira** (about 12): sees, and still echo-clicks; carries **the Chronicle** ("Look after it"). Its last written line: "On the third morning, the fire woke." Then blank pages.
- **Cinder**: her bond with the Lampwright is plain now; spark bright and steady.
- **Sister Hale** and **the Remnant** (a few hundred, scattered) are on the Hill; her arm is in a sling; the Lampwright mended her crutch with a strip of brass.
- **The hundred unfinished lamps** stand on their racks in the workshop: brass bodies, no chimney, no wick, no flame.
- **Varrick** dropped his sword and ran when he saw the Lampwright's scarred hands. Still unnamed. **Lord Edric** is the Queen's prisoner. **Old Ash** "isn't lost. He's waiting." Wren's parents: "Trust me with them." The lullaby: Wren knows only "Morning comes, morning comes … and the dark shall not keep us".
- The sun over Cinder Hill is "a little warmer" — not golden. Not yet.

## 2. New and returning cast (FIXED names)
**Aren** — Captain Varrick's real name, revealed in Ch 11 (no one says it before the Lampwright does in Ch 9). Thirty years ago he was **Aren, Old Ash's apprentice**, the youngest Lamplighter on Emberwick's wall; the burn scar across his right palm is from the Lamplighters' oath-flame. When the Guild expelled Ash, Aren, about nineteen and furious that the true Flame "did nothing" while the Guild grew fat, went looking for a power that would *do* something — and found the Queen. She renamed him Varrick. He has served her for thirty years: polite, tired, very good at the offer. Since Cinder Hill he cannot sleep; he has become harsher, not softer (Saul breathing threats): he hunts the Flame-bearers to drown what he saw. **After Ch 9**: blind for three days, then sees; quiet, plain-spoken, ashamed, very steady; he stops rubbing his palm. He never makes speeches about his change. He calls Wren "Miss Ashby" until she tells him not to (Ch 13), then "Wren". He calls Tobin "Harrowgate" (then "Tobin"). Ash's grief over him is honoured: he says "He called me lad. Right to the end. Every time."
**Guild Master Cuthbert Prowle** — returns (Book 1: "he'll be back"). With Edric gone, he has made himself **Lord Steward of Emberwick**, with the Queen's quiet backing. He has had the **Great Beacon's stair bricked up** and put a **toll-gate** at its foot: the free Flame Wren gave the city is now "Guild-held", and anyone who wants to light a lamp from it pays the Steward's Fee. He calls it "regulation". He calls the Flame-bearers "the Cinder sect". Lavender, gold chain, long words. He tries to **buy** the gift (Ch 6) and the lamp burns him. He is a comic villain and a frightened little man, never a monster; he flees to the Queen in Ch 14.
**Sergeant (now Captain) Bracken** — grizzled, tired, decent; made captain of the guard by Prowle and hates it. He is the jailer of Ch 8: when the door opens of itself and nobody runs, he asks Tobin, "What do I have to do?" — and the Flame rests on him. From then on he is one of them and stays to keep Emberwick's Flame.
**Nettle** (now 8) — lives with **Mrs Dolly Fenn** at the bakery in Soot Lane; front teeth grown in, but still the lisp; fierce now; the first child in Emberwick the Flame rests on (Ch 5), and she is unbearable about it.
**Mrs Dolly Fenn** — floury, kind, five children (plus Nettle). Her bakery is the Flame-bearers' house in Emberwick.
**Gristle Mott** — restored in Book 1; now a reformed, wheezing, embarrassing friend who keeps trying to give Wren things.
**Snib** — the Gutterling; buttons; squeaky rhymes ("Shiny-shiny, mine-mine!"). The Flame rests on him too (Ch 8) — the first Gutterling ever to carry it, which astonishes everybody and offends the other Gutterlings. He makes up a new rhyme ("Warm-warm, mine-mine — no! — yours-yours, given!").
**The Highland folk** — the **Harrowgate Highlands** east of Emberwick: high moors, heather (grey, until the Flame comes), stone walls, sheep, tarns, a few small Ring-towns. The town of **Harrowgate** itself — where Tobin's family came from long ago, before they moved to Emberwick (his great-grandfather's house is a ruin above the town; the Highlanders call Tobin "young Harrowgate" and expect him to be grand). Its Ring is twelve lamps, failing.
**Granny Kettle** — a Highland shepherd-woman, very old, very deaf, who shouts; keeps a tarn-side bothy; feeds Wren when she runs off in Ch 13; never knows the story and says exactly the right thing by accident ("You can't keep a coal and a grudge in the same pocket, hen. One of them goes out.").
**Ewan and Isla Moffat** — the Highland weaver couple whose house at the end of Weir Street in Harrowgate the blinded Aren is led to; they are Remnant, and terrified of him.
**The Queen** — not seen in person in Book 5; only her voice, once (Ch 9, inside Varrick's head), and her ash-crows. She is afraid now, and that makes her quieter, not louder.
**The Lampwright** — appears ONCE in person (Ch 9, on the road, to Varrick), and is otherwise felt, not seen: the warmth at Midsummer, a hand that turns a key in Ch 8, and the voice Mira hears in Ch 10. Same restraint as Book 4 §5. He calls Varrick "Aren".

## 3. Places & things
- **Cinder Hill** (Ch 1–3): green coming up through the ash; the workshop; the Remnant camping round it in their hundreds; the iron door open.
- **The Midsummer Gift** (Ch 2): at Midsummer dawn, a warm wind comes up out of the Burned Country with no cloud and no storm in it, smelling of bread and summer; it goes through the workshop like a breath; the cold forges light themselves; **all hundred unfinished lamps kindle at once** — each with its own small gold flame, though they have no wick and no chimney; and a flame, small as a candle-flame, comes to rest **on each person** there — on the crown of the head, for a moment, like a hand — and sinks into them. After it, every one of them can give the Flame: their hands are warm; a lamp lit by them lights. The colour comes up in their faces. Everyone who hears them sing that morning — the Delver refugees, a Highland drover, the Vaelport fisherfolk — hears it as if it were sung in their own mother's way (light touch, ONE paragraph, never explained).
- **The lamp in Book 5** (OUTLINE §3 rules still hold): it is still Wren's, and it is still *the* lamp — the Lampwright's own — but it is no longer the only light. Lamps lit from it, or from anyone the Flame rests on, burn true and do not weaken it. Wren is first glad, then (Ch 3–4) a little jealous and a little lost ("Then what am I *for*?"), and then she finds out (Ch 14). **Used to hurt it barely glows** — Ch 13, when Wren wants it to burn Aren, it goes down to a coal, and she knows why.
- **Tobin's question ripens** (Ch 2): watching the hundred lamps kindle from nowhere, he answers it himself: "It isn't arithmetic. It's *fire*. You don't divide a fire. You spread it." Sister Hale says that's what she's been saying for seventy years; he says no she hasn't; she says well, she *meant* it.
- **Emberwick under the Steward** (Ch 5–8): the Ring burns gold still (Book 1), but the Low Quarter is dim again because the Guild has stopped people lighting their own lamps from the Beacon; the Beacon stair is bricked up; a **toll-gate** in Beacon Square ("STEWARD'S FEE — ONE SILVER PENNY THE LIGHT"); Flame tokens sold again; the Warden's Hall shut and dusty; Tobin's attic workshop and the **Orrery** still there (Ch 7: on it, little lights are coming on all over the Low Quarter — Tobin's beads see the Flame spreading).
- **The strongroom** (Ch 8): under the Warden's Hall, 61 steps down, pitch dark, iron door (Book 1). Tobin, who was terrified of it in Book 1, sings in it.
- **The Highland Road** (Ch 9): a grey drove-road over the moors to Harrowgate. At noon on it, a light — not from the sun — and a voice.
- **Harrowgate** (Ch 10–15): a grey stone town on a hill above a weir, with a twelve-lamp Ring on a low wall; Weir Street; the Moffats' weaving house with its looms; the ruin of the old Harrowgate house; the moors; Granny Kettle's bothy by a black tarn.
- **The singing-stone** (Wren's, from Book 2) hums at Hollow-cold. It hums all through Ch 14.
- **Nightfall Spire and the Sunless Sea**: told of only (Ch 12, 15), by Aren — the Queen's tower on an island in a black sea to the east of the Highlands, where the sun never rises; where she keeps the greyed she has taken (the Hall of Sleepers), and Lord Edric, and "the old king". Do not go there in Book 5.

## 4. Wren's parents (the knot of the book — Ch 12–13)
- Wren's mother was **Linnet Ashby** and her father **Tam Ashby**. They were of the Remnant in Emberwick: Tam was a lamp-mender in the Low Quarter, Linnet sang the old songs. On the turn of the night they went up on the North Wall with the few others who still remembered, and sang "Morning comes" by the Ring-lamps — the old Lamplighters' wall-song (Book 4: "sung on the walls at the turning of the night, sung by more than one mother").
- Eight years ago (Wren was four) the Queen sent Varrick to break the singers. He took eleven of them off the wall in one night, Tam and Linnet among them, and carried them greyed to Nightfall Spire. Linnet hid Wren in a coal-hole first and told her to hum and not come out till morning. Wren did. A neighbour found her; Mott took her for the sweep-yard "out of charity" and called it board money ever after. Wren remembers none of this except the tune. (Never show the taking in a frightening way — Aren tells it plainly and briefly, ashamed.)
- **Aren remembers the child's humming from the coal-hole.** He heard it and walked past. That is the thing he confesses (Ch 12). It is also the first crack in him: he did not give her up.
- The greyed do not die in the Spire. They stand, and forget. Aren believes Tam and Linnet are there still. (Book 6 finds them.)
- Wren's arc: from "I'll never forgive him" — said out loud, and meant — through the lamp going down to a coal in her hands on the moor, to holding it out to him in Ch 13. It costs her everything, like asking Tobin's forgiveness did in Book 4, but more. Do not make it quick, and do not make it a speech.

## 5. Voices (Book 5)
- **Wren**: herself again — quick, cheeky, Soot Lane; impatient with waiting ("Midsummer's *ages*"); kind in a gruff way; proud of the lamp, and then not proud. In Ch 12–13 she goes very quiet and very cold, which frightens everyone more than shouting.
- **Tobin**: the precision is all back, and the "Actually—", but softer; he laughs at himself now. He is the one who vouches for Aren (Ch 11): "I sold Ash for a letter. I know what it's like to be the one nobody should trust. Somebody trusted me anyway."
- **Mira**: precise, sensory, mischievous, brave. Sent to Aren (Ch 10), she refuses first, then goes; she, who was blind, teaches the blinded captain to listen ("You click. Like this. No — you sound like a hen. Again.").
- **Sister Hale**: joyful, earthy, "duck"; wise; stays to lead on Cinder Hill after Ch 3 and comes to Harrowgate in Ch 14 with the Remnant's lamps.
- **Aren**: before Ch 9, Varrick: smooth as oiled iron, polite, tired, cruel by being reasonable. After: few words; plain; he says "I don't know" a lot, which Varrick never said.
- **Prowle**: long words, lavender, terror of anything he can't put a price on.
- **The narrator**: as ever — warm, present, sparing ("I am glad to say…"). This is a happier book; let the narrator enjoy it.

## 6. Chapter beat sheet (~3,000–3,300 words each)

### 1. Waiting
- Cinder Hill in late spring: green spreading through the ash; the Remnant camped round the workshop in their hundreds; more arriving every day from farms and towns with news of the Hill. Comic: waiting is the hardest thing Wren has ever done ("I could have been to Emberwick and back three times"); Tobin builds a shadow-stick calendar on the workshop roof and announces the days; Mira reads the Chronicle to the children and turns, every evening, to the blank pages and runs her fingers over them; Cinder sleeps on the bench where the Lampwright set down the half-finished lamp.
- Wren tries to light one of the hundred lamps from hers: it will not take (no wick, no chimney). She is annoyed and does not know why it matters.
- News by a refugee drover: Prowle is back in Emberwick as Lord Steward; the Beacon is bricked up; "Cinder sect" folk are being arrested; the grey captain is riding the roads, burning the Remnant's barns, worse than ever.
- Midsummer Eve: everyone in or round the workshop; nobody can sleep. End: just before dawn, the air goes still, and then warm.

### 2. The Gift
- Midsummer dawn. The warm wind out of the Burned Country (§3). The forges light. The hundred lamps kindle at once. The flame on each head, like a hand, then sinking in. Wren feels it rest on her, too, though she already has the lamp — "as if somebody had said her name in a crowd". Cinder's spark blazes. Sister Hale sings and everyone sings, and the refugees at the foot of the Hill hear it each their own way.
- Tobin's question ripens (§3). The colour in everyone's faces. The ribbon.
- They discover what it means: a Remnant boy lights a candle-stub with his bare warm hand — and it burns true. Laughter, tears, chaos; Sister Hale has to bang her crutch on an anvil.
- End: Wren, alone with her lamp at the workshop door, glad and a little lost: "Then what am I *for*?" Cinder leans on her leg.

### 3. Every Ring and Past Them
- Sister Hale sends them out in bands, two and three together, each with lamps from the racks: to Thornmere, to Vaelport (Mira's Pim and the Pellows!), to Deepholm, to the farms. Wren, Tobin, Mira and Cinder take Emberwick — the hardest, Sister Hale says, because it's home, and because of the Steward.
- On the road west, the **Scarecrow Farms**: the Heskeths (Book 4) — Posy, well now, runs to meet Wren. They go into the fields at dusk to the grey "scarecrows" and give the Flame; the farmers turn their heads, and walk in out of the fields to their suppers. Will Hesketh receives the Flame and lights the next farm himself. Wren watches the lights go on across the dark fields without her, farm by farm, and does not know what she feels.
- End: from the ridge, a line of lights going away across the land in every direction, and one grey rider on the skyline, watching.

### 4. The Grey Captain
- On the road: they find a Remnant barn burned, the people taken. Ash-crows. A Ring-town (Stallbridge) where the Warden bars his gate against the "Cinder sect" — and the children of the town climb the wall to fetch the Flame anyway.
- Varrick's Hollow Knights nearly catch them in a sunken lane; Mira's ears save them; they hide in a ditch while the knights go by with prisoners roped behind them, Remnant folk with lamps taken. Varrick rides past within an arm's length. His face is grey and drawn, and he is rubbing his palm so hard it must hurt.
- Wren, shaking with fury in the ditch: "I hope the Flame burns him to nothing."
- End: Emberwick's Ring on the horizon — gold. But the Low Quarter, inside it, dark.

### 5. The Steward's Fee
- Emberwick: the toll-gate in Beacon Square; the bricked-up stair; Flame tokens sold again; criers calling the "Cinder sect" witches; the Low Quarter dim. Comedy of return: Mott, weepingly glad, tries to give Wren his best hat; Snib steals Tobin's spectacles; Mrs Fenn's bakery; **Nettle**, who flings herself on Wren and then refuses to let go for an hour.
- Bracken, now Captain, in his new uniform, miserable, warns them off quietly.
- That night in the bakery the Flame rests on Nettle when Wren gives her a lamp — she lights every candle in the shop and Mrs Fenn's oven, which nearly sets the bread on fire. Next morning they start going door to door down Soot Lane, as Wren did in Book 1 — but this time everyone they light goes and lights their neighbour.
- End: Prowle's carriage at the end of Soot Lane, and Prowle in it, staring.

### 6. The Price of Fire
- Prowle comes to the bakery in person, all lavender and graciousness, with a strongbox. He doesn't want to arrest them. He wants to **buy the knack** — "Name your figure, Miss Ashby. The Guild has always paid for expertise." Wren laughs at him. He tries to take one of the hundred lamps to "study" — it burns his fingers (no mark on anyone else's); he drops it; it lands upright and goes on burning on the flagstones. Wren: "It can't be bought. Not by you, not by anybody. You can have it for nothing. That's the only way there is." He cannot bear it.
- He leaves white-faced and vowing. That afternoon a decree: lighting a lamp without the Steward's Fee is theft from the Guild. Arrest the Cinder sect.
- End: guards at the bakery door. Tobin and Mira are taken (Wren is out on the roofs with Snib and gets away — Tobin makes sure of it).

### 7. The Warden's Hall
- Tobin POV. Taken up the Hill to his own father's Hall, now Prowle's. His father's empty study, dust on the chair. Prowle questions him, oily and cruel by being kind: "Your father understood bargains. What a pity you don't."
- Bracken, escorting him, tells him in a whisper the truth: Edric did not run. Varrick took him to the Queen, to **Nightfall Spire**, across the Sunless Sea.
- In passing, his attic: the **Orrery of the Ring** — and on it, all across the Low Quarter, beads Tobin never wired are glowing, one after another, as people light their neighbours' lamps. He laughs out loud. Bracken stares at him.
- End: down the 61 steps to the strongroom, with Mira; the iron door shuts; total dark.

### 8. Sixty-One Steps
- In the strongroom. In Book 1 the dark down here was Tobin's worst fear; now he sits in it and finds he wants to sing. He and Mira sing the Lamplighters' Psalm in the dark. Outside, at midnight, Wren and Nettle and Mrs Fenn and half of Soot Lane are singing in Beacon Square under the toll-gate.
- The lock turns. Nobody is there. The door swings open. (A hand on the key that they never see; the warmth; nothing explained.) Up the steps: Bracken, asleep at his table. He wakes, sees the open door, and is terrified (the Steward will hang him) — and then sees they have not run.
- Bracken: "What do I have to do?" Tobin, who once over-explained everything, says only: "Hold out your hands." The Flame rests on him.
- Bracken walks them out of the front door of the Warden's Hall. Meanwhile Snib, on the roof with Wren, gets the Flame by accident, holding her lamp for her, and does not know what to do with himself; he lights every chimney-pot on the Hill. At dawn the people take down the bricks of the Beacon stair with their hands. Prowle flees by the South Gate, again, with his strongbox, eastward. (He will run to the Queen.)
- End: Bracken, now keeper of the Beacon, tells them which way Varrick took the prisoners: east, by the Highland road, toward the sea. Tobin: "Harrowgate."

### 9. The Road to Harrowgate
- **Varrick POV**, the only chapter told from inside him. Riding the Highland drove-road with his Hollow Knights and a string of roped Remnant prisoners, carrying their confiscated lamps in a sack that is too warm and too heavy. He has not slept since Cinder Hill. He hears Ash in his head saying "lad". He hears the Queen saying it is easier in the grey. He drives the column harder to drown both. Brief, plain memories (never a flashback chapter): the Leaning Tower, the wall, the oath-flame, Ash's dreadful tea, the night the Guild threw Ash out and nothing happened.
- Noon on the moor. A light — not the sun — round him, so bright it is like being inside the Flame. He falls from his horse. A voice, which he knows: "Aren. Why are you hunting me?" He: "Who are you?" — though he knows. "I'm the one you're hunting. Every lamp you take is mine." Then: "Get up. Go to the town. You'll be told."
- He gets up blind. The Hollow Knights have scattered across the moor like smoke. The sack of lamps has burst open and the lamps are burning in the heather. The prisoners, unroped (the ropes burned through), stand and stare at him. One of them, a weaver's wife, after a long time, takes his hand to lead him. It is the people he captured who lead their captor into Harrowgate.
- End: in the dark that is all he has now, he says his own name out loud for the first time in thirty years. "Aren."

### 10. A Man Who Cannot See
- The company comes east over the moors to Harrowgate (two days). The Highland folk, the twelve-lamp Ring failing, sheep, heather; the ruin of Tobin's great-grandfather's house; Highlanders who call him "young Harrowgate" and expect grandeur (comedy: he is all elbows and soot). They hear the news: the grey captain lies blind in the Moffats' house on Weir Street and has not eaten in three days.
- Mira, in the night, hears the voice (like mist on a river): "Go to the house at the end of Weir Street. There is a man there who cannot see." She refuses — out loud, to the dark — "He took our people. He nearly killed Wren. He broke the Remnant's barns." "Go. I have work for him." She goes, alone, not telling Wren.
- In the dark room she sits with him. She was blind twelve years; she knows what it is. She teaches him to listen, to click (comedy and tenderness). Then she gives him the Flame. It is like grey scales falling off his eyes; he sees — her face first. He weeps like a boy.
- End: Wren, at the door, who followed her. Her face.

### 11. His Name Was Aren
- He tells them who he is (§2): Aren, Ash's apprentice; the oath; the Guild; the Queen; thirty years. Plainly, not making himself sound better or worse. Wren will not stay in the room.
- The Remnant of Harrowgate will not have him; the Moffats are frightened; a Highland lad throws a stone. They want him driven out on the moor.
- **Tobin speaks for him** (Barnabas) — §5. Tobin is shaking, but he does it. Mira backs him. Wren says nothing, which is worse.
- Aren goes up on the town's failing wall that night and, with a lamp Mira gives him, relights one of the twelve Ring-lamps with his own scarred hand — and his old oath-scar, which has been grey for thirty years, shines gold through his skin. The town sees.
- End: Aren to Tobin, quietly: there is something he has to tell Miss Ashby, and he does not know how.

### 12. What Aren Remembered
- Aren tells Wren about the North Wall eight years ago (§4): the singers; Tam and Linnet Ashby; the coal-hole; the humming; how he walked past. And where they are: Nightfall Spire, with the greyed, and Lord Edric, and "the old king the Queen keeps in her tower — Ardric, who cannot die".
- Wren learns, all at once, that her parents had names, that they were Lamplighters, that she was not *lost* but hidden, that the song she kept was a gift her mother gave her in the dark — and that the man who did it is sitting in front of her, asking for nothing.
- She goes very quiet and very cold. "I'll never forgive you. Not ever. I'd sooner it went out." She walks out of Harrowgate onto the moor in the dusk, alone except for Cinder, who will not be left.
- End: on the moor, she takes out the lamp, and it is a coal.

### 13. A Coal and a Grudge
- Night and the next day on the high moor. Wren walks and walks. The lamp stays low; she knows exactly why and is furious with it. Granny Kettle's bothy by the black tarn: porridge, shouting, sheep in the kitchen; "You can't keep a coal and a grudge in the same pocket, hen. One of them goes out." (She is talking about the fire. Isn't she?)
- Wren remembers, unwillingly: the toast — "It's given"; her own words to Tobin in Vaelport — "I wish it had been you" — and how she was forgiven them; Ash calling Varrick "lad" with grief, right to the end. Ash had known what Varrick was, and grieved, and never stopped calling him lad.
- She goes back. Not because she feels like it. In the Moffats' weaving room, in front of everyone, she holds out the lamp to Aren — not to burn him; to give. "It's given," she says. "Don't make me say it twice." It blazes. Her ribbon goes red to the knot. He cannot speak. She tells him to stop calling her Miss Ashby. (It must cost her; let the reader feel it cost.)
- End: the singing-stone in Wren's pocket begins to hum, and will not stop. Across the moor, a grey tide is coming: Hollows, and the Hollow Knights, and a carriage that smells of lavender.

### 14. The Highland Ring
- The siege of Harrowgate at night. The Queen has sent her Hollow host, with the Knights, and Prowle in the carriage offering terms — "the Steward's protection" if they hand over "the deserter Varrick" and the lamp. The twelve-lamp Ring is not enough; three lamps fail.
- Then along the drove-roads from the west come lights: Sister Hale and the Remnant with the hundred lamps; Bracken with Emberwick folk; the Heskeths; Nettle (who was told to stay home and didn't), and Snib. Not one lamp: hundreds. They stand along the wall of Harrowgate shoulder to shoulder, and the Ring is *them*.
- Aren goes out of the gate alone to his own Hollow Knights, with no sword, and calls them by the names they had before — he knows every one. Some turn from him; some stop, and he gives them the Flame, and a grey man becomes a frightened Highland farmer again in the middle of the field.
- Wren, on the wall, finds out what she is for: not the only light, but the first one — the one the Lampwright carried down the Mountain, that lights the lighters. She holds it up and every flame along the wall leans toward it, and the Hollows break. Prowle's carriage overturns in a ditch; he runs off into the dark toward the sea, losing his chain.
- End: dawn over the Highlands, and the heather — which nobody had known was purple.

### 15. Toward the Sunless Sea
- Morning after. Joy and mending. The Highlanders name the colour of heather. Tobin in the ruin of his great-grandfather's house finds a lamp-niche carved over the door with a flame and his family's name: the Harrowgates were Lamplighters once.
- Council: Aren tells them about Nightfall Spire and the Sunless Sea (§3, Book 6). The Queen is afraid; she has called every Hollow home; she is gathering everything into the Spire. Edric is there. The greyed are there — Tam and Linnet Ashby among them, if they are anywhere. Sister Hale says they must wait, and pray, and gather. Wren says: "I've waited my whole life." Sister Hale looks at her a long time, and says, "Yes, duck. I think you have." They will go.
- Highland fishermen at the coast below the moors have boats; old ones; and nobody has sailed east into the black water for three hundred years.
- Wren on the moor at dusk with Aren, who is teaching Tobin the old wall-songs. She hums her mother's tune and, for the first time, does not stop at "and the dark shall not keep us" because it hurts — she stops because she doesn't know the rest *yet*. The narrator notices the difference.
- Close: Tobin opens his mother's compass. Since Book 1 it has pointed to the Bright Mountain. Now it swings, and settles — due east, toward the sea, where there is no sun at all — and the needle is warm. A quiet, eager, forward-looking last line.

## 7. Rules for this book
- All OUTLINE §1 voice rules hold. Never name God, Jesus, the Bible, church, heaven, sin or salvation; never explain the allegory. "The Maker" may appear rarely and plainly. The Remnant's meeting-places are "meeting-houses", never "church".
- The Lampwright: one appearance in person (Ch 9, to Varrick). Never "I am…". Never explained.
- No Scripture quoted. The Ch 9 words ("Why are you hunting me?") are the book's one echo of Scripture, kept close in sense only; nobody comments on it.
- Peril real but not gory. The taking of Wren's parents is told, briefly, never shown. No deaths in Book 5.
- Aren's change is real but not instant in others' eyes: the Remnant's fear of him is fair, and the book honours it.
- Do NOT go to Nightfall Spire. Do NOT finish the lullaby. Do NOT reveal what happened to Wren's parents beyond Aren's account (they are greyed in the Spire, he believes; Book 6 finds them). Do NOT bring Old Ash back.
