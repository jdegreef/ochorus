# FIRE AGAINST THE DARK — Story Bible & Beat Sheet
Book 1 of *The Lamplighters of Gloamhaven*. An Ochorus Original. Ages 9–12 (good read-aloud for 7–8).
Theme verse (never quoted in the text): John 1:5 — the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.
Core truth: grace — the gift you cannot earn, buy or steal; and what is given, grows (Ephesians 2:8–9).

## 1. VOICE & STYLE (read twice)
Write in a voice in the tradition of C. S. Lewis's Narnia books — but never imitate or quote his actual sentences, and never mention Narnia, Aslan, Lewis or any real book.
- **A warm, present storyteller-narrator** in past tense, close third person, who now and then speaks directly to the reader, briefly and kindly: "If you have ever tried to sleep in a room where the fire has gone out, you will know…", "I am sorry to say that…", "This is the part of the story that Wren never liked to tell." Use this sparingly — two to four times a chapter at most, never for a whole paragraph of lecturing.
- **Plain, clear, concrete sentences.** Mostly short; some longer rolling ones for wonder. Concrete nouns and strong verbs. Sensory detail (soot, cold, smell of lamp-oil, grey light, the warmth of the Flame).
- **Humour** woven through: Old Ash's dreadful tea, Cinder's sneezed sparks and biscuit-stealing, Gutterlings' squeaky rhymes, Wren's sharp tongue, Tobin's over-explaining. Children forgive darkness when they laugh in between.
- **Moral clarity without sermons.** Never explain the allegory. Never name God, Jesus, the Bible, church, heaven, sin or salvation. The Lampwright is never explained, only glimpsed and told of in stories. Let actions carry the meaning.
- **British spelling and idiom** (colour, grey, neighbour, realise, mum not mom, biscuits). Not archaic; no "thee/thou".
- **Vocabulary**: rich but readable for a 10-year-old; an unusual word now and then (gutter, garret, flue, guttering, wick, gloaming), made clear by context.
- **Dialogue** brisk, distinct per character (see voices below). Use straight double quotes " and straight apostrophes ' in the files (they will be curled automatically). Use an em dash — for interruptions.
- **Each chapter ~2,900–3,200 words**, 2–4 scenes, ends on a hook or a turn. Start each chapter fresh and vivid, not with a recap.
- **Peril**: real but not gory. No blood beyond a scraped knee, no deaths in Book 1. Hollows are frightening (cold, silent, colour-draining). No romance. No profanity (Wren may say "blast" or "rot").
- **Magic rule**: good power is always RECEIVED (the Flame is given); evil power is grasped or bargained for. No spells, no wands.

## 2. THE WORLD
**Gloamhaven** — a land under a cursed sun. Three hundred years ago King **Ardric** climbed the Bright Mountain and bargained with **Morvaine, the Umbral Queen**: the sun's fire for an undying crown. The fire went into his crown; the curse fell on everyone.
- **The sun, Solenne**, still rises, but pale "as a coin seen through dirty water". Noon is like a winter dusk. It barely warms anything. Crops grow small and sour. **Colours have faded**: the world is greys, browns, dun and dull blue-ish; people have forgotten the names of most colours. Near true Flame, colours *come back* (a key visual motif): Wren's ribbon is a faded pinkish grey, but near the lamp it glows **red**.
- **Rings**: each city is protected by a Ring of great lamps on its wall, lit long ago from the Original Flame and kept burning by the **Lamp Guild** with oil. Emberwick's Ring is **144 great lamps** on the city wall, all fed from the **Great Beacon** in the centre of the city. Ring-lamps keep Hollows out while they burn. They are failing: dimmer every year, and the Guild charges more for oil.
- **Hollows**: grey, faceless shapes like smoke poured into the outline of a person; silent; bitterly cold; they drift rather than walk. Their touch drains **colour, memory and hope**: a touched person turns grey, goes quiet and listless, forgets names and faces ("greyed"). Ordinary oil-light holds them back; **true Flame** burns them and they flee shrieking (the only sound they make). True Flame given freely restores a greyed person.
- **No Hollow has crossed Emberwick's Ring for a hundred years** — until Chapter 5.

## 3. THE LAMP — RULES (keep consistent!)
- A small, battered **brass lamp**, dented, with a glass chimney that has a hairline crack; a little hook on top; NO oil reservoir — it needs no fuel. Flame is golden-white and warm, and smells faintly of bread and summer.
- It **relights itself** if blown out.
- **Its law** (Old Ash teaches it in Ch 8): *"It cannot be bought, stolen or earned. Only given."* And: *"What is given, grows."*
- It **burns** the hand of anyone who tries to **buy or sell** it (Grubb, Ch 2). It does NOT burn the one it was given to.
- It **comes back** to the one it was given to (Wren) — canal, burial, etc., Ch 2 — **but only while she still wants it**. In Ch 12 Wren, bitter, throws it down — "Take it, then! I never asked for it!" — and it goes **cold and dark** and does not come back. It warms again in Tobin's hands only when he carries it down to give back to her (Ch 12).
- Used as a **weapon** (to hurt, to get something) it barely glows. Held out as a **gift** with nothing asked, it **blazes** (Ch 11).
- When its flame is given to another lamp, **it does not weaken** — the Flame spreads (Ch 14).

## 4. CAST (names are FIXED — do not invent alternatives)
**Wren Ashby** (12) — chimney-sweep orphan of Soot Lane. Small, wiry, quick; black hair she cuts herself with a knife; sharp chin; always hungry; a pickpocket and liar when she must be; fiercely protective of Nettle. Believes **nothing in life is free** ("Everything costs. If it doesn't cost you, it'll cost you later."). Owes "board money" to Mott and can never pay it off. Only possession from her mother: a **faded ribbon** tied round her left wrist (it glows red near the Flame). She half-remembers her mother singing **"Morning comes, morning comes"** — a lullaby whose other words she has lost. Lost her parents at about four; she doesn't know how (seed: never resolved in Book 1). Voice: quick, cheeky, Soot Lane slang ("Rot!", "Blast", "you great lump", "nob" for a rich person), short sentences, deflects feelings with jokes.

**Tobin Harrowgate** (13) — the Warden's son. Tall, gangly, all elbows, ink-stained fingers, untidy fair hair, spectacles he pushes up his nose. Brilliant with machines and maps; over-explains when nervous. **Terrified of the dark** (secret): sleeps with a small Guild-oil night-lamp that was his mother's. His mother, **Lady Elinor**, died when he was five; she loved the old Lamplighter songs and stories, which his father has forbidden. Her gift to him: a **brass compass** with an inscription inside the lid: *"Home is where the light is."* Voice: precise, polite, long words, "Actually—", "Technically speaking", stammers when afraid.

**Old Ash** — the last true Lamplighter. Very tall, stooped, long white beard singed brown on one side, patched blue-grey coat (once the Lamplighters' blue), carries a long brass-hooked **lamplighter's pole**. Once Master Lamplighter of Emberwick; expelled by the Guild thirty years ago for refusing to charge for light. The city thinks him mad: every night he walks the wall relighting Ring-lamps the Guild lets go dim. Lives in the **Leaning Tower** on the North Wall. Makes **dreadful tea** ("it tasted, Wren said afterwards, like a boot that had been boiled in a puddle"). Gruff, kind, fond of riddles and of saying "Hm." Knows more about the Lampwright than he tells. Voice: slow, dry, old-fashioned turns of phrase, "child", "lad", "Hm."

**Cinder** — Ash's **ember-fox**: a small fox, coat the grey of old ashes, with a tail-tip that holds a **living spark** that glows brighter when she's happy or near the Flame. Cannot talk but understands everything. Steals biscuits. **Sneezes sparks** when annoyed. Growls at Hollows before anyone sees them. Fearless.

**Nettle** (7) — a tiny sweep who follows Wren everywhere; freckles, two front teeth missing, a lisp on "s" ("Wren, you're the betht"), always cold. Wren protects her like a little sister.

**Gristle Mott** — Wren's sweep-master: big, red-knuckled, wheezing, smells of gin and soot; keeps orphans in debt; would sell anyone for a coin. Sells Wren out in Ch 12.

**Silas Grubb** — pawnbroker at Crookback Market: thin, long-nosed, rubs his hands, speaks in prices.

**Lord Edric Harrowgate** — Warden of Emberwick; Tobin's father. Tall, stern, silver at the temples, never raises his voice; frightened of losing his place and of the dark beyond the wall; ashamed of a son who would rather tinker than fence. Bargains with Varrick for the lamp (Ch 9). Not a pure villain: a man choosing safety over truth. In Ch 13 he learns Varrick lied, and hides in his Hall; in Ch 15 he is shaken, silent, cannot look at Tobin.

**Guild Master Cuthbert Prowle** — head of the Lamp Guild: round and glossy, smells of lavender oil, wears a heavy gold chain of office, loves ceremonies and long words; secretly waters the oil and sells **Flame tokens** (little stamped tin discs "blessed at the Beacon") to the poor. Calls the lamp "witch-light".

**Sergeant Bracken** — one of the Warden's guards: grizzled, tired, not cruel; arrests Wren in Ch 12 but is gentle; in Ch 15 shakes her hand.

**Mrs Dolly Fenn** — the baker of Soot Lane, kind, floury, five children; greyed by a Hollow in Ch 5 (forgets her children's names); restored in Ch 14/15.

**Snib** — a **Gutterling** (see below) who can be bribed with buttons; starts as a nuisance (Ch 1, 10), helps the children over the rooftops in Ch 13 for a button and then — surprisingly — for nothing.

**Captain Varrick** — the Umbral Queen's captain of the **Hollow Knights**. Tall, pale, grey armour, grey cloak, grey eyes, a voice "smooth as oiled iron". Polite; bargains rather than brawls; his weapon is **the offer**. **Was once Old Ash's student and a Lamplighter**: Ash calls him "lad" with grief; Varrick has an old **burn scar across his right palm** (from the Lamplighters' oath-flame) that he rubs without thinking. His real name is NOT revealed in Book 1 (do not invent one). Ash's words to him in Ch 14 make him hesitate for one heartbeat.

**Morvaine, the Umbral Queen** — never seen in Book 1. Only a **voice** in the dark: sweet, reasonable, "like warm milk going cold". She never threatens; she makes giving up sound sensible. "Why fight? Why hope? Lie down. It is easier in the grey."

**The Lampwright** — never named as present; glimpsed only, readers should guess:
  1. Ch 2 — an **old man with a bread basket** at Crookback Market who gives Wren a warm loaf and says "No charge. It's given." (He has kind eyes and a scar on his hand Wren half-notices.) Gone when she looks back.
  2. Ch 2 — the **voice in the chimney** of her garret: *"It was given, child. Not lost."*
  3. Ch 14 — at the top of the Beacon, Wren feels **a warm hand on her shoulder** steadying her as she gives the Flame. No one is there.
  He is otherwise only in **stories** (Ch 8).

**Hollow Knights** — Varrick's soldiers: hollowed men in grey armour with grey faces and empty eyes; they obey without will. A few appear in Ch 9, 12–14.

**Gutterlings** — knee-high soot-imps that live in chimneys and flues: black as soot, big white eyes, long fingers, squeak in rhymes ("Shiny-shiny, mine-mine!"), steal anything that glints; mostly mischief, not wicked; easily bribed — by the Queen too.

## 5. PLACES (Emberwick)
- **Emberwick**: smoky Ring-city of canals, chimneys and lamp-towers, inside a high wall. Rich on **the Hill** (Warden's Hall, Guild Hall, big houses); poor in **the Low Quarter** (Soot Lane, the tanneries, the canal).
- **Thornbury House**: a great abandoned mansion at the edge of the Hill, shut up for fifty years; rumoured to have belonged to the last Lamplighter family; tall twisting chimneys; the sealed flue where the lamp lies.
- **Soot Lane**: crooked lane of leaning houses, washing lines, rooftops; Mott's sweep yard; Wren's **garret** (attic room with a slanting roof, a straw mattress, a chimney-breast that's cold because Mott won't buy coal); Mrs Fenn's bakery.
- **Crookback Market**: crowded bazaar by the canal; Grubb's pawnshop "GRUBB — LOANS — NO QUESTIONS".
- **The Soot Canal**: black, oily, slow.
- **The Warden's Hall**: grand, cold, grey stone on top of the Hill; Tobin's **attic workshop** under the roof; deep beneath, the **strongroom** — down 61 steps, pitch dark, iron door.
- **The Guild Hall**: gilded, smelling of lavender and lamp-oil; the **vault** under it with the ledgers.
- **The Leaning Tower**: Ash's crooked stone tower on the North Wall, cluttered with maps, lamp parts, teacups, books, a stuffed owl; a round window that looks over the wall to the Greywaste.
- **The Great Beacon**: the tallest tower in Emberwick, in **Beacon Square** at the city's heart; a spiral stair of **300 steps**; at the top a vast iron bowl where the Beacon-fire should burn; from it, copper **flame-pipes** run under the city to all 144 Ring-lamps (so a flame given at the Beacon can spread to the whole Ring).
- **The Grey Gate**: north gate, out to the **Greywaste** (colourless plain of grey grass and dead trees).
- **The Bright Mountain**: far away, in stories; where the Lampwright came from; the goal of the quest.
- **Cinder Hill**: in stories only; where the Lampwright was snuffed out.

## 6. LORE (as Ash tells it — Ch 7–8)
- Ardric's bargain (above).
- **The Lampwright**: long ago, after the curse, a stranger came down from the Bright Mountain carrying true fire in a small brass lamp. He walked through the cities lighting lamps for anyone who asked — never charging. He touched blind eyes and they saw; grey faces and they flushed with colour. He founded the **Lamplighters**, whose oath was to give light freely. The Queen hated him. On **Cinder Hill** her darkness took him and snuffed him out. His friends wept. **Three days later**, the Flame was burning again on the mountain — and he was gone. The Lamplighters lit the Rings from his Flame. Over the years the Lamplighters became the **Guild**, and the Guild began charging for oil, and the light grew dim.
- Ash keeps the story deliberately partial: "There is more. But it is not mine to tell. Not yet." (Book 4 will tell it via the Chronicle.)

## 7. SEEDS TO PLANT (for later books — light touch, don't explain)
- Wren's **parents**: the ribbon; the half-remembered lullaby "Morning comes, morning comes…"; she doesn't know how she lost them.
- Tobin's **mother**: Lady Elinor, the night-lamp, the old songs, the compass "Home is where the light is".
- The **compass**: Wren steals it (Ch 4), returns it (Ch 6); Tobin picks the cell-lock with its needle (Ch 12); in Ch 15 **Ash borrows it overnight** and gives it back "tuned", saying "A compass ought to know the way home."
- **Varrick–Ash**: "lad"; the scar on Varrick's palm; one heartbeat of hesitation (Ch 14).
- **The Lampwright** appearances (§4).
- **Snib**: a Gutterling who helps for nothing (Ch 13).

## 8. CHAPTER BEAT SHEET (15 chapters, ~3,000 words each)

### 1. A Lamp in the Flue
- Open with the narrator and the cursed sun: Emberwick at "noon", grey light, no one remembers what colour the sky used to be. Introduce Wren on the roof of Thornbury House, sent by Gristle Mott (he's been paid by a mysterious buyer's agent to "see if the chimneys are sound" — it doesn't matter who).
- Wren in the flues: the dark doesn't frighten her; she knows chimneys. Gutterlings (Snib among them) chase her, steal a brush, squeak rhymes. Comic scramble.
- A **sealed-off flue** behind loose bricks; warmth where no fire has burned for fifty years. She finds the brass lamp wedged in a niche, warm, with no oil, burning a tiny golden flame.
- She blows it out to hide its light (the Gutterlings want it). A heartbeat later — it relights itself. Her ribbon looks **red** in its light for the first time; she stares. "Rot," she whispers.
- She stuffs it in her coat; it's warm against her ribs; plan: sell it and eat for a month. Back at the yard Mott shouts, docks her pay; she says nothing about the lamp. In her cold garret she peeks at it once; the room is warm. End hook: "That was the first night in Wren's life that she was warm all the way through. It was not, I am glad to say, the last."

### 2. The Lamp That Would Not Stay Lost
- Morning: Crookback Market. Grubb sees the lamp, offers a fortune — "Twenty silver crowns!" — reaches for it, and it **burns** his fingers (no mark on Wren's). He drops it, yelps, calls it cursed, throws her out.
- Hungry, Wren passes an **old man with a bread basket** (Lampwright #1): he gives her a warm loaf, "No charge. It's given." She suspects a trick, takes it, turns to say something cheeky — he's gone. (Light touch: kind eyes, an old scar on his hand.)
- Wren shares the bread with Nettle. Then, angry at the "cursed" lamp, she throws it into the **Soot Canal**; it glows under the black water like a sunken star. She tries other ways to be rid of it (bury it in the tannery yard, leave it on Tallow Bridge). Each time, it's back — on her garret windowsill by evening.
- Night in the garret, alone: she tells the lamp off. A **voice in the chimney**, low and kind: *"It was given, child. Not lost."* She's frightened for the first time she can remember — and strangely comforted. She hums the scrap of the lullaby "Morning comes, morning comes…" and doesn't know the rest. Falls asleep warm.

### 3. The Warden's Son  (Tobin POV)
- Tobin in his attic workshop in the Warden's Hall: the **Orrery of the Ring** — a brass model of Emberwick's wall with 144 tiny glass beads joined by copper "flame-wires" that warm and glow faintly when the real Ring-lamps burn. Over-explanation humour. He's proud of it.
- His secret: he can't sleep without his mother's little night-lamp; the dark makes his hands shake. Lady Elinor and her songs; the compass "Home is where the light is"; his father forbids the old stories ("Lamplighter nonsense").
- Supper with his father: cold, formal; Edric wishes he would fence, ride, be a Warden's son. Tobin tries to tell him about the Orrery and is cut off.
- The Warden's council: Guild Master Prowle (lavender, gold chain, long words) reports the Ring dimming faster than ever; asks for more money for oil; blames "the dark times". Edric worries; the Greywaste watch reports grey shapes.
- That night Tobin notices on the Orrery: in the **Low Quarter** one bead glowing **bright** — and it's **moving** — but there's no Ring-lamp there. It moves down Soot Lane. He decides to find out what it is. Ends on him pulling on his coat, terrified, carrying his night-lamp.

### 4. Flicker
- Tobin, out of his depth in the Low Quarter at dusk, follows his pocket-model (or a copper "flicker-needle" he's rigged) toward the bright spot. Comic: he's obviously a nob; people stare.
- He finds Wren. She sees a rich boy and **picks his pocket** — takes the **compass** — and bolts. **Rooftop chase** (her territory; he's clumsy but brave-ish and clever, uses the washing lines).
- He catches up only because she has **stopped to share her bread with Nettle**. He's moved; she's furious at being caught.
- Argument; he sees the lamp glowing in her coat; nearby the street's single old Ring-lamp on the wall **flares brighter** as she passes. Tobin: "It's you. The bright bead. It's *you*." He asks for his compass back; she bargains (a sausage? a coin?) — she keeps it for now ("Finders keepers").
- From the rooftop, both see **grey shapes moving beyond the city wall** in the gloaming, many of them, drifting toward the Ring. Nettle whispers "Hollowth". End.

### 5. Over the Wall
- Next day, market day in Soot Lane. Wren and Nettle at Mrs Fenn's bakery (kind Mrs Fenn slips them crusts). Tobin turns up, awkward, wanting his compass and answers.
- Late afternoon the old Ring-lamps on the Low Quarter wall **gutter**; cold rolls over the wall like fog; **Hollows** pour in — the first in a hundred years. Silence, then screams. Colour drains from everything they pass.
- A Hollow touches **Mrs Fenn**: she goes grey, sits down on the step, and can't remember her children's names. Terrible, quiet moment.
- A Hollow reaches for Nettle; Wren pulls the lamp out without thinking — it **flares white-gold**; the Hollows **shriek** (their only sound) and flee back over the wall. But they've *seen* it.
- After: a whole stretch of the Ring stays dark. The crowd turns to stare at the girl holding the light. Someone says "witch-light". Wren runs, dragging Nettle. Tobin runs after her.

### 6. The Guild's Verdict
- Next morning, a crier and Prowle on the steps of the Guild Hall: the lamp is "witch-light" poisoning the Ring; the girl who carries it is to be arrested; reward offered. Lord Edric signs the order.
- Tobin is told by his father to help the guard hunt her (he knows the Low Quarter now). Instead he finds Wren (Nettle leads him) and **hides her in his attic workshop**. She's wary; they **argue** about trust, theft and fear — she: "Everyone wants something." He: "I just want to understand it." She gives him back the compass, grudgingly ("It's not even gold"). He shows her the Orrery; she's impressed despite herself.
- Gristle Mott, scenting the reward, has told the guard Wren's haunts. Guards search the Hall; near capture in the attic (comic hiding among clockwork). Tobin lies to his father's steward for her — his first real lie for someone else.
- They flee over the roofs. Where to go? "Nobody looks for anyone at the Leaning Tower," says Wren. "Not even the guard goes near mad old Ash." They climb the North Wall at dusk toward the crooked tower, where one lamp burns in the round window.

### 7. Old Ash
- The Leaning Tower. Old Ash opens the door before they knock ("Hm. You took your time."). Cluttered room: maps, lamp-parts, teacups, a stuffed owl, books. **Cinder** the ember-fox — sneezes sparks at Tobin, steals Wren's biscuit, then curls up against Wren.
- The dreadful tea scene (comedy).
- Wren shows the lamp. Ash goes **very still**; his hands shake as he looks without touching. "Where did you find this?" "Thornbury House." "Hm. Of course it was."
- Ash tells — briefly, vividly — the story of **King Ardric's bargain**, the stolen fire, the cursed sun. Wren realises the pale sun she has always known **was once gold**; Tobin realises the colours his mother sang about were real.
- Ash watches the wall through the round window: more grey shapes. He says they can stay. End: Wren, unable to sleep, sees Ash out on the wall in the dark with his pole, relighting a guttered Ring-lamp, humming the same tune Wren's mother sang ("Morning comes…") — she freezes.

### 8. The Tale of the Lampwright
- Wren confronts Ash about the tune; he says it's an old Lamplighter song, "sung by more than one mother". (Seed; don't resolve.)
- That evening Ash tells the tale of **the Lampwright** — as a **shadow-play**: he hangs a sheet, sets the lamp behind it, and makes shadow-figures with his hands, his pole and cut-paper shapes; Cinder keeps walking in front of the light; Wren scoffs and interrupts; Tobin asks technical questions. The story still lands: the stranger with true fire, lighting lamps for free, the blind seeing, the grey flushing with colour, the Queen's hatred, Cinder Hill, the darkness taking him, the three days — and the Flame burning again on the mountain. (Keep it moving; the interruptions carry it.)
- Ash reveals: this is **the Lampwright's own lamp**, carrying the **Original Flame**. Lost for fifty years since the last Lamplighter family of Thornbury House died out.
- **The lamp's law**: "It cannot be bought, stolen or earned. Only given." Wren: "Nothing in the world is free. Everything costs." Ash: "Then you've never met him." Silence. Ash: "There's more to the story. But it is not mine to tell. Not yet."
- Tobin asks if the Lampwright could come back. Ash only says "Hm," and looks at the lamp for a long time.

### 9. The Captain in Grey  (mostly Tobin POV)
- Tobin goes home to keep up appearances (and to find out what his father is planning). A rider at the gate: **Captain Varrick**, grey armour, grey cloak, Hollow Knights behind him, "envoy of the Queen's peace". The city murmurs.
- From a **ceiling beam** above his father's study (Tobin has a secret crawlway), he overhears: Varrick offers the Warden protection from the Hollows — the Ring will hold, the Hollows will leave — in exchange for "a certain lamp", and "for a man of your quality, perhaps more: a seat at a larger table." Edric hesitates, then **agrees**. Varrick rubs the old scar on his palm.
- Tobin climbs down shaking. His father's betrayal. Should he tell Wren?
- At the Leaning Tower Ash sees Varrick riding along the wall below. Ash's face: grief. "Hm. So it's you they sent, lad." Wren and Tobin learn Varrick was once a Lamplighter — Ash's best student. "He fell," says Ash. "Bargains will do that."
- Tobin **tells Wren the truth** about his father. She expects him to choose his father; he doesn't. A small, real trust begins. Ash: they must go to the Bright Mountain — but for that he needs his old **Lamplighter's charter and map**, which the Guild confiscated when they expelled him. They're in the Guild vault.

### 10. Gutterlings and Glass
- Night heist at the Guild Hall: Wren (thief), Tobin (locks and clockwork), Cinder (nose and spark). Plan, comedy, tension. Tobin opens the vault's clockwork lock.
- In the vault: the map and charter — and **the Guild's ledgers**: the oil has been watered for years; the poor have been sold **Flame tokens** (stamped tin discs "blessed at the Beacon") that do nothing. Tobin is outraged; Wren is not surprised.
- **Gutterlings** raid the vault (they love the gilt) and nearly give them away; **Snib** squeaks "Shiny-shiny!" at Wren's buttons. Wren bribes him with a brass button; he squeaks a warning just before Prowle's night-watchmen arrive.
- Escape across the gilded roof, under the great glass dome (glass creaking; Tobin's fear of heights adds to fear of dark).
- Back at the tower with the map and a ledger. Ash: "Proof. Hm. The Guild will hate you now." Ending turn: from the round window, the Low Quarter's lamps begin, one after another, to **go out**.

### 11. The Long Dark of Soot Lane
- The Ring fails completely along the Low Quarter wall. Hollows drift into Soot Lane in the night. Wren runs to find Nettle; Tobin follows, terrified; Cinder with them; Ash stays to relight lamps on the wall.
- Horror in the dark streets: greyed people sitting still in doorways. Wren finds **Nettle greyed** — silent, grey, not knowing Wren's name. Devastating.
- Wren, angry, swings the lamp at the Hollows **like a weapon** — it **barely glows**; they press closer.
- Then, kneeling, she stops trying to fight and simply **holds the lamp out to Nettle** — "Here. It's for you. You don't have to do anything." It **blazes**; warmth and gold; Nettle's freckles come back, then her pink cheeks, then her voice: "Wren?" The Hollows flee the light.
- Wren half-understands the law ("Only given") and doesn't like it — it scares her more than the Hollows. As they shelter in Mrs Fenn's bakery, a lantern-procession of guardsmen comes up Soot Lane — led by **Gristle Mott**, pointing.

### 12. Taken  (Wren POV first half; Tobin POV second half)
- Mott has sold her for the reward. Sergeant Bracken (not unkind) arrests Wren; Tobin is dragged home by his father's men. At the same hour, Varrick's Knights **seize Old Ash** on the wall (Cinder escapes into the dark).
- In the Warden's Hall, Lord Edric demands the lamp. Wren, frightened, furious and sure everyone always takes everything in the end, **throws it at his feet**: "Take it, then! I never asked for it!" The lamp goes **cold and dark**. Edric has it locked in the **strongroom** (61 steps down, pitch black) to hand to Varrick at dawn; Wren is put in a cell. In the cell she weeps for the first time in the book and wishes she hadn't said it.
- Tobin POV: the only one who can reach the strongroom is him (he knows where the key-ring hangs). He goes **down into the dark alone**, night-lamp in his shaking hand — it gutters and goes out halfway. Total darkness. He freezes. And, for the first time in his life, he **prays** — not knowing quite to whom: "If you're there — whoever lit the first lamp — I can't do this. Please." He keeps going, counting steps.
- In the strongroom he finds the lamp, cold. He says to it: "It's not mine. I'm taking it back to her." It **warms** in his hands and lights the stair.
- He frees Wren by **picking the cell lock with his compass needle**. Wren takes the lamp back — "I didn't mean it" — and it blazes. Outside, bells: something is wrong in the city.

### 13. The Last Lamps of Emberwick
- Varrick, impatient, has let the Hollows loose: all **144 Ring-lamps fail at once**. Hollows flood every street. The **Queen's voice** rolls through the dark, sweet and reasonable: "Lie down. It is easier in the grey. Give me the lamp and I will let you sleep."
- Panic: families hiding, the Guild fleeing with its strongboxes, Prowle among them. Lord Edric, learning from Bracken that Varrick never meant to keep any bargain, locks himself in his Hall and does nothing (Tobin sees his father's face at a window — a terrible moment).
- Ash's voice in the Ring-pipes? No — keep simple: **Cinder** finds the children; she has a Gutterling with her — **Snib**, who leads them over the rooftops by Gutterling paths ("Buttons later? Buttons later!"), past Hollows on the streets below. Snib frees Ash from Varrick's makeshift cage at the Grey Gate? — Let Cinder and Snib bring news that Ash has escaped and is fighting his way toward Beacon Square.
- The Beacon: the only thing that can relight the whole Ring is the **Great Beacon**, whose flame-pipes reach all 144 lamps. Tobin works this out from the Orrery ("It's a network! If the source burns, the whole Ring burns!").
- They reach Beacon Square. **Varrick waits at the foot of the Beacon**, Hollow Knights around him, knowing she must come. Snib, offered a whole jar of buttons by Varrick, hesitates — and then helps the children anyway, **for nothing** ("Snib gives! Snib gives!"). They slip in through a coal-door. End: the 300 dark steps above them.

### 14. Given Away
- The climb: Wren goes up with the lamp; **Tobin and Cinder hold the stairs** against Hollows below; **Old Ash** arrives, battered, his pole in his hands, and stands with Tobin ("Hm. Up you go, child."). Tobin's courage in the dark, with Ash beside him.
- The top: a vast cold iron bowl of the Beacon, empty for years. Grey sky; the pale sun is down. **Varrick** is there before her (he came up the outer stair). He makes his **offer**: keep the lamp, leave the city by the Grey Gate tonight, live, never be poor or hungry again. "Why give it to people who called you a witch?" The **Queen's voice** adds the sweetest offer: she hums Wren's mother's lullaby, the missing words, and says "Come with me and I will sing you the rest."
- Wren's real fear: the Beacon is huge; the little flame will be **swallowed**; she will **lose the lamp** — her only possession, her way out — and be the hungry nobody again. Everything costs. She thinks of Nettle's grey face and Mrs Fenn on the step. "Only given."
- She chooses: she tips the lamp to the Beacon's wick-bed and **gives the Flame**. As she does, she feels **a warm hand on her shoulder**, steadying her (no one there). The Flame leaps from lamp to Beacon — a roar of gold — down the flame-pipes — and the Ring lights lamp by lamp around the whole city, a circle of fire. **The lamp is not weaker; it is brighter.** "What is given, grows."
- Hollows shriek and flee over the wall; colour runs back into the city like dawn on water. Varrick shields his eyes. Ash has come up the stair; he looks at Varrick and says, "Lad. You used to love this." For **one heartbeat** Varrick hesitates, his hand on his scarred palm — then he turns and goes down into the dark, and is gone. Wren's ribbon is blazing red.

### 15. Into the Greywaste
- Morning after: the pale sun rises over a city whose Ring burns gold. Greyed people — Mrs Fenn, Nettle (already restored), others — are restored as Flame-bearers? (No: in Book 1 the lamp restores them when Wren goes round with it all morning, freely, door to door. Keep Pentecost for Book 5.) Mrs Fenn hugs her children and remembers every name.
- Reckoning: Tobin hands the Guild ledger to Sergeant Bracken; Prowle is disgraced (fled, actually — he'll be back). Lord Edric is shaken and silent and cannot look at his son. Bracken shakes Wren's hand.
- Ash: the Queen will come back with more. The only hope lies at the **Bright Mountain**, where the Flame began. He means to go. The children choose to go with him.
- Ash **borrows Tobin's compass overnight** and gives it back in the morning: "Tuned. A compass ought to know the way home." Tobin writes his father a letter (short; he still loves him). Nettle gives Wren a heel of bread "for the road" — **the first gift Wren accepts without suspicion**, and she says thank you.
- Dawn: Wren, Tobin, Ash and Cinder walk out through the Grey Gate into the Greywaste, the lamp warm in Wren's hands, her ribbon red. Wren hums "Morning comes, morning comes" — and for the first time it doesn't feel like a song about something that will never happen.
- Last beat: far behind them on the grey plain, **a grey rider follows**. Final narrator line, warm and forward-looking.
