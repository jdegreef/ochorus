# THE BLACK STAIR — Book 2 bible & beat sheet
Book 2 of *The Lamplighters of Gloamhaven*. ~15 chapters, ~45,000–49,000 words. Ages 9–12.
**Read `OUTLINE.md` first** — it is the series canon (voice & style §1, world §2, the lamp's rules §3, the Book 1 cast §4, places §5, lore §6, seeds §7). Everything there still holds. This file adds Book 2.
Then read Book 1's last two chapters (`fire-against-the-dark.md`, "## Given Away" and "## Into the Greywaste") so you know exactly where everyone stands.

Theme (never quoted): Psalm 23:4 — though I walk through the valley of the shadow; and John 9 — the man born blind. Core truths: **courage is trusting while afraid**; **faith that does not need to see**; **light given, not earned** (the lamp's law, now tested by Wren *giving the lamp to someone else*).

## 1. Where Book 1 left them
- Dawn, days after the Ring was relit. Wren (with the lamp, red ribbon, a heel of Nettle's bread), Tobin (spectacles; his mother's compass, "tuned" by Ash, now points north-and-a-bit-east toward the Bright Mountain, a faint pale tooth on the horizon), Old Ash (long brass-hooked pole; his leg hurt in the battle — he walks stiffly), and Cinder walk out of the Grey Gate onto the Greywaste.
- A single grey rider follows, far off: **Captain Varrick**.
- Back in Emberwick: Nettle lives with Mrs Fenn at the bakery; Mott was restored and forgave Wren's debt; Prowle fled by the South Gate; Lord Edric is shaken and silent and could not look at Tobin; Tobin left him a letter.
- Wren still doesn't know the rest of her mother's lullaby "Morning comes, morning comes…". She is learning that gifts are real, and she still hates asking for help.

## 2. New cast (FIXED names)
**Mira** (about 12) — a Delver girl, blind from birth, quick-witted, brave, curious, funny in a dry way; "sees" by **echo-clicks** of her tongue and by touch, and is very good at it — never helpless. She believes the old Delver story of the Warm Man though she has never seen light. She is fascinated by Wren. Voice: precise, sensory ("Your voice is bright today", "I hear the room is long"), formal Delver phrasing softened by mischief. When the Ferryman heals her (Ch 12) her echo-sense stays; sight is a gift added, not a fix for something wrong in her.
**Speaker Gorrim** — head of the Delver Elders; proud, sceptical, protective; deep slow voice; believes "light" is a story to frighten children; condemns the company; humbled when the light saves his son.
**Tavi** (about 9) — Gorrim's son; lively; taken (greyed) by the Hollows in Ch 9; restored by the lamp in Ch 11.
**Mother Hessa** — the old Delver healer who tends Ash's leg; kind, brisk, hums.
**The Ferryman** — an old boatman on the Gloam River who asks no fee; kind eyes; a scar on his hand (readers should suspect the Lampwright; nobody says so; Ash smiles and says nothing).
**Captain Varrick** and his **Hollow Knights** follow the company and break into the Deeps (Ch 9).
**A Hollow courier** — a greyed rider carrying a satchel of letters (Ch 14).

## 3. New places & things
- **The Greywaste**: wide colourless plain of grey grass, dead black trees, dry stream-beds, cold wind; old Lamplighter **waystones** (stone posts with an empty lamp-niche) mark the road.
- **Mothwings**: huge moths with wings like grey stained glass that turn faintly colourful near flame; gentle but dangerous — a swarm smothers any light and can suffocate travellers. They come in "mothwing weather", a warm still dusk.
- **The Delver Deeps / Deepholm**: a vast cavern city under the Greywaste built for SOUND, not sight: carved echo-halls, singing-stones, water-music, rope bridges, terraced houses cut into stone. Totally dark. Delvers are pale, gentle, musical, superb stone-workers; they navigate by sound and touch; they have never seen light — they think it a fairy tale "to frighten children". They feel the lamp's warmth as a strange "warm-sickness".
- **The Silent Pit**: the Delvers' place of exile, a shaft where no sound returns.
- **The Whispering Galleries**: tunnels where every whisper carries for miles.
- **The Story-Wall**: a hidden chamber whose wall is carved, to be read by touch, with the tale of **the Warm Man** who once walked the Deeps and promised that one day the Delvers would see.
- **The Black Stair**: **a thousand steps** of total darkness spiralling from the Deeps up to the surface; the only way out.
- **The Gloam River**: wide, misty, slow, no bridge.
- **The singing-stone**: the Delvers' parting gift — a smooth stone that hums when danger is near.
- **Edric's letter**: in the courier's satchel, a letter from Lord Edric Harrowgate to the Umbral Queen, promising the lamp in exchange for "a throne over all the Rings".
- **Vaelport**: on the coast to the south-west, a city blazing with cold white light (glowstones — Book 3). Seen only from afar at the end.

## 4. The lamp in Book 2 (rules from OUTLINE §3 still hold)
- It still lights for Wren because it was given to her. At the Black Stair (Ch 10) Wren must **give it to Tobin** — truly give, not lend — so he can lead the Delvers up while she holds the bridge. It blazes for him. This is Wren's hardest act of trust. After, Tobin gives it back to her (freely) — and it is not one bit the less.
- Held out as a gift it restores the greyed (Tavi, Delvers).
- Mothwings are drawn to it: it must be hidden in the swarm (Ch 3).

## 5. Lampwright appearances (light touch, no naming)
- Ch 7: the Story-Wall's Warm Man (history).
- Ch 12: the Ferryman, who heals Mira and vanishes in the mist.
- Optional, one more glimpse: a warmth on the Black Stair (Tobin feels steadier, as if someone climbs just behind him) — do not overdo.

## 6. Chapter beat sheet (~3,000–3,300 words each)

### 1. The Pale Road
- The company crosses the Greywaste: grey grass, dead trees, the waystones with empty niches; Ash relights one waystone from the lamp — it blazes — "what is given, grows".
- Ash, limping, teaches the night-watch and the old **Lamplighters' Psalm** ("Though I walk through the gloaming, I will fear no dark, for the Flame is with me…" — invent it, never quote Scripture verbatim).
- Tobin hides how badly the dark frightens him at camp; Wren sleeps with one hand on the lamp, not yet trusting anyone.
- Cinder senses something following; once, far back, the grey rider on a rise.

### 2. The Hollow Hunt
- A pack of Hollows attacks at dusk in a dry stream-bed. Tobin freezes in fear; Ash steps in front of him and is hurt again (his bad leg; he cannot walk far).
- The lamp drives them off. Wren blames Tobin bitterly; they quarrel; Tobin, ashamed, lies awake.
- Ash, in pain, tells Tobin quietly that fear is not the same as cowardice.

### 3. Mothwing Weather
- A warm, still dusk: mothwing weather. A great swarm of mothwings sweeps over the plain, drawn to the lamp.
- They must hide the lamp's light — Wren buries it in her coat and they run blind; Cinder guides.
- The ground gives way: they fall through a sinkhole into the earth, down a long slope of scree.
- Absolute darkness underground; Ash hurt; then — in the dark — clicking sounds.

### 4. Down Among the Delvers
- Captured by a Delver patrol who move silently and "see" by clicks. The Delvers think the lamp's warmth is a sickness.
- Introduce **Mira**, set to guard the strangers; fascinated by Wren's voice and the warm thing; their sparky first conversation.
- Marched through tunnels to Deepholm; Tobin, in total dark, trusts Mira's hand on his sleeve.

### 5. The City of Echoes
- Deepholm, a city built for sound: echo-halls, singing-stones, water-music, rope bridges. The travellers (by lamplight — the Delvers can't see it) are astonished; the Delvers are astonished that strangers keep bumping into things.
- **Mother Hessa** tends Ash's leg. Tobin is fascinated by Delver engineering and forgets his fear.
- Delver customs; they believe light is a fairy tale. Wren is moved by how the Delvers share everything.

### 6. The Elders' Judgment
- Trial before the Elders, led by **Speaker Gorrim**. The lamp is called a trick; the claim of "light" a lie and a danger.
- Mira speaks in their defence and is silenced. Verdict: the strangers will be lowered into the **Silent Pit**.
- That night Wren — who never asks anyone for anything — asks Mira for help.

### 7. Mira's Secret
- Mira leads Wren to a hidden chamber: the **Story-Wall**, read by touch — the Warm Man who walked the Deeps and promised that one day the Delvers would see.
- Mira has believed it alone all her life. "I can't see your lamp. But I can feel that it's true."
- Wren is moved by faith that doesn't need sight; she lets Mira hold the lamp (it is warm to her — she was given it to hold).

### 8. The Whispering Galleries
- Mira frees the company and leads them through the Whispering Galleries, where every sound carries for miles; total silence, carrying the hurt Ash.
- Tobin's mapmaking and Mira's echo-sense together find the way; Wren learns to follow someone else's lead.
- Nearly out — the tunnels shake.

### 9. Varrick in the Deeps
- Captain Varrick and the Hollow Knights (and Hollows) break into Deepholm, hunting the lamp. Their touch silences the Delvers' songs; **Tavi** is greyed.
- The Delvers panic, trapped; the only way out is the **Black Stair**. The company could escape — Wren turns back.
- Varrick, polite as ever: "Give me the lamp, child, and I leave them their songs."

### 10. The Black Stair
- A thousand steps of total dark. Ash cannot climb; Wren must hold a narrow rope bridge against the Knights with Mira and Cinder.
- Wren **gives the lamp to Tobin** — really gives it — so he can lead the Delvers up. Her hardest moment.
- Tobin, shaking, sets off with hundreds of Delvers holding a rope behind him; the dark presses; he nearly turns back; he recites the Lamplighters' Psalm; step by step, carrying the light for others, he climbs. (Optional glimpse: a warmth just behind him on the stair.)

### 11. Light in the Low Places
- Tobin leads the Delvers upward; the lamp brightens with each step. Below, Wren, Mira and Cinder hold the bridge in the dark — Mira's echo-sense makes her the only one who can fight there.
- Tavi is carried up; at the top, in the lamp's light, given freely by Tobin, Tavi is restored; Gorrim weeps.
- Varrick, blocked when the Delvers cut the bridge (Mira's idea), retreats into the Deeps, swearing to finish it elsewhere. Everyone emerges onto the surface at night under a pale moon. Tobin gives the lamp back to Wren.

### 12. The Ferryman of the Gloam River
- The company (and the Delvers, who stay to rest) reach the wide Gloam River; no bridge.
- An old **Ferryman** offers to carry them across and asks no fee. Halfway over, he gently touches Mira's eyes.
- Mira **sees** for the first time — and the first thing she sees is the lamp.
- On the far bank, the ferryman and his boat are gone in the mist. Ash smiles and says nothing.

### 13. Colours
- Mira learns to see: faces, water, fire, the few colours left. Her joy makes Wren laugh — properly — for the first time in the series. Mira keeps her clicks and laughs at how slow eyes are in the dark.
- The Delvers, led by a humbled Gorrim, decide to journey toward the Rings and the light; Gorrim apologises and gives the company the **singing-stone**.
- Mira asks to join the quest; she is accepted.

### 14. A Letter Sealed in Grey
- The singing-stone hums; the company ambushes a greyed **Hollow courier** and finds a satchel of letters.
- One is from **Lord Edric Harrowgate** to the Umbral Queen, promising the lamp for a throne over all the Rings.
- Tobin is devastated — his father has not been weak, he has betrayed everything. Wren, awkwardly, comforts him (she sits beside him; she gives him the last of Nettle's bread).

### 15. The Lights of Vaelport
- Days later they reach the coast; on the horizon a city blazes with cold white light brighter than any Ring.
- Ash is uneasy: "That isn't Flame." Tobin wonders if a city that bright could help free his father. Mira is dazzled. Wren is suspicious.
- They walk toward the glittering gates of Vaelport. A forward-looking last line that makes a child want Book 3.

## 7. Seeds for later books (light touch)
- Varrick's palm scar and his hesitation when Ash speaks to him (Book 5 redemption): give him one moment of hesitation again in the Deeps when Ash calls him "lad".
- Mira's faith-without-sight (Book 4 Remnant ties to the Story-Wall).
- Edric's letter → Book 3's temptation for Tobin.
- Wren's lullaby: the Delvers have an old song with the same tune; Mira knows one more line of it — "…and the dark shall not keep us" — Wren hears it and keeps it (do not give the whole song).
