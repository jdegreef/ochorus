## Appendix A: Key Insights from the Whole Series

Every chapter of every volume closes with three key insights. Here they are all gathered by theme, each pointing back to its volume and chapter and to that chapter's key verse.

### The Father's Love

- **God’s glory is His goodness, and we see it most clearly in Jesus.** Vol. 1, ch. 4, *"Let Me See Your Glory"* (Exodus 33:18)
- **The best thing we can ask of God is God Himself.** Vol. 1, ch. 7, *One Thing I Ask* (Psalm 27:4)
- **No one has fallen too far to come home to God.** Vol. 1, ch. 8, *A Pure Heart* (Psalm 51:10)
- **Prayer begins with relationship, not requests.** Vol. 2, ch. 4, *Our Father in Heaven* (Matthew 6:9-10)
- **The secret room is a meeting, not a ritual.** Vol. 2, ch. 6, *The Secret Room* (Matthew 6:6)
- **We do not pray to wear God down.** Vol. 2, ch. 8, *Always Pray and Never Give Up* (Luke 18:1)
- **The Father Himself loves us.** Vol. 2, ch. 15, *Asking in His Name* (John 16:24)
- **We can die, and live, in trust.** Vol. 2, ch. 19, *Prayers from the Cross* (Luke 23:34)
- **God remembers every sincere prayer.** Vol. 3, ch. 7, *Prayers That Rose Before God* (Acts 10:4)
- **God is never too busy for His children.** Vol. 3, ch. 17, *Never Stop Praying* (1 Thessalonians 5:16-18)
- **We call God "Father" only because His Spirit gives us the right.** Vol. 4, ch. 8, *Why We Say "Our" Father* (Galatians 4:6)
- **God leads us into quiet places to speak tenderly to us, not to punish us.** Vol. 4, ch. 9, *Into the Desert to Pray* (Hosea 2:14)
- **We pray not to inform God but to become His close friends.** Vol. 4, ch. 14, *Prayer, the Light of the Soul* (Psalm 18:28)
- **We were made for God, and our hearts find rest only in Him.** Vol. 4, ch. 16, *"Our Heart Is Restless"* (Psalm 62:1)
- **Even the best minds must ask God to teach them how to seek Him.** Vol. 5, ch. 6, *Faith Seeking Your Face* (Psalm 27:7-8)
- **Our love for God is always an answer to His love for us.** Vol. 5, ch. 7, *Jesus, the Very Thought of You* (1 John 4:19)
- **God holds all things, even the smallest, because He loves them.** Vol. 5, ch. 13, *"All Shall Be Well"* (Romans 8:28)
- **Prayer is a friendship with Jesus, kept alive by frequent and honest conversation.** Vol. 6, ch. 6, *Prayer as Friendship* (John 15:15)
- **We can work carefully without working anxiously, because our Father holds our hand.** Vol. 6, ch. 9, *Prayer for Busy People* (Colossians 3:17)
- **Christ does not throw away bruised people; He bends down to mend them.** Vol. 6, ch. 13, *The Bruised Reed* (Isaiah 42:3)
- **God is a Father to orphans, and He loves to meet their needs through people who pray.** Vol. 7, ch. 3, *An Orphanage Built on Prayer* (Psalm 68:5)
- **Thirst for God is not a sign of failure but an invitation to come and drink.** Vol. 8, ch. 4, *My Soul Thirsts for You* (Psalm 63:1)
- **Anyone may come to God as a child comes to a father, without priest, journey or sacrifice.** Vol. 8, ch. 16, *A Bishop on His Knees* (Psalm 72:8)
- **The way into God’s kingdom is not climbing up but being carried, as a child is carried by her father.** Vol. 8, ch. 19, *The Little Way* (Matthew 18:3)
- **God pours out His Spirit on those whom the world overlooks, and He makes them messengers of His word.** Vol. 9, ch. 2, *Your Daughters Will Prophesy* (Joel 2:28)
- **Christ meets those who honestly cry out to Him, even those who once fought against Him.** Vol. 9, ch. 7, *Prayer Is Breathing* (Psalm 16:11)
- **The new birth turns set phrases into the prayer of a child speaking to a Father.** Vol. 9, ch. 12, *Prayer Bands Across Asia* (Acts 3:19-20)
- **Hearing the Shepherd’s voice begins with belonging to the Shepherd.** Vol. 10, ch. 11, *My Sheep Hear My Voice* (John 10:27)
- **God’s house is meant to be a house of prayer where every people and every outsider is welcome.** Vol. 10, ch. 12, *Fresh Wind, Fresh Fire* (Isaiah 56:7)
- **True prayer holds together awe before a great King and the closeness of a child with a Father.** Vol. 10, ch. 20, *Awe and Intimacy* (Psalm 63:3)
- **The Great God is a Father, and we come to Him through Jesus, not through a long road of our own making.** Vol. 11, ch. 2, *What the Witnesses Agree On* (Hebrews 11:6)
- **A fast kept for the Father’s eyes alone is a secret He delights to reward.** Vol. 11, ch. 4, *Prayer and Fasting* (Matthew 6:17-18)
- **Intercession for the world rests on the one Mediator who gave Himself for all.** Vol. 11, ch. 8, *For All People* (1 Timothy 2:3-4)

## Appendix D: Prayer Methods Toolkit

The witnesses in this series prayed in many ways, and some of their ways are simple enough for anyone to learn. Here are eighteen of them. None is a rule, and none makes God hear us: He hears us because of Jesus, not because of our method. Try one at a time, keep what helps, and follow the pointers to read the full stories in this series.

### Praying the Psalms

The Psalms are the prayer book God gave His people, and every age of the church has prayed them. David's laments teach us to bring God our real feelings (Volume 1, chapter 16). Benedict built his monks' day around the Psalms (Volume 5, chapter 1), Luther warmed his cold heart with his little book of psalms (Volume 6, chapter 2), and Bonhoeffer called them the prayer book of the Bible (Volume 9, chapter 14).

1. Choose one psalm. Psalms 23, 27, 51, 63, 103 and 139 are good places to begin.
2. Read it slowly, aloud if you can.
3. Stop at a line that speaks to you, and turn it into your own words to God.
4. Tomorrow, go on to the next psalm.

Do not skip the sad psalms: "How long, LORD?" (Psalm 13:1) is a prayer of faith.

### Praying the Lord's Prayer phrase by phrase

Jesus gave His disciples a short prayer as a pattern (Volume 2, chapters 4 and 5). The *Didache* told new believers to pray it three times a day (Volume 4, chapter 1), and Cyprian taught why we say "our" Father (Volume 4, chapter 8). Many believers pray it slowly, one line at a time, letting each line open into their own prayers.

1. "Our Father in heaven, may Your name be kept holy" (Matthew 6:9). Thank God that He is your Father, and praise Him.
2. "May Your kingdom come" (Matthew 6:10). Pray for His rule in your home, your church, your town and the nations.
3. "Give us today the bread we need" (Matthew 6:11). Bring Him today's needs, yours and others'.
4. "Forgive us what we owe You" (Matthew 6:12). Confess your sins, and forgive those who have wronged you.
5. "Do not lead us into testing, but rescue us from the evil one" (Matthew 6:13). Ask Him to protect you and those you love.
If you have only five minutes, pray one line well.

### Luther's four strands

When Peter the barber asked Martin Luther how to pray, Luther wrote him a little book, *A Simple Way to Pray* (Volume 6, chapter 2). Take one line of the Lord's Prayer, one commandment or one line of the Creed, he said, and weave it into a garland of four strands. It needs no library, only words of Scripture carried in the memory.

1. Choose one short line, such as "Give us today the bread we need."
2. **Instruction:** What is God teaching me here?
3. **Thanksgiving:** What can I thank Him for?
4. **Confession:** What sin does this show me?
5. **Prayer:** What shall I ask, for myself and for others?

### Lectio: reading Scripture slowly into prayer

*Lectio* is Latin for "reading." Benedict's monks shared their day between prayer, work and reading the Scriptures (Volume 5, chapter 1). George Müller found that this changed his mornings (Volume 8, chapter 5). Instead of beginning the day with wandering prayer, he began by reading the Bible slowly, verse by verse, to feed his soul, and after a few minutes his reading turned, almost by itself, into prayer.

1. Pray first: "Take the veil from my eyes, so that I may see the wonders in Your instruction" (Psalm 119:18).
2. Read a few verses only, slowly.
3. At each verse ask: What does this say about God? What does it say to me?
4. Let the verse lead you into thanks, confession, asking or prayer for others.
5. Carry one phrase through the day, and repeat it quietly to yourself (Psalm 1:2).

Read little, and meet God in it. Chapter 3 of this book, "Prayer and the Word," says more.

### The examen

Ignatius of Loyola taught a short daily prayer he called the General Examen (Volume 6, chapter 8). It is a walk back through the day with God, and he said that simple exercises like it suit even those who cannot read.

1. **Give thanks.** Name three good gifts from today, and thank God for each.
2. **Ask for light.** Pray, "God, look deep into me and know my heart" (Psalm 139:23).
3. **Look back.** Walk through the day. Where were you near to God? Where did you turn from Him in thought, word or deed?
4. **Ask forgiveness.** Confess what you have seen, and receive Christ's forgiveness.
5. **Begin again.** Ask His help for one thing tomorrow. End with the Lord's Prayer.

Thanks comes first: the examen is not a courtroom. Ten minutes before sleep are enough.

### The Jesus Prayer

Believers in the deserts of Egypt and Palestine joined two cries from the Gospels, the blind man's "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!" (Luke 18:38) and the tax collector's "God, be merciful to me, the sinner!" (Luke 18:13). From them came the Jesus Prayer (Volume 4, chapter 18). It became the heart of Orthodox prayer, as we saw with the monks of Mount Athos (Volume 5, chapter 12) and a Russian pilgrim (Volume 8, chapter 6).

1. Sit quietly and pray gently: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner."
2. Some pray it with their breathing, or with a prayer rope, a cord of knots.
3. When your mind wanders, come back without scolding yourself.
4. Through the day, pray it again whenever you remember.

There is no magic in it. It is a beggar's cry to a Savior who stops for beggars.

### Arrow prayers

An arrow prayer is a short prayer sent up silently in the middle of life. When the king asked Nehemiah what he wanted, "I prayed to the God of heaven" (Nehemiah 2:4), and then he answered (Volume 1, chapter 15). In Volume 4, chapter 11, John Cassian learned from the desert teachers to pray one verse all day: "God, come and rescue me! LORD, be quick to help me!" (Psalm 70:1). On Iona and in the Scottish Highlands, believers blessed their milk, their fires and their journeys with short prayers (Volume 5, chapter 4).

1. Learn one short verse by heart, such as Psalm 70:1.
2. Pray it in the moment: before a hard conversation, in traffic, when fear or temptation comes, and when joy comes too.
3. Bless ordinary tasks as you begin them: "Lord, bless this meal, this work, this journey."

Arrow prayers fly far from a life of longer prayer, as Nehemiah's did.

### Set hours of prayer

David prayed "at evening, at morning and at noon" (Psalm 55:17), and Daniel knelt three times a day (Volume 1, chapter 14). In Volume 3, chapter 3, Peter and John went to the temple "for the afternoon prayer at three o'clock" (Acts 3:1). The *Didache* told new believers to pray the Lord's Prayer three times a day (Volume 4, chapter 1), and Benedict's monks stopped their work to pray seven times a day (Volume 5, chapter 1).

1. Choose two or three times: morning, midday and evening is the oldest pattern.
2. Tie each to something you already do, such as waking, eating or going to bed.
3. Keep each time short: a psalm, the Lord's Prayer, a few words of your own.
4. If you miss one, do not give up. Pray at the next.

The hours do not wall prayer in; they turn the whole day toward God.

### Dawn prayer

Jesus rose "long before daylight" to pray (Mark 1:35), and the psalmist said, "LORD, at daybreak You hear my voice" (Psalm 5:3). In Pyongyang, the elder Kil Sun-ju opened the church before dawn to everyone who wanted to pray, and Korean churches around the world still meet before sunrise (Volume 9, chapter 5).

1. Go to bed a little earlier, and rise before your household or work begins.
2. Sing or read a psalm, read a few verses, and pray for the day ahead.
3. If you can, meet with other believers at dawn, at church or in a home.

Dawn prayer is a gift, not a test. If illness, night work or small children make it impossible, God hears you at every hour.

### Praying aloud together

In January 1907, in Pyongyang, more than a thousand men prayed aloud at the same time, each in his own words (Volume 9, chapter 4). Koreans call this *tongsŏng kido*. Believers at Mukti in India (Volume 9, chapter 2) and in Africa's night vigils (Volume 10, chapter 17) have prayed in the same way.

1. Agree on what to pray for: one need, one person or your town.
2. At the leader's word, everyone prays aloud at once, each in his or her own words and language.
3. After some minutes the leader closes with a short prayer, and all say "Amen."

This helps shy people, because no one listens to them alone.

### A concert of prayer

In the 1700s a "concert" meant an agreement. In 1744 Scottish ministers invited believers to pray at the same set times for the spread of the gospel, and in 1747 Jonathan Edwards urged Americans to join them in *A Humble Attempt* (Volume 7, chapter 9). In 1784 English Baptists read Edwards's book and set apart the first Monday evening of every month for prayer (Volume 7, chapter 16).

1. Find believers, near or far, who will pray with you.
2. Agree on a time, such as the first Monday of each month.
3. Agree on what to ask: the spread of the gospel, revival, one country.
4. At that time, pray wherever you are, alone or together.

A sick person or a believer in a hostile land can join without leaving home.

### ACTS

ACTS is a modern teaching aid, not a method of the old witnesses. Its letters stand for Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving and Supplication (asking). But each part is old. Müller found his Bible reading led him "to confession, or to thanksgiving, or to intercession, or to supplication" (Volume 8, chapter 5), and Paul urged "requests, prayers, intercessions and thanks" for everyone (1 Timothy 2:1).

1. **Adoration.** Praise God for who He is: holy, faithful, loving, all-powerful.
2. **Confession.** Tell Him your sins honestly, and trust His forgiveness in Christ.
3. **Thanksgiving.** Thank Him for specific gifts.
4. **Supplication.** Ask for your needs and the needs of others.

It is a help, not a law: some days you will begin with your tears.

### Praying by name

At Mukti in India, Pandita Ramabai's household prayed for more than twenty-nine thousand people by name (Volume 9, chapter 2). Lancelot Andrewes kept a notebook of prayers for each day of the week, with lists of people to pray for (Volume 6, chapter 11).

1. Write down names: family, friends, neighbors, church leaders, people who do not yet know Christ.
2. Spread them over the days of the week, a few names for each day.
3. You can write each name on a small card, with one or two needs.
4. Pray for each person by name, with one clear request.
5. Note answers when they come, and add names as God brings them to mind.

If you cannot write, choose seven people and remember one on each day of the week.

### Prayer walking

Prayer walking means praying for a place as you walk through it. God told His exiles, "Seek the peace of the city ... and pray to the LORD for it" (Jeremiah 29:7). Believers in Argentina prayed for their cities street by street, and teams walked quietly through distant cities during the 10/40 Window prayer movement (Volume 10, chapters 13 and 14). Charles Finney once urged believers in Bolton to go two by two and pray in every house that would let them (Volume 10, chapter 14).

1. Choose a route: your street, the road to work, the area around your church.
2. Walk slowly, praying silently or quietly, eyes open.
3. As you pass each home or shop, pray, "Peace be on this household" (Luke 10:5).
4. If a neighbor greets you, you may ask, "How can I pray for you?"

Pray to bless, not to be seen. Where Christians are a watched minority, pray silently and draw no attention.

### Fasting with prayer

Fasting means going without food, or something else, for a time, to give that time to God. Nehemiah fasted and prayed when he heard the news from Jerusalem (Volume 1, chapter 15). In Volume 3, chapter 9, the church at Antioch was "serving the Lord in worship and fasting" when the Spirit sent out Barnabas and Saul (Acts 13:2). The *Didache* set Wednesdays and Fridays as fast days (Volume 4, chapter 1), and in our own time Bill Bright and Derek Prince called many to fast (Volume 10, chapter 10). Chapter 4 of this book, "Prayer and Fasting," gathers what the witnesses teach.

1. Begin small: skip one meal and give that time to prayer.
2. Keep it quiet, "so that no one but your Father, who is there in secret, will know" (Matthew 6:18).
3. Let each hunger pang remind you of your request.
4. Share what you saved with the hungry, as Isaiah 58 teaches.

**Health first.** Some should not go without food at all: pregnant or nursing mothers, children, people with diabetes, heart or kidney disease, people who have struggled with eating disorders, and people on certain medicines. If you are not sure, ask a doctor or health worker. You can fast from something else instead, such as your phone or a favorite food.

### Praying through the night

Jesus "stayed up all night in prayer to God" before choosing His apostles (Luke 6:12). The psalmist wrote, "In the middle of the night I get up to thank You" (Psalm 119:62), and Lancelot Andrewes counted midnight among the times of prayer (Volume 6, chapter 11). The Moravians at Herrnhut kept a watch of prayer one hour at a time, day and night (Volume 7, chapter 4), and believers across Africa gather for all-night vigils (Volume 10, chapter 17).

1. Do not begin with a whole night alone. Join others, or take one hour, as the Moravians did.
2. Plan the time: songs, Scripture, confession, thanks and a list of needs.
3. Change your posture: sit, stand, kneel, walk. Take short breaks.
4. If you wake in the night, pray for a few minutes instead of worrying (Psalm 63:6).

**With care.** A vigil does not earn God's answer, and those who cannot come are not second-class believers. God "gives sleep to the one He loves" (Psalm 127:2). The sick, the old, parents of small children and those with heavy work next day should guard their rest.

### A family prayer time

Moses told parents to speak of God's words "as you sit in your house and as you go on your way, as you lie down and as you rise" (Deuteronomy 6:7). Macrina made her family home a house of prayer (Volume 4, chapter 12), and Susanna Wesley, in a crowded house, kept her times with God and gave each child an evening alone with her (Volume 7, chapter 6). Chapter 10 of this book, "The Family Altar," says more.

1. Choose a time that suits your home, such as after the evening meal.
2. Keep it short: ten minutes, and less for small children.
3. Read a few verses or a Bible story, and welcome the children's questions.
4. Let each person name one thing to thank God for and one thing to ask.
5. Let everyone pray, even the youngest. End with the Lord's Prayer or a song.

If you are the only believer at home, pray for your family by name.

### Praying for the persecuted church

"Keep in mind those in prison, as if you were in chains alongside them" (Hebrews 13:3). In Volume 3, chapter 8, while Peter was in prison, "the church was praying to God for him without letup" (Acts 12:5). In our own time we met Helen Berhane in her shipping container in Eritrea (Volume 10, chapter 18), the house churches of China (Volume 10, chapter 9) and Iran (Volume 10, chapter 19), and Brother Andrew, who carried Bibles across closed borders (Volume 10, chapter 3).

1. Choose one country where Christians suffer for their faith, and pray for its believers daily.
2. Pray for their needs: food, health and safety for prisoners and their families.
3. Pray for their faith: courage, joy and the comfort of the Scriptures.
4. Pray for those who persecute them, as Clement prayed for the rulers of Rome (Volume 4, chapter 2).

Protect those you pray for. Never share the names, places or meeting details of believers in danger, even in a prayer request or online.
