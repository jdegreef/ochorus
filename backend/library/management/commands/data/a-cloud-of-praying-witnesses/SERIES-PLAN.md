# A Cloud of Praying Witnesses — series plan

*Draft for the founder's approval. Nothing here is written prose yet; this is the
map the volumes will be written from.*

> "Since we are surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses…" (Hebrews 12:1)
> "…golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints." (Revelation 5:8)

## Decisions (founder, 2026-10-28)

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Series name | **A Cloud of Praying Witnesses** |
| Shape | Series of 11 volumes, chronological, each standing on its own |
| Breadth | Whole Church: Orthodox, Catholic, Protestant, Pentecostal; gentle notes where traditions differ |
| Old Testament prelude | Yes |
| Byline | Ochorus (author `ochorus-originals`, as every house Original) |

| Modern era | Named people are fine, living ones included |

### One technical note on numbering

Ochorus counts series volumes from **1** (a database rule; the cover draws the
numeral). So the Old Testament prelude is **Volume 1**, and Jesus is Volume 2.
The prelude stays a true prelude in feel: its title and introduction say so.

## Shape of every volume

- **Introduction: "Where we are in the story"** (1 page): the era, the map, what to watch for.
- **15–20 chapters**, about 1,000–1,400 words each (a 6–9 minute read).
- **Conclusion: "Passing the torch"**: what this era hands to the next volume.
- **Appendix A: Key Insights**, grouped by theme, each pointing to chapter and person.
- **Appendix B: Scripture Index**, in Bible order, each verse pointing to chapters and people.

Both appendices are generated by script from each chapter's data record (person,
era, region, anchor verse, other verses, insights tagged by theme), so they never
drift from the text and rebuild in every language.

## Shape of every chapter

1. **Anchor scripture**: one OWB verse, quoted in full
2. **The scene**: a story moment
3. **Who they were**: era, place, why it matters
4. **How they prayed**: their own words where we may quote them (verbatim from
   the Ochorus library where the book is there)
5. **Three key insights**
6. **For us today**
7. **Try this**: one prayer practice for the week
8. **A closing prayer**
9. **Questions for reflection** (2–3)
10. **Read more on Ochorus**: their books, sermons and author page

★ below = already has an Ochorus author page, so the chapter can link straight to
their own words.

Where a famous story or saying is uncertain, the chapter says so ("the story is
told…"). Known cases are flagged ⚑ below.

## ⚑ Stories to handle honestly

- **Francis of Assisi**: "Make me an instrument of Your peace" is not his. It first
  appears in 1912. Use the *Canticle of the Creatures* and the San Damiano prayer instead.
- **Luther**: "So much to do that I shall spend the first three hours in prayer" is
  attributed, not verified. Tell it as a saying, and lean on *A Simple Way to Pray* (1535).
- **Knox**: "Give me Scotland or I die" is tradition. Tell it as such.
- **M'Cheyne**: "What a man is on his knees before God…" is widely attributed.
  Check the *Memoir* (Bonar) before quoting it as his.
- **Daniel Nash**: most of what we know comes through Finney's circle. Keep it modest.
- **John Hyde**: the "souls a day" accounts come from devotional memoirs. Flag them as such.

## Copyright care

- **Before 1929**: quote freely. Use the Ochorus library text verbatim where we hold the book.
- **Later voices** (Bonhoeffer in English, Tozer, Corrie ten Boom, Watchman Nee,
  Elisabeth Elliot, Keller, Cymbala, Greig and others): brief, attributed quotations
  only. Their stories are told in Ochorus's own words.

## Writing order

1. **Volume 2** (Jesus): sets the voice and the chapter template.
2. **Volume 8** (the Great Century): the strongest stories, to showcase the series.
3. Then the rest. **Volume 11** comes last, because its appendices index all the others.
