## Very Early in the Morning

> "Long before daylight, He got up and went out. He went off to a lonely place and spent time there in prayer." (Mark 1:35)

It had been an extraordinary day.

It began on the Sabbath in the synagogue at Capernaum, a fishing town on the shore of the Sea of Galilee. Jesus stood up to teach, and the people were amazed, because He taught like someone with authority. In the middle of the service a man with an evil spirit cried out, and Jesus set him free with a word. From the synagogue He walked to the house of Simon Peter, where Peter's mother-in-law lay in bed with a fever. He took her by the hand and lifted her up, and the fever left her.

By evening the news had reached every house in town. Mark tells us what happened when the sun went down and the Sabbath ended:

> "When evening came and the sun had set, people began bringing to Him everyone who was ill or controlled by demons. The entire town had crowded together outside the door." (Mark 1:32-33)

Picture that doorway. Parents carrying sick children. Old men leaning on their sons. People who had been in pain for years, pushing forward in the dark. Jesus healed many of them, one by one, late into the night.

Then, a few hours later, while the town was still asleep, He got up. He did not wait for the light. He walked out past the quiet houses, out beyond the edge of town, to a place where no one was. And there He prayed.

When Peter and the others woke up, the crowd was already gathering again. They searched for Him, found Him, and said what every busy person has heard a thousand times: "Everybody is out looking for You!" (Mark 1:37).

His answer surprised them. He did not go back to the house in Capernaum. He said, "Let us move on to the market towns nearby, so that I can proclaim the message there as well. This is what I came out to do" (Mark 1:38).

### The Son who prayed

We are beginning a journey through two thousand years of prayer. We will meet martyrs and monks, reformers and revivalists, mothers and missionaries, from every part of the world. But we begin with Jesus, because every one of them learned to pray by looking at Him.

That is the first thing that should stop us. Jesus is the eternal Son of God. He was there before the world began. He healed the sick and cast out demons with a word. And yet He prayed. He did not pray as a show for His disciples, or only when He was in trouble. He prayed because He lived His whole life in dependence on His Father. He once said, "The Son cannot do anything on His own; He does only what He sees the Father doing" (John 5:19). Prayer was where He saw what the Father was doing.

Mark 1:35 is not a one-time event. Luke, describing the same months, uses a word that tells us it was His habit:

> "But word about Jesus went out farther than ever. Large crowds kept gathering to listen and to be healed of their illnesses. Yet He kept slipping away to places in the wilderness to pray." (Luke 5:15-16)

Look at that little word "yet." The crowds grew larger; yet He kept slipping away. The more people needed Him, the more He prayed. Most of us do the opposite. When life gets busy, prayer is the first thing we cut.

### How He prayed

The Gospels never give us a timetable of Jesus' prayer life, but they show us a pattern.

He prayed **alone**. Mark says He went to "a lonely place." Matthew tells us that after feeding the five thousand "He climbed up into the hills by Himself to pray" (Matthew 14:23).

He prayed **early**, and sometimes **all night**. Before He chose the twelve apostles, "He stayed up all night in prayer to God" (Luke 6:12).

He prayed at **turning points**: at His baptism (Luke 3:21), before choosing His disciples, before the cross. The great decisions of His life were made on His knees.

And He prayed **when He was tired**. That is what makes Mark 1:35 so striking. He had every reason to sleep in. He had worked late. No one would have blamed Him. But He knew where His strength came from.

Andrew Murray, the South African pastor who wrote some of the most loved books on prayer ever published, looked at this verse and saw what Jesus knew:

> "He knew how the holiest service, preaching and healing, can exhaust the spirit; how too much intercourse with men could cloud the fellowship with God; how time, time, full time, is needed if the spirit is to rest and root in Him; how no pressure of duty among men can free from the absolute need of much prayer."
> Andrew Murray, *The Ministry of Intercession*

"Time, time, full time." Murray does not mean that we earn God's love by the hour. He means that friendship takes time. Roots grow slowly, out of sight. Jesus gave His Father unhurried time, and He came back from that lonely place knowing exactly what He had come to do.

### Three key insights

1. **If Jesus needed to pray, so do we.** Prayer is not only for the weak, the anxious or the extra-spiritual. It is how the Son of God Himself lived. If He depended on His Father, we can stop pretending that we can manage without Him.
2. **Busyness is a reason to pray, not an excuse not to.** The busier Jesus became, the more He slipped away. Prayer was not something He fitted in after the work. It was where the work began.
3. **Prayer gives direction.** Everyone in Capernaum had a plan for Jesus' day. Alone with His Father, He remembered His own: "This is what I came out to do." In prayer we stop being driven by the crowd's needs and start being led by God.

### For us today

Perhaps you are thinking, "But I don't have a lonely place." Many readers of this book live in small homes full of family. Some share a room with brothers and sisters or with other workers. Some live in cities where it is never quiet. Jesus did not have a private room either. He had a hillside before dawn. Your lonely place may be a corner of the kitchen, a bench on your way to work, a walk around the field, or a few minutes in a parked car or on a crowded bus with your eyes closed. In a later volume we will meet a mother of many children who prayed with her apron pulled over her head, and her children knew not to disturb her. The place matters less than the meeting.

Perhaps you are thinking, "But I can't pray early." You may work at night, or care for a newborn baby, or rise at four to reach the fields. The point of Mark 1:35 is not the clock. It is that Jesus gave His Father the *first* of His day, before the demands came knocking. Find your "first," whenever it is, and give it to God before you give it to anyone else, including your phone.

The psalmist knew this rhythm long before Jesus was born:

> "LORD, at daybreak You hear my voice. At daybreak I set everything out before You and keep watch." (Psalm 5:3)

Notice the two parts: setting everything out before God, and then keeping watch. Prayer is not only speaking. It is also waiting, watching and listening. The prophet Isaiah, speaking of the Servant of the Lord, said, "Each morning He rouses me; He rouses my ear to listen as a learner listens" (Isaiah 50:4). Christians have always heard Jesus in those words. Every morning, the Teacher became the learner, and listened.

Two thousand years later, E. M. Bounds, an American Methodist pastor who rose at four every morning to pray, put it plainly:

> "The men who have done the most for God in this world have been early on their knees."
> E. M. Bounds, *Power Through Prayer*

You do not need to become famous to follow that pattern. You only need to show up.

### Try this

For the next seven days, before you look at your phone or hear the news, take ten minutes with God. Go to your "lonely place," wherever it is.

- **Be still.** Read Mark 1:35 slowly. Picture Jesus walking out in the dark to meet His Father.
- **Set everything out.** Like Psalm 5:3, name the things your day holds, one by one: the people you will see, the work you will do, the worries you are carrying. Place each one before God.
- **Listen.** Ask, "Father, what have I come out to do today?" Sit quietly for a minute or two. You may not hear anything special. That is fine. You are learning to listen, as Jesus did.

At the end of the week, notice what changed.

### A closing prayer

Lord Jesus, You rose while it was still dark to be alone with Your Father. You were tired, and the whole town needed You, and still You prayed. Teach me to love Your Father as You do. Give me a place to meet Him and the will to keep the appointment. When my day is crowded, draw me away to pray. When I don't know what to do, show me what I have come out to do. And as I learn from You, make me a person of prayer. Amen.

### Questions for reflection

- What usually fills the first minutes of your day? What would it cost you to give them to God?
- Jesus prayed more as the crowds grew larger. Why do you think we tend to do the opposite?
- Where is your "lonely place"? If you don't have one yet, where could it be?
