# Tell Me the Story – Book 8: To the Ends of the Earth

*New Testament Stories from Acts 13 to Revelation, for children aged 6 to 10*

Scripture quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible (BSB), public domain.

---

## Introduction: A Light for Every Nation

> "I have made you a light for the Gentiles, to bring salvation to the ends of the earth." — Acts 13:47

At the end of the last book, the church in Antioch sent Barnabas and Saul off on a ship, to tell people far away about Jesus. On the journey, Luke starts calling Saul by his other name, Paul. And Paul is the name you'll hear all through this book.

Paul and his friends travelled thousands of miles. They walked along dusty Roman roads and sailed across stormy seas. They went to big cities full of temples to false gods, and to little towns where nobody had ever heard of Jesus.

It wasn't easy. Paul was laughed at, chased out of towns, thrown into prison, and shipwrecked. But wherever he went, people believed in Jesus, and new churches began.

You will meet a woman who sold purple cloth, a prison guard woken by an earthquake, a boy who fell asleep in a window, and Paul's own nephew, who saved his life. You will be on board a ship in a terrible storm. And you will read some of the letters Paul wrote to his friends, letters that are now part of the Bible.

At the very end of this book, the last book of Tell Me the Story, you will see what the Bible says about the very end of the whole story: what God is going to do one day for the whole world.

Do you remember how the story began, in a garden, with God making everything good? It ends somewhere even better.

*Dear God, thank you for Paul and all the people who carried the good news about Jesus so far. As we read these last stories, help us to see how the whole Bible fits together, and how much you love the whole world. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Who is Paul?
  A: He is Saul, who once hurt Christians and then met Jesus on the road to Damascus. On his journeys, Luke calls him by his other name, Paul.
- Q: Where did God send Paul and his friends?
  A: To many cities and countries, far across land and sea, to tell people who had never heard about Jesus.
- Q: The Bible's story began in a garden. Where do you think it ends?
  A: Let your child guess. You'll find out in the last story of this book!

## Mistaken for Gods

> "We too are only men, human like you. We are bringing you good news." — Acts 14:15

Paul and Barnabas travelled from town to town, telling people about Jesus. One day they came to a town called Lystra.

In Lystra there was a man who had never walked in his whole life. He had been born that way. He sat listening to Paul speak about Jesus. Paul looked straight at him, and saw that he had faith to be healed.

"Stand up on your feet!" Paul called out in a loud voice.

And the man jumped up and began to walk!

When the crowd saw what had happened, they got very excited. They started shouting in their own language: "The gods have come down to us in human form!"

They thought Barnabas was Zeus, the king of their gods. And because Paul did most of the talking, they thought he was Hermes, the messenger of the gods.

The priest of Zeus, whose temple was just outside the town, brought bulls and garlands of flowers to the town gates. He and the crowd wanted to offer sacrifices to Paul and Barnabas!

When Paul and Barnabas realised what was happening, they were horrified. They tore their clothes and rushed into the crowd, shouting:

"Friends, why are you doing this? We are only human, just like you! We have come to bring you good news: that you should turn away from these useless things and turn to the living God, who made heaven and earth and the sea and everything in them."

Even so, they could hardly stop the crowd from offering sacrifices to them.

Then some enemies of Paul arrived from other towns, and they turned the crowd against him. The same people who had wanted to worship Paul now threw stones at him and dragged him out of the town. They thought he was dead.

But when the believers gathered around him, Paul got up and walked back into the town! And the next day he and Barnabas went on to the next town, to keep telling people about Jesus.

Paul could have let people treat him like a god. Lots of people would like to be admired and praised. But Paul knew that all the glory belongs to God, not to him. He pointed people away from himself, to the living God.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 14:8–20

*Dear God, all the glory belongs to you. When people praise me, help me to point them to you. Thank you that you are the living God who made everything. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why did the people of Lystra think Paul and Barnabas were gods?
  A: Because Paul healed a man who had never walked, and they thought only gods could do something like that.
- Q: What did Paul and Barnabas do when the people wanted to worship them?
  A: They tore their clothes, rushed into the crowd and told them they were only human, and that they should worship the living God who made everything.
- Q: Paul and Barnabas didn't want praise for themselves. How can we give God the glory when we do something well?
  A: By thanking God for our gifts, being humble, and remembering that everything good we have comes from Him.

## Lydia by the River

> "The Lord opened her heart to respond to Paul's message." — Acts 16:14

Paul kept travelling. Now a young man called Timothy was with him, and a friend called Silas, and a doctor called Luke, who wrote the book of Acts.

One night Paul had a vision. A man from Macedonia, across the sea in Europe, was standing and begging him, "Come over to Macedonia and help us!"

So they got ready straight away and sailed across the sea. They came to a city called Philippi.

On the Sabbath day, they went outside the city gate, down to the river, where they thought there might be a place where people gathered to pray. They found some women there, and they sat down and began to talk with them.

One of the women was called Lydia. She came from a city called Thyatira, and she was a businesswoman. She sold purple cloth. Purple cloth was very expensive, because the dye was hard to make, and only rich and important people could afford it.

Lydia already worshipped God. And as she listened to Paul, the Lord opened her heart, and she believed what Paul said about Jesus.

Lydia and everyone in her household were baptised, right there by the river.

Then Lydia said to Paul and his friends, "If you agree that I truly believe in the Lord, come and stay at my house." She kept on asking until they said yes.

So Paul, Silas, Timothy and Luke went to stay in Lydia's home. And her house became the meeting place for one of the very first churches in Europe.

Notice who opened Lydia's heart. It was the Lord. Paul told her about Jesus, but it was God who helped her to understand and believe. That's how it always is. We can tell people about Jesus, but it's God who opens their hearts.

And as soon as Lydia believed, she wanted to share what she had. She opened her home. When God opens our hearts, we want to open our hands and our homes to others too.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 16:6–15

*Dear God, thank you for opening hearts to believe in Jesus. Please open the hearts of my friends and family who don't know you yet. And help me to share what I have, like Lydia did. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Where did Paul meet Lydia, and what did she sell?
  A: By the river outside the city of Philippi, where people gathered to pray. She sold expensive purple cloth.
- Q: Who opened Lydia's heart to believe?
  A: The Lord. Paul told her about Jesus, but God helped her to understand and believe.
- Q: Lydia opened her home to Paul and his friends. How could your family welcome or share with others?
  A: By inviting someone for a meal, having friends over, sharing food, or welcoming someone who is new. Talk about an idea together.

## Singing at Midnight

> "About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the other prisoners were listening to them." — Acts 16:25

In Philippi, some men got Paul and Silas into trouble. They dragged them in front of the city leaders and accused them of stirring up the city. A crowd joined in, and the leaders ordered that Paul and Silas be beaten. Then they were thrown into prison.

The jailer put them in the innermost cell, the darkest and most secure part of the prison, and locked their feet into wooden stocks so they couldn't move.

Paul and Silas were hurting. They were sore from the beating. They were locked in the dark. They had done nothing wrong.

And what were they doing at midnight?

They were praying and singing hymns to God! And all the other prisoners were listening.

Suddenly there was a great earthquake! The whole prison shook, right down to its foundations. At once all the prison doors flew open, and everybody's chains came loose!

The jailer woke up. When he saw the prison doors standing open, he thought all the prisoners had escaped. He knew he would be punished terribly. In his fear, he was about to kill himself with his sword.

But Paul shouted, "Don't hurt yourself! We are all here!"

Nobody had run away!

The jailer called for lights and rushed in. He fell down, trembling, in front of Paul and Silas. Then he brought them out and asked, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?"

"Believe in the Lord Jesus," they said, "and you will be saved, you and your household."

Then they told the jailer and everyone in his house all about Jesus.

That very hour of the night, the jailer washed the wounds on Paul and Silas's backs. And straight away, he and his whole family were baptised. Then he took them into his house and gave them a meal. He and all his family were filled with joy, because they had come to believe in God.

Paul and Silas could have complained in that prison. They could have been angry and bitter. Instead they sang to God. And because they did, a jailer and his whole family came to know Jesus.

When things are hard, we can still praise God. And sometimes it's in the hardest places that other people are listening most closely.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 16:16–34

*Dear God, help me to praise you even when things are hard. Thank you that anyone can be saved by believing in Jesus. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What were Paul and Silas doing in prison at midnight?
  A: Praying and singing hymns to God, while the other prisoners listened.
- Q: What did the jailer ask Paul and Silas, and what did they answer?
  A: He asked, "What must I do to be saved?" They answered, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household."
- Q: Why do you think the prisoners didn't run away when the doors opened?
  A: The Bible doesn't say. Perhaps they were amazed at Paul and Silas and wanted to hear more about their God. Because they stayed, the jailer was saved instead of punished.

## To an Unknown God

> "For in Him we live and move and have our being." — Acts 17:28

Paul travelled on to the famous city of Athens, in Greece. Athens was full of clever people: thinkers and teachers who loved to discuss new ideas.

But as Paul walked around the city, he was very upset. Everywhere he looked, there were idols: statues of all kinds of gods and goddesses. The whole city was full of them.

So Paul talked with people in the synagogue, and in the marketplace every day, with anyone who happened to be there. Some of the clever thinkers heard him. "What is this babbler trying to say?" some of them said. "He seems to be talking about foreign gods." Because Paul was telling them about Jesus and the resurrection.

So they took Paul to a meeting of the important council of Athens, called the Areopagus. "Tell us about this new teaching," they said. The people of Athens loved nothing better than talking about the latest ideas.

Paul stood up in front of them all.

"People of Athens," he said, "I can see that you are very religious. As I walked around your city and looked at the things you worship, I even found an altar with this written on it: TO AN UNKNOWN GOD. Well, the God you worship without knowing Him, I am going to tell you about.

"He is the God who made the world and everything in it. He is the Lord of heaven and earth. He doesn't live in temples built by people. And He doesn't need us to look after Him, as if He needed anything. He is the one who gives everyone life and breath and everything else.

"God did all this so that people would look for Him, and reach out for Him, and find Him. And He is not far from any one of us. For in Him we live and move and exist. As some of your own poets have said, 'We are His children.'

"Now God commands everyone everywhere to turn back to Him. For He has set a day when He will judge the world fairly, by the Man He has chosen. And He has proved this to everyone by raising Him from the dead."

When they heard about someone rising from the dead, some of the people laughed at Paul. But others said, "We want to hear more about this." And a few people believed, including a member of the council called Dionysius, and a woman called Damaris.

Paul started where the people were. He talked about their altar and their own poets. And then he led them to the true God, who isn't unknown at all. He made us, He gives us every breath, and He is never far away.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 17:16–34

*Dear God, thank you that you are not unknown, and that you are not far away from any of us. Thank you for every breath you give me. Help me to know you better every day. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did Paul find written on an altar in Athens?
  A: "TO AN UNKNOWN GOD."
- Q: What did Paul tell the people of Athens about the true God?
  A: That He made the world and everything in it, that He gives everyone life and breath, that He isn't far from any of us, and that He raised Jesus from the dead.
- Q: Paul said God "is not far from any one of us." How does it help to know that God is always close?
  A: We are never alone. We can talk to God anywhere, at any time, and He hears us.

## Priscilla and Aquila

> "When Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately." — Acts 18:26

From Athens, Paul went on to the busy city of Corinth. There he met a husband and wife called Aquila and Priscilla. They were Jewish, and they had recently moved from Rome, because the emperor had ordered all the Jews to leave the city.

Aquila and Priscilla made tents for a living, sewing together leather and cloth. And Paul was a tentmaker too! So Paul stayed with them, and they all worked together, making tents.

Every Sabbath, Paul went to the synagogue and told people about Jesus. And the rest of the week, he worked hard at his trade, so that he wouldn't have to ask anyone for money.

Paul stayed in Corinth for a year and a half, teaching God's Word. Then he set sail again, and Priscilla and Aquila went with him, as far as the city of Ephesus. Paul left them there, and went on his way.

While Priscilla and Aquila were in Ephesus, a man called Apollos came to the city. He came from Alexandria in Egypt. Apollos was a wonderful speaker. He knew the Scriptures very well, and he taught about Jesus with great enthusiasm. But there were some things about Jesus he didn't fully understand yet.

When Priscilla and Aquila heard Apollos speaking boldly in the synagogue, they didn't stand up and argue with him in front of everyone. And they didn't grumble about him behind his back.

Instead, they invited him to their home. And there, kindly and quietly, they explained the way of God to him more fully.

Apollos listened. And he went on to become a great teacher, helping many people to believe in Jesus.

Priscilla and Aquila weren't famous preachers. They were tentmakers. They worked with their hands, ran a business, and opened their home. But Paul later called them his "fellow workers in Christ Jesus", who had risked their lives for him (Romans 16:3–4). Churches met in their house, wherever they lived.

God uses ordinary people in ordinary jobs, a husband and wife sewing tents, to do His work. And sometimes the best way to help someone isn't to correct them in public, but to welcome them home and explain things kindly.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 18:1–4, 18–28

*Dear God, thank you that you use ordinary people, in ordinary jobs, to do your work. Help me to be kind when I help others, like Priscilla and Aquila were. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What job did Paul, Priscilla and Aquila do?
  A: They were tentmakers, sewing tents from leather and cloth.
- Q: How did Priscilla and Aquila help Apollos?
  A: They invited him to their home and kindly explained the way of God to him more fully, instead of arguing with him in public.
- Q: When someone gets something wrong, what's a kind way to help them?
  A: Talk to them privately and gently, instead of making them feel silly in front of others. That's what Priscilla and Aquila did.

## The Riot at Ephesus

> "So the word of the Lord powerfully continued to spread and prevail." — Acts 19:20

Paul came back to Ephesus, and he stayed there for more than two years. Ephesus was a huge city, and it was famous for its enormous temple to a goddess called Artemis.

God did amazing miracles through Paul in Ephesus. And many people turned to Jesus. Some of them had practised magic. They brought their magic books and burned them in front of everyone. The books were worth fifty thousand silver coins! But they didn't want them any more, now that they followed Jesus.

So the word of the Lord kept spreading and growing stronger.

But not everyone was happy.

There was a silversmith called Demetrius, who made little silver models of the temple of Artemis. People bought them, and he made a lot of money.

Demetrius called together all the other craftsmen who made things like that. "Men," he said, "you know we make good money from this business. But this man Paul has persuaded lots of people, here in Ephesus and all over the province, that gods made by human hands are no gods at all. Soon nobody will buy what we make! And people might even stop respecting the great goddess Artemis!"

When the craftsmen heard this, they were furious. They started shouting, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

Soon the whole city was in an uproar. The crowd grabbed two of Paul's travelling friends and rushed into the huge open-air theatre. Paul wanted to go in and speak to the crowd, but his friends wouldn't let him. It was too dangerous.

Inside the theatre, everyone was shouting. Some were shouting one thing and some another. Most of the people didn't even know why they were there!

For about two hours, the crowd shouted over and over again, "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians! Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!"

At last the city clerk, an important official, managed to quieten the crowd. "People of Ephesus," he said, "these men haven't robbed any temples or said anything against our goddess. If Demetrius has a complaint, there are proper courts for that. We could get into serious trouble for this riot today!" And he sent the crowd home.

When the uproar was over, Paul gathered the believers, encouraged them, said goodbye, and set off on his travels again.

Demetrius was really worried about his money. When people come to know Jesus, things change. Sometimes it upsets people who were making money from things that weren't right.

But a shouting crowd couldn't stop the good news. It just kept on spreading.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 19:11–20, 23–41

*Dear God, thank you that nothing can stop your Word from spreading. Help me to love you more than money or anything else. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why was Demetrius the silversmith so angry?
  A: Because people were turning to Jesus and stopping buying the little silver models of the temple of Artemis he made, so he was losing money.
- Q: What did the crowd shout for two hours in the theatre?
  A: "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" Most of them didn't even know why they were there.
- Q: The people who used to do magic burned their valuable books when they followed Jesus. Is there anything you might need to give up to follow Jesus better?
  A: Talk about it gently. Perhaps a habit, a game, or something that pulls us away from God. Following Jesus is worth more than anything we give up.

## The Boy Who Fell Asleep

> "And the people were greatly relieved to take the boy home alive." — Acts 20:12

Paul was on his way back to Jerusalem. On the way, he stopped for a week in a town called Troas.

On Sunday, the first day of the week, the believers in Troas met together to break bread and remember Jesus. They met in an upstairs room, three floors up, lit by lots of oil lamps.

Paul was leaving the next day, and he had so much to tell them. So he kept on talking, and talking, and talking. He talked until midnight!

A young man called Eutychus was sitting on the window sill. It was late, and the room was warm with all the people and all the lamps. And as Paul talked on and on, Eutychus got sleepier and sleepier. His eyes closed. His head began to nod.

And then, fast asleep, he fell out of the window! He fell all the way down from the third floor to the ground.

The people rushed downstairs. And when they picked him up, he was dead.

But Paul went down and threw himself on the young man and put his arms around him. "Don't be alarmed!" Paul said. "He's alive!"

Then Paul went back upstairs. They broke bread together and ate. And Paul kept on talking until daylight! Then he left.

And the people took Eutychus home alive. They were so relieved and so comforted.

This is a true story. It's also a funny one, in a way. Have you ever fallen asleep when somebody was talking for a very long time? Eutychus did, and he fell out of a window!

But it's also a wonderful story. God gave Eutychus his life back. Paul, like Peter, had seen Jesus raise people from the dead. Now God used Paul to do the same.

And look at what the believers did afterwards. They didn't all go home. They went back upstairs and carried on! They loved being together, and hearing about Jesus, so much that they stayed up all night long.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 20:7–12

*Dear God, thank you that you are stronger than death. Thank you for my church family and the times we spend together. Help me to listen well, even when it's hard to stay awake! Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why did Eutychus fall asleep?
  A: Because it was late at night, the room was warm with people and lamps, and Paul kept talking until midnight.
- Q: What happened after Eutychus fell out of the window?
  A: The people found him dead, but Paul put his arms around him and said he was alive, and God gave him his life back.
- Q: The believers loved being together so much that they stayed up all night. What do you love about being with your church family?
  A: Let your child answer. You could talk about friends, singing, learning about God, or eating together.

## Goodbye at Miletus

> "It is more blessed to give than to receive." — Acts 20:35

Paul was hurrying to get to Jerusalem. His ship stopped at a port called Miletus, not far from Ephesus. So Paul sent a message to the leaders of the church in Ephesus, asking them to come and see him.

When they arrived, Paul spoke to them.

"You know how I lived the whole time I was with you," he said. "I served the Lord humbly, sometimes with tears. I didn't hold back from telling you anything that would help you. I taught you in public and from house to house. I told everyone to turn to God and to have faith in our Lord Jesus.

"Now I'm going to Jerusalem. I don't know what will happen to me there. But the Holy Spirit warns me that prison and trouble are waiting for me in every city.

"But my life isn't what matters most. I just want to finish the race and complete the job the Lord Jesus gave me: to tell people the good news of God's grace.

"Now I know that none of you will ever see me again.

"Take care of yourselves, and of all God's people. Look after them, like shepherds look after their sheep.

"I never wanted anybody's silver or gold or clothes. You know that I worked with these hands of mine to provide for myself and the people with me. I showed you that by working hard we must help the weak. Remember the words the Lord Jesus Himself said: 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"

When Paul had finished speaking, he knelt down with all of them and prayed.

They all cried. They hugged Paul and kissed him. What made them saddest of all was that he had said they would never see him again.

Then they walked with him down to the ship.

Paul loved the people he had taught about Jesus, and they loved him. It's very hard to say goodbye to people you love. But Paul trusted God with whatever was coming, and he trusted God to look after his friends after he had gone.

And do you notice the words of Jesus that Paul quoted? "It is more blessed to give than to receive." Those words aren't written in any of the four Gospels. Paul remembered them and passed them on. Giving makes us happier than getting.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 20:17–38

*Dear God, thank you for the people who have taught me about you. Help me to remember that it is more blessed to give than to receive. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why were the church leaders so sad at Miletus?
  A: Because Paul said they would never see him again.
- Q: What words of Jesus did Paul remind them of?
  A: "It is more blessed to give than to receive."
- Q: Why do you think giving can make us happier than receiving?
  A: Because giving is what love does, and it makes other people happy too. Think of a time you gave something and it made you glad, and talk about it together.

## Seized in the Temple

> "I am ready not only to be bound, but also to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus." — Acts 21:13

Paul's friends begged him not to go to Jerusalem. A prophet had warned that Paul would be tied up and handed over to his enemies there. Everyone was crying.

"Why are you crying and breaking my heart?" said Paul. "I am ready not only to be tied up, but even to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus."

When they saw that Paul wouldn't change his mind, they said, "The Lord's will be done."

So Paul went to Jerusalem. The believers there welcomed him warmly, and he told them all the wonderful things God had done among the people who weren't Jews.

But a few days later, while Paul was at the temple, some men who hated him saw him there. They stirred up the whole crowd. "This is the man who teaches everyone everywhere against our people and our law and this temple!" they shouted.

The whole city was in an uproar. People came running from everywhere. They grabbed Paul, dragged him out of the temple, and began to beat him.

Word reached the commander of the Roman soldiers in Jerusalem that the whole city was rioting. He took soldiers and officers and ran down to the crowd. When the people saw the soldiers, they stopped beating Paul.

The commander arrested Paul and had him bound with two chains. "Who is this man, and what has he done?" he asked. But some people in the crowd shouted one thing and some another. The noise was so loud that he couldn't find out the truth. So the soldiers carried Paul up the steps towards the army barracks, because the crowd was so violent.

At the top of the steps, Paul asked the commander, "May I speak to the people?"

The commander said yes. So Paul stood on the steps, in chains, and held up his hand. A great hush fell over the crowd. And Paul began to speak to them in their own language.

He told them his story: how he had hated the followers of Jesus, how he had been on his way to Damascus to arrest them, how a bright light had shone around him and Jesus had spoken to him, and how his life had changed for ever.

Paul had just been beaten by an angry mob. He was standing in chains. But the first thing he wanted to do was tell those same people about Jesus!

That night, the Lord stood near Paul and said, "Take courage! You have told people about Me here in Jerusalem. You must tell people about Me in Rome too."

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 21:10–40; 22:1–21; 23:11

*Dear Jesus, thank you that you stand near your people when things are hard. Please give me courage to tell people about you, like Paul did. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why did Paul's friends beg him not to go to Jerusalem?
  A: Because a prophet had warned that he would be tied up and handed over to his enemies there.
- Q: What did Paul do when the commander let him speak to the crowd?
  A: Standing on the steps in chains, he told them the story of how he met Jesus and how his life changed.
- Q: The night after the riot, the Lord said to Paul, "Take courage!" When do you need courage?
  A: Let your child share. Jesus is near us too when we feel afraid, just as He was near Paul.

## Paul's Nephew

> "But when the son of Paul's sister heard about the plot, he went into the barracks and told Paul." — Acts 23:16

Paul was being kept in the Roman army barracks in Jerusalem. The leaders who hated him were furious that he was still alive.

So the next morning, more than forty men made a secret plan. They made a promise to each other: they wouldn't eat or drink anything until they had killed Paul!

They went to the chief priests and leaders. "Ask the commander to bring Paul to you tomorrow," they said, "as if you want to ask him some more questions. We'll be waiting on the way, ready to kill him before he gets there."

It was a wicked plan. And it might have worked.

But somebody heard about it. Paul's nephew, the son of Paul's sister, found out about the plot. And he went straight to the barracks and told Paul.

Paul called one of the officers. "Take this young man to the commander," he said. "He has something to tell him."

So the officer took Paul's nephew to the commander. And the commander took him by the hand, led him to one side, and asked, "What is it you want to tell me?"

"The leaders have agreed to ask you to bring Paul to their council tomorrow," the young man said, "pretending they want to find out more about him. Don't believe them! More than forty men are hiding, waiting for him. They've promised not to eat or drink anything until they've killed him. They're ready now, just waiting for you to say yes."

The commander sent the young man away. "Don't tell anyone that you've told me this," he said.

Then the commander called two of his officers. "Get two hundred soldiers ready to go to the city of Caesarea tonight, at nine o'clock," he said, "with seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen. And get horses for Paul to ride, and take him safely to Governor Felix."

So that night, Paul rode out of Jerusalem in the dark, surrounded by almost five hundred soldiers! He arrived safely in Caesarea.

The forty men who had promised not to eat until they killed Paul were left waiting. They never got their chance.

We don't know the name of Paul's nephew. We don't know how old he was. The Bible calls him a "young man". But we do know he was brave. He could have stayed quiet. Instead, he went into a Roman army fort and spoke to the commander. And because he did, Paul's life was saved.

God used a young person to protect Paul. And God can use young people today too.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 23:12–24

*Dear God, thank you that you protected Paul through his brave nephew. Please make me brave to speak up when I know something is wrong. Thank you that you can use young people. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What was the secret plan against Paul?
  A: More than forty men promised not to eat or drink until they had killed him, and planned to attack him on the way to the council.
- Q: How did Paul's nephew save his life?
  A: He heard about the plot, went into the army barracks and told Paul, and then told the commander, who sent Paul away safely that night with almost five hundred soldiers.
- Q: Paul's nephew spoke up when he knew something was wrong. When should you tell a grown-up about something you know?
  A: Whenever someone might get hurt or is being treated badly, even if it feels scary. Telling a trusted grown-up is brave, not tattling.

## Before the King

> "I wish to God that not only you but all who hear me this day may become what I am, except for these chains." — Acts 26:29

Paul was kept as a prisoner in Caesarea for two whole years. The governors kept putting off deciding what to do with him.

Then a new governor came, called Festus. The leaders in Jerusalem still wanted Paul killed. So Paul did something he had the right to do, because he was a Roman citizen. He said, "I appeal to Caesar!" That meant his case would be heard by the emperor himself, in Rome.

Before Paul was sent to Rome, a visiting king came to Caesarea. His name was King Agrippa, and his sister Bernice came with him. They wanted to hear Paul for themselves.

So the next day, Agrippa and Bernice came into the great hall with great pomp: fine clothes, royal robes, important army commanders and leading men of the city. And Paul was brought in, in chains.

"You may speak for yourself," said Agrippa.

So Paul told the king his story. How he had been a strict Pharisee. How he had hated the followers of Jesus and put many of them in prison. How, on the road to Damascus, at midday, a light brighter than the sun had shone around him. How Jesus had spoken to him and sent him to open people's eyes, to turn them from darkness to light. And how, ever since, he had obeyed that vision from heaven.

"I'm only saying what Moses and the prophets said would happen," said Paul. "That the Christ would suffer, and be the first to rise from the dead, and bring light to his own people and to all the nations."

Festus interrupted. "Paul, you're out of your mind!" he shouted. "All your learning is driving you mad!"

"I'm not mad, most excellent Festus," Paul answered. "What I'm saying is true and reasonable. The king knows about these things. None of this was done in a corner. King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know you do."

Agrippa said to Paul, "Do you think you can persuade me to become a Christian so quickly?"

And Paul said, "Whether quickly or not, I pray to God that not only you, but everyone who is listening to me today, may become what I am, except for these chains."

The king and the governor got up and went out. "This man isn't doing anything that deserves death or prison," they said to each other. "He could have been set free, if he hadn't appealed to Caesar."

Paul was the prisoner. Agrippa was the king. But Paul was the one who was really free. He knew Jesus. And even in chains, his greatest wish was for the king to know Jesus too.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 25:23–26:32

*Dear God, thank you that knowing Jesus is the greatest freedom of all. Help me to want everyone I meet to know Him too, even important people. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why was Paul going to be sent to Rome?
  A: Because he appealed to Caesar, which meant his case would be heard by the emperor in Rome.
- Q: What did Paul say when Agrippa asked if Paul thought he could persuade him to become a Christian so quickly?
  A: That he prayed that the king and everyone listening would become like him, a follower of Jesus, except for the chains.
- Q: Paul was a prisoner, but he was more free than the king. What do you think that means?
  A: That knowing Jesus and being forgiven gives a freedom in our hearts that no chains can take away. Agrippa had power and riches, but he didn't yet know Jesus.

## The Great Storm

> "So take courage, men, for I believe God that it will happen just as He told me." — Acts 27:25

At last Paul set sail for Rome. He was a prisoner, guarded by a Roman officer called Julius. Luke went with him, and so did his friend Aristarchus. They sailed on a big ship carrying grain, with 276 people on board.

It was late in the year, and the sailing season was nearly over. The winds were against them, and sailing was slow and dangerous. Paul warned them, "Men, I can see that this voyage will be a disaster, with great loss to the ship and its cargo, and our own lives too." But the captain and the owner of the ship wanted to sail on to a better harbour for the winter. So they set off.

At first a gentle south wind began to blow, and they thought they would be fine. But soon a wind of hurricane force, called the Northeaster, swept down on them.

The ship was caught by the storm. It couldn't turn into the wind, so they had to let it be driven along. The sailors wrapped ropes underneath the ship to hold it together. The next day they started throwing the cargo overboard. The day after, they threw the ship's equipment over the side with their own hands.

For many days, they couldn't see the sun or the stars. The storm kept raging. And at last everyone gave up hope of being saved.

Nobody had eaten for a long time.

Then Paul stood up in front of them.

"Men," he said, "you should have listened to me and not sailed. But now I urge you to keep up your courage. Not one of you will lose your life. Only the ship will be lost. Last night an angel of the God I belong to and serve stood beside me. He said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar. And God has graciously given you the lives of everyone sailing with you.'

"So take courage, men! For I believe God, that it will happen just as He told me. But we will run aground on some island."

Everyone else had lost hope. The sailors had lost hope. The soldiers had lost hope. The captain had lost hope.

But Paul believed God.

Paul wasn't the captain of the ship. He was a prisoner! But in the middle of the storm, he was the one everyone needed to listen to, because he trusted God.

Paul didn't say the storm would stop. He didn't say everything would be easy. He said God had promised, and God would keep His promise.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 27:1–26

*Dear God, when I am in a storm and everything seems hopeless, help me to believe you, like Paul did. Thank you that you always keep your promises. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: How did the sailors try to save the ship in the storm?
  A: They wrapped ropes underneath it to hold it together, and threw the cargo and the ship's equipment overboard.
- Q: What did the angel tell Paul?
  A: Not to be afraid, that he must stand before Caesar, and that God had given him the lives of everyone sailing with him.
- Q: Paul said, "I believe God." Why did that give everyone courage?
  A: Because even though the storm was still raging, Paul trusted God's promise. When we believe what God says, we can be brave even in hard times.

## Shipwreck!

> "In this way everyone was brought safely to land." — Acts 27:44

For fourteen nights the ship was driven across the sea by the storm. Then, about midnight on the fourteenth night, the sailors sensed that they were getting near land. They measured the depth of the water, and it was getting shallower and shallower.

They were afraid the ship would be smashed on the rocks. So they dropped four anchors from the back of the ship, and prayed for daylight.

Just before dawn, Paul urged everyone to eat something. "For fourteen days you have been waiting, and you haven't eaten anything," he said. "Please eat some food. You need it to survive. Not one of you will lose a single hair from your head."

Then Paul took some bread, gave thanks to God in front of them all, broke it, and began to eat. And everyone was encouraged, and ate some food themselves.

When daylight came, they could see a bay with a sandy beach. They decided to try to run the ship onto the beach. So they cut the anchors loose, raised the front sail to the wind, and headed for the shore.

But the ship hit a sandbank. The front of the ship stuck fast and wouldn't move. And the back of the ship began to break into pieces under the pounding of the huge waves.

The soldiers planned to kill the prisoners, so that none of them could swim away and escape. But the officer, Julius, wanted to save Paul's life. So he stopped them.

He ordered everyone who could swim to jump overboard first and swim for land. The rest were to hold on to planks and pieces of the broken ship.

And so, one by one, swimming and floating and splashing through the waves, every single person reached the shore safely.

Two hundred and seventy-six people. Not one was lost.

God had promised, through the angel, that everyone on the ship would live. And God kept His promise, exactly. The ship was smashed to pieces, but every person was safe.

Sometimes God doesn't stop the storm. Sometimes the ship still breaks. But God brings His people through. "In this way everyone was brought safely to land."

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 27:27–44

*Dear God, thank you that you kept your promise and brought everyone safely to land. Thank you that you bring your people through hard times. Help me to trust you. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did Paul do before dawn, when everyone was hungry and frightened?
  A: He urged them to eat, then took bread, gave thanks to God in front of them all, and began to eat, and everyone was encouraged.
- Q: What happened to the ship, and what happened to the people?
  A: The ship stuck on a sandbank and broke apart in the waves, but every one of the 276 people reached the shore safely, swimming or holding on to planks.
- Q: God didn't stop the storm, but He brought everyone through it. How might God bring us through hard times?
  A: Talk about a hard time your family has been through. God doesn't always take hard things away, but He is with us in them and helps us through.

## The Snake on Malta

> "The islanders showed us extraordinary kindness." — Acts 28:2

Everyone from the shipwreck had reached land safely. They were soaking wet and freezing cold, and it was raining. They found out that the island they had landed on was called Malta.

The people who lived on Malta were wonderfully kind to them. They lit a big fire on the beach and welcomed everyone, because it was raining and cold.

Paul gathered a bundle of sticks to help keep the fire going. But as he put them on the fire, a poisonous snake, driven out by the heat, bit him and held on to his hand!

When the islanders saw the snake hanging from Paul's hand, they said to each other, "This man must be a murderer! He escaped from the sea, but justice won't let him live."

But Paul just shook the snake off into the fire. And he wasn't hurt at all.

The islanders waited and watched. They expected Paul's hand to swell up, or for him to suddenly fall down dead. But after waiting a long time, nothing happened to him.

So they changed their minds completely. "He must be a god!" they said.

First they thought Paul was a murderer. Then they thought he was a god. He was neither! He was a servant of the living God, and God was protecting him.

Near the beach was an estate belonging to a man called Publius, the chief official of the island. He welcomed Paul and his friends into his home, and looked after them kindly for three days.

Publius's father was ill in bed, with a fever and a bad stomach illness. So Paul went in to see him. He prayed, put his hands on him, and healed him.

When this happened, everyone else on the island who was ill came to Paul, and they were healed too.

The people of Malta gave Paul and his friends many gifts to honour them. And when it was time to sail again, three months later, they gave them everything they needed for the journey.

The people of Malta had been kind to the shipwrecked strangers. And through Paul, God was kind to the people of Malta.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 28:1–10

*Dear God, thank you that you protect your people. Thank you for kind people who help strangers. Help me to be kind to people who need help, like the people of Malta were. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: How did the people of Malta welcome the shipwrecked strangers?
  A: They were very kind, and lit a big fire to warm everyone because it was raining and cold.
- Q: What did the islanders think when the snake bit Paul, and what did they think when nothing happened?
  A: At first they thought he must be a murderer being punished. When he wasn't hurt, they thought he must be a god.
- Q: The people of Malta were kind to strangers they had never met. How could you be kind to someone new or in need?
  A: By welcoming a new child at school, sharing with someone who has little, or helping someone who is lost or upset.

## Rome at Last

> "Boldly and freely he proclaimed the kingdom of God and taught about the Lord Jesus Christ." — Acts 28:31

After three months on Malta, Paul and the others set sail again, on another ship that had spent the winter on the island. And at last they came to Italy.

As they travelled along the road towards Rome, some Christians from Rome heard that Paul was coming. They came out a long way to meet him, all the way to places called the Forum of Appius and the Three Taverns. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and was encouraged.

Do you remember what Jesus had said to Paul in Jerusalem? "You must tell people about Me in Rome too." Through riots and trials and storms and shipwrecks and a snake bite, God had brought Paul all the way to Rome, just as He said He would.

In Rome, Paul was allowed to live in a house by himself, with a soldier to guard him. He was still a prisoner, still chained to a soldier. But he could have visitors.

Paul invited the Jewish leaders in Rome to come and see him. From morning until evening, he explained to them about the kingdom of God, and tried to persuade them about Jesus from the Law of Moses and the Prophets. Some of them believed. Others didn't.

For two whole years, Paul stayed there in his own rented house. And he welcomed everyone who came to see him. Boldly, and without anyone stopping him, he told them about the kingdom of God and taught them about the Lord Jesus Christ.

The soldiers who guarded Paul took turns. So one by one, the Roman soldiers chained to Paul heard about Jesus too! Paul later wrote that everyone in the whole palace guard knew he was in chains because of Christ (Philippians 1:13).

That is where the book of Acts ends. Paul, in chains, in the greatest city in the world, telling everyone about Jesus.

Do you remember how Acts began? Jesus said His followers would be His witnesses in Jerusalem, and Judea, and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth. It started with about a hundred and twenty people in one room in Jerusalem. Now the good news had reached Rome, the centre of the whole Roman Empire. And from there, it would go everywhere.

It's still going. The story of Acts didn't really end. It's still being written today, every time someone tells someone else about Jesus.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 28:11–31

*Dear God, thank you that you kept your promise and brought Paul to Rome. Thank you that the good news has travelled all the way to me. Help me to be part of the story, and to tell others about Jesus. Amen.*

  A: They came out a long way along the road to meet him, and Paul thanked God and was encouraged.
- Q: How did Paul tell people about Jesus in Rome, even though he was a prisoner?
  A: He welcomed everyone who came to his house, and taught them about Jesus for two years. Even the soldiers chained to him heard the good news.
- Q: The story of Acts is still being written today. How could you be part of it?
  A: By telling someone about Jesus, praying for people who don't know Him, or helping missionaries who take the good news to other places.

## Don't Let Anyone Look Down on You

> "Let no one despise your youth, but set an example for the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity." — 1 Timothy 4:12

Do you remember Timothy? He was a young man from the town of Lystra, the town where Paul healed the man who couldn't walk. Timothy's mother, Eunice, was a believer, and so was his grandmother, Lois. Ever since he was a little child, they had taught him the Holy Scriptures.

When Paul came back through Lystra on his travels, he met Timothy. And he wanted Timothy to come with him. So Timothy joined Paul, and travelled with him to many places.

Paul loved Timothy like a son. He called him "my true son in the faith". He trusted Timothy to visit churches for him, to encourage the believers, and to bring back news.

Later, Paul left Timothy in the city of Ephesus, to help lead the church there. But Timothy was young. Some people might have thought he was too young to be a leader. Some of the older people in the church might have looked down on him.

So Paul wrote Timothy a letter. Two letters, in fact. They are in the Bible, and they're called 1 Timothy and 2 Timothy.

In his first letter, Paul wrote: "Don't let anyone look down on you because you are young. Instead, set an example for the believers in the way you speak, in the way you live, in love, in faith, and in purity."

Paul didn't say, "Wait until you're grown up, and then you can serve God." He said, "You can be an example right now, even though you're young. Show everyone how to follow Jesus by how you speak, how you live, how you love, how you trust God, and how you keep yourself pure."

And in his second letter, Paul reminded Timothy of the faith he had learned from his grandmother and his mother. "I remember your sincere faith," he wrote, "which first lived in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and I am sure now lives in you also." And he wrote: "From the time you were a child, you have known the Holy Scriptures, which can make you wise and lead you to be saved by faith in Christ Jesus."

Timothy learned about God from the Bible when he was a little child, just like you. His grandmother and mother read God's Word with him. And that's where his faith began.

Children don't have to wait until they're grown up to follow Jesus and set an example. You can do it right now.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 16:1–3; 1 Timothy 4:12; 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:14–15

*Dear God, thank you that I don't have to wait until I'm grown up to follow you. Help me to be an example in how I speak, how I live, and how I love. Thank you for the people who teach me about you. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Who taught Timothy about God when he was a child?
  A: His grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice, who taught him the Holy Scriptures.
- Q: What did Paul tell Timothy to do, even though he was young?
  A: Not to let anyone look down on him for being young, but to be an example in how he spoke and lived, and in love, faith and purity.
- Q: How can you be an example to others, even now?
  A: Choose one of the five things together, such as kind words, honest actions, love, trusting God, or purity, and think of one way to live it out this week.

## The Runaway Slave

> "No longer as a slave, but better than a slave, as a beloved brother." — Philemon 1:16

While Paul was a prisoner, he met a young man called Onesimus.

Onesimus had run away from his master, a man called Philemon. In those days, many people were slaves, owned by other people. Onesimus was a slave who had run away from his master's house, and he may have taken some money with him too. Running away was a serious crime. If he was caught, he could be punished very harshly.

Somehow, Onesimus found his way to Paul. And while he was with Paul, Onesimus came to believe in Jesus. He became a Christian, and a great help to Paul.

But here's the surprising thing. Paul knew Philemon! Philemon was a Christian too, a friend of Paul's. The church in his town even met in Philemon's house.

So Paul decided to send Onesimus back to Philemon. But he didn't send him back alone. He sent a letter with him. It's a short letter, and it's in the Bible. It's called Philemon.

"I am writing to you about my son Onesimus," Paul wrote. "He became my son while I was in chains. Once he was useless to you, but now he has become useful, both to you and to me." That was a little joke, because the name Onesimus means "useful"!

"I would have liked to keep him with me, to help me while I'm in prison," Paul wrote. "But I didn't want to do anything without your agreement. Perhaps the reason he was separated from you for a while was so that you could have him back for ever, no longer as a slave, but better than a slave, as a dear brother."

Then Paul wrote, "So if you think of me as your partner, welcome him as you would welcome me. And if he has done you any wrong, or owes you anything, charge it to my account. I will pay it back."

Do you see what Paul did? He stood up for Onesimus. He asked Philemon to forgive him and welcome him home. And he offered to pay whatever Onesimus owed, out of his own pocket.

That is a picture of what Jesus does for us. We have run away from God, like Onesimus ran away from his master. We owe a debt we can never pay back. But Jesus says to His Father, "Welcome them as You would welcome Me. Whatever they owe, charge it to My account." And He paid it all, on the cross.

In God's family, there are no slaves and masters. There are only brothers and sisters, all welcomed home.

**Read it in your Bible:** Philemon 1:1–25

*Dear Jesus, thank you that you paid everything I owed, and that you welcome me into God's family. Help me to forgive people, and to welcome them as brothers and sisters. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Who was Onesimus, and what had he done?
  A: A slave who had run away from his master, Philemon, perhaps taking some money, which was a serious crime.
- Q: What did Paul ask Philemon to do?
  A: To forgive Onesimus and welcome him back not as a slave, but as a dear brother in Christ, and to charge anything he owed to Paul's account.
- Q: How is what Paul did for Onesimus like what Jesus does for us?
  A: We have run away from God and owe a debt we can't pay. Jesus asks His Father to welcome us, and He paid everything we owe on the cross. (Slavery was common then; the Bible's message that slave and master are brothers in Christ helped, over time, to bring it to an end.)

## What Love Is Like

> "And now these three remain: faith, hope, and love; but the greatest of these is love." — 1 Corinthians 13:13

Paul wrote lots of letters to the churches he had started. One of them was to the church in Corinth, the city where he had made tents with Priscilla and Aquila.

The church in Corinth had some problems. People were arguing. Some of them were proud and showing off. They thought some people in the church were more important than others, because they had more impressive gifts.

So Paul wrote to them about the most important thing of all. Love.

"If I can speak in every language of people and even of angels," Paul wrote, "but I don't have love, I'm just a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal."

Imagine someone banging a cymbal over and over again. Clang! Clang! Clang! That's what all our clever words sound like to God if we don't have love.

"If I know everything there is to know, and if I have faith that can move mountains, but I don't have love, I am nothing. If I give away everything I own to the poor, but I don't have love, it does me no good at all."

Then Paul described what real love is like:

"Love is patient.
Love is kind.
It does not envy.
It does not boast.
It is not proud.
It is not rude.
It is not selfish.
It is not easily angered.
It keeps no record of wrongs.
Love takes no pleasure in evil, but rejoices in the truth.
It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always keeps going.
Love never fails."

That's a lot to live up to, isn't it? Try this: read the list again, but every time it says "love", put your own name instead. "Sam is patient. Sam is kind. Sam does not boast..." It's hard to say all of those about ourselves!

But now try putting "Jesus" instead. "Jesus is patient. Jesus is kind. Jesus is not proud. Jesus keeps no record of wrongs..." Every single one is true. Jesus is what love looks like. And the more we know Him, the more His love grows in us.

"Three things last for ever," Paul wrote. "Faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love."

**Read it in your Bible:** 1 Corinthians 13

*Dear Jesus, thank you that you love me with a love that is patient and kind and never fails. Please help me to love other people like that. Make me patient and kind. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did Paul say we are like if we can do amazing things but don't have love?
  A: A noisy gong or a clanging cymbal: just noise.
- Q: Can you remember some of the things Paul said love is, and isn't?
  A: Love is patient and kind. It isn't jealous, boastful, proud, rude or selfish. It isn't easily angered and keeps no record of wrongs.
- Q: Which part of the love list do you find hardest? Which part is easiest?
  A: Talk about it honestly; grown-ups find parts of it hard too. Then pray together for God's help with the hard part.

## The Armour of God

> "Put on the full armor of God, so that you can make your stand against the devil's schemes." — Ephesians 6:11

When Paul was a prisoner, probably in Rome, he was chained to a Roman soldier. Day after day, Paul could look at the soldier's armour: his belt and breastplate, his shield and helmet and sword.

While he was there, Paul wrote a letter to the church in Ephesus, the city where the riot had happened. And at the end of the letter, he wrote about armour. Not the kind of armour soldiers wear, but the armour of God.

"Be strong in the Lord and in His mighty power," Paul wrote. "Put on the full armour of God, so that you can stand firm against the devil's tricks."

Christians are in a battle, Paul said. Not against people, but against the devil and the powers of evil. So we need God's armour to stand firm.

Then Paul went through each piece of armour, one by one.

"Stand firm, with the **belt of truth** buckled around your waist." A soldier's belt held everything else together. God's truth holds our whole life together.

"With the **breastplate of righteousness**." A breastplate protected the soldier's heart. Jesus makes us right with God, and that protects our hearts.

"With your feet fitted with the readiness of the **gospel of peace**." Soldiers needed good strong shoes. We need to be ready to go and share the good news of peace with God.

"Take up the **shield of faith**, which can put out all the flaming arrows of the evil one." Roman soldiers carried big shields that could stop even flaming arrows. When the devil fires lies at us, trusting God is our shield.

"Take the **helmet of salvation**." A helmet protects the head. Knowing that Jesus has saved us protects our minds.

"And the **sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God**." This is the only piece of armour for fighting back. Do you remember how Jesus answered the devil in the desert? "It is written!" God's Word, the Bible, is our sword.

"And pray at all times," Paul wrote.

Paul had a soldier right next to him, chained to his wrist. But Paul knew that God's armour is much stronger than any soldier's. And it's armour that even children can wear.

**Read it in your Bible:** Ephesians 6:10–20

*Dear God, thank you for your armour. Help me to put on the belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the shoes of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, your Word. Help me to stand firm. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Where was Paul when he wrote about the armour of God, and what could he see?
  A: He was a prisoner, probably in Rome, chained to a Roman soldier, so he could see the soldier's armour every day.
- Q: Can you name the pieces of the armour of God?
  A: The belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the shoes of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God.
- Q: The sword of the Spirit is God's Word. How can you use it when you're tempted or afraid?
  A: By remembering Bible verses, like Jesus did in the desert. You could choose one verse to learn together this week, such as "The Lord is my shepherd."

## John on Patmos

> "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last, the Living One. I was dead, and behold, now I am alive forever and ever!" — Revelation 1:17–18

Many years went by. Paul had finished his race. Peter too. Most of the first followers of Jesus had gone to be with Him.

But one of them was still alive, now a very old man. It was John, the fisherman, one of Jesus' closest friends, the one who had stood by the cross.

John had been sent away to a lonely, rocky island called Patmos, because he kept telling people about Jesus. He was a prisoner there, far from his friends.

One Sunday, the Lord's Day, John was worshipping God. And suddenly he heard a loud voice behind him, like a trumpet!

John turned around to see who was speaking. And he saw seven golden lampstands. And standing among them was someone like the Son of Man.

It was Jesus! But not the way John had seen Him on the hills of Galilee, or walking by the lake. Jesus was dressed in a long robe with a golden sash around His chest. His hair was white like wool, as white as snow. His eyes were like blazing fire. His voice was like the roar of rushing waters. In His right hand He held seven stars. And His face was shining like the sun at its brightest.

When John saw Him, he fell down at His feet, as if he were dead.

But Jesus put His right hand on John, and said, "Do not be afraid. I am the First and the Last. I am the Living One. I was dead, and look, now I am alive for ever and ever! And I hold the keys of death."

It was the same gentle touch John had known when he was a young man. The same "Do not be afraid." This was the same Jesus, the friend John had walked with. But now John was seeing Him in all His glory as King of everything.

"Write down what you see," Jesus told him.

So John wrote it all down. He wrote letters from Jesus to seven churches, encouraging them and warning them. And then Jesus showed John amazing visions of heaven, and of what will happen at the end of the world. John wrote them down in a book. It's the very last book of the Bible, called Revelation, which means "uncovering" or "showing".

Some parts of Revelation are mysterious and hard to understand, even for grown-ups. They are full of pictures and symbols. But the main message is clear: Jesus is King. He has won. Evil will not win in the end. And one day, Jesus is coming back.

John was old, and alone, and a prisoner on a lonely island. But he wasn't really alone. Jesus came to him there. And Jesus showed him how the whole story will end.

**Read it in your Bible:** Revelation 1:9–19

*Dear Jesus, thank you that you are alive for ever and ever, and that you are King of everything. When I am afraid, help me to hear you say, "Do not be afraid." Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Where was John, and why?
  A: On a lonely rocky island called Patmos, sent there as a prisoner because he kept telling people about Jesus.
- Q: What did Jesus look like when John saw Him?
  A: His hair was white as snow, His eyes like blazing fire, His voice like rushing waters, and His face shining like the sun.
- Q: When John fell down, Jesus touched him and said, "Do not be afraid." What does that tell us about Jesus, even in all His glory?
  A: That He is still gentle and kind to His friends. The King of everything is also our friend who says, "Do not be afraid."

## All Things New

> "And the One seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I make all things new.'" — Revelation 21:5

At the end of the book of Revelation, Jesus showed John the most wonderful thing of all.

"I saw a new heaven and a new earth," John wrote. "The first heaven and the first earth had passed away."

Then John saw a beautiful city, the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God. It was shining with the glory of God, like a precious jewel, clear as crystal. Its gates were made of pearls, each gate a single huge pearl. And its main street was made of pure gold, so pure it was like clear glass.

And John heard a loud voice from the throne: "Look! God's home is now with people! He will live with them, and they will be His people. God Himself will be with them and be their God."

"He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death, or sadness, or crying, or pain. All the old things have passed away."

Then the One sitting on the throne said, "Look! I am making all things new!"

There was no temple in the city, because God Himself and Jesus, the Lamb, are its temple. There was no need for the sun or the moon, because the glory of God gives it light, and Jesus, the Lamb, is its lamp. Its gates will never be shut, because there will be no night there.

Then the angel showed John a river, the river of the water of life, sparkling like crystal. It flowed from the throne of God and of the Lamb, down the middle of the city's great street.

And on each side of the river stood the tree of life, with twelve kinds of fruit, a new crop every month. And the leaves of the tree are for the healing of the nations.

Do you remember? The tree of life was in the very first garden, in the very first book of the Bible. When Adam and Eve disobeyed God, they had to leave the garden, and the way to the tree of life was closed. But now here it is again! The way is open. Everything that was lost in the garden has been given back, and much, much more.

"No longer will there be any curse," John wrote. Everything that went wrong on that sad day in the garden has been put right.

And God's people will see His face.

That is how the story ends. It began in a garden, with God and His people together. It ends in a garden city, with God and His people together for ever. Everything in between, all the promises, all the rescues, Abraham and Moses and David and the prophets, the baby in the manger, the cross and the empty tomb, the church going out to the ends of the earth, has been leading here.

The very last words of the Bible are a promise and a prayer. Jesus says, "Yes, I am coming soon." And John answers, "Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!"

That is the best story of all. And it's true. And it's for you.

**The promise kept:** "No longer will there be any curse" (Revelation 22:3). In the first book, God promised that a child of Eve would crush the snake's head. In the last book of the Bible, the snake is defeated, the curse is gone, and the tree of life is open to everyone. God kept every promise.

**Read it in your Bible:** Revelation 21:1–7, 22–27; 22:1–5, 20–21

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did John see at the end of Revelation?
  A: A new heaven and a new earth, and the holy city coming down from heaven, with gates of pearl, a street of gold, the river of life and the tree of life.
- Q: What will God do for His people there?
  A: Live with them for ever, and wipe away every tear. There will be no more death, sadness, crying or pain.
- Q: The Bible's story began in a garden and ends in a garden city. What is your favourite part of the whole story, from all eight books?
  A: Let your child choose and tell it in their own words. You could finish by thanking God together that His story is true, and that it's for us.
