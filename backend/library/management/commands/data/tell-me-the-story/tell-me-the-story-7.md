# Tell Me the Story – Book 7: The Church Begins

*New Testament Stories from Acts 1–13, for children aged 6 to 10*

Scripture quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible (BSB), public domain.

---

## Introduction: The Story Goes On

> "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth." — Acts 1:8

At the end of the last book, Jesus went back to heaven. But before He went, He gave His followers a job to do, and a promise.

The job was to tell everyone about Him: first in Jerusalem, then in the countryside all around, then in Samaria, and then all the way to the ends of the earth.

The promise was that they wouldn't have to do it on their own. God would send the Holy Spirit to give them power.

So Jesus' followers waited in Jerusalem, praying together. There were about a hundred and twenty of them. They were ordinary people: fishermen and tax collectors, mothers and brothers and friends. They had no money, no army and no important friends. How could people like that ever tell the whole world about Jesus?

This book begins the third part of Tell Me the Story: New Testament Stories. It comes from a book of the Bible called Acts, which was written by Luke, the same man who wrote the Gospel of Luke. Acts tells what happened after Jesus went back to heaven.

You will see wind and fire, and a crowd of thousands hearing about Jesus in their own languages. You will see a man who couldn't walk leap up and praise God, and prison doors swing open in the night. You will meet brave Stephen, and Philip on a desert road, and Saul, the enemy of the church who became its greatest friend.

The story of Jesus didn't stop when He went back to heaven. It just kept on going. And it's still going on today.

*Dear God, thank you for sending the Holy Spirit to help Jesus' followers. As we read these stories, please help us to be brave and to tell other people about Jesus too. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What job did Jesus give His followers before He went back to heaven?
  A: To tell everyone about Him, in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and all the way to the ends of the earth.
- Q: What did Jesus promise them?
  A: That the Holy Spirit would come and give them power to do it.
- Q: Jesus' followers were ordinary people. Why do you think God chose ordinary people to tell the world about Jesus?
  A: So that everyone could see it was God's power at work, not theirs. God still uses ordinary people today, including children.

## Wind and Fire

> "And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them." — Acts 2:4

Not long after Jesus went back to heaven, it was the day of a big festival called Pentecost. Jerusalem was crowded with Jewish people who had come from all over the world to celebrate.

Jesus' followers were all together in one place.

Suddenly there was a sound from heaven like a mighty rushing wind, and it filled the whole house where they were sitting. Then they saw what looked like little flames of fire, which separated and came to rest on each one of them.

And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit. They began to speak in other languages, languages they had never learned, as the Spirit gave them the words.

The sound was so loud that a big crowd came running to see what was happening. And everyone in the crowd was amazed. People from all over the world could hear Jesus' followers speaking in their very own language!

"Aren't all these people from Galilee?" they said. "So how is it that each of us hears them speaking in our own language?"

There were people from Parthia and Media and Mesopotamia, from Egypt and Libya and Rome, from Crete and Arabia, and many more countries. "We hear them telling the wonderful things God has done," they said, "in our own languages!"

"What does this mean?" they asked each other.

But some people laughed and made fun. "They've had too much wine!" they said.

Do you remember the tower of Babel, in the first book? People wanted to make a great name for themselves, so God mixed up their languages, and they couldn't understand each other any more. Now, on the day of Pentecost, God did something wonderful. People from many languages heard the good news about Jesus, all at the same time, each in their own language.

The good news about Jesus is for every nation and every language in the world.

The Holy Spirit had come, just as Jesus had promised. And Jesus' frightened, ordinary followers were about to become very brave.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 2:1–13

*Dear God, thank you for sending the Holy Spirit. Thank you that the good news about Jesus is for every language and every nation. Please fill me with your Spirit too. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did Jesus' followers hear and see when the Holy Spirit came?
  A: A sound like a mighty rushing wind, and what looked like little flames of fire resting on each of them.
- Q: What was amazing about the way they spoke?
  A: They spoke in languages they had never learned, and people from all over the world heard them in their own language.
- Q: How is Pentecost like the opposite of the tower of Babel?
  A: At Babel, God mixed up people's languages so they couldn't understand each other. At Pentecost, people of many languages all heard and understood the good news about Jesus.

## Peter Speaks Up

> "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins." — Acts 2:38

When some people in the crowd said Jesus' followers must have had too much wine, Peter stood up with the other eleven apostles. He raised his voice and spoke to the whole crowd.

Was this the same Peter who, just a few weeks before, had been too scared to admit he knew Jesus? It was! But now he was filled with the Holy Spirit, and he wasn't afraid any more.

"Listen carefully to what I say!" Peter said. "These people aren't drunk, as you think. It's only nine o'clock in the morning! No, this is what the prophet Joel was talking about long ago, when God said, 'In the last days, I will pour out My Spirit on all people.'"

Then Peter told them about Jesus.

"Jesus of Nazareth did amazing miracles and signs among you, as you know. You put Him to death by nailing Him to a cross. But it was all part of God's plan. And God raised Him from the dead, because it was impossible for death to keep hold of Him! We are all witnesses: we have seen Him alive. Now He is at the right hand of God in heaven, and He has poured out the Holy Spirit, which you have seen and heard today.

"So let everyone in Israel know for certain: God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Christ!"

When the people heard this, it was as if a knife had gone into their hearts. They realised what they had done. "Brothers," they said to Peter and the other apostles, "what shall we do?"

"Repent," said Peter, "turn away from your sins and turn back to God, and be baptised, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ, so that your sins will be forgiven. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. This promise is for you and your children, and for everyone who is far away, everyone the Lord our God will call."

That very day, about three thousand people believed and were baptised!

When Jesus called Peter to follow Him, He said, "From now on you will catch people." On the day of Pentecost, Peter's net was very full indeed.

**The promise kept:** Peter said, "This is what was spoken by the prophet Joel" (Acts 2:16), and quoted God's promise: "I will pour out My Spirit on all people." Hundreds of years later, on the day of Pentecost, God kept it.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 2:14–41

*Dear God, thank you that you forgive everyone who turns to you. Thank you that your promise is for children too. Please make me brave to tell others about Jesus, like Peter. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: How was Peter different on the day of Pentecost from the night Jesus was arrested?
  A: On the night Jesus was arrested he was too scared to say he knew Jesus. At Pentecost, filled with the Holy Spirit, he boldly told thousands of people about Jesus.
- Q: What did Peter tell the crowd to do?
  A: To repent, turning away from their sins and back to God, and to be baptised in the name of Jesus Christ, so their sins would be forgiven and they would receive the Holy Spirit.
- Q: Peter said the promise was "for you and your children". What does that mean for you?
  A: That God's forgiveness and the gift of His Spirit are for children as well as grown-ups. Anyone who trusts Jesus can receive them.

## Sharing Everything

> "They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and to the fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer." — Acts 2:42

After the day of Pentecost, there were thousands of new believers in Jerusalem. The very first church had begun! What was it like?

The believers loved to learn. Every day they listened to the apostles teaching them about Jesus.

They loved to be together. They met in the temple courts, and they ate meals together in each other's homes, with glad and generous hearts.

They remembered Jesus by breaking bread together, the way He had shown them at the Last Supper.

And they prayed together.

Amazing things happened through the apostles, and everyone was filled with awe. The believers praised God, and all the people of Jerusalem liked them. And every single day, God added more and more people who were being saved.

But one of the most amazing things was how they shared.

The believers were one in heart and mind. Nobody said, "This is mine, and you can't have it." They shared everything they had. If someone owned a field or a house, sometimes they would sell it, and bring the money to the apostles. Then the apostles gave it to anyone who needed it.

And the Bible says there wasn't a single needy person among them. Nobody went hungry. Nobody went without.

One of the believers was a man called Joseph, from the island of Cyprus. The apostles gave him a nickname: Barnabas, which means "Son of Encouragement", because he was so good at cheering people up and helping them. Barnabas sold a field he owned, and brought all the money and laid it at the apostles' feet, to help people in need.

You'll meet Barnabas again later in this book. He kept on being an encourager.

Why did the first Christians share like this? Because Jesus had given everything for them. When you know how much Jesus has given you, you want to give to other people too. Their love for each other showed everyone in Jerusalem what Jesus' love is like.

That's what the church is meant to be like. Not just a building, but a family: learning about Jesus together, eating together, praying together, and taking care of each other.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 2:42–47; 4:32–37

*Dear God, thank you for my church family. Please help us to learn about you, to pray together, and to take care of each other, like the first Christians did. Help me to share. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What four things did the first Christians devote themselves to?
  A: The apostles' teaching, fellowship (being together), the breaking of bread, and prayer.
- Q: Why was there no needy person among them?
  A: Because they shared everything, and people who had extra sold things and gave the money to anyone who needed it.
- Q: Barnabas was called "Son of Encouragement". How could you be an encourager this week?
  A: By cheering someone up, saying something kind, helping someone who is struggling, or noticing someone who is left out.

## The Man at the Beautiful Gate

> "Silver or gold I do not have, but what I have I give you: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk!" — Acts 3:6

One afternoon, at three o'clock, the time for prayer, Peter and John were going up to the temple.

At one of the temple gates, called the Beautiful Gate, there sat a man who had never been able to walk. He had been born that way. Every day, people carried him to the gate and put him there, so that he could beg for money from the people going into the temple. He was more than forty years old (Acts 4:22). He had been begging there for years and years.

When the man saw Peter and John about to go in, he asked them for money.

Peter and John stopped and looked straight at him. "Look at us!" said Peter.

So the man looked at them, expecting to get some money.

"I don't have any silver or gold," said Peter. "But what I do have, I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk!"

Then Peter took him by his right hand and helped him up. And at once the man's feet and ankles became strong!

He jumped to his feet and began to walk. Then he went into the temple courts with Peter and John, walking and jumping and praising God!

All the people saw him walking and praising God. They knew he was the man who used to sit begging at the Beautiful Gate. They were filled with wonder and amazement at what had happened to him.

The man held on tight to Peter and John, and the whole crowd came running to see.

"Why are you so surprised?" Peter said to them. "Why are you staring at us, as if we made this man walk by our own power or goodness? We didn't! It was Jesus. You handed Jesus over to be killed. But God raised Him from the dead, and we have seen it. It is faith in the name of Jesus that has made this man strong and completely well, as you can all see."

The man had asked for money. Money would have helped him for a day or two. But Peter and John gave him something much better. They gave him Jesus.

Peter and John didn't have money. But they had something more precious than silver or gold. They knew Jesus. And that's something we can share with people too.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 3:1–16

*Dear Jesus, thank you that your name is powerful. Thank you that knowing you is better than silver or gold. Help me to share you with other people. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did the man at the Beautiful Gate ask Peter and John for, and what did he get?
  A: He asked for money, but instead Peter healed him in the name of Jesus, and he walked and jumped and praised God.
- Q: Who did Peter say had made the man well?
  A: Not Peter and John, but Jesus. It was faith in Jesus' name that healed him.
- Q: Peter said, "What I have, I give you." What do you have that you could give to someone, even without money?
  A: Kindness, time, a helping hand, a prayer, or telling them about Jesus. These can be worth more than money.

## "We Cannot Stop Speaking"

> "For we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard." — Acts 4:20

While Peter and John were still talking to the crowd in the temple, the priests and the captain of the temple guard came up to them. They were very angry that the apostles were teaching the people that Jesus had risen from the dead. So they arrested Peter and John and put them in prison overnight.

But it was too late to stop the message. Many people who heard it believed, and the number of men who believed in Jesus grew to about five thousand!

The next day, the rulers and elders and teachers of the law met together, including the high priest. These were the same men who had put Jesus on trial. They had Peter and John brought in.

"By what power, or what name, did you do this?" they asked.

Then Peter, filled with the Holy Spirit, answered them.

"If we are being questioned today about a kind deed to a man who couldn't walk," Peter said, "then let all of you know this: he was healed in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom you crucified, but whom God raised from the dead. This Jesus is 'the stone you builders rejected, which has become the cornerstone.' Salvation is found in no one else. There is no other name under heaven by which we must be saved."

The leaders were amazed at how bold Peter and John were. They could tell they were ordinary men, who hadn't been to special schools. And they realised that these men had been with Jesus.

And there, standing with them, was the man who had been healed! What could they say?

So they sent Peter and John out of the room and talked together. "What are we going to do with these men?" they said. "Everyone in Jerusalem knows about this miracle. We can't deny it. But to stop it spreading any further, we must warn them not to speak to anyone in this name again."

They called Peter and John back in and commanded them never to speak or teach in the name of Jesus at all.

But Peter and John answered, "You decide whether it is right in God's eyes to listen to you rather than to God. As for us, we cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard!"

The leaders threatened them some more, and then let them go.

Peter and John went back to the other believers and told them everything. And they all prayed together. They didn't pray, "Lord, keep us safe." They prayed, "Lord, look at their threats, and help Your servants to speak Your word with great boldness."

When they had prayed, the place where they were meeting shook! And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and they spoke the word of God boldly.

**The promise kept:** Peter called Jesus "the stone you builders rejected, which has become the cornerstone" (Acts 4:11), words from Psalm 118. The cornerstone is the most important stone in a building, the one that holds it all together.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 4:1–31

*Dear God, please help me to be bold when I talk about Jesus, even when it's hard. Thank you that I can pray to you for courage, like the first Christians did. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why were the leaders amazed at Peter and John?
  A: Because they were so bold, even though they were ordinary, unschooled men. The leaders realised they had been with Jesus.
- Q: What did Peter and John say when they were told never to speak about Jesus again?
  A: "We cannot stop speaking about what we have seen and heard!"
- Q: When the believers were threatened, they prayed for boldness, not safety. What would you like to pray for courage about?
  A: Perhaps being kind when others aren't, standing up for someone, or saying you believe in Jesus. Pray about it together.

## Out Through the Prison Doors

> "We must obey God rather than men." — Acts 5:29

The apostles kept on telling people about Jesus. They did many miracles, and people brought sick people out into the streets on beds and mats, hoping Peter's shadow would fall on them as he walked past. Crowds came from the towns all around Jerusalem, bringing their sick, and all of them were healed.

The high priest and his friends were filled with jealousy. They arrested the apostles and put them in the public jail.

But during the night, an angel of the Lord opened the prison doors and brought them out. "Go and stand in the temple courts," the angel said, "and tell the people all about this new life."

So at daybreak, the apostles went into the temple courts and began to teach.

Meanwhile, the high priest called together the whole council of leaders. They sent to the jail to have the apostles brought to them.

But when the officers got to the jail, the apostles weren't there! The officers came back and said, "We found the jail locked up tight, and the guards standing at the doors. But when we opened them, there was nobody inside!"

The leaders were completely puzzled. What could have happened?

Then someone came in and said, "Look! The men you put in jail are standing in the temple courts, teaching the people!"

So the captain went and brought the apostles back, but gently, because they were afraid the people might throw stones at them.

"We gave you strict orders not to teach in this name," said the high priest. "But you have filled Jerusalem with your teaching!"

Peter and the other apostles replied, "We must obey God rather than people!" And they told the council about Jesus all over again.

The leaders were so angry that they wanted to put the apostles to death. But a wise and respected teacher called Gamaliel stood up. "Be careful what you do to these men," he said. "Leave them alone. Let them go! If what they are doing is only a human idea, it will fail. But if it's from God, you won't be able to stop them. You might even find yourselves fighting against God!"

The council agreed with Gamaliel. But they had the apostles whipped. Then they ordered them not to speak in the name of Jesus, and let them go.

And how do you think the apostles felt? They went away full of joy! They were happy that God thought they were worthy to suffer for Jesus' name.

And every single day, in the temple courts and from house to house, they never stopped teaching and telling the good news that Jesus is the Christ.

Gamaliel was right. You can't stop God's work.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 5:12–42

*Dear God, thank you that nobody can stop your good news from spreading. Help me always to obey you, even when it's hard. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: How did the apostles get out of prison?
  A: An angel of the Lord opened the prison doors during the night and brought them out.
- Q: What did Gamaliel tell the council?
  A: To leave the apostles alone, because if their work was from God, nobody would be able to stop it, and they might find themselves fighting against God.
- Q: The apostles said, "We must obey God rather than people." When might obeying God be different from what other people want?
  A: For example, when friends want us to lie, be unkind or leave someone out. God's way is always right, even when it's not popular. (Usually, obeying God includes obeying parents and teachers; this is about when someone asks us to do wrong.)

## Seven Helpers

> "So the word of God continued to spread." — Acts 6:7

The church kept on growing and growing. But as it grew, a problem came up.

Every day, the believers gave out food to widows, women whose husbands had died and who had nobody to look after them. Some of the believers had grown up speaking Greek, and others had grown up speaking Hebrew. And the Greek-speaking believers began to grumble. "Our widows are being left out when the food is handed out each day!" they said.

It would have been easy for the church to start arguing and splitting into groups. But the twelve apostles called all the believers together.

"It wouldn't be right for us to stop teaching God's Word in order to hand out food," the apostles said. "So, brothers and sisters, choose seven men from among you who are known to be full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom. We'll put them in charge of this job. And we will give our attention to prayer and to teaching God's Word."

Everyone liked this idea. So they chose seven men. One of them was called Stephen, a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit. Another was called Philip. Look out for both of them in the next stories!

Did you notice something about the names of the seven men? They were all Greek names. The church chose people from the very group who had been left out, to make sure everyone was treated fairly.

The believers brought the seven men to the apostles, who prayed for them and laid their hands on them.

And the word of God kept on spreading. The number of believers in Jerusalem grew very quickly, and even a large number of priests came to believe in Jesus.

Handing out food might not seem as important as preaching. But the apostles chose men full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom to do it. Every job in God's church matters, the jobs that everyone sees and the jobs that nobody notices. And when everyone does their part, God's work keeps growing.

The church found a way to make sure nobody was left out. It's still a good question for any church, or any family, or any class at school: is anyone being left out? And what can we do about it?

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 6:1–7

*Dear God, thank you that every job in your family matters. Help me to notice when someone is being left out, and to do something about it. Show me how I can help. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What problem came up in the church as it grew?
  A: The Greek-speaking believers complained that their widows were being left out when food was handed out each day.
- Q: How did the apostles solve the problem?
  A: They asked the believers to choose seven wise men full of the Holy Spirit to be in charge of giving out food, so the apostles could keep praying and teaching.
- Q: Is there anyone being left out in your class, your church or your family? What could you do?
  A: Think together about who might feel left out, and one practical way to include them.

## Stephen Sees Jesus

> "Lord, do not hold this sin against them." — Acts 7:60

Stephen was one of the seven helpers. He was full of God's grace and power, and he did great wonders and miracles among the people.

Some men began to argue with Stephen. But they couldn't win, because Stephen spoke with great wisdom, given by the Holy Spirit. So they paid some men to tell lies about him. "We heard him say terrible things against Moses and against God!" they said.

So Stephen was arrested and brought before the council of leaders. And all the people in the council looked at him, and they saw that his face was shining like the face of an angel.

"Are these things true?" the high priest asked.

Stephen answered with a long speech. He told the whole story of God's people, all the way from Abraham to Joseph and Moses, and David and Solomon. You know most of those stories! And he showed how, again and again, God's people had turned away from the people God sent to them. "And now," Stephen said, "you have done the same thing. You have betrayed and killed the Righteous One, Jesus."

When the leaders heard this, they were furious.

But Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked up to heaven. And he saw the glory of God, and Jesus standing at God's right hand.

"Look!" said Stephen. "I see heaven open, and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!"

At this, the leaders covered their ears and shouted at the top of their voices. They all rushed at him and dragged him out of the city, and began to throw stones at him. The men who were throwing stones took off their coats and laid them at the feet of a young man named Saul.

While they were throwing stones at him, Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit." Then he fell on his knees and cried out, "Lord, do not hold this sin against them!"

And when he had said this, he fell asleep. Stephen had died.

Stephen was the first person to die because he followed Jesus. And did you notice what he said? Just like Jesus on the cross, Stephen prayed for the people who were killing him. "Lord, do not hold this sin against them."

Jesus was standing to welcome Stephen home. Stephen didn't die alone or afraid. He saw Jesus waiting for him.

And Saul was there, watching, agreeing that Stephen should die.

That day, a terrible persecution began against the church in Jerusalem. Saul went from house to house, dragging Christians off to prison. And the believers were scattered all over Judea and Samaria.

But you'll see what happened next. God had a plan, even for this. And He had a plan for Saul too.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 6:8–15; 7:54–8:3

*Dear Jesus, thank you that you are waiting for everyone who loves you. Please help me to forgive people who hurt me, the way Stephen did, and the way you did. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: What did Stephen see when he looked up to heaven?
  A: The glory of God, and Jesus standing at God's right hand.
- Q: What did Stephen pray for the people who were throwing stones at him?
  A: "Lord, do not hold this sin against them," just as Jesus prayed for the people who crucified Him.
- Q: Stephen forgave the people who hurt him. Why is that so hard, and how can God help us?
  A: It's very hard to forgive people who hurt us. But Jesus forgave us, and the Holy Spirit can help us to forgive too. (This story is about a martyr's death; let your child ask about it, and point to Jesus waiting to welcome Stephen.)

## Good News in Samaria

> "So there was great joy in that city." — Acts 8:8

After Stephen died, the believers in Jerusalem were in great danger. Saul and others were hunting them down and putting them in prison. So many of them ran away from Jerusalem and scattered to towns and villages all over the country.

That sounds like bad news for the church, doesn't it? The believers had to leave their homes. They were scattered far and wide.

But here's what the Bible says happened next: "Those who had been scattered preached the word wherever they went."

Everywhere they went, they told people about Jesus! The persecution was meant to stop the good news. Instead, it spread the good news further than ever.

Philip, another of the seven helpers, went to a city in Samaria.

Do you remember the Samaritans? The Jews and the Samaritans didn't like each other at all. But Jesus had talked with the Samaritan woman at the well. He had told the story of the Good Samaritan. And He had said His followers would be witnesses "in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria." Now it was happening.

Philip told the people in Samaria about Jesus, the Christ. And the crowds listened carefully to everything he said. God did amazing miracles through Philip. People who were sick were healed. People who couldn't walk got up and walked. Evil spirits came out of people with a loud cry.

So there was great joy in that city!

Many people in Samaria believed in Jesus and were baptised, both men and women.

When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that the Samaritans had accepted God's word, they sent Peter and John to them. Peter and John prayed for the new believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, just as the believers in Jerusalem had.

The Samaritans weren't second-class Christians. They were part of the same family, with the same Holy Spirit.

The people who scattered the believers thought they were stopping God's work. But God used it to spread the good news further. Even when bad things happen, God can use them for good. Do you remember Joseph, in the first book? "You meant it for evil, but God meant it for good."

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 8:1–17

*Dear God, thank you that you can use even bad things for good. Thank you that the good news about Jesus is for everyone, everywhere. Help me to tell people about Jesus wherever I go. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why did the believers have to leave Jerusalem?
  A: Because after Stephen died, Saul and others began hunting them down and putting them in prison.
- Q: What did the believers do wherever they went?
  A: They told people about Jesus, so the good news spread further than ever.
- Q: How did God use something bad (the persecution) for good?
  A: The believers who had to run away took the good news to new places, like Samaria, where many people believed. God can bring good out of bad things, as He did for Joseph.

## Philip and the Man from Ethiopia

> "And the eunuch saw him no more, but went on his way rejoicing." — Acts 8:39

One day an angel of the Lord spoke to Philip. "Get up and go south, to the desert road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza."

That was a strange thing to say. Philip was busy in Samaria, where crowds of people were coming to believe in Jesus. Why would God send him away to an empty desert road?

But Philip got up and went.

On the road, Philip saw a chariot. In it was an important man from Ethiopia, a country far away in Africa. He was the treasurer for the Queen of Ethiopia, in charge of all her money. He had been to Jerusalem to worship God, and now he was on his long way home.

As his chariot rolled along, the man was reading out loud from a scroll of the prophet Isaiah.

The Holy Spirit said to Philip, "Go over to that chariot and stay close to it."

So Philip ran up to the chariot. He heard the man reading from Isaiah. "Do you understand what you're reading?" Philip asked.

"How can I," said the man, "unless someone explains it to me?" And he invited Philip to climb up and sit with him.

This is what the man was reading:

"He was led like a sheep to be killed. And as a lamb is silent before the one who cuts its wool, so He did not open His mouth."

"Please tell me," said the man, "who is the prophet talking about? Himself, or someone else?"

Then Philip began with that very part of the Bible, and told him the good news about Jesus.

Do you remember Isaiah's Servant, in the third book, who was pierced for our sins? Philip explained that Isaiah was talking about Jesus. Jesus was the Lamb who was led to be killed. He didn't fight back. He died for our sins, and then He rose again.

As they travelled along the road, they came to some water. "Look, here's water!" said the man. "Is there any reason why I can't be baptised?"

So he ordered the chariot to stop. Philip and the man went down into the water, and Philip baptised him.

When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord suddenly took Philip away. The man didn't see him again. But he went on his way home to Ethiopia, rejoicing!

God sent Philip all the way to a desert road for just one man. God cares about every single person. And through that one man, the good news about Jesus went all the way to Africa.

**The promise kept:** The man was reading Isaiah 53, "He was led like a sheep to the slaughter" (Acts 8:32). Philip "began with this very Scripture and told him the good news about Jesus" (Acts 8:35).

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 8:26–40

*Dear God, thank you that you care about every single person. Help me to listen to you and go where you send me, like Philip did. And help me to understand the Bible and explain it to others. Amen.*

  A: The book of Isaiah, the part about someone being led like a sheep to be killed.
- Q: Who did Philip say Isaiah was talking about?
  A: Jesus, the Lamb who died for our sins and rose again.

  A: That every single person matters to God, enough to send someone a long way just to tell them about Jesus.

## The Light on the Road

> "'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting,' He replied." — Acts 9:5

Do you remember Saul, the young man who watched while Stephen died? After that, Saul hated the followers of Jesus more and more. He went from house to house in Jerusalem, dragging men and women off to prison.

But that wasn't enough for Saul. He went to the high priest and asked for letters to take to the synagogues in the city of Damascus, far away to the north. The letters said that if Saul found anyone there who followed Jesus, he could arrest them and bring them back to Jerusalem as prisoners.

So Saul set off for Damascus with some other men.

But as he came near the city, suddenly a light from heaven flashed all around him. It was brighter than the midday sun. Saul fell to the ground.

And he heard a voice say to him, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?"

"Who are You, Lord?" Saul asked.

"I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting," the voice replied. "Now get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do."

The men travelling with Saul stood there, unable to speak. They heard the sound, but they couldn't see anyone.

Saul got up from the ground. But when he opened his eyes, he couldn't see anything at all. He was blind! So the men took him by the hand and led him into Damascus.

For three days Saul couldn't see. And he didn't eat or drink anything.

Think about what Jesus said: "Why are you persecuting Me?" Saul thought he was hurting Christians. But Jesus said Saul was hurting Him. When someone hurts the people who follow Jesus, Jesus feels it as if it were happening to Him. That's how much He loves His people.

Saul had been so sure he was right. He thought he was serving God by attacking the followers of Jesus. But now he knew: Jesus was alive. Jesus was the Lord. And Saul had been fighting against Him.

Saul sat in the dark for three days, with nothing to eat or drink, and with plenty of time to think. Everything he had believed had been turned upside down.

What would happen to him now?

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 9:1–9

*Dear Jesus, thank you that nobody is too far away for you to reach, not even someone who hates you. Thank you that you love your people so much. Amen.*

### Talk about it together

- Q: Why was Saul going to Damascus?
  A: To arrest any followers of Jesus he found there and bring them back to Jerusalem as prisoners.
- Q: What happened to Saul on the road?
  A: A bright light from heaven flashed around him, he fell to the ground, and Jesus spoke to him. When he got up, he was blind.
- Q: Jesus said Saul was persecuting Him, not just the Christians. What does that tell us about how Jesus feels about His people?
  A: That He loves them so much that when they are hurt, He feels it Himself.

## Brave Ananias

> "This man is My chosen instrument to carry My name before the Gentiles and their kings, and before the people of Israel." — Acts 9:15

In Damascus there lived a follower of Jesus called Ananias. One day the Lord spoke to him in a vision. "Ananias!"

"Yes, Lord," he answered.

"Go to the house of Judas on Straight Street," the Lord said, "and ask for a man from Tarsus called Saul. He is praying. In a vision he has seen a man called Ananias come and put his hands on him, so that he can see again."

Ananias was horrified! "Lord," he said, "I've heard so many people talk about this man, and all the terrible things he has done to Your people in Jerusalem. And he has come here with permission from the chief priests to arrest everyone who calls on Your name!"

Ananias was afraid. Going to see Saul would be like walking into a lion's den.

But the Lord said, "Go! This man is My chosen instrument to carry My name to people who aren't Jews, and to their kings, and to the people of Israel."

So Ananias went. He found the house and went in. And he put his hands on Saul.

Then Ananias said two words that must have been very hard to say: "Brother Saul."

Brother. Saul had come to Damascus to arrest Ananias and his friends. And Ananias called him "Brother".

"Brother Saul," said Ananias, "the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road as you were coming here, has sent me so that you can see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

Immediately, something like scales fell from Saul's eyes, and he could see again! He got up and was baptised. Then he ate some food, and got his strength back.

And straight away, Saul began to go to the synagogues in Damascus and tell everyone, "Jesus is the Son of God!"

Everyone who heard him was astonished. "Isn't this the man who caused so much trouble for the followers of Jesus in Jerusalem?" they said. "Didn't he come here to arrest them?"

But Saul grew more and more powerful in his preaching. He proved that Jesus is the Christ.

Saul, the man who hated Jesus, became one of Jesus' greatest followers. Later he was known by another name, Paul. And he wrote many of the books in the New Testament.

But none of it would have happened if Ananias hadn't been brave enough to obey God, and kind enough to call his enemy "Brother".

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 9:10–22

*Dear God, thank you that you can change anyone's heart. Please make me brave like Ananias, to obey you even when I'm scared, and to be kind even to people who have been unkind. Amen.*

  A: Because Saul had done terrible things to Jesus' followers and had come to Damascus to arrest them.
- Q: What were the first words Ananias said to Saul?
  A: "Brother Saul." He welcomed his former enemy as part of God's family.
- Q: Ananias obeyed God even though he was scared. When might God want you to be brave and kind like Ananias?
  A: Perhaps by being friendly to someone who has been unkind, or welcoming someone others are scared of or avoid.

## Down the Wall in a Basket

> "One night, however, his disciples took him and lowered him in a basket through a window in the wall." — Acts 9:25

Saul kept preaching about Jesus in Damascus, and many people listened. But some of the leaders there were furious. The man who was supposed to arrest Christians had become a Christian himself!

So they made a plan to kill Saul. Day and night, they watched the city gates, waiting to catch him when he tried to leave.

But Saul found out about their plot.

Damascus had a huge wall all around it. Some houses were built right into the wall, with windows looking out over the edge. So one night, Saul's friends put him in a big basket, tied ropes to it, and let him down through a window in the city wall! The basket swung down the outside of the wall, all the way to the ground. And Saul escaped into the dark.

The man who had come to Damascus to drag Christians off to prison now had to escape from Damascus in a basket, like a parcel!

Saul went back to Jerusalem. He tried to join the followers of Jesus there. But they were all afraid of him. They didn't believe he had really become a follower of Jesus. They thought it might be a trick, so that he could find them and arrest them.

Who could blame them? Saul had dragged their friends off to prison. He had watched Stephen die.

But one man was brave enough to give Saul a chance. Do you remember Barnabas, the "Son of Encouragement"? Barnabas took Saul and brought him to the apostles. He told them how Saul had seen the Lord on the road, and how the Lord had spoken to him, and how Saul had preached boldly about Jesus in Damascus.

Because Barnabas stood up for him, the believers welcomed Saul. He stayed with them, and went all around Jerusalem, speaking boldly in the name of the Lord.

But soon some people wanted to kill Saul in Jerusalem too. So the believers took him down to the coast and sent him home to Tarsus, the city where he had grown up.

Then the church all over Judea, Galilee and Samaria had a time of peace. It grew stronger, and more and more people became followers of Jesus.

Everybody needs a Barnabas: someone who believes in them and gives them a chance. And we can be a Barnabas for other people.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 9:23–31

*Dear God, thank you for the people who have encouraged me. Please help me to be like Barnabas: to give people a chance, to stand up for them, and to welcome them. Amen.*

- Q: How did Saul escape from Damascus?
  A: His friends let him down over the city wall in a big basket at night.
- Q: Why were the believers in Jerusalem afraid of Saul, and who helped him?
  A: Because he used to arrest and hurt Christians, and they didn't believe he had changed. Barnabas brought him to the apostles and told them what had happened to him.
- Q: Barnabas gave Saul a chance when nobody else would. Who could you be a "Barnabas" for?
  A: Someone new, someone others don't trust, or someone trying to make a fresh start. You could welcome them, stand up for them or encourage them.

## "Tabitha, Get Up!"

> "In Joppa there was a disciple named Tabitha (which is translated as Dorcas), who was always occupied with works of kindness and charity." — Acts 9:36

Peter travelled around visiting the believers in different towns. In a town called Lydda, he met a man called Aeneas, who was paralysed and had been stuck in bed for eight years.

"Aeneas," Peter said to him, "Jesus Christ heals you! Get up and roll up your mat." And straight away Aeneas got up! Everyone who lived in that area saw him, and they turned to the Lord.

Not far away, by the sea, was a town called Joppa. In Joppa there lived a woman who followed Jesus. Her name was Tabitha, and in Greek her name was Dorcas. Both names mean "gazelle".

Dorcas was always doing good and helping poor people. She especially loved to sew. She made coats and clothes for the widows in Joppa, women whose husbands had died and who had nobody to take care of them.

But one day, Dorcas became ill and died. Her friends washed her body and laid her in an upstairs room.

The believers in Joppa heard that Peter was in Lydda, nearby. So they sent two men to him. "Please come straight away!" they begged.

Peter got up and went with them. When he arrived, they took him upstairs to the room where Dorcas lay. All the widows stood around Peter, crying. And they showed him the coats and other clothes that Dorcas had made for them while she was alive.

Can you imagine them? "Look, she made me this coat!" "And she made this for me!" Dorcas had shown her love for Jesus by caring for them, stitch by stitch.

Peter sent them all out of the room. Then he knelt down and prayed.

Then he turned towards the body and said, "Tabitha, get up!"

And she opened her eyes! When she saw Peter, she sat up.

Do you remember what Jesus said to Jairus's daughter? "Little girl, get up!" Peter had been in that room with Jesus. Now Jesus was working through Peter in the same way.

Peter took her by the hand and helped her to her feet. Then he called the believers and the widows, and showed them that she was alive!

The news spread all over Joppa, and many people believed in the Lord.

Dorcas didn't preach sermons or do great miracles. She sewed clothes for people who needed them. But her kindness was so precious that a whole town cried when she died. And when she came back to life, many people believed in Jesus.

You don't have to do big, important things to serve Jesus. Small acts of kindness, done with love, matter to God.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 9:32–43

*Dear God, thank you for people like Dorcas, who show your love through kindness. Help me to use what I'm good at to help other people. Amen.*

  A: She was always doing good and helping the poor, especially by sewing clothes for widows.
- Q: What did the widows show Peter when he arrived?
  A: The coats and clothes Dorcas had made for them.
- Q: Dorcas used her sewing to help people. What are you good at that you could use to help someone?
  A: Drawing, baking, making things, helping, listening, playing music. Any gift can be used to show God's love to others.

## Peter's Strange Dream

> "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean." — Acts 10:15

In the city of Caesarea, there lived a Roman army officer called Cornelius. He wasn't a Jew. But he and his whole family loved God. He gave generously to people who were poor, and he prayed to God every day.

One afternoon, at about three o'clock, Cornelius saw an angel of God in a vision. "Cornelius!" said the angel.

Cornelius stared at him, frightened. "What is it, Lord?" he asked.

"God has heard your prayers and seen your gifts to the poor," said the angel. "Now send some men to the town of Joppa to bring back a man called Simon Peter. He is staying with Simon the tanner, whose house is by the sea."

So Cornelius sent two servants and a soldier to Joppa.

The next day, at about noon, while they were on their way, Peter went up on the flat roof of the house to pray. He was hungry, and he wanted something to eat. While the meal was being prepared, Peter had a vision.

He saw heaven open, and something like a huge sheet being let down to earth by its four corners. In it were all kinds of animals, reptiles and birds, ones that Jewish people weren't allowed to eat.

Then a voice said to him, "Get up, Peter. Kill and eat."

"No, Lord!" said Peter. "I have never eaten anything that God's law says is unclean!"

The voice spoke to him a second time: "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean."

This happened three times. Then the sheet was taken back up into heaven.

Peter was very puzzled. What could it mean?

While Peter was still thinking about it, the men from Cornelius arrived at the gate. And the Holy Spirit said to Peter, "Three men are looking for you. Get up and go downstairs. Go with them, and don't worry, because I have sent them."

So Peter went down and met the men. "I'm the one you're looking for," he said. "Why have you come?"

They told him about Cornelius and the angel. So Peter invited them in to stay the night. And the next day, he set off with them to Caesarea.

In those days, Jewish people didn't go into the homes of people who weren't Jews, or eat with them. They thought of them as "unclean". But God was teaching Peter something new. The dream wasn't really about food. It was about people.

God was about to show Peter that the good news about Jesus is for everyone, Jews and non-Jews alike. Nobody is "unclean" to God. Jesus died for everyone.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 10:1–23

*Dear God, thank you that you love people from every country and every background. Please help me never to think anyone is less important than me. Amen.*

  A: A big sheet full of animals Jewish people weren't allowed to eat, and a voice saying, "Do not call anything impure that God has made clean."
- Q: The dream was really about people, not food. What was God teaching Peter?
  A: That the good news about Jesus is for everyone, and that nobody should be treated as "unclean" or less important.

## Good News for Everyone

> "I now truly understand that God does not show favoritism, but welcomes those from every nation who fear Him and do what is right." — Acts 10:34–35

When Peter arrived at Cornelius's house in Caesarea, Cornelius was waiting for him. He had invited all his relatives and close friends.

As Peter came in, Cornelius met him and fell down at his feet to worship him. But Peter pulled him up. "Stand up," he said. "I'm only a man myself."

Peter went in and found a large crowd of people waiting. And he said to them, "You know that it's against our law for a Jew to visit someone who isn't Jewish, or go into their house. But God has shown me that I shouldn't call anyone unclean. So when you sent for me, I came without arguing. Now, may I ask why you sent for me?"

Cornelius told Peter about the angel. "So now we are all here in the presence of God," he said, "to listen to everything the Lord has told you to say to us."

Then Peter began to speak. "I now truly understand that God doesn't have favourites. He welcomes people from every nation who honour Him and do what is right."

And Peter told them all about Jesus. How Jesus went around doing good and healing people. How He was put to death on a cross. How God raised Him from the dead on the third day. How Peter and the others had seen Him and even eaten with Him after He rose. "All the prophets speak about Him," said Peter, "and say that everyone who believes in Him will have their sins forgiven through His name."

While Peter was still speaking, the Holy Spirit came on everyone who was listening!

The Jewish believers who had come with Peter were astonished. The Holy Spirit had been poured out even on people who weren't Jews! They heard them speaking in other languages and praising God, just like on the day of Pentecost.

"Can anyone stop these people from being baptised with water?" said Peter. "They have received the Holy Spirit just as we did!"

So Cornelius and his family and friends were baptised in the name of Jesus Christ. And they asked Peter to stay with them for a few more days.

When Peter went back to Jerusalem, some of the believers there criticised him. "You went into the house of people who aren't Jews, and ate with them!" they said. So Peter told them the whole story, from the beginning.

When they heard it, they had nothing more to say. They praised God. "So God has given even people who aren't Jews the chance to turn to Him and have life!"

Do you remember God's very first promise to Abraham? "All the families of the earth will be blessed through you." Now it was really happening. The door was wide open, for everyone.

**The promise kept:** Peter told Cornelius, "All the prophets testify about Him that everyone who believes in Him receives forgiveness of sins through His name" (Acts 10:43). And God's first promise to Abraham, that all nations would be blessed through his family, was opening up to the whole world.

  A: The Holy Spirit came on everyone listening, even though they weren't Jews, just as He had on the day of Pentecost.
- Q: Peter learned that "God doesn't have favourites." How can we treat people the way God does?
  A: By being kind and welcoming to everyone, whatever their country, colour, language or background, because God loves them all.

## They Called Them Christians

> "The disciples were first called Christians at Antioch." — Acts 11:26

Do you remember the believers who were scattered after Stephen died? Some of them travelled a long way, to places like the island of Cyprus and the big city of Antioch, in Syria.

At first, they only told the good news to Jewish people. But some of them, men from Cyprus and from Cyrene in North Africa, started telling Greek people about the Lord Jesus too.

And the Lord's power was with them. A great number of people in Antioch believed and turned to the Lord!

When the church in Jerusalem heard about it, they sent Barnabas to Antioch to see what was happening.

When Barnabas arrived and saw how God was working, he was so glad! And of course, he did what he always did: he encouraged them. He urged them all to stay true to the Lord with all their hearts. Barnabas was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and faith. And even more people came to know the Lord.

There were so many new believers in Antioch that Barnabas needed help to teach them all. And he knew just the man.

Barnabas went all the way to Tarsus to look for Saul. When he found him, he brought him back to Antioch.

For a whole year, Barnabas and Saul met with the church and taught lots and lots of people about Jesus.

And it was in Antioch that the followers of Jesus were first called "Christians". The name means "Christ's people", or "the ones who belong to Christ".

People in Antioch noticed that these believers talked about Christ all the time, and lived like Christ. So they gave them a name. It might have started as a nickname, maybe even a joke. But it stuck. And followers of Jesus all over the world have been called Christians ever since.

Then a prophet called Agabus came to Antioch. Through the Holy Spirit, he said that a great famine was coming. And it happened, just as he said. Food became scarce, and the believers back in Judea were going hungry.

So the Christians in Antioch decided to help. Each one gave whatever they could. And they sent their gifts to the believers in Judea, carried by Barnabas and Saul.

The new church in Antioch, full of people who weren't Jews, sent help to the church in Jerusalem, full of Jewish believers. That's what it means to be one family in Christ.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 11:19–30

*Dear Jesus, thank you that I can belong to you. Please help me to live in a way that shows I am one of your people. Help me to share with people who need help. Amen.*

- Q: Where were the followers of Jesus first called "Christians", and what does it mean?
  A: In Antioch. It means "Christ's people", the ones who belong to Christ.
- Q: People called them Christians because they talked about Christ and lived like Him. What would people notice about you that shows you belong to Jesus?

## Peter in Prison

> "Peter was kept in prison, but the church was fervently praying to God for him." — Acts 12:5

There was a king called Herod, who ruled over Judea. He was the grandson of the King Herod who tried to kill baby Jesus. And this King Herod began to attack the church.

He had James, the brother of John, put to death with the sword. James was one of Jesus' first followers, one of the fishermen who left their nets.

When Herod saw that this pleased some of the people, he arrested Peter too. It was the time of the Passover festival. Herod put Peter in prison, with four groups of four soldiers to guard him. He planned to put Peter on trial after the Passover.

So Peter was kept in prison. But the church was praying hard to God for him.

On the night before Herod was going to bring him out for trial, Peter was asleep, chained between two soldiers. More guards stood at the prison door.

Peter was sleeping! He knew he might be put to death the next day. But he was so peaceful, trusting God, that he fell fast asleep.

Suddenly an angel of the Lord appeared, and a light shone in the prison cell. The angel tapped Peter on the side and woke him up. "Quick, get up!" he said.

And the chains fell off Peter's wrists!

"Get dressed and put on your sandals," said the angel. Peter did. "Wrap your coat around you and follow me," said the angel.

So Peter followed him out of the prison. But he didn't know if it was really happening. He thought he must be dreaming!

They walked past the first guard, and then the second. Then they came to the big iron gate that led out into the city. And it opened for them all by itself!

They went out and walked to the end of the street. Then suddenly the angel was gone.

Peter stood there in the night air. And at last he realised it was real. "Now I know for sure," he said, "that the Lord sent His angel and rescued me from Herod!"

James had died, and Peter had been rescued. We don't always understand why God rescues some people and not others. But God was with both of them. James went to be with Jesus, and Peter was set free to keep on telling people about Him.

And the church had been praying. God heard their prayers.

Herod tried to stop the good news. But the Bible says, "the word of God continued to spread and multiply."

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 12:1–11, 24

## Rhoda at the Gate

> "When she recognized Peter's voice, she was so overjoyed that she forgot to open the gate." — Acts 12:14

When Peter realised that the angel had really rescued him, he went to the house of a woman called Mary, the mother of John Mark. Many believers had gathered there, and they were praying.

They were praying for Peter.

Peter knocked at the outer door. A servant girl called Rhoda came to answer it.

"Who's there?" she might have asked.

And when she recognised Peter's voice, she was so happy that she forgot to open the door! Instead she ran back inside and told everyone, "Peter is standing at the door!"

"You're out of your mind!" they told her.

But Rhoda kept insisting it was true.

"It must be his angel," they said.

Isn't that funny? They had been praying and praying for Peter to be set free. And when God answered their prayer, they didn't believe it! They thought Rhoda was being silly.

Meanwhile, poor Peter was still standing outside in the street, in the middle of the night, knocking and knocking. Herod's soldiers might come looking for him at any moment!

At last they opened the door. And there was Peter! They were completely amazed.

Peter waved his hand to tell them to be quiet. He told them how the Lord had brought him out of prison. "Tell James and the other brothers about this," he said. (This was a different James, probably Jesus' brother, who became a leader of the church in Jerusalem.) Then Peter left, and went somewhere safe.

The next morning, there was a big commotion among the soldiers. "What happened to Peter?" They searched everywhere for him. But they couldn't find him.

Rhoda was just a servant girl. But she was the first to know that God had answered the church's prayers. And she wouldn't stop saying so, even when the grown-ups told her she was out of her mind.

Sometimes God answers our prayers in ways we don't expect. Sometimes the answer is standing right outside the door, knocking! Rhoda reminds us to believe that God really does hear and answer.

**Read it in your Bible:** Acts 12:12–19

*Dear God, thank you that you hear and answer prayer. Help me to believe that you will answer, and to be glad when you do. Amen.*

- Q: Why is it funny that the believers didn't believe Rhoda?
  A: Because they had been praying for Peter to be set free, and when God answered their prayer, they couldn't believe it!
- Q: Has God ever answered one of your prayers? How did you know?
  A: Let your child share. You could also share an answered prayer from your own life, and thank God together.

## Sent Out

> "Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them." — Acts 13:2

The church in Antioch kept on growing. There were prophets and teachers there, people God had given special gifts to lead and teach the church.

There was Barnabas, the encourager. There was Simeon, who was called Niger, which means "black", and Lucius from Cyrene in North Africa. There was Manaen, who had grown up with King Herod. And there was Saul.

What a mix of people! Different countries, different colours, different backgrounds. But all one family in Christ.

One day, while they were worshipping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit spoke to them. "Set apart Barnabas and Saul for Me, for the work I have called them to do."

What was that work? Jesus had said His followers would be His witnesses "to the ends of the earth". So far, the good news had spread from Jerusalem to Judea and Samaria, and as far as Antioch. Now it was time for it to go further. Much further.

So the church fasted and prayed some more. Then they placed their hands on Barnabas and Saul, and sent them off.

The two of them went down to the coast, and got on a ship. And they sailed away across the sea, to the island of Cyprus, where Barnabas had grown up.

It was the beginning of a great adventure, an adventure that would take them across seas and mountains, to cities and countries where nobody had ever heard of Jesus. There would be shipwrecks and storms, prisons and riots, earthquakes and narrow escapes. And in town after town, people would believe in Jesus, and new churches would begin.

On this journey, Luke starts calling Saul by his other name, Paul (Acts 13:9). And that is the name most people know him by today.

Look back at how far the story has come in this book. It started with about a hundred and twenty frightened people in one room in Jerusalem. Now there were thousands and thousands of Christians. There were churches in Jerusalem and Samaria and Damascus and Antioch. The good news had reached a Roman officer and an African treasurer. And now Barnabas and Paul were setting off to take it to the world.

Where would they go? What would happen to them? That is the story of the last book.

*Dear God, thank you for the people who went out to tell the world about Jesus. Thank you for missionaries today. Please show me how I can help the good news reach people who haven't heard it. Amen.*
