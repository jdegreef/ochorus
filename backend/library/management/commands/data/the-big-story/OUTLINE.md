# The Big Story: outline

The whole Bible as one story, for readers aged 13 to 17 (an Ochorus Original). The manuscript in this folder (`the-big-story.md`) is the source of truth; `build_the_big_story` converts it (the closed Markdown subset of `build_rooted.parse`) and checks its shape: Introduction, thirty chapters, Conclusion; every chapter has "Read it:", "Where Jesus is in this", "Think about it:" and "Go deeper:".

## Stance

Mainstream evangelical, as *Real Questions*: Scripture is God's trustworthy Word and one story with Jesus at its centre (Luke 24:27). Where faithful Christians genuinely differ — how to read Genesis 1 (young-earth, old-earth, literary views), the end-times timeline in Revelation, the millennium — the chapter says so in a sentence or two, names what all agree on, and takes no side.

## Each chapter

- `**Read it:**` the passage(s) to read in a Bible, short enough for one sitting (about 1–4 chapters), e.g. "Genesis 1–2".
- The story (750–950 words): retold vividly and accurately in plain words, with short BSB quotations; where it sits in the bigger story; honest about the hard parts (violence, failure, suffering).
- `### Where Jesus is in this`: one or two paragraphs showing how this part of the story points to, or is fulfilled in, Jesus — grounded in the New Testament's own use of it, never fanciful.
- `**Think about it:**` one real question.
- `**Go deeper:**` one chapter of a classic in the Ochorus library, summarised truly; no chapter used twice.

## The thirty chapters

**Part 1 · In the Beginning** (Genesis 1–11)
1. The God Who Speaks (Genesis 1–2) — creation, image of God, the garden
2. The Day Everything Broke (Genesis 3) — the fall and the first promise (3:15)
3. Floods and Towers (Genesis 4–11) — Cain, Noah, Babel

**Part 2 · A Family Chosen** (Genesis 12–50)
4. A Promise to Abraham (Genesis 12; 15) — blessing for all nations
5. The Lamb God Provides (Genesis 22) — Isaac on Moriah
6. The Wrestler (Genesis 28; 32) — Jacob becomes Israel
7. Meant for Good (Genesis 37; 45; 50) — Joseph

**Part 3 · Rescued and Called** (Exodus–Deuteronomy)
8. The God Who Hears (Exodus 1–3) — slavery, Moses, the burning bush
9. Blood on the Doorposts (Exodus 12–14) — Passover and the sea
10. Ten Words on a Mountain (Exodus 19–20) — covenant and law
11. God Moves In (Exodus 25; 40; Leviticus 16) — tabernacle, sacrifice, Day of Atonement
12. Forty Years (Numbers 13–14; Deuteronomy 6) — unbelief, wilderness, "Hear, O Israel"

**Part 4 · The Land and the Kings** (Joshua–Kings)
13. Into the Land (Joshua 1–6; 24) — courage, Jericho, choose this day (the conquest handled honestly)
14. Everyone Did What Was Right in Their Own Eyes (Judges 2; Ruth 1–4) — the cycle, and a foreign woman's loyalty
15. A King Like the Nations (1 Samuel 8; 16–17) — Saul, David chosen, Goliath
16. A House Forever (2 Samuel 7; 11–12; Psalm 51) — the promise to David, and his great sin
17. The Kingdom Splits (1 Kings 3; 11–12) — Solomon's wisdom and fall, two kingdoms

**Part 5 · Prophets, Exile and Waiting**
18. Fire on the Mountain (1 Kings 18–19) — Elijah, and the prophets' call to return
19. Songs and Wisdom (Psalm 23; Proverbs 1; Job 38; Ecclesiastes 12) — the Bible's poetry
20. By the Rivers of Babylon (2 Kings 25; Jeremiah 29–31; Daniel 1; 6) — exile and a new covenant promised
21. Coming Home (Ezra 1; Nehemiah 8; Esther 4) — return and rebuilding
22. The Servant Who Suffers (Isaiah 9; 53; Malachi 3–4) — the promises, and four hundred quiet years

**Part 6 · The King Comes** (the Gospels)
23. The Word Became Flesh (Luke 1–2; John 1) — the birth of Jesus
24. The Kingdom Is Here (Mark 1–2; Matthew 5–7) — preaching, healing, the Sermon on the Mount
25. Who Do You Say I Am? (Mark 8–9; John 11) — Peter's confession, transfiguration, Lazarus
26. It Is Finished (Mark 14–15; John 19) — the last supper and the cross
27. He Is Risen (Luke 24; John 20–21) — resurrection, Emmaus, Thomas, Peter restored

**Part 7 · The Story Goes On**
28. Wind and Fire (Acts 1–2) — ascension, Pentecost, the church
29. To the Ends of the Earth (Acts 9; 15; Romans 8) — Paul, the nations, the letters
30. All Things New (Revelation 21–22) — the new creation, the end that is a beginning

Introduction: "One Story" (how the 66 books fit; why the Bible is hard to read and how this book helps).
Conclusion: "Your Place in the Story" (the story isn't over; how to keep reading the Bible yourself; a simple plan).
