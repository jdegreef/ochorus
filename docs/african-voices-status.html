1 · Executive summary

Five African-authored books, and Ochorus's first sub-Saharan voice.

This initiative adds African and African-diaspora authors — their bios, books and sermons — to Ochorus, and deepens those already present. Ochorus is public-domain only, and that single constraint shapes every choice below.

Since it began, five African-authored books have shipped — three of them built in this push. Together they gave Ochorus its first sub-Saharan African book and completed a cluster of early Black women preachers, a seam of vivid nineteenth-century spiritual memoir that now reads as a chorus rather than a single voice.

The chief lesson of the work so far is a practical one about where effort goes. It is not the writing or the import that is expensive — it is the state of the source text.

The lesson that governs every estimate

Source cleanliness governs effort. Clean transcriptions — CCEL, Project Gutenberg, DocSouth — import in about an hour. Internet Archive OCR scans cost hours of per-book text repair (Crowther's journal alone needed some 350 corrections). Always prefer a clean source; when only OCR exists, budget for it.

Zilpha Elaw

In progress

Memoirs (1846)

The only public-domain source is a single badly OCR'd Internet Archive scan — systematic damage like “tliis”→this, plus whole garbage lines. On top of that, the memoir is a continuous narrative with no chapter divisions, so it needs editorial chapter-splitting.

That makes it the hardest of the four: Crowther-level OCR repair and the chapterization that Lee's journal required, in one book.

02

North African church fathers, via CCEL

Recommended

The single biggest African gap · a clean source

CCEL carries clean transcriptions, so these import fast. Cyprian of Carthage already has a bio but no works — start with On the Lord's Prayer. Then Tertullian (On Prayer, To the Martyrs), Perpetua (The Passion of Perpetua and Felicity — the earliest surviving writing by a Christian woman), Clement of Alexandria, Origen (On Prayer), and Macarius of Egypt.

Watch the translation-copyright gate: use pre-1929 translators.

Recommended

6 · Lessons & constraints

What the work has taught, in four lines.

Public-domain gate

Modern figures — the Revival generation — can be honoured with bio pages only; their books won't be public domain for decades.

Source cleanliness is everything

CCEL, Gutenberg and DocSouth are clean transcriptions (an import is about an hour); Internet Archive OCR is hours of hand-repair per book (Crowther ~350 fixes; Elaw comparable). Always prefer a clean source; when only OCR exists, budget for it.

Survey before building

The library moves fast — parallel sessions shipped Allen and Smith. Always check the catalog, authors.json and fixtures first; a near-duplicate of Allen was caught this way.

New author + book needs no migration

The seed creates them together. A bio-only new author, however, does need a migration.
