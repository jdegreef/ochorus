# Tukutendereza — claims a reader from the region should check

Twelve of these fourteen chapters are about real people, most of whom died
within living memory and several of whom have living families. This file
records what the book asserts, what it declines to assert, and where the
evidence is thin.

## Sources and their limits

Research was done by web search. **Two of the best sources could not be read
directly**: `dacb.org` (the Dictionary of African Christian Biography) and
`en.wikipedia.org` are both blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so
their content reached me only through search-result summaries. That is a real
limitation on the depth of what follows, and a reviewer with access to DACB
should check this book against it before approval.

## Asserted more cautiously than the sources state

- **Kigozi's death is now stated precisely**: 26 January 1936, aged twenty-six,
  buried on Cathedral Hill at Namirembe at a crowded funeral officiated by his
  brother Simeon Nsibambi, with the word he carried to the synod cut into the
  headstone. An earlier draft hedged on all of this; it is attested.
- **Sabiti's Command Post encounter with Amin (early 1972).** The bag, the
  flask, the diabetes, the Bible and the threat repeated three times come from
  a Ugandan newspaper account. **Chapter 7 deliberately reports only the
  gesture** and states in the text that accounts of what was said vary.
- **Luwum's "I am prepared to die in the army of Jesus."** Widely attributed;
  chapter 10 introduces it as "some version of", not as a citation.
- **The complaint about Nsibambi's evangelism** ("he's gone mad… he's just left
  my gardener") is given as remembered speech, not a verbatim quotation.

## Deliberately NOT asserted

- **No named woman appears in chapter 12.** Women were at least half the
  movement, and their names are far less well recorded than the men's. The
  chapter says so explicitly and describes what the revival did in women's
  lives — voice in the meeting, leverage inside marriage, a public identity —
  rather than constructing a representative figure. **If a reviewer can supply
  named, documented women of the revival, this chapter should be rewritten
  around them.** That would be a clear improvement.
- ~~Nsibambi's schooling, war service and decoration, and his father's rank~~
  **— CORRECTED.** These are attested (Christian History Institute) and are now
  stated in *A Hidden Fire* ch1. My first research pass missed the sources and
  I wrongly recorded them as unverifiable. See that book's verification file.
- **The internal divisions of the 1960s–70s** are acknowledged in chapter 6 in
  general terms. I did not have enough to attribute positions to individuals,
  and did not try.

## Relationship to existing library content

- **Nsibambi** has an author bio and the book *A Hidden Fire*. Chapter 2 is
  restricted to the OUTWARD angle — what he set off in others — so it does not
  retell either. *A Hidden Fire* keeps the interior question and the hidden years.
- **Kivengere** has a 1,786-word author bio. Chapter 9 is restricted to one
  thing: the 1977 book and the problem of loving the man who killed his
  archbishop.
- **Sabiti** now has an author bio added in the same change. The bio narrates
  the life; chapter 7 states it and turns it on the reader.
