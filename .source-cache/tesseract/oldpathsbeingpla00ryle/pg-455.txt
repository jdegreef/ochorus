I. You have, first, a description of all true Christians.
It is most simple, most beautiful, and most true. Great
is the contrast between the description given by the Holy
Ghost of a Christian, aud the description which is given
by man! With man it is often enough to say that such a
one “is a Churchman,” or that such a one “belongs to
this body of Christians, or to that.” Jt is not so when the
Holy Ghost draws the picture. The Eloly Ghost describes
a Christian as a man “who comes unto God by Christ.”

True Christians come nnto God. They are not as many
who turn their backs upon Him :—who “go into a far
country,” like the prodigal son:—“ who go out,” like Cain,
“from the presence of the Lord:”—who are “alienated,
strangers, and enemies im their mind by wicked works.”
(Coloss. i. 21.) They are reconciled to God and friends of
God. They are not as many, who dislike everything that
belongs to God,—His word, Ilis day, His ordinances, His
people, Ilis house. They love all that belongs to their
Master. The very footprints of His steps are dear unto
them. “His name is as ointment poured forth.” (Cant.
i. 3.)—They are not as many, who are content with coming
to church, or with coming to chapel, or with coming to the
Lord’s table. They go further than that. They “come
unto God,” and in communion with God they live.
