I know not that I can wind up this part of my subject
better than by quoting the latter part of the Seventeenth
Article of the Church of England. J commend it to the
special attention of all my readers, and particularly the
last paragraph.—‘ As the godly consideration of Predesti-
nation, and our Election in Christ, is full of sweet, pleasant,
and unspeakable comfort to godly persons, and such as
feel in themselves the working of the Spirit of Christ,
mortifying the works of the flesh, and their earthly
members, and drawing up their mind to high and heavenly
things, as well because it doth greatly establish and confirm
their faith of eternal Salvation to be enjoyed through
Christ, as because it doth fervently kindle their love
towards God: so, for curious and carnal persons, lacking
the Spirit of Christ, to have continually before their eyes
the sentence of God’s Predestination, is a most dangerous
downfall, whereby the Devil dotl: thrust them either into
desperation, or into wretchedness of most unclean living,
no less perilous than desperation.
