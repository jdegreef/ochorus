Ii. The next thing that I wish to do is to fence the
doctrine of Election with eautions, and to guard it
against abuse.

This is a branch of the subject which I hold to be of
vast importance. All revealed truth is liable to be wrested
and perverted. It is one of Satan’s chief devices to make
the Gospel odious by tempting men to distort it. Perhaps
no part of Christian theology bas suffered so much damage
in this way as the doctrine of personal Election. Let me
proceed to explain what I mean.

“Tam not one of God’s Elect,” says one man. “It is
no use for me to do anything atallin religion. It is waste
of time for me to kcep the Sabbath, attend the public
worship of God, read my Bible, say my prayers. If I am
to be saved, I shall be saved. If I am to be lost, 1 shall
be lost. In the mean time I sit still and wait.” This is
a sore disease of soul, But I fear it is a very common
one !

“T am one of God’s Elect,” says another man. “I am
sure to be saved and go to heaven at last, no matter how
I may live and go on, Iixhortations to holiness are legal.
Recommendations to watch, and crucify self, are bondage.
Though I fall, God sees no sin in me and loves me all the
same, Though I often give way to temptation, God will
not let me be altogether lost. Where is the use of doubts
and fears and anxieties? I am confident I am one of
the Elect, and as such I shall be found in glory.” This
again, is a sore disease. But I fear it is not altogether
uncommon.
