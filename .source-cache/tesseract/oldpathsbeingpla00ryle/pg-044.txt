(c) Some object that there are occasional statements in
the Bible which are monstrous, absurd, and imeredible.
Are they really obliged to believe that Eve was tempted
by the devil in the form of a serpent,—that Noah was
saved in an ark,—that the Israelites crossed the Red Sea
between two walls of watcr,—that Balaam’s ass spoke,—
and that Jonah actually went into the whale’s belly ?_ Are
all these statements inspired ?—My answer is that Christ’s
apostles speak of these things as historical facts, and were
more likely to know the truth about them than we are,
After all, do we believe in miracles or not? Do we be-
lieve that Christ Himself rose from the dead? Let us
stick to that one grand miracle first, and disprove it if we
can. If we do believe it, it is foolish to object to things
because they are miraculous.

(d) Some object that there are things mentioned occa-
sionally in the Bible which are so trifling that they are
unworthy to be called inspired. They point to St. Paul’s
writing about his cloak, and books, and parchments, and ask
if we really think that the Apostle wrote about such little
matters by inspiration of God ?—I answer that the least
things affecting any of God’s children are not too small tor
the notice of Him who “numbers the hairs of our heads.”
There are excellent and edifying lessons to be learned
from the cloak and the parchments, as Robert Haldane
has shown most convincingly, in his work on the Evidences
