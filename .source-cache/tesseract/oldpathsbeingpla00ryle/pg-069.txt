will then demand attention, and must be considered.
The value of the soul in the hght of time is one thing,
but seen in the light of eternity it is quite another.
Never does living man know the value of the soul so
well as when he is dying, and can keep the world no
longer.

Does any one wish to have a still clearer idea of the
soul’s value? Then go and measure it by the opinions cf
the dead. Read in the sixteenth chapter of St. Luke the
parable of the rich man and Lazarus. When the rich
man awoke in hell and in torments, what did he say to
Abraham? “Send Lazarus to my father’s house :—For I
have five brethren,—that he may testify to them,—lest
they also come to this place of torment.” That rich man
probably thought little or nothing of the souls of others
while he lived upon the earth. Once dead and in the
place of torment, he sees things in their true colours.
Then he thinks of his brethren, and begins to care for,
their salvation. Then he cries, “Send Lazarus to my
father’s house. I have five brethren, Let him testify unto
them.” If that wonderful parable did nothing else, it
