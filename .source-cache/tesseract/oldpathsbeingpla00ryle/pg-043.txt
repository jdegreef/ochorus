(«) Some object that there are occasional statements in
the Bible which contradicet the fucts of history. Are these
all verbally inspired 2—My answer is that it is far more
easy to assert this than to prove it. There is nothing of
which we have so few trustworthy remains as very ancient
history, and if ancient uninspired history and Bible his-
tory seem to disagree, it is generally safer and wiser to
believe that Bible history is right and other history wrong.
At any rate, it is a singular fact tint all recent researches
in Assyria, Babylon, Palestine, and Egypt, show an extra-
ordinary tendency to confirm the perfect accuracy of the
Word of God. The lamented Mr. Smith’s discoveries at
Babylon are a remarkable example of what I mean. There
are buried evidences which God seems to keep in reserve
for these last days. If Bible history and other histories
cannot be made to agree at present, it is safest to
walt.

the Bible which contradict the facts of natural science.
Are these all inspired ?—My answer is again, that it is far
more easy to assert this than to prove it. The Bible was
not written to teach a system of geology, botany, or as-
tronomy, or a history of birds, insects, and animals, and on
matters touching these subjects it wisely uses popular
language, such as common people can understand. No
one thinks of saying that the Astronomer Royal contradicts
