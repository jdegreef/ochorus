The subject is one of peculiar importance in the
present day. Infidelity and scepticism abound everywhere.
Tn one form or another they are to be found in every rank
and class of society. Thousands of Englishmen are not
ashamed to say that they regard the Bible as an old
obsolete Jewish book, which has no special claim on our
faith and obedience, and that it contains many inaccuracies
and defects, Myriads who will not go so far as this are
wavering and shaken in their belief, and show plainly by
their lives that they are not quite sure the Bible is true.
In a day like this the truce Christian should be able to set
his foot down firmly, and to render a reason of his con-
fidence in God’s Word. He should be able by sound
arguments to meet and silence the gainsaycr, if he cannot
convince him. He should be able to show good cause why
he thinks the Bible is “from heaven, and not of men.”

The subject without doubt is a very dificult one. It
cannot be followed up without entering on ground which
is dark and mysterious to mortal man. It involves the
discussion of things which are miraculous, and supernatural,
and above reason, and cannot be fully explained. But
difficulties must not turn us away from any subject in
religion. There is not a science in the world about which
questions may not be asked which no one can answer. It
is poor philosophy to say we will believe nothing unless
we can understand everything! We must not give up the
subject of inspiration in despair because it contains things
“hard to be understood.” There still remains a vast
amount of ground which is plain to cyery common under-
standing. J invite my readers to occupy this ground with
me to-day, and to hear what I have got to say on the
Divine authority of God’s Word.
