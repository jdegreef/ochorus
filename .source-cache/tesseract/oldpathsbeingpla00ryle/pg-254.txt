Beware that you do not help Satan by becoming an
unjust accuser of yourself, and an unbeliever in the reality
of God’s work of grace. I advise you to pray for more
knowledge of your own heart, of the fulness of Jesus, and
of the devices of the devil. Let doubts and fears drive you
to the throne of grace, stir you up to more prayer, send
you more frequently to Christ. But do not let doubts and
fears rob you of your peace. Believe me, you must be
content to go to heaven as a sinner saved by grace. And
you must not be surprised to find daily proof that you
really are a sinner so long as you live.

5. Let me, in the last place, offer some counsel to all
who have peace with God, and desire to keep up a lively
sense of it.

It must never be forgotten that a believer’s sense of his
own justification and acceptance with God admits of many
degrees and variations. At one time it may be bright and
clear; at another dull and dim. At one time it may be
high and full, like the flood tide; at another low, like the
ebb. Our justification is a fixed, changeless, immovable
thing. But our sense of justification is hable to many
changes.

What then are the best means of preserving in a
believer’s heart that lively sense of justification which is
so precious to the soul that knows it? I offer a few hints
to believers. I lay no claim to infallibility in setting
down these hints, for I am only a man, But such as they
are I offer them,

(a) To keep up a lively sense of peace, there must be
constant looking to Jesus. As the pilot keeps his eye
on the mark by which he steers, so must we keep our eye
on Christ.
