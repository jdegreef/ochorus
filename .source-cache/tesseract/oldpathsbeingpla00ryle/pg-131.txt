(2) My second word of application shall be a request.
I make it to all readers of this paper who feel they have
no hope and desire to have it. It is a short simple re-
quest. I entreat them to seck “a good hope” while it can
be found.

A good hope is within the reach of any man, if he is
only willing to seek it. It is called emphatically in Serip-
ture, a “good hope through grace.” It is freely oftered,
even as it was freely purchased: it may be freely obtained,
“without money and without price.” Our past lives do
not make it impossible to obtain it, however bad they may
have been; our present weaknesses and infirmities do nut
shut us out, however great they may be. The same grace
which provided mankind with a hope, makes a free, full,
and unlimited invitation :—“ Whosoever will, let him take
the water of life freely;”—“Ask, and it shall be given
you ; seek, and ye shall find.” (Rev. xxi. 17; Matt. vii. 7.)

The Lord Jesus Christ is able and willing to give “a
good hope” to all who really want it. He is sealed and
appointed by God the Father to give the bread of life to
all that hunger, and the water of life to all that thirst. “ It
pleased the Father that in Him should all fulness dwell.”
(Coloss. i. 19.) In Him there is pardon and peace with
God, bought by the precious blood which He shed upon
the cross. In Him there is joy and peace for any be-
liever, and a solid, well-grounded expectation of good
things to come. In Him there is rest for the weary,
refuge for the fearful, a cleansing fountain for the unclean,
