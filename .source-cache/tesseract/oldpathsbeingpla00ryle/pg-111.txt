IV,

OUR HOPE!

“ Good hope through grace.” —2 Tuuss. ii. 16.

“T Hops,” is a very common expression. Everybody
can say, “I hope.” About no subject is the expression used
so commonly as it is about religion. Nothing is more
frequent than to hear men turn off some home-thrust at
conscience by this convenient form of words, “I hope.”
—“f hope it will be all right at last.’—*I hope I shalt
be a better man some day.”—“TI hope we shall all get to
heaven.’—But why do they hope? On what is their hope
built 2? Too often they cannot tell you! Too often it isa
mere excuse for avoiding a disagreeable subject. “Hoping,”
they live on. “ Hoping,” they grow old. “ Hoping,” they
die at last,—and find too often that they are lost for ever
in hell.

Task the serious attention of all who read this paper.
The subject is one of the deepest importance: “We are
saved by hope.” (Rom. viii. 24.) Let us, then, make sure
that our hope is sound.—Have we a hope that our sins are
pardoned, our hearts renewed, and our souls at peace
with God? Then let us see to it that our hope is “ good,”
and “lively,’ and one ‘that maketh not ashamed.”
(2 Thess. ii. 16; 1 Pet. i.8; Rom. v. 5.) Let us consider
our ways. Let us not shrink from honest, searching in-
